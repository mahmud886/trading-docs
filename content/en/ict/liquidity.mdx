## What is Liquidity in ICT?

In ICT methodology, **liquidity** refers to resting orders in the market — primarily **stop losses** and **pending orders** that sit above swing highs and below swing lows. The market-making algorithm's PRIMARY objective is to deliver price to these liquidity pools.

<Callout type="info" title="ICT Core Truth">
"Price is always seeking liquidity. Every move, every manipulation, every displacement — it's all about liquidity." — ICT
</Callout>

---

## Types of Liquidity

### Buy-Side Liquidity (BSL)
Resting orders **above** swing highs:
- Stop losses from short sellers
- Buy stop orders from breakout traders
- Forms above equal highs, relative highs, and old highs
- When swept, provides sell-side counterparty for institutions entering shorts

### Sell-Side Liquidity (SSL)
Resting orders **below** swing lows:
- Stop losses from long traders
- Sell stop orders from breakdown traders
- Forms below equal lows, relative lows, and old lows
- When swept, provides buy-side counterparty for institutions entering longs

---

## Liquidity Pools

### Types of Liquidity Pools

**1. Relative Equal Highs/Lows**
- Multiple swing points at similar price levels
- Creates obvious "support/resistance" that retail traders use for stops
- Most targeted by the algorithm

**2. Session Highs/Lows**
- Asian session high/low
- London session high/low
- Previous day high/low (PDH/PDL)
- Previous week high/low (PWH/PWL)

**3. Old Highs/Lows**
- Swing highs/lows from days or weeks ago
- Larger liquidity pools = bigger institutional interest
- Weekly/Monthly highs and lows are major targets

**4. Trendline Liquidity**
- Stop losses placed along trendlines
- When trendline "breaks," it's sweeping this liquidity
- Classic retail trap setup

## How Institutions Use Liquidity

### The Liquidity Cycle
1. **Identify** — Algorithm locates the nearest liquidity pool
2. **Engineer** — Price consolidates to build more resting orders
3. **Sweep** — Quick move to trigger stops and pending orders
4. **Absorb** — Institution fills their position against these triggered orders
5. **Displace** — True directional move begins

### Real Example: EUR/USD
- Price forms 3 equal lows at 1.0850 (SSL builds up)
- Retail traders place buy stops above and sell stops below
- Price dips to 1.0845 — sweeps SSL
- Institutions absorb sell orders to build long positions
- Price displaces upward to 1.0950 targeting BSL

## Liquidity Sweep vs. Liquidity Grab

| Aspect | Liquidity Sweep | Liquidity Grab |
|--------|----------------|----------------|
| **Speed** | Quick wick, fast rejection | May hold briefly beyond level |
| **Candle** | Long wick, small body | Can be full body then reversal |
| **Timeframe** | Visible on LTF | Visible on all timeframes |
| **Confirmation** | Immediate displacement opposite | MSS/CHOCH after the grab |
| **Reliability** | Very high when aligned with HTF | High but needs confirmation |

## Engineered Liquidity

Sometimes the market **creates** liquidity before sweeping it:

### How It's Engineered
1. Price creates a tight range (consolidation)
2. Retail traders place stops above and below the range
3. Equal highs/lows form, attracting more orders
4. The algorithm waits until sufficient liquidity builds
5. Then sweeps one side → reverses to sweep the other side

### The "Judas Swing" Connection
- London open often sweeps Asian range liquidity first (Judas Swing)
- This is engineered manipulation to collect liquidity
- After sweeping, the TRUE direction of the day begins

## Trading with Liquidity

### Step-by-Step Process
1. **Mark PDH/PDL, PWH/PWL** on your chart
2. **Identify equal highs/lows** — these are the targets
3. **Determine daily bias** — which liquidity pool will be targeted?
4. **Wait for sweep** — price takes out the liquidity
5. **Confirm reversal** — MSS on LTF after the sweep
6. **Enter** — At OB/FVG after the sweep with stop beyond the extreme

### Example: Gold Liquidity Sweep Trade
- **PDH:** 2,050 (BSL resting above)
- **Daily Bias:** Bearish (price in premium)
- **Action:** Price sweeps 2,052 (takes BSL)
- **Confirmation:** 5M MSS bearish after sweep
- **Entry:** Sell at 2,048 (bearish OB)
- **Stop:** 2,053 (above sweep)
- **Target:** SSL at 2,020
- **R:R:** 1:5.6
- **BD Time:** 7:45 PM (NY AM Kill Zone)

## Common Mistakes

<Callout type="warning" title="Liquidity Trading Errors">

**❌ Fading every sweep** — Not every liquidity sweep leads to reversal. Need HTF alignment.

**❌ Ignoring the draw** — Always ask "what is the draw on liquidity?" before trading.

**❌ Placing stops at obvious levels** — Your stops ARE the liquidity. Place beyond manipulation zone.

**❌ Trading before the sweep** — Wait for liquidity to be taken before entering.

**❌ Not marking session levels** — PDH/PDL, Asian high/low are critical reference points.

</Callout>
