## What is a Fair Value Gap?

A Fair Value Gap (FVG) is a **3-candle price imbalance** where the wicks of candle 1 and candle 3 do NOT overlap, leaving a gap created by candle 2's body. This gap represents an area where price was delivered too aggressively in one direction — an inefficiency the algorithm will likely revisit.

<Callout type="info" title="ICT Definition">
"A Fair Value Gap is the range between the high of candle 1 and the low of candle 3 (bullish) or the low of candle 1 and the high of candle 3 (bearish). It represents an imbalance in price delivery." — ICT
</Callout>

---

## Bullish vs Bearish FVG

### Bullish FVG (BISI — Buy-Side Imbalance, Sell-Side Inefficiency)
- Candle 1: Any candle
- Candle 2: Strong bullish displacement candle
- Candle 3: Any candle
- **Gap** = High of Candle 1 to Low of Candle 3
- Price returns DOWN to fill this gap (buy entry)

### Bearish FVG (SIBI — Sell-Side Imbalance, Buy-Side Inefficiency)
- Candle 1: Any candle
- Candle 2: Strong bearish displacement candle  
- Candle 3: Any candle
- **Gap** = Low of Candle 1 to High of Candle 3
- Price returns UP to fill this gap (sell entry)

## Consequent Encroachment (CE)

The **midpoint (50%)** of any FVG is called Consequent Encroachment. ICT teaches that:

- CE is the **optimal entry point** within an FVG
- If price trades through CE, the FVG is considered **violated**
- A respected CE = strong institutional interest
- Use CE as your precision entry rather than the full FVG range

### How to Calculate CE
```
Bullish FVG CE = (High of Candle 1 + Low of Candle 3) / 2
Bearish FVG CE = (Low of Candle 1 + High of Candle 3) / 2
```

## Types of FVGs in ICT

### 1. Standard FVG
Regular 3-candle imbalance — most common, used for entries

### 2. Implied FVG (IFVG)  
When wicks DO slightly overlap but the bodies don't — still treated as an FVG because the body range was inefficient

### 3. Inversion FVG
A previously bearish FVG that gets fully filled and then acts as **support** (or vice versa). The FVG "inverts" its role:
- Bearish FVG filled → becomes bullish support
- Bullish FVG filled → becomes bearish resistance

### 4. Volume Imbalance (VI)
A gap between the close of one candle and the open of the next — different from FVG but related concept

### 5. Opening Range Gap
Gap between previous day's close and current day's open — institutional gap

## FVG Trading Rules

### Entry Rules
1. **HTF Direction** — Only trade FVGs that align with Daily/4H bias
2. **Premium/Discount** — Bullish FVGs in discount zones, bearish FVGs in premium zones
3. **Kill Zone Timing** — FVGs formed during Kill Zones are highest probability
4. **Displacement** — The candle creating the FVG should show strong momentum
5. **CE Entry** — Enter at the 50% (CE) of the FVG for optimal risk:reward

### Invalidation Rules
- FVG is **invalid** if price closes through it entirely (full body close beyond)
- FVG is **weakened** if price trades through CE without respecting it
- FVGs on higher timeframes override lower timeframe FVGs
- Old FVGs (more than 40 candles back) lose significance

## Real Trading Examples

### Example 1: EUR/USD — Bullish FVG Entry
- **Date Context:** London session
- **HTF Bias:** Daily bullish — price in discount, targeting BSL at 1.0950
- **Setup:** 15M shows displacement up from 1.0880, creating bullish FVG (1.0885 - 1.0895)
- **CE Level:** 1.0890
- **Entry:** Limit buy at 1.0890 (CE)
- **Stop Loss:** Below FVG low at 1.0882 (8 pips)
- **Target:** BSL at 1.0950 (60 pips)
- **R:R:** 1:7.5
- **Time (BD):** Setup formed at 2:30 PM BD (London Kill Zone)

### Example 2: Gold (XAU/USD) — Bearish FVG
- **HTF Bias:** 4H bearish — price in premium after rally to 2,045
- **Setup:** 5M NY AM session shows bearish FVG at 2,042-2,038 after MSS
- **CE Level:** 2,040
- **Entry:** Limit sell at 2,040
- **Stop Loss:** Above FVG high at 2,043 (30 pips)
- **Target:** SSL at 2,015 (250 pips)
- **R:R:** 1:8.3
- **Time (BD):** 8:00 PM BD (NY AM Kill Zone)

### Example 3: NAS100 — Inversion FVG
- **Setup:** Previous bearish FVG at 17,850-17,820 gets fully filled
- **Price returns** to this same zone and it now acts as SUPPORT
- **Entry:** Buy at the top of the old bearish FVG (17,850)
- **Concept:** The algorithm rebalanced the inefficiency; now the level inverts

## Multi-Timeframe FVG Analysis

| Timeframe | FVG Role | Usage |
|-----------|----------|-------|
| **Monthly/Weekly** | Major imbalances — price will travel to fill these | Directional bias, swing targets |
| **Daily** | Key levels for the week | Intraday bias, area of interest |
| **4H** | Refined entry zones | Swing trade entries |
| **15M/5M** | Precision entries | Scalp/day trade entries |
| **1M** | Sniper entries | Exact entry at CE |

### Rule: HTF FVGs > LTF FVGs
A 15M FVG inside a Daily FVG = very high probability. A 15M FVG opposing the Daily FVG = low probability.

## Common Mistakes

<Callout type="warning" title="FVG Trading Errors">

**❌ Trading every FVG** — Only trade FVGs aligned with HTF bias and in proper PD zone

**❌ Ignoring CE** — Entering at the edge of FVG gives poor R:R. Use CE for precision

**❌ No displacement** — Weak, small-bodied FVGs are not institutional. Look for strong candle 2

**❌ Trading old FVGs** — FVGs older than 20-40 candles lose relevance

**❌ Forgetting time** — FVGs created outside Kill Zones are lower probability

</Callout>
