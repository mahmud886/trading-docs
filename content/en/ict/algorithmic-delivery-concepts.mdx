## What Is Algorithmic Price Delivery?

ICT describes price as being **delivered** by what he calls the **Interbank Price Delivery Algorithm (IPDA)**: price moves from one pool of liquidity to another, and rebalances inefficiencies (imbalances) on the way.

Whether or not a single "algorithm" exists, the model is a useful **map**: it tells you what price is likely reaching for next (liquidity) and where it may pause or reverse on the way (imbalances).

<Callout type="warning" title="A model, not a fact">
  The original stated that markets "are NOT driven by random supply and demand" but by the IPDA, and quoted ICT without
  a source. That's ICT's claim, not an established fact about how markets work. Use the model for what it does well,
  organising liquidity and imbalances, and judge it by your own results.
</Callout>

## The Sequence

| Step | What happens                                              | What you see                                |
| ---- | --------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| ①    | Price runs a pool of **liquidity** (A)                    | A sweep of equal highs/lows or an old swing |
| ②    | It reverses with displacement and leaves an **imbalance** | An FVG or liquidity void                    |
| ③    | It often returns to **rebalance** part of it              | A retrace into the FVG                      |
| ④    | It moves on to the next pool of **liquidity** (B)         | The opposite highs/lows are taken           |

## Using the 20/40/60-Day Ranges

The IPDA ranges give the "where":

1. On the daily chart, mark the high and low of the last **20, 40 and 60** trading days.
2. Untouched extremes are potential targets.
3. Your **daily bias** decides which side price is likely to seek first.

**Example:** the 20-day range is **1.0800 – 1.0950** and price is at **1.0870**. Both extremes are untouched. With a bullish bias, 1.0950 is the draw; with a bearish one, 1.0800.

A full walkthrough with a chart is in [Monthly Profiles](/en/ict/monthly-profiles).

## Three Patterns

| Pattern                         | In practice                                                                                               |
| ------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Liquidity → FVG → liquidity** | The sequence above; enter on the step-③ retrace                                                           |
| **FVG rebalancing**             | Price often revisits recent FVGs, but not always, and not always fully                                    |
| **Time-based delivery**         | Displacement tends to start in kill zones and macros; see [Time and Price](/en/ict/time-and-price-theory) |

<Callout type="warning" title="Corrected: 'price always comes back to the gap'">
  The original said this is "why price always comes back to the gap". It doesn't always. Strong trends leave FVGs that
  stay open for a long time. Treat an FVG as a likely reaction zone, not a promise.
</Callout>

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake                              | Fix                                     |
| ------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------- |
| Believing every move is "programmed" | It's a model; plenty of moves don't fit |
| Targeting both pools at once         | Bias decides which pool comes first     |
| Expecting every FVG to fill          | Some never do; wait for confirmation    |
| Ignoring news                        | Big data can override any map           |

## Practice

1. On 20 days of 1H charts, mark the nearest liquidity above and below before London opens.
2. Note which was taken first and whether an FVG was rebalanced on the way.
3. Log the results in your [trading journal](/en/tools/journal-template).

## Summary

| Element         | Role                                         |
| --------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| **Liquidity**   | Where price is going (pools A and B)         |
| **Imbalance**   | Where it may pause or turn on the way        |
| **IPDA ranges** | Which pools matter (20/40/60-day highs/lows) |
| **Bias**        | Which pool comes first                       |
| **Time**        | When the move is likely to start             |
