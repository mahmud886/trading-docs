## Why the London Open Matters

The London session brings the first big wave of volume of the trading day. For EUR, GBP and gold in particular, the open often:

- **takes one side of the Asian range**, where stops from the quiet overnight session have collected;
- forms a **false first move**, the [Judas swing](/en/ict/judas-swing), before the real direction starts;
- sets a high or low that **holds for the rest of the day**.

<Callout type="warning" title="Removed: the '60 %' claim">
  The original lesson said that "studies show the daily high or low is formed during the London kill zone over 60 % of
  the time". No study was cited, and the figure depends heavily on the instrument and the period. Test it on your own
  pair (see Practice) instead of relying on a number.
</Callout>

## Timeline in Bangladesh Time

| Phase               | New York time  | BD — US summer      | BD — US winter  | What to do                         |
| ------------------- | -------------- | ------------------- | --------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| Pre-London          | 1:00 – 2:00 AM | 11:00 AM – 12:00 PM | 12:00 – 1:00 PM | Mark the Asian range; set the bias |
| London KZ opens     | 2:00 AM        | 12:00 PM            | 1:00 PM         | Watch for the false move           |
| Typical Judas swing | 2:30 – 3:30 AM | 12:30 – 1:30 PM     | 1:30 – 2:30 PM  | Wait for the sweep to finish       |
| London KZ ends      | 5:00 AM        | 3:00 PM             | 4:00 PM         | No new London entries after this   |

US summer time runs from mid-March to early November. See [Kill Zones](/en/ict/ict-kill-zones) for the full table.

## The London Open Model

### Step 1 — Mark the Asian range

- Asian session high (ASH) and low (ASL)
- The range midpoint (50 %)
- Any equal highs or lows inside it

### Step 2 — Decide the daily bias before London

Use the higher-timeframe structure, the [dealing range](/en/ict/dealing-range), and the [draw on liquidity](/en/ict/liquidity). See [Daily Bias](/en/ict/daily-bias).

### Step 3 — Wait for the Judas swing

| Bias        | Expect London to…                    | Liquidity taken |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------ | --------------- |
| **Bullish** | First drop **below the Asian low**   | Sell-side (SSL) |
| **Bearish** | First spike **above the Asian high** | Buy-side (BSL)  |

### Step 4 — Confirm and enter

1. A close back inside the range, then a 5m or 15m [MSS](/en/ict/mss) with displacement.
2. Enter on the retrace into the [order block](/en/smc/order-blocks) or [FVG](/en/ict/fvg) the displacement left.
3. Stop beyond the Judas swing extreme; target the opposite side's liquidity.

<Callout type="tip" title="When London doesn't fake">
  Sometimes London breaks out of the Asian range and keeps going. If the break **holds** (closes beyond, retest holds)
  and agrees with your bias, there's no Judas swing to fade. Either trade the continuation or stand aside, but don't
  fade a break that agrees with the bias.
</Callout>

## Example 1 — Bullish London Open, EUR/USD (5m)

![Bullish London open — EUR/USD 5m: Judas swing below the Asian low, MSS, order-block entry](/images/ict/london-open/eurusd-london-open-bull-dark.png)

<Callout type="info" title="About these examples">
  The charts use **synthetic candles** built around the levels in this lesson (US summer time). They show how the method
  works and are not records of real trades. R:R values are the planned risk-to-reward, not results.
</Callout>

1. **Asian range:** **1.0860 – 1.0878**. The daily bias is **bullish**.
2. **Judas swing:** at **12:20 PM BD** London drops below the Asian low to **1.0854**.
3. **MSS:** at **12:35 PM BD** a 13-pip displacement closes above the 1.0868 lower high.
4. **Entry:** the last down-close candle before the move is the bullish OB. Price returns to it. Buy at **1.0862**.

| Level   | Price       | Note                         |
| ------- | ----------- | ---------------------------- |
| Entry   | 1.0862      | Bullish OB                   |
| Stop    | 1.0850      | Below the 1.0854 swing low   |
| Target  | 1.0920      | Buy-side liquidity           |
| **R:R** | **1 : 4.8** | Risk 12 pips, reward 58 pips |

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake                                      | Fix                                                              |
| -------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Buying the first breakout of the Asian range | The first move is often the trap; wait for the close back inside |
| No bias before London                        | Decide direction before 12 PM BD (summer)                        |
| Entering during the Judas swing              | Wait for the MSS                                                 |
| Entering after 3 PM BD (summer)              | The London window has closed; wait for NY                        |
| Forgetting the winter shift                  | Add one hour from early November to mid-March                    |

## Practice

1. For 20 London sessions, record which side of the Asian range was taken first and whether price then reversed.
2. Record on how many days the day's high or low formed in the London window. That gives you your own statistic for your pair.
3. Mark the MSS and entry for each reversal day, and calculate the R:R.
4. Log it in your [trading journal](/en/tools/journal-template).

## Summary

| Aspect        | Key point                                          |
| ------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| **Window**    | 12–3 PM BD (summer), 1–4 PM BD (winter)            |
| **Reference** | The Asian range high and low                       |
| **Model**     | Bias → Judas swing against it → MSS → OB/FVG entry |
| **Stop**      | Beyond the Judas swing extreme                     |
| **Don't**     | Fade a break that agrees with your bias and holds  |
