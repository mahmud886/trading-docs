## Why Options Flow Matters

The options market is where many large traders take positions on direction and volatility. Two things make it useful to a spot or futures trader:

1. **Positioning:** large, unusual trades can show where some participants expect price to go.
2. **Hedging flows:** the dealers on the other side hedge with the underlying, and that hedging can push price or hold it near big strikes.

<Callout type="info" title="Information, not a signal on its own">
  The original said a single large options trade "tells you more than a week of price action" and that unusual activity
  "often leads spot by 1–3 days". Neither has a source. Many big trades are hedges or spreads, not directional bets.
  Treat flow as context for levels you've already found on the chart.
</Callout>

---

## Who Is on Each Side

| Participant                     | Typical role                                                  |
| ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Funds, speculators, hedgers** | Buy or sell options to bet on, or protect against, a move     |
| **Dealers / market makers**     | Take the other side, then **delta-hedge** with the underlying |
| **The hedging**                 | Is what shows up on your chart                                |

| Who bought the options? | Dealer is…      | As price rises, dealer…  | Effect on price                                     |
| ----------------------- | --------------- | ------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------- |
| **Clients bought**      | **Short gamma** | Buys more to stay hedged | **Adds to moves** (both ways)                       |
| **Clients sold**        | **Long gamma**  | Sells into the rally     | **Fades moves**; price can **pin** near big strikes |

<Callout type="warning" title="Corrected: the hedging example">
  The original's example had a fund buying 10,000 calls at 1.1000, the dealer buying spot to hedge, and that buying
  "pushing price toward 1.1000", then "pinning" near expiry. That mixes up the two cases. A dealer who **sold** calls is
  **short gamma**: as price rises they must buy more, which can push price **through** the strike, not pin it. Pinning
  comes from dealers who are **long gamma**, usually because clients sold options at that strike.
</Callout>

## What to Look At

| Data                        | What it can tell you                                   | Caveat                                                                      |
| --------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Open interest by strike** | Strikes where hedging flows may concentrate            | OI doesn't say who is long or short                                         |
| **Unusual volume**          | A trade much larger than usual for that contract       | Could be a hedge, a spread or closing                                       |
| **Gamma exposure (GEX)**    | Whether dealers are likely long or short gamma overall | Estimates rest on assumptions; see [Gamma Exposure](/en/ict/gamma-exposure) |
| **Max pain**                | The strike where option holders lose most at expiry    | Weak on its own; see [Max Pain](/en/ict/max-pain-theory)                    |
| **Put/call ratio**          | Sentiment extremes                                     | Noisy; see [Put/Call Ratio](/en/ict/put-call-ratio)                         |
| **Expiry dates**            | When pinning or unwinding is most likely               | See [Expiry Pinning](/en/ict/options-expiry-pinning)                        |

<Callout type="info" title="Data availability">
  Options flow is most useful for US equities and indices (SPX, SPY, QQQ, single stocks), where volume and open interest
  are public. FX options trade mostly over the counter: you'll see summaries such as 25-delta risk reversals and
  reported large expiries, not full flow. Crypto options (mainly on Deribit) are public but thinner.
</Callout>

## Combining Flow With ICT

| Situation                                                           | How to use it                                             |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| A big open-interest strike sits on an ICT level (OB, FVG, old high) | The level gets more weight                                |
| Dealers estimated long gamma, price near a big strike               | Expect ranges and sweeps that fail; favour fades          |
| Dealers estimated short gamma                                       | Expect moves to extend; favour continuation after the MSS |
| Monthly expiry week                                                 | Expect pinning into expiry and a release after it         |

The original claimed max pain "aligns with ICT equilibrium" and GEX levels "often align with order blocks". Sometimes they do; check each case rather than assuming it.

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake                               | Fix                                              |
| ------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| Reading every big call buy as bullish | It could be a hedge, a spread or someone closing |
| Assuming dealers always pin price     | Only long-gamma dealers dampen moves             |
| Using equity flow ideas on FX blindly | FX data is partial and over the counter          |
| Trading flow without a chart level    | Flow adds context; the chart gives the entry     |

## Practice

1. Pick SPY or QQQ. Each Monday, note the three strikes with the largest open interest for Friday's expiry.
2. On Friday, record how close the close was to those strikes.
3. Note whether that week had high or low estimated GEX. Log it in your [trading journal](/en/tools/journal-template).

## Summary

| Idea            | Key point                                              |
| --------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Flow**        | Shows positioning, but many trades aren't directional  |
| **Short gamma** | Dealer hedging adds to moves                           |
| **Long gamma**  | Dealer hedging fades moves; pinning near strikes       |
| **Best use**    | Extra weight for levels you already found on the chart |
