## What Is a Judas Swing?

The **Judas swing** is the **false first move** of a session, in the opposite direction to the day's real move. It takes the liquidity on one side of the range, traps the traders who followed it, and then reverses.

The name refers to betrayal: the move looks like a genuine breakout, but the direction is the wrong one. In the [Power of 3](/en/ict/power-of-3) model it is the **manipulation** leg.

<Callout type="info" title="One-line version">
  Decide the bias before the session. Then expect the open to move **against** that bias first. The Judas swing is that
  move, and its end is where you look for the real entry.
</Callout>

## Bullish Day vs Bearish Day

![The Judas swing on a bullish day and on a bearish day](/images/ict/judas-swing/judas-swing-days-dark.png)

| Step | **Bullish day**                                  | **Bearish day**                                 |
| ---- | ------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------- |
| 1    | The session opens                                | The session opens                               |
| 2    | Price **drops** first, below the Asian low       | Price **rallies** first, above the Asian high   |
| 3    | Sell stops below the range are taken (SSL)       | Buy stops above the range are taken (BSL)       |
| 4    | Breakdown sellers join; the move looks bearish   | Breakout buyers join; the move looks bullish    |
| 5    | Price reverses up with displacement (MSS)        | Price reverses down with displacement (MSS)     |
| 6    | Trapped shorts' stops add fuel; target BSL above | Trapped longs' stops add fuel; target SSL below |

## When It Happens (Bangladesh Time)

The Judas swing usually forms in the **first part of a kill zone**:

| Session      | Kill zone (BD, US summer) | Kill zone (BD, US winter) | Typical Judas window                                                                   |
| ------------ | ------------------------- | ------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **London**   | 12:00 – 3:00 PM           | 1:00 – 4:00 PM            | First 30–90 minutes                                                                    |
| **New York** | 5:00 – 8:00 PM            | 6:00 – 9:00 PM            | Around the 8:30 AM NY data and the 9:30 AM NY stock open (6:30 – 8:30 PM BD in summer) |

<Callout type="warning" title="Corrected times">
  The original lesson gave the Asian range as "12 AM – 9 AM BD" and the London Judas window as "2 – 4 PM BD". Neither
  matches the rest of the site. The Asian range is **6 AM – 12 PM BD** in US summer (7 AM – 1 PM in winter). The example
  below uses the original's times, which fit **US winter**, so it's labelled that way.
</Callout>

## Judas Swing or Real Breakout?

| Clue                   | Judas swing                             | Real breakout                         |
| ---------------------- | --------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| Direction vs your bias | **Against** it                          | **With** it                           |
| After taking the level | Closes back inside, then displaces away | Closes beyond and holds; retest holds |
| Structure              | MSS in the opposite direction           | Continues in the breakout direction   |
| Timing                 | Early in the kill zone                  | Any time                              |

A breakout **in the direction of your bias** that holds is not a Judas swing. Don't fade it. See [Sweep or Run](/en/ict/liquidity).

## Trading It, Step by Step

1. **Before the session:** set the daily bias using the higher timeframe and the [draw on liquidity](/en/ict/liquidity).
2. **Mark the Asian range:** its high, low and midpoint.
3. **Wait** for the open to move against your bias and take one side of the range.
4. **Confirm:** a close back inside, then an [MSS](/en/ict/mss) or CHOCH with displacement on 5m/15m.
5. **Enter** at the [order block](/en/smc/order-blocks) or [FVG](/en/ict/fvg) left by the reversal.
6. **Stop** beyond the Judas swing extreme; **target** the opposite side's liquidity.

1. **Bias:** bullish (daily demand). The Asian range is **1.0880 – 1.0900**.
2. **Judas swing:** at **2:00 PM BD** London drops through the Asian low to **1.0868**.
3. **CHOCH:** at **2:45 PM BD** an 18-pip displacement closes above the 1.0889 lower high.
4. **Entry:** the last down-close candle before the move is the bullish OB. Price returns to it. Buy at **1.0875**.

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake                                 | Fix                                           |
| --------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| Chasing the first move                  | It's the trap; wait for the reversal          |
| No bias before the session              | Without a bias, every breakout looks real     |
| Fading a breakout that agrees with bias | Only fade moves _against_ your bias           |
| Entering before the MSS                 | Wait for the close back inside and the shift  |
| Using summer times in winter            | Add one hour from early November to mid-March |

## Practice

1. For 20 sessions, write the bias before the open, then mark which way the first move went.
2. Count how often the first move was against the bias and then reversed, versus continued.
3. For each Judas day, mark the MSS, the entry and the R:R.
4. Log it in your [trading journal](/en/tools/journal-template).

## Summary

| Aspect          | Key point                                                   |
| --------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Definition**  | The session's false first move, against the day's direction |
| **Bullish day** | Drops first, takes SSL, then rallies                        |
| **Bearish day** | Rallies first, takes BSL, then drops                        |
| **When**        | Early in the London or NY kill zone                         |
| **Key**         | Bias before the session; MSS before the entry               |
