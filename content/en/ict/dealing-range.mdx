## What is a Dealing Range?

A Dealing Range is the **price range between the most recent significant swing high and swing low**. It defines the "playing field" where price is currently operating. The equilibrium (50%) of this range divides price into premium (sell zone) and discount (buy zone).

<Callout type="info" title="Institutional Framework">
  The Dealing Range gives you context for EVERY trade. Are you buying in discount? Selling in premium? Without knowing
  the dealing range, you can't determine premium vs discount. This is exactly how JP Morgan, Goldman Sachs, and Citadel
  frame their directional risk.
</Callout>

---

## How to Identify the Dealing Range

### Steps

1. Find the most recent **significant swing high** (BSL target above)
2. Find the most recent **significant swing low** (SSL target below)
3. The range between these = your Dealing Range
4. Mark the **50% equilibrium** — this divides premium and discount
5. Update when either extreme is broken (new dealing range forms)

### Timeframe Hierarchy

- **Monthly Dealing Range** — Macro context (central banks, sovereign wealth funds)
- **Weekly Dealing Range** — Swing trade context (hedge funds, prop desks)
- **Daily Dealing Range** — Day trading context (institutional day desks)
- **4H Dealing Range** — Intraday precision (prop firm traders)
- **Session Dealing Range** — Scalping context (HFT-like execution)

### What Makes a "Significant" Swing?

- Creates BSL or SSL above/below it (resting orders exist)
- Causes displacement on the move away from it
- Is visible on the timeframe you're analyzing
- Has multiple touches or equal highs/lows nearby
- Was created during a Kill Zone or significant session

## Premium, Discount & Equilibrium

### The Three Zones

| Zone            | Range                    | Action                  | Institutional Logic                  |
| --------------- | ------------------------ | ----------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| **Premium**     | 50% to 100% (swing high) | SELL setups only        | Banks distribute/sell positions here |
| **Equilibrium** | Exactly 50%              | Neutral — avoid entries | Fair value — no edge for either side |
| **Discount**    | 0% (swing low) to 50%    | BUY setups only         | Banks accumulate/buy positions here  |

### How to Calculate

```
Equilibrium = (Swing High + Swing Low) / 2
Premium zone = Equilibrium to Swing High
Discount zone = Swing Low to Equilibrium
```

### Example Calculation

- Swing High: 1.1000
- Swing Low: 1.0800
- Equilibrium: (1.1000 + 1.0800) / 2 = **1.0900**
- Premium: 1.0900 – 1.1000 (sell zone)
- Discount: 1.0800 – 1.0900 (buy zone)

### Fibonacci Overlay

ICT uses the dealing range with Fibonacci levels for precision:

- **0%** = Swing Low (extreme discount — deepest buy zone)
- **25%** = Deep discount (ideal accumulation zone for banks)
- **50%** = Equilibrium (CE of dealing range — fair value)
- **62%-79%** = OTE zone (Optimal Trade Entry for sells in premium)
- **75%** = Premium (ideal distribution zone for banks)
- **100%** = Swing High (extreme premium — deepest sell zone)

## Nested Dealing Ranges (Fractal Nature)

Markets are **fractal** — dealing ranges exist within dealing ranges. Understanding this hierarchy is what separates institutional traders from retail:

### Macro Dealing Range (Monthly/Weekly)

- Determines the BIG picture directional bias
- Banks and central banks operate primarily on this level
- Takes weeks to months to fully traverse
- Breaking this range = major trend change

### Internal Dealing Range (Daily/4H)

- Sits INSIDE the macro range
- Where institutional day desks and swing traders operate
- Determines your daily bias within the larger context
- Multiple internal ranges can form within one macro range

### Micro Dealing Range (1H/15M)

- For precision entry timing only
- Your execution framework within the internal range
- Where you actually place limit orders
- Changes rapidly — must be updated frequently

### The Alignment Rule

**When ALL timeframe dealing ranges agree on direction = highest probability:**

- Weekly in discount + Daily in discount + 4H showing bullish MSS = STRONG BUY
- Weekly in premium + Daily in premium + 4H showing bearish MSS = STRONG SELL
- Conflict between timeframes = WAIT or REDUCE SIZE

## Dealing Range and Institutional Bias

### How Banks Use Dealing Ranges

1. **Accumulation happens in discount** — Banks buy when price is cheap (below 50%). They use SSL sweeps to get fills at better prices.
2. **Distribution happens in premium** — Banks sell when price is expensive (above 50%). They use BSL sweeps to sell at better prices.
3. **Manipulation crosses equilibrium** — Stop hunts push price through the 50% to trigger resting orders on both sides.
4. **Expansion targets opposing liquidity** — After accumulation in discount, price targets BSL in premium (and vice versa).

### Daily Bias from Dealing Range

**If price is in the DISCOUNT of your HTF dealing range:**

- Look for BUY setups on LTF
- Target: opposing BSL above equilibrium
- Expect manipulation below Asian range before the move up
- Best time: London or NY AM Kill Zone

**If price is in the PREMIUM of your HTF dealing range:**

- Look for SELL setups on LTF
- Target: opposing SSL below equilibrium
- Expect manipulation above before the move down
- Best time: London or NY AM Kill Zone

**If price is AT equilibrium (50%):**

- DO NOT TRADE — wait for commitment to one side
- Equilibrium is "no man's land" — no institutional edge
- Wait for price to push into premium or discount before acting

## PD Arrays Within the Dealing Range

### In Discount (Buy Zone) — Look for Bullish PD Arrays:

- Bullish Order Blocks (last bearish candle before rally)
- Bullish FVGs / BISI (imbalances from upward displacement)
- Bullish Breaker Blocks (failed bearish OBs inverted)
- Mitigation Blocks (previous failed longs broken even)
- Liquidity Voids below (price may drop to fill before rallying)

### In Premium (Sell Zone) — Look for Bearish PD Arrays:

- Bearish Order Blocks (last bullish candle before drop)
- Bearish FVGs / SIBI (imbalances from downward displacement)
- Bearish Breaker Blocks (failed bullish OBs inverted)
- Mitigation Blocks (previous failed shorts broken even)
- Liquidity Voids above (price may rally to fill before dropping)

### The Confluence Rule (Institutional Grade)

The HIGHEST probability setups occur when:

1. PD Array is in the correct zone (discount for buys, premium for sells)
2. PD Array aligns with HTF dealing range direction
3. Liquidity has been swept BEFORE reaching the PD Array
4. An MSS occurs on LTF confirming direction change
5. Time is within a Kill Zone (London: 12-3 PM BD / NY: 5-8 PM BD)
6. FVG + OB overlap (Unicorn setup)

- Bullish FVG at 1.0785-1.0795 created
- Entry: Buy limit at 1.0790 (CE of FVG)

**BD Time:** Setup formed at 1:30 PM (London Kill Zone)

**Trade Setup:**

## Dealing Range Lifecycle

### Phase 1: Formation

- Strong swing high and swing low are established
- Liquidity builds above high (BSL) and below low (SSL)
- Range is clearly defined — this is the new "playing field"

### Phase 2: Internal Activity (Where Most Trading Happens)

- Price oscillates between premium and discount
- **Internal liquidity** (minor swings within range) gets swept first
- Algorithm seeks internal liquidity BEFORE external
- Multiple PO3 cycles occur within the range
- Range can last days (Daily DR) to weeks (Weekly DR)

### Phase 3: Expansion (Range Break)

- After internal liquidity is exhausted
- Price breaks out to **external liquidity** (BSL or SSL at extremes)
- Old dealing range is violated — no longer valid
- NEW dealing range forms using the breakout extreme
- This is where the biggest moves happen

### When to Update Your Dealing Range

- When price **closes through** the swing high = update to new DR (bullish expansion)
- When price **closes through** the swing low = update to new DR (bearish expansion)
- A wick beyond doesn't count — need body close through
- Always identify the NEW dealing range immediately after a break

## Advanced: Dealing Range + Time

### Best Times for Dealing Range Reversals (BD Time)

- **12:00 PM - 3:00 PM BD** (London KZ) — Most common reversal from premium/discount
- **5:00 PM - 8:00 PM BD** (NY AM KZ) — Second best for reversals
- **6:00 AM - 10:00 AM BD** (Asian) — Range formation, not reversals

### Dealing Range + Power of 3

- **Accumulation** → price stays in discount (or premium for sells)
- **Manipulation** → price crosses equilibrium briefly (fake breakout)
- **Distribution** → price expands to opposing extreme of dealing range

## Common Mistakes

<Callout type="warning" title="Dealing Range Errors">

**❌ Not identifying the correct dealing range** — Using minor swings instead of significant ones. The swing must have created liquidity.

**❌ Buying in premium** — If price is above 50%, only look for sells. This is a cardinal rule.

**❌ Selling in discount** — If price is below 50%, only look for buys. Don't fight the discount.

**❌ Ignoring HTF dealing range** — Your LTF dealing range must align with HTF for high probability.

**❌ Not updating after a break** — Once a dealing range extreme is broken with a body close, identify the new range immediately.

**❌ Confusing internal vs external liquidity** — Internal = minor swings within range; External = the range extremes themselves.

**❌ Trading at equilibrium** — The 50% level offers no edge. Wait for price to commit to premium or discount.

</Callout>
