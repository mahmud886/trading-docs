## What Is Max Pain?

**Max pain** is the price at which, if the underlying settled there at expiry, **all the open options together would be worth the least** to the people holding them. In other words, the strike where option buyers lose the most and option sellers keep the most premium.

The **theory** is that price tends to drift toward max pain into expiry. The calculation is exact; the theory is not.

## How It's Calculated

For each possible settlement price **S** (usually each strike):

1. **Calls:** for every call strike K below S, add open interest × (S − K) × contract multiplier.
2. **Puts:** for every put strike K above S, add open interest × (K − S) × multiplier.
3. **Total** = calls + puts.

The strike with the **lowest total** is max pain.

Max pain here is **450**: put open interest is heavy below, call open interest heavy above, and 450 sits between them.

## Why Price Might Drift Toward It

The usual explanation is **dealer hedging**, not anyone "steering" price:

- If dealers are net **long** options around those strikes (clients sold them), they are **long gamma**: they sell rallies and buy dips, which can hold price near heavy strikes. See [Options Flow](/en/ict/options-flow-signals).
- As expiry nears, gamma at the nearest strikes grows, so that effect is strongest in the last day or two.

When dealers are **short** gamma instead, their hedging pushes price away from those strikes, and max pain has little pull.

<Callout type="warning" title="Limits">
  Max pain assumes every open contract is held by a buyer against a seller in the simplest way. In practice many
  positions are spreads and hedges, open interest changes daily, and big news overrides everything. Studies of the
  effect show it is weak and mostly limited to heavily traded single stocks and index ETFs in the final days.
</Callout>

## How to Use It

| Situation                                                    | Use                                     |
| ------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------- |
| Expiry is 1–3 days away, price is near max pain              | Expect ranging; avoid chasing breakouts |
| Max pain lines up with a chart level (OB, FVG, old high/low) | Give that level a little more weight    |
| Price is far from max pain early in the week                 | Ignore it; other forces dominate        |
| Big news before expiry                                       | Ignore it                               |

The original said max pain "aligns with ICT equilibrium = strong magnet". That happens sometimes, not as a rule.

## Where to Find It

Max pain is published for US stocks and ETFs by many options-data sites, usually by expiry date. For FX and most futures there is no public full open-interest picture, so max pain isn't available in the same way. Crypto option venues publish open interest that some sites turn into max pain.

---

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake                             | Fix                                                |
| ----------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| Treating max pain as a price target | It's a weak tendency in the last days only         |
| Using a stale number                | Open interest changes daily; recheck before expiry |
| Ignoring which expiry               | Weekly and monthly expiries give different numbers |
| Using it for FX                     | The data isn't there                               |
