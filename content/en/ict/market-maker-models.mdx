## What Are Market Maker Models?

**Market Maker Models** are ICT's map of a full swing: where price ranges, where it reverses, and where it expands. The **Market Maker Buy Model (MMBM)** describes a bullish swing; the **Market Maker Sell Model (MMSM)** is its mirror. They are [AMD](/en/ict/amd) on a larger scale.

Their use is orientation: knowing which stage you're in tells you whether to wait, prepare, enter or take profit.

<Callout type="info" title="The classic picture">
  ICT usually draws the MMBM as a curve: price steps **down** through a series of consolidations (the sell side of the
  curve), reverses at a smart-money reversal, then steps back **up** through the same levels (the buy side of the curve)
  toward the original consolidation. The stage list below is the buy-side half, which is where the trades are.
</Callout>

---

## The Stages

| Stage | MMBM (bullish)                                              | MMSM (bearish)                                         |
| ----- | ----------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| ①     | Original consolidation at the low                           | Original consolidation at the high                     |
| ②     | **Smart-money reversal**: sell-side swept, then MSS up      | Buy-side swept, then MSS down                          |
| ③     | First displacement leg up, leaving FVGs and OBs             | First leg down, leaving bearish FVGs and OBs           |
| ④     | **Re-accumulation**: pullback into those arrays — the entry | **Re-distribution**: pullback up into them — the entry |
| ⑤     | Second leg up toward buy-side liquidity                     | Second leg down toward sell-side liquidity             |
| ⑥     | Price reaches premium and stalls                            | Price reaches discount and stalls                      |

**Stop:** beyond the stage-② reversal extreme. **Target:** the liquidity at the far side of the curve.

<Callout type="info" title="About this example">
  The chart uses **synthetic candles** built around the levels in this lesson. It shows how the model works and is not a
  record of a real trade. R:R is the planned risk-to-reward.

1. **Consolidation:** five days at **1.0800 – 1.0830**.
2. **Reversal:** sell-side swept to **1.0785**, close back inside, then MSS above 1.0830.
3. **First leg:** to **1.0880**, leaving an FVG at **1.0840 – 1.0855**.
4. **Re-accumulation:** the pullback reaches the FVG's CE at **1.0848**. Buy.
5. **Second leg:** expansion toward buy-side at **1.0970**.

| Level   | Price       | Note                          |
| ------- | ----------- | ----------------------------- |
| Entry   | 1.0848      | FVG CE                        |
| Stop    | 1.0780      | Below the 1.0785 reversal low |
| Target  | 1.0970      | Buy-side liquidity            |
| **R:R** | **1 : 1.8** | Risk 68 pips, reward 122 pips |

<Callout type="warning" title="Corrected: R:R and OTE">
  The original said the pullback hit "FVG CE + OTE zone" and gave **R:R 1:8+**. Neither holds. With the stop below the
  reversal low, as the model says, the risk is 68 pips and the reward 122: **1 : 1.8**. And the OTE zone of the 1.0785 →
  1.0880 leg is **1.0805 – 1.0821**; 1.0848 is only a 34% retracement, so it's an FVG entry, not an OTE entry. A better
  R:R needs either an entry deeper in OTE or a tighter stop below the FVG, which is more likely to be hit.
</Callout>

### MMSM — NAS100 (4H, no chart)

Consolidation 18,000 – 18,100, buy-side swept to 18,150 and a bearish MSS, first leg down to 17,900, retrace to the bearish OB at **18,020**: sell. The stop above the reversal is **18,160**, the target **17,500**: risk 140, reward 520 points, **R:R 1 : 3.7**. The original said 1:7, which would need a stop of about 74 points, inside the reversal range. The OTE zone of that leg is 18,055 – 18,098, so 18,020 is again shallower than OTE.

## Where Am I in the Model?

| If price is…                      | Stage             | Action                         |
| --------------------------------- | ----------------- | ------------------------------ |
| Ranging after a decline           | ① Consolidation   | Wait for the sweep             |
| Sweeping lows, then displacing up | ②–③ Reversal      | Mark the new FVGs/OBs          |
| Pulling back into those arrays    | ④ Re-accumulation | Enter                          |
| Expanding toward highs            | ⑤ Expansion       | Hold; take partials            |
| Stalling in premium               | ⑥                 | Take profit; watch for an MMSM |

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake                            | Fix                                           |
| ---------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| Entering during consolidation      | It can last weeks; wait for the reversal      |
| Chasing the first leg              | Wait for the stage-④ pullback                 |
| Claiming big R:R with a tight stop | Measure risk from where the model invalidates |
| Wrong timeframe                    | Daily/4H for the model, 15m–1H for the entry  |
| Forgetting the cycle               | An MMBM's top is where an MMSM can start      |

## Practice

1. On daily charts of three pairs, find one MMBM and one MMSM from the past year.
2. Label the six stages and measure the R:R with the stop beyond stage ②.
3. Check whether the stage-④ pullback reached OTE or only the FVG. Log it in your [trading journal](/en/tools/journal-template).

## Summary

| Element     | MMBM                                           |
| ----------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| **Trigger** | Sell-side sweep + MSS (stage ②)                |
| **Entry**   | Pullback into the leg's FVG/OB (stage ④)       |
| **Stop**    | Below the reversal low                         |
| **Target**  | Buy-side liquidity                             |
| **R:R**     | Whatever the stop and target give — measure it |
