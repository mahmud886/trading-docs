## What Is the Put/Call Ratio?

The **put/call ratio (PCR)** divides put activity by call activity:

**PCR = put volume ÷ call volume** (or the same with open interest).

- A **high** ratio means traders are buying a lot of puts: **fear** or hedging.
- A **low** ratio means calls dominate: **optimism** or complacency.

Used as a **contrarian** gauge, extremes suggest the crowd is leaning too far one way.

## Which Ratio?

| Ratio          | What it includes          | Notes                                                          |
| -------------- | ------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Equity PCR** | Single-stock options      | Driven more by retail; the usual sentiment gauge               |
| **Index PCR**  | Index options (SPX, etc.) | Dominated by institutional hedging; often above 1 all the time |
| **Total PCR**  | Both                      | A blend                                                        |

Because index puts are used for portfolio hedging, a high index ratio is normal and says less about sentiment. Most traders watch the **equity** ratio, often as a 5- or 10-day average to cut noise.

What the chart shows:

1. In each decline the ratio climbed above 1.00 (**fear**) and **stayed there for days** while price kept falling.
2. The lows came as the ratio **fell back** below the fear line, not when it first crossed it.
3. Complacency readings during rallies came early, too: price kept rising for a while after the first one.

<Callout type="warning" title="Corrected: 'reliable contrarian indicator'">

  to confirm the turn.
</Callout>

## Using It With Price

| Ratio reading                    | Price confirmation to wait for                                                          |
| -------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Fear extreme, then falling back  | A sell-side [liquidity sweep](/en/ict/liquidity-sweep) and a bullish [MSS](/en/ict/mss) |
| Complacency extreme, then rising | A buy-side sweep and a bearish MSS                                                      |
| Neutral                          | Ignore the ratio                                                                        |

---

## Where to Get It

Cboe publishes daily equity, index and total put/call ratios for US options; many charting platforms show them too. There's no equivalent public ratio for spot FX. For FX, the closest sentiment measures are **25-delta risk reversals** and broker positioning data.

---

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake                             | Fix                                               |
| ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| Trading the first extreme reading   | Wait for the ratio to turn and price to confirm   |
| Using the index ratio for sentiment | Use the equity ratio; index PCR is mostly hedging |
| Using fixed thresholds forever      | Use percentiles of recent history                 |
| Reading one day                     | Smooth with a 5–10 day average                    |

## Practice

1. Download a year of the Cboe equity put/call ratio and the S&P 500 closes.
2. Mark the top and bottom 5% of 10-day-average readings.
3. Record what the index did over the following 10 days each time. Log it in your [trading journal](/en/tools/journal-template).
