## What Are Monthly Profiles?

**Monthly profiles** apply the same idea as [Weekly Profiles](/en/ict/weekly-profiles) to a month or quarter: price often builds a range, makes a false move that sets the month's high or low, and then expands toward liquidity.

Their practical use is **context**. They tell you which higher-timeframe levels price is likely drawing toward over the next few weeks, so that your daily bias points the same way.

<Callout type="info" title="Context, not a calendar">
  The original described quarterly "predictable characteristics" that "institutional traders follow". Seasonal patterns
  exist, but they are weak and change from year to year. Use the look-back ranges below first; use seasonality only as a
  tie-breaker.
</Callout>

## IPDA Look-Back Ranges

ICT's **IPDA data ranges** look back **20, 40 and 60 trading days** (roughly one, two and three months). The highs and lows of those windows are the obvious liquidity for the weeks ahead.

### How to use them

1. On the daily chart, mark the high and low of the last **20** trading days.
2. Then the last **40** and **60**. A longer window adds a level only when it reaches beyond the shorter one.
3. Decide which side is **untouched** and closer. In the chart, price sits near the 20-day high (1.0777); the next levels above are the 40/60-day high (1.0864).
4. If your weekly and daily bias are bullish, those highs are the targets. If bearish, the lows are.
5. Update the windows each week: they roll forward.

## The Monthly Template

| Part of the month     | Often…                                               | What to do                                 |
| --------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| **First week**        | Builds the opening range; may run one side of it     | Mark the month's open and first-week range |
| **First 1–2 weeks**   | The month's high or low forms (the manipulation)     | Wait for a daily MSS away from it          |
| **Rest of month**     | Expansion toward the 20/40/60-day liquidity          | Trade with it on daily bias                |
| **Month/quarter end** | Rebalancing flows can cause sharp, short-lived moves | Reduce size in the last 2–3 days           |

See [Power of 3](/en/ict/power-of-3) for the same template on a day.

## Seasonal and Quarterly Tendencies

The original listed a profile for each quarter. These are commonly cited, but none is reliable enough to trade on by itself:

| Period                         | Commonly cited tendency                      | Caveat                                               |
| ------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| January                        | New positioning; can set the tone for Q1     | Often reverses mid-month                             |
| Late March / June / Sept / Dec | Quarter-end rebalancing                      | Short-lived; direction depends on the quarter's move |
| May ("sell in May")            | Equity weakness into summer                  | A weak, inconsistent equity effect                   |
| July – August                  | Lower volume; thin-liquidity spikes possible | Spreads widen; size down                             |
| September – October            | Volatility returns                           | Varies widely by year                                |
| Late December                  | Thin holiday liquidity after mid-month       | Many traders stand aside                             |

<Callout type="warning" title="Removed claims">
  The original said Q1 "often sets the yearly low", Q2 is "the strongest trending quarter" and Q4 "often sets the yearly
  high or low". These were stated without data, so they're left out here. Check them on your own pair with past years
  before using them.
</Callout>

## Case Study — USD/JPY, Q1 2024

A real example of a quarter that kept going the way the month after the low started:

| Period        | What happened                                                                    |
| ------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| December 2023 | Fell to about **140** late in the month: the quarter's low                       |
| January 2024  | Bounced and started a bullish daily structure                                    |
| February      | Continued above the December high, through 150                                   |
| March         | Despite the Bank of Japan's first rate rise (19 March), extended to near **152** |
| Q2            | The trend continued                                                              |

<Callout type="info" title="Corrected">
  The original gave the Q1 high as 151.50. USD/JPY traded up to about **152** in late March 2024. Treat these levels as
  approximate; check your broker's data.
</Callout>

The lesson: once the December low held and January broke bullish structure, the 20/40/60-day highs above were the draw, and each month's pullback was a buying opportunity.

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake                           | Fix                                                             |
| --------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Trading seasonality on its own    | Use look-back ranges and structure first                        |
| Ignoring quarter-end              | Reduce size in the last days of a quarter                       |
| "Monthly bias always beats daily" | It sets context; a daily MSS against it is a warning, not noise |
| Forgetting to roll the windows    | Re-mark the 20/40/60-day ranges each week                       |

## Practice

1. Mark the 20/40/60-day ranges on EUR/USD, gold and your main pair each Monday.
2. Track how often price reaches the nearest untouched extreme within four weeks.
3. For 12 months, note the week in which each month's high and low formed. Log it in your [trading journal](/en/tools/journal-template).
