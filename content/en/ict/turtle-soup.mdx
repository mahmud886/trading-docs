## What Is Turtle Soup?

**Turtle Soup** is a reversal setup that **fades a false breakout** of a significant high or low.

1. Price breaks beyond the level.
2. Breakout traders enter, and stops beyond the level are triggered.
3. Price quickly comes back inside.
4. You trade against the breakout. The stop goes just beyond the failed move.

<Callout type="info" title="Where the name comes from">
  The pattern and its name come from Linda Bradford Raschke and Laurence Connors, in their book *Street Smarts* (1995).
  The famous "Turtle" traders bought breakouts to new 20-day highs. Turtle Soup was designed to profit when those
  breakouts failed. ICT adapted the idea to his liquidity framework. In ICT terms, Turtle Soup is a way to trade a
  **liquidity sweep** of an obvious high or low.
</Callout>

### Who gets trapped

| Side              | Bearish Turtle Soup (false breakout above a high)       | Bullish Turtle Soup (false breakdown below a low)         |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Triggered**     | Shorts' stop losses (buy stops) and breakout buy orders | Longs' stop losses (sell stops) and breakdown sell orders |
| **Trapped**       | Breakout buyers who bought above the high               | Breakdown sellers who sold below the low                  |
| **When it fails** | Their exits (sell orders) add fuel to the move down     | Their exits (buy orders) add fuel to the move up          |
| **Your trade**    | **Short**, stop above the failed-breakout high          | **Long**, stop below the failed-breakdown low             |

## Turtle Soup vs the Sweep + MSS Model

Both setups start with the same event: price trades beyond a pool of liquidity and fails. The difference is **how early you enter**.

|                  | **Turtle Soup (close back inside)**                               | **Sweep + MSS** ([Liquidity Sweep](/en/ict/liquidity-sweep)) |
| ---------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Trigger**      | A candle closes back inside the level                             | Displacement with an MSS, then a retrace into the FVG or OB  |
| **Entry**        | At that close (or as price reclaims the level)                    | At the FVG CE or OB edge on the retrace                      |
| **Speed**        | Early: you catch moves that never retrace                         | Later: you miss moves that don't retrace                     |
| **Confirmation** | Less: no MSS yet                                                  | More: order flow has visibly turned                          |
| **Stop**         | Beyond the failed-breakout extreme                                | Beyond the sweep extreme                                     |
| **Best when**    | The level is major (PWH/PWL, PDH/PDL) and the reversal is violent | Any marked pool, especially lower-timeframe ones             |

The two examples below show both. Example 1 is a classic Turtle Soup entry at the close back inside. Example 2 waits for the reclaim and an MSS, so it sits between the two models.

## Identification Rules

| #   | Rule                                                                               | Why                                                             |
| --- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | **Significant level:** PWH/PWL, PDH/PDL, clean equal highs/lows, a major old swing | Obvious levels attract the most breakout orders and stops       |
| 2   | **Clear trade beyond:** price clearly breaks the level, not just a touch           | No breakout means no trapped traders                            |
| 3   | **Quick failure:** back inside within **1–3 candles** of your entry timeframe      | A slow drift back is a different thing (and often a run)        |
| 4   | **Aggressive return:** the candle that comes back inside is large (displacement)   | Shows that the breakout orders were absorbed                    |
| 5   | **Kill-zone timing:** London (12–3 PM BD) or NY AM (5–8 PM BD)                     | Most false breakouts at major levels happen when volume arrives |
| 6   | **HTF alignment:** the breakout goes _against_ the higher-timeframe bias           | A breakout _with_ the HTF bias is more likely to be real        |

In the original Raschke–Connors version, the level is a **new 20-day low (or high)**, and the previous 20-day extreme must have been set at least a few days earlier. That ensures the level is mature and widely watched. ICT's version uses the liquidity levels above instead, but the idea is the same.

<Callout type="warning" title="Turtle Soup is not 'fade every breakout'">
  Most breakouts of minor levels are not tradable failures. Rules 1, 3 and 6 together are what separate a Turtle Soup
  from simply standing in front of a trend.
</Callout>

## Example 1 — Bearish Turtle Soup, EUR/USD (15m, NY AM)

<Callout type="info" title="About these examples">

  not records of real trades. R:R values are the planned risk-to-reward, not results.

**Before the session:** the previous week high is **1.0950**. Breakout buy orders and shorts' stops rest above it. A London low at **1.0880** holds sell-side liquidity below it, which is the target.

**Observations**

1. **The breakout:** at **6:30 PM BD**, in the NY AM kill zone, price runs to **1.0958**. The candle even **closes above** the PWH, so breakout traders are now long.
2. **The trap:** the very next candle is an 8-pip bearish displacement, against a 3.8-pip average. It **closes back below** the PWH at **1.0945**. The breakout has failed within one candle.
3. **Entry:** Turtle Soup sells at that close.

| Level   | Price       | Rationale                                      |
| ------- | ----------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| Entry   | 1.0945      | Close back inside (classic Turtle Soup)        |
| Stop    | 1.0960      | Above the 1.0958 failed-breakout high + 2 pips |
| Target  | 1.0880      | SSL below                                      |
| Risk    | 15 pips     | 1.0960 − 1.0945                                |
| Reward  | 65 pips     | 1.0945 − 1.0880                                |
| **R:R** | **1 : 4.3** | 65 ÷ 15                                        |

**Invalidation:** a 15m close above 1.0958. The breakout would be back on, and the trapped traders wouldn't be trapped any more.

**Note:** if you had waited for a retrace into an FVG, as in the sweep + MSS model, there might have been no entry, because price fell away from the level. That is the trade-off Turtle Soup accepts: an earlier entry with less confirmation.

## Example 2 — Bullish Turtle Soup, Gold (5m, London)

![Bullish Turtle Soup — Gold 5m: breakdown below equal lows 2,020 to 2,015 is reclaimed; MSS; buy 2,022](/images/ict/turtle-soup/gold-bullish-turtle-soup-dark.png)

**Before the session:** three relative equal lows near **2,020** hold longs' stops and breakdown sell orders below them. A prior high at **2,055** holds buy-side liquidity, which is the target.

**Observations**

1. **The breakdown:** at **1:00 PM BD**, in the London kill zone, price drops to **2,015** and closes below the equal lows. Breakdown sellers are now short.
2. **Reclaim + MSS:** the next candle closes back above 2,020. Then a 4-point bullish displacement, against a 1.5-point average, closes above the **2,023.8** lower high. That is the 5m MSS.
3. **Entry:** at **1:15 PM BD** price dips back, and the buy at **2,022** fills.

| Level   | Price       | Rationale                                       |
| ------- | ----------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| Entry   | 2,022       | After the reclaim and MSS, on the dip           |
| Stop    | 2,013       | Below the 2,015 failed-breakdown low + 2 points |
| Target  | 2,055       | BSL at the prior high                           |
| Risk    | 9 pts       | 2,022 − 2,013                                   |
| Reward  | 33 pts      | 2,055 − 2,022                                   |
| **R:R** | **1 : 3.7** | 33 ÷ 9                                          |

**Invalidation:** a 5m close back below 2,015.

## Two Entry Variants

| Variant                              | Entry                                            | Use it when                                             |
| ------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| **A. Close back inside** (Example 1) | At the close of the candle that returns inside   | Major level, violent one-candle failure, clear HTF bias |
| **B. Reclaim + MSS** (Example 2)     | After the MSS, on the first dip toward the level | Less obvious level, or the return is slower             |

With either variant, the stop goes **beyond the extreme of the failed breakout**, plus a small buffer. In both examples the buffer is 2 pips or 2 points. Never place the stop at the level itself, because the level can be tested again.

---

## When Not to Trade Turtle Soup

- The breakout goes **with** the higher-timeframe trend, and the HTF draw on liquidity is still ahead.
- Several candles **close beyond** the level and a retest holds. That is a [run, not a sweep](/en/ict/liquidity).
- The level is minor or was found after the fact.
- A high-impact news release is due, or price is mid-release.
- Outside the kill zones, especially during the NY lunch period (10:00–11:30 PM BD).

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake                                               | Fix                                                                       |
| ----------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Fading every breakout                                 | Only significant levels, against the HTF bias, in a kill zone             |
| Entering before the close back inside                 | Wait for the candle to close; a wick still inside the move proves nothing |
| Stop at the broken level                              | Stop beyond the failed-breakout extreme, plus a buffer                    |
| Giving the trade a "second chance" after invalidation | One attempt per level; a close beyond the extreme ends it                 |
| Holding the trade through a slow, grinding return     | Rule 3: if it isn't back inside within 1–3 candles, skip it               |

## Risk Management

| Component              | Guideline                                                                  |
| ---------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Risk per trade**     | 1–2% of the account; less while learning                                   |
| **Position size**      | Account risk ÷ stop distance ([calculator](/en/tools/position-calculator)) |
| **Stop**               | Beyond the failed-breakout extreme + buffer                                |
| **Partial (optional)** | At the first internal liquidity (nearest FVG or swing)                     |
| **Variant A sizing**   | Consider a smaller size than for confirmed (MSS) entries                   |

## Practice

1. Mark PWH/PWL and PDH/PDL on a 15m chart for the last four weeks. Find every breakout of those levels.
2. Label each breakout **failed within 1–3 candles** or **held**. Note the session and the HTF bias for each.
3. For the failures, write down the variant A and variant B entries, the stop and the opposite pool, then calculate R:R.
4. Compare the two variants in your [trading journal](/en/tools/journal-template). Which one suits your pair and your schedule?

## FAQ

### Is a Judas Swing a Turtle Soup?

Often, yes. A [Judas Swing](/en/ict/judas-swing) is a false move at the session open that runs a nearby high or low before the real move. When that false move breaks a significant level and fails quickly, it is a Turtle Soup.

### How far beyond the level must price go?

There is no fixed distance; it depends on volatility. What matters is a clear break that triggers orders, followed by a quick close back inside.

### Should I always wait for an MSS?

Not for variant A. That is the point of the classic Turtle Soup. But without an MSS you are trading on less confirmation, so keep the size down and be strict about rules 1, 3 and 6.
