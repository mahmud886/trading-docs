## Three Linked Markets

| Pair                      | Common tendency             | Why people expect it                                                       |
| ------------------------- | --------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Gold vs the dollar**    | Move in opposite directions | Gold is priced in dollars, so a stronger dollar makes it dearer for others |
| **Gold vs real yields**   | Move in opposite directions | Gold pays no interest; higher real yields raise the cost of holding it     |
| **Dollar vs real yields** | Move in the same direction  | Higher US real yields attract capital into dollar assets                   |

A **real yield** is the nominal yield minus inflation expectations (see [Yield Curve Dynamics](/en/intermarket/yield-curve)).

<Callout type="warning" title="Corrected and toned down">
  The original called this a "trinity" institutions use for "hedging, rotation and directional signals". These are
  common tendencies, not rules, and we found no source for the institutional claims. Gold has at times risen alongside
  higher real yields, for example when central banks and other buyers were accumulating it. Re-measure before relying on
  it.
</Callout>

In the second period gold rises from about 1,800 (day 100) to 2,146 while the real yield ends at 1.78% against 1.65% at day 100: **higher real yields did not stop gold**, because something else was driving it. The lesson is about the method: a relationship you rely on needs to be re-checked.

## Using It as Context

| Situation                                            | Reasonable reading                                                                        |
| ---------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Dollar and real yields rise, gold falls              | The usual pattern is working: the gold downtrend has support                              |
| Dollar and real yields rise, gold **holds or rises** | Another driver is active (safe-haven demand, central-bank buying); the usual link is weak |
| Real yields fall, dollar weakens, gold rises         | Everything agrees: confidence in a gold long is higher, not certain                       |
| Signals disagree                                     | Smaller size, or wait                                                                     |

### Example (no chart) — gold long with agreeing context

Real yields and the dollar have both been falling for two weeks and gold breaks above **2,040**. Plan: buy a retest at **2,042**, stop **2,018**, target **2,090**: risk 24, reward 48, **1 : 2.0** planned. If the dollar and real yields turn up together while gold closes back below **2,030**, the supporting context has gone and the plan is off.

## Limits

| Limit                     | Detail                                                           |
| ------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Other drivers             | Safe-haven demand, central-bank buying, geopolitics, positioning |
| Windows matter            | A 20-day and a 250-day correlation can disagree                  |
| Real yields are estimated | Breakeven inflation moves with its own drivers                   |
| Event days                | Inflation data and central-bank decisions move all three at once |

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake                                      | Fix                                                                                |
| -------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Treating the inverse link as a law           | Re-measure; look at the rolling value                                              |
| Shorting gold just because real yields rose  | Check whether gold is already reacting                                             |
| Using nominal yields in place of real yields | Check the breakeven or TIPS yield                                                  |
| Ignoring the dollar's own drivers            | DXY is mostly the euro; see [DXY and Major Pairs](/en/intermarket/dxy-correlation) |
| Trading only on the other markets            | The gold chart and your plan come first                                            |

## Practice

1. Download daily gold, DXY and 10-year TIPS yields for a year. Compute the three correlations of daily changes.
2. Compute them for the first and second half separately. Did anything change?
3. Find one stretch where gold ignored real yields. What else was going on?
4. Log it in your [trading journal](/en/tools/journal-template).

## Summary

| Idea       | Detail                                                           |
| ---------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Tendencies | Gold against the dollar and real yields; dollar with real yields |
| Breaks     | Other drivers can make gold ignore the link                      |
| Method     | Re-measure correlations on your window                           |
| Use        | Context that raises or lowers confidence, never a signal alone   |
