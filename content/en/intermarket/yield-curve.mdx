## What Is the Yield Curve?

The **yield curve** plots the yield of government bonds against their maturity. Traders usually watch a few points on it:

| Point              | What it mostly reflects                                                                 |
| ------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **2-year**         | Expected path of the central bank's policy rate over the next couple of years           |
| **10-year**        | Longer-run growth, inflation and the extra yield investors ask for holding longer bonds |
| **Spread (2s10s)** | 10-year yield minus 2-year yield — the curve's slope                                    |

A **normal** curve slopes up (10-year above 2-year). An **inverted** curve has the 2-year above the 10-year, which means the market prices policy rates coming **down** from today's level.

<Callout type="warning" title="Corrected and toned down">
  The original described yield-curve analysis as what "macro funds" use and said bond markets "predict" currency and
  equity moves. The curve describes how the market **prices rates**. An inversion has often come before US recessions,
  but with long and varying lead times, and it has not been a trading signal on its own. We don't claim it predicts
  anything.
</Callout>

## Real Yields

A **real yield** is a yield after inflation. For the 10-year it can be approximated as the **nominal yield minus the 10-year breakeven inflation rate** (this is close to the 10-year inflation-linked, TIPS, yield).

In the chart the 10-year nominal yield is flat (4.6% to 5.0%) but breakeven inflation falls from 2.6% to about 1.95%, so the real yield **rises** by about a point. Real yields matter because:

| Relationship               | Common tendency (not a rule)                                                                                             |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Real yields and gold       | Often move inversely (gold pays no yield); the link has weakened at times, for example when central banks bought heavily |
| Real yields and the dollar | Higher US real yields often support the dollar                                                                           |
| Real yields and equities   | Rising real yields can pressure high-valuation growth stocks                                                             |

## Yields and Currencies

The usual link is through **rate differentials**: if US 2-year yields rise relative to German 2-year yields, EUR/USD often weakens, and the reverse. The 2-year is used because it tracks expected policy rates.

| Situation                             | Idea to test                                            |
| ------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| US 2-year yield rises, rest unchanged | Supportive for USD pairs                                |
| Differential widens in favour of USD  | EUR/USD, GBP/USD may be pressured; USD/JPY may rise     |
| Curve inverts or steepens quickly     | Markets are repricing the rate path — expect volatility |

### Example (no chart) — differentials as context

The US–German 2-year spread has widened for three weeks and EUR/USD has been falling. EUR/USD rallies to **1.0880**. Plan: sell **1.0880**, stop **1.0905**, target **1.0830**: risk 25 pips, reward 50, **1 : 2.0** planned. If the spread starts narrowing and EUR/USD closes above **1.0905**, the rate-differential reason has weakened and the plan is off.

## Limits

| Limit                                     | Detail                                                          |
| ----------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Slow signal                               | Curve moves play out over months; it is poor for timing entries |
| Term premium and supply                   | The 10-year isn't only about policy rates                       |
| Different central banks, different timing | Differentials matter relative to each other                     |
| Data events                               | A single release can move the front end sharply                 |
| Correlations change                       | See [the introduction](/en/intermarket/introduction)            |

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake                                      | Fix                                        |
| -------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| Treating an inversion as a sell signal       | It describes pricing; timing is unreliable |
| Mixing up the 2s10s and other curve measures | State which spread you use                 |
| Using nominal yields when real yields matter | Check the breakeven/TIPS yield             |
| Ignoring the other country's yield           | Currency pairs react to differentials      |
| Reading the curve on one day's move          | Look at the trend over weeks               |

## Summary

| Idea       | Detail                                                |
| ---------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| Curve      | Yields across maturities; 2s10s is the slope          |
| Inversion  | 2-year above 10-year: policy rates priced lower later |
| Real yield | Nominal minus breakeven inflation                     |
| FX link    | Rate differentials, mainly the 2-year                 |
| Use        | Context for currencies, gold and stocks, not a timer  |
