## Stock Market Basics

Stock trading is one of the most accessible markets for beginners. Understanding how stocks work is essential.

### What is a Stock?

**Stock** = Share of ownership in a company

**Characteristics:**
- Represents partial ownership
- Company divided into shares
- Each share = voting rights + profit participation
- Traded on stock exchanges (NYSE, NASDAQ, LSE, TSE, etc.)

**Example:**
- Apple has 16 billion shares outstanding
- You own 1 share = 1÷16,000,000,000 = 0.0000000063% ownership
- Stock price × shares = market capitalization

### Stock Market Mechanics

#### How Stocks are Traded:

1. **Place Order** → 2. **Bid-Ask Match** → 3. **Order Fills** → 4. **Settlement** (T+2 in most markets)

**Bid-Ask:**
- Bid: Buyers' offer (what you get if selling)
- Ask: Sellers' offer (what you pay if buying)
- Spread: Difference between bid and ask

**Trading Hours:**
- Pre-market: 4-9:30 AM EST (limited)
- Regular: 9:30 AM - 4 PM EST
- After-hours: 4-8 PM EST (limited)
- Wider spreads outside regular hours

### Stock Types

#### 1. **Blue-Chip Stocks**
- Large, established companies
- Examples: Apple, Microsoft, Coca-Cola, ExxonMobil
- Lower volatility
- Good for investing
- Steady, predictable
- Dividend payers often

#### 2. **Growth Stocks**
- Smaller companies with high growth potential
- Higher volatility
- No dividends (reinvest profits)
- Examples: Tesla, Shopify, Square
- Good for trading (higher moves)
- Riskier

#### 3. **Value Stocks**
- Undervalued companies
- Trading below intrinsic value
- Often dividend payers
- Recovery opportunities
- Lower growth but established

#### 4. **Penny Stocks**
- Very small companies (under $5 per share)
- Extreme volatility
- Manipulation risk
- Not recommended for most traders
- Illiquid (hard to exit)

#### 5. **Dividend Stocks**
- Pay regular dividends to shareholders
- Good for income investors
- Often mature companies
- Blue-chip overlap
- Dividends are taxed as income

### Fundamental Analysis for Stocks

**Key Metrics:**

**P/E Ratio (Price-to-Earnings):**
- Stock price ÷ Earnings per share
- Low P/E: Cheap valuation (potential value)
- High P/E: Expensive (growth expected or overvalued)
- Compare to industry average

**EPS (Earnings Per Share):**
- Profit per share
- Growing EPS = Good sign
- Shrinking EPS = Problem

**Dividend Yield:**
- Annual dividend ÷ Stock price
- 2-4% normal
- Above 5% might be unsustainable
- 0% means no dividend

**Book Value:**
- Assets - Liabilities ÷ Shares
- P/B ratio = Stock price ÷ Book value
- Lower P/B can indicate undervalue

**Debt to Equity:**
- Total debt ÷ Total equity
- Lower is more stable
- Higher indicates higher financial risk

### Earnings Reports

**Critical Event:**
- Companies report quarterly earnings (every 3 months)
- Huge price movements often occur
- Expectations vs actual drive stock price changes

**Earnings Surprise:**
- Beat expectations → Stock up
- Miss expectations → Stock down
- Forward guidance affects stock more

**Trading Earnings:**
- High volatility during earnings
- Volatility crush after announcement
- Can be profitable with options
- Or avoid for directional traders

### Stock Trading Strategies

#### 1. **Momentum Trading**
- Trade with strong trends
- Higher highs and higher lows
- Use technical indicators
- Exit on momentum loss

#### 2. **Swing Trading**
- Hold 2-10 days
- Support/resistance bounces
- Trend continuation trades
- Good risk-reward setups

#### 3. **Breakout Trading**
- Trade breakouts from consolidation
- Volume confirmation important
- Follow-through usually continues
- Watch for false breakouts

#### 4. **Earnings Trading**
- Trade earnings surprise expectation
- High volatility around announcements
- Options advantage: defined risk
- Timing is critical

#### 5. **Dividend Capture**
- Buy before ex-dividend date
- Hold through dividend payment
- Sell after
- Steady income strategy

### Stock Basics You Need to Know

**Bull Market:**
- Prices rising (trend up)
- General optimism
- Can last years
- Buy and hold profitable

**Bear Market:**
- Prices falling (trend down)
- General pessimism
- Often 20%+ declines
- Shorts profitable

**Corrections:**
- 10-20% declines in bull market
- Temporary pullbacks
- Historical normal
- Buying opportunities (often)

**Crashes:**
- 20%+ declines rapidly
- Panic selling
- Opportunities for value buyers
- Risk management critical during crashes

### Stock Market Regulations (US)

**Pattern Day Trader Rule:**
- 3+ day trades in 5 rolling business days
- Requires account >= $25,000
- Violations result in account freeze
- Brokers monitor and enforce

**Short Sale Rules:**
- Must be done in margin account
- Need to borrow shares first
- Cannot short-sell on downtick
- More regulated than buying

**Insider Trading:**
- Illegal to trade on non-public information
- Company insiders restricted
- SEC enforcement strict
- Penalties severe

## Summary

**Stocks are:**
- Company ownership shares
- Traded on exchanges during market hours
- Suitable for trading and investing
- Driven by fundamentals and technicals
- Regulated (less risky than unregulated)

**Stock Trading Requires:**
- Understanding mechanics
- Knowledge of valuation
- Technical or fundamental analysis
- Sector knowledge
