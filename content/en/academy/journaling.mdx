## What is a Trading Journal?

A trading journal is a **systematic record** of every trade you take, including the reasoning behind each decision, the outcome, and your emotional state. It is your most powerful tool for identifying patterns in your behavior, refining your strategy, and achieving consistency.

<Callout type="info" title="The Truth About Journals">
  Every consistently profitable trader keeps a journal. It is not optional — it is the feedback loop that turns random
  trading into a professional business.
</Callout>

---

## Why Keep a Trading Journal?

### Without a Journal:

- You repeat the same mistakes without realizing it
- You cannot identify which setups actually work
- Emotions drive decisions instead of data
- Improvement is random and slow

### With a Journal:

- You spot recurring mistakes within weeks
- You know your exact win rate, R:R, and profit factor
- You make decisions based on statistical evidence
- Improvement is systematic and measurable

| Field                | What to Record                | Why It Matters                    |

## Sample Journal Entry

### Trade #47 — EUR/USD Long

**Date:** May 12, 2026, 15:00 (London-NY Overlap)

**Setup:** Bullish Order Block on 1H during uptrend on 4H. Price swept Asian session low, then showed bullish engulfing inside the OB. FVG confluence above.

**Entry:** 1.0852 | **SL:** 1.0822 (30 pips) | **TP:** 1.0912 (60 pips)

**Position Size:** 0.33 lots | **Risk:** $100 (1% of $10,000)

**R:R Planned:** 1:2 | **R:R Actual:** 1:1.8

**Result:** Partial TP at 1:1, remaining closed at 1.0906 (manual)

**P/L:** +$154

**Emotions:** Calm entry, felt urge to close early at +20 pips but followed the plan. Slightly anxious during 15-min pullback.

**Lessons:** The pullback after entry is normal — trust the setup. Partial TP strategy working well for managing emotions.

**Rating:** 4/5 (good execution, slightly early final exit)

## Journal Templates

### Spreadsheet Method (Google Sheets / Excel)

Create columns for each field. Use formulas to auto-calculate:

**Key Formulas:**

- **Win Rate:** = COUNTIF(Result, "Win") / COUNT(Result)
- **Average R:R:** = AVERAGE(Actual_RR)
- **Profit Factor:** = SUM(Wins) / ABS(SUM(Losses))
- **Expectancy:** = (Win% x Avg Win) - (Loss% x Avg Loss)

## Key Metrics to Track

### Primary Metrics

1. **Win Rate** — Percentage of winning trades
   - Scalpers: 60-70% typical
   - Swing traders: 40-55% typical (higher R:R compensates)

2. **Average Risk:Reward** — How much you win vs how much you lose
   - Minimum 1:1.5 for most strategies
   - Ideal: 1:2 or higher

3. **Profit Factor** — Gross profit / Gross loss
   - Below 1.0 = losing money
   - 1.0-1.5 = barely profitable
   - 1.5-2.0 = good
   - Above 2.0 = excellent

4. **Expectancy** — Average amount you expect to make per trade
   - Formula: (Win% x Avg Win) - (Loss% x Avg Loss)
   - Must be positive for a viable strategy

### Secondary Metrics

5. **Max Drawdown** — Largest peak-to-trough decline
6. **Recovery Factor** — Net profit / Max drawdown
7. **Average Holding Time** — How long you stay in trades
8. **Best/Worst Day** — Identify emotional extremes
9. **Consecutive Losses** — Know your worst streak
10. **Setup Win Rate** — Which setups perform best

## Weekly Review Process

Every weekend, spend 30-60 minutes reviewing your journal:

### Step 1: Compile Statistics

- Total trades this week
- Win rate for the week
- Total P/L in dollar and R terms
- Largest win and largest loss

### Step 2: Pattern Analysis

- Which setups had the highest win rate?
- Which sessions were most profitable?
- Did you follow your trading plan on every trade?
- Which trades were impulse/revenge trades?

### Step 3: Emotional Review

- Were there any revenge trades?
- Did FOMO cause any entries?
- How did you handle losses?
- Were winning trades managed properly or closed too early?

### Step 4: Action Items

- Write 2-3 specific improvements for next week
- Example: "Only trade during London-NY overlap" or "No trades after 2 consecutive losses"

---

## Monthly Review Process

At the end of each month:

1. **Compare with previous months** — Is your win rate improving?
2. **Identify your A+ setup** — The one setup with the highest expectancy
3. **Calculate monthly R** — How many R did you gain/lose?
4. **Review rules compliance** — How often did you follow your plan?
5. **Adjust trading plan** — Based on data, not feelings

## Common Journaling Mistakes

<Callout type="warning" title="Avoid These Errors">

**❌ Only recording winners** — You learn more from losses. Record EVERY trade.

**❌ Not including emotions** — Technical data alone misses half the picture.

**❌ Journaling inconsistently** — One week on, one week off defeats the purpose.

**❌ Not reviewing** — A journal you never read is just a diary. Review weekly.

**❌ Making it too complicated** — Start simple. You can add fields later.

**❌ Skipping screenshots** — Visual review catches things numbers miss.

</Callout>
