## Commodity Markets Guide

Commodity trading offers unique opportunities and risks. Understanding commodity-specific mechanics is essential.

### What Are Commodities?

**Commodities** = Raw materials or primary agricultural products traded in global markets

**Types:**
1. **Metals:** Gold, Silver, Copper, Platinum
2. **Energy:** Crude Oil, Natural Gas, Heating Oil
3. **Agriculture:** Wheat, Corn, Soybeans, Coffee, Sugar
4. **Softs:** Cocoa, Cotton, Orange Juice

### Major Commodities

#### **Gold (Most Popular for Traders)**
- Ticker: XAUUSD (ounces per US dollar)
- Trading hours: 24/5
- High liquidity
- Typical daily move: 5-20+ pips
- Safe haven asset (rises in crises)
- Inverse correlation to USD

#### **Oil (WTI Crude)**
- Ticker: CL (NYMEX crude oil futures)
- Trading hours: Specific futures hours + extended
- Geopolitical sensitivity
- OPEC announcements critical
- Weather impacts (refineries)
- Economic indicator (demand)

#### **Natural Gas**
- Ticker: NG
- Highly volatile
- Weather-dependent (heating/cooling demand)
- Supply disruptions cause spikes
- Seasonal patterns strong

#### **Agricultural Commodities**
- Wheat, Corn, Soybeans, Coffee, Sugar
- Seasonal patterns extreme
- Weather-driven
- Supply shocks possible
- Lower liquidity than gold/oil

### Commodity Trading Characteristics

**High Leverage:**
- Futures leverage: 10:1 to 50:1 typical
- Margin requirements: Often 5-10%
- Small moves = big P&L changes
- Blowup risk significant

**Cyclicality:**
- Boom-bust cycles
- Supply-demand swings
- Geopolitical disruptions
- Seasonal patterns (agriculture especially)

**Correlation:**
- Commodities often move together (commodity index up)
- Inverse to USD usually (strong dollar = weak commodities)
- Economic data sensitive (growth expectations)
- Positive carry (vs carry currencies)

### Trading Commodities vs Instruments

**Gold:**
- Trades on forex as XAUUSD
- 24/5 availability
- Lower leverage (typically)
- Best for swing traders
- Relatively low volatility (compared to oil)

**Oil:**
- Trades as futures (CL - crude oil)
- Specific futures hours
- Higher leverage
- More volatile than gold
- High liquidity during NYMEX hours

**Agriculture:**
- Futures contracts only
- Specific contracts
- Seasonal trading opportunities
- Lower volume trading
- Weather risk high

### Commodity Price Drivers

#### **Supply:**
- Production capacity
- geopolitical disruptions (OPEC quotas, wars)
- Technology improvements (unconventional oil fracking)
- Environmental restrictions
- Natural disasters (crops, refineries)

#### **Demand:**
- Economic growth (recession = lower demand)
- Industrial demand
- Consumer demand
- Alternative sources (renewables for energy)
- Storage capacity (Oil Strategic Reserve)

#### **Macroeconomic:**
- USD strength (inverse relationship)
- Interest rates (carrying cost of storage)
- Inflation expectations (commodities as hedge)
- Real yields

### Commodity Trading Strategies

#### **Trend Trading:**
- Follow commodity trends
- Use technical analysis
- Support/resistance levels
- Best in strong directional moves

#### **Mean Reversion:**
- Commodity extreme moves often revert
- Overbought/oversold levels
- Support/resistance bounces
- Volatility expansions

#### **Seasonal Trading:**
- Agriculture: Plant and harvest seasons
- Heating: Winter demand (natural gas)
- Cooling: Summer demand (electricity)
- Vacation seasons (oil demand summer)

#### **Geopolitical Trading:**
- Political tensions increase oil
- Supply disruptions = spikes
- OPEC announcements
- Sanctions
- Wars

### Commodity Hedging

**Why Industries Hedge:**
- Airlines hedge oil (operating cost)
- Farmers hedge crops (price protection)
- Manufacturers hedge raw materials
- Traders hedge currency risk

**Hedging costs:**
- Options premiums
- Futures margin costs
- Roll costs (futures contracts expire)

### Unique Commodity Risks

**Storage Costs:**
- Physical commodities require storage
- Futures can reflect storage cost (contango)
- Agricultural commodities perishable
- Oil storage (tank capacity limits)

**Contango vs Backwardation:**
- **Contango:** Future prices > Spot price (normal, reflects storage)
- **Backwardation:** Future prices < Spot price (unusual, supply stress)
- Affects roll costs for futures traders
- Contango = Expensive to hold (negative carry)

**Weather Risk:**
- Agricultural commodities highly weather sensitive
- Frosts, droughts destroy crops
- Hurricanes disrupt oil production
- Unpredictable (can cause gaps)

**Geopolitical Risk:**
- Wars disrupt supply
- Sanctions reduce production
- OPEC production cuts
- Political instability (oil-producing regions)

## Summary

**Commodities offer:**
- High leverage opportunities
- Unique supply-demand drivers
- Seasonal trading patterns
- Geopolitical sensitivity
- Hedging benefits

**Key risks:**
- High volatility
- Geopolitical disruptions
- Weather unpredictability
- Storage cost considerations
- Futures expiration (roll costs)

Commodity trading requires understanding both technical trading and fundamental commodity-specific factors.
