## Bonds & ETFs - Fixed Income & Diversification

Bonds and ETFs provide portfolio stability and diversification. Understanding them is important for holistic investing.

### What Are Bonds?

**Bond** = Debt instrument (you lend money, get paid back with interest)

**Key Features:**
- **Par Value:** Face value (usually $1,000)
- **Coupon:** Annual interest payment
- **Yield:** Coupon ÷ Price = Current yield
- **Maturity:** When you get principal back
- **Credit Risk:** Issuer default risk

**Example:**
- $1,000 US Treasury bond
- 3% coupon = $30 annual
- 10-year maturity
- When you buy at par ($1,000), yield = 3%

### Bond Types

#### **Government Bonds:**
- **US Treasury:** Safest, lowest yield
- **UK Gilts:** Similar to Treasurys
- **German Bunds:** Euro-denominated
- Backed by government credit
- Used as risk-free rate benchmark

#### **Corporate Bonds:**
- Issued by companies
- Higher yield than government (risk premium)
- Investment grade (safer) vs High yield/Junk (riskier)
- Credit rating determines risk

#### **Municipal Bonds:**
- Issued by states/cities
- Often tax-exempt (US)
- Lower yields (due to tax benefit)
- State/local default risk

#### **High Yield (Junk) Bonds:**
- Lower credit quality
- 5-15%+ yields
- Significant default risk
- Behave like stocks (sell-off in recessions)

### Bond Price Dynamics

**Inverse Relationship with Rates:**
- Rates up → Bond prices down
- Rates down → Bond prices up

**Example:**
- 3% coupon bond worth $1,000 at 3% rates
- Rates rise to 4% → Bond now worth less ($750)
- Why? New buyers can get 4% elsewhere
- Rates fall to 2% → Bond now worth more ($1,250)

**Duration:**
- Sensitivity measure to rate changes
- Longer duration = Higher rate sensitivity
- 10-year bond moves more than 2-year bond for same rate change

### What Are ETFs?

**ETF** = Exchange-Traded Fund (basket of securities in one package)

**Characteristics:**
- **Diversified:** Hold multiple assets
- **Liquid:** Trade like stocks
- **Low cost:** Typical expense ratio 0.03-0.50%
- **Transparent:** Holdings known daily
- **Tax efficient:** Fewer distributions than mutual funds

### ETF Types

#### **Bond ETFs:**
- **BND:** Total US bond market
- **TLT:** Long-term Treasurys
- **JNK:** High-yield bonds
- Lower volatility than stocks
- Income generation
- Rate-sensitive

#### **Stock ETFs:**
- **SPY, VOO, IVV:** S&P 500 (large cap)
- **QQQ:** Nasdaq 100 (tech-heavy)
- **VTI:** Total US stock market
- **VXUS:** International stocks
- Diversification across many stocks

#### **Commodity ETFs:**
- **GLD:** Gold
- **USO:** Oil
- **DBC:** Commodity index
- Direct commodity exposure
- Easier than futures

#### **International ETFs:**
- **EWJ:** Japan
- **EWW:** Mexico
- **EWU:** UK
- Access to foreign markets
- Currency exposure

#### **Sector ETFs:**
- **XLK:** Technology
- **XLF:** Financials
- **XLV:** Healthcare
- **XLE:** Energy
- Focus on specific industries

### Bond Trading vs Holding

#### **Bond Trading:**
- Buy and sell frequently
- Profit from price changes (rates moving)
- Not holding to maturity
- Similar to stock trading
- Sensitive to rate changes

#### **Bond Investing:**
- Buy and hold to maturity
- Collect coupon payments (income)
- Predictable outcome (if no default)
- Less volatile
- Less affected by rates (if held to maturity)

### ETF vs Mutual Funds

| Factor | ETF | Mutual Fund |
|--------|-----|------------|
| **Trading** | Trade like stocks | Bought at end of day |
| **Cost** | Often cheaper (0.03-0.50%) | Often expensive (0.5-2%) |
| **Liquidity** | Highly liquid | Less liquid |
| **Tax** | More tax efficient | More distributions |
| **Minimum** | $1 share price | Often $1,000+ |
| **Hours** | Trade during market | Once daily |

**ETFs typically superior for active traders and cost-conscious investors**

### Diversification Benefits

**Bonds reduce stock volatility:**
- Stocks up 30%/year, bonds flat → Balanced growth
- Stocks down 30%, bonds up 5% → Cushion downside
- Different drivers (bonds react to rates, stocks to earnings)
- Portfolio stability

**ETF Diversification:**
- One ETF = 500 stocks (S&P 500)
- vs. owning 1 stock (single-company risk)
- Sector diversification automatically
- International exposure available

**Portfolio Allocation Example:**
- 70% stocks (growth)
- 20% bonds (stability)
- 10% alternatives (hedge)
- This 70/20/10 is conservative but common

### Bond Yields and Investing

**Yield Curve:**
- 2-year Treasury vs 10-year = "curve"
- Normal: Steep (10Y > 2Y)
- Flat: 10Y ≈ 2Y (economic uncertainty)
- Inverted: 10Y < 2Y (recession predictor)

**Yield Curve Inversions:**
- Historically predicted recessions
- 2019 inversion preceded COVID
- 2023 brief inversion (to be seen)
- Not foolproof but important signal

### ETF Selection

**Criteria:**
- Low expense ratio (under 0.25% for stock ETFs)
- High trading volume (liquid)
- Transparent holdings
- Aligned with strategy
- Multi-year track record

**Stock ETF Expense Ratios:**
- Index ETFs: 0.03-0.10% (very cheap)
- Active ETFs: 0.25-1.50% (more expensive)
- Actively managed: Rarely beat index

**Bond ETF Expense Ratios:**
- Treasury ETFs: 0.03-0.08% (cheap)
- Corporate bond ETFs: 0.10-0.30%
- High yield bond ETFs: 0.25-0.50%

### Risks in Bonds & ETFs

**Bond Risks:**
- **Interest rate risk:** Rising rates = falling prices
- **Credit risk:** Issuer default
- **Inflation risk:** Inflation erodes purchasing power
- **Liquidity risk:** Some bonds hard to sell
- **Call risk:** Early redemption by issuer

**ETF Risks:**
- **Tracking error:** ETF performance ≠ index (usually minor)
- **Liquidity risk:** Some ETFs trade thin
- **Complexity risk:** Leveraged/inverse ETFs confusing
- **Correlation risk:** "Safe" assets can be correlated in crashes

### When to Hold vs Trade

**Hold bonds if:**
- Need stable income
- Want capital preservation
- Risk-averse
- Can afford to hold to maturity
- Don't need liquidity

**Trade bonds if:**
- Expect rate moves
- Active trader
- Want capital appreciation
- Short-term horizon
- Can afford volatility

## Summary

**Bonds:**
- Fixed income investments
- Inversely correlated to rates
- Lower volatility than stocks
- Diversification benefit
- Income generation

**ETFs:**
- Diversified baskets
- Very liquid
- Low cost
- Easy access to many assets
- Better than most mutual funds

**Portfolio Use:**
- Bonds for stability
- ETFs for diversification
- Combined for balanced portfolio
- Critical for long-term wealth building

Bonds and ETFs are foundational tools for building a balanced, diversified investment portfolio.
