## Margin Explained - Leverage Risks

Margin is borrowed money from your broker allowing you to control larger positions than your account size. It's powerful but dangerous if misused.

### What is Margin?

**Margin** is a loan from your broker to control positions larger than your account balance.

**Key Concept:**
- Your money = Equity
- Broker's money = Margin/Leverage
- Total position = Equity + Margin

**Example:**
- Account balance: $1,000
- Using 10:1 leverage
- Can control: $10,000 (the broker lends $9,000)

### Margin Account vs Cash Account

**Margin Account:**
- Can borrow from broker
- Must maintain minimum balance
- Subject to margin calls
- Most common for active traders

**Cash Account:**
- No borrowing allowed (rare)
- Only trade with own capital
- No margin calls possible
- Very restrictive

### Leverage Explained

**Leverage Ratio:**
- 10:1 = $1 of capital controls $10
- 50:1 = $1 of capital controls $50
- 100:1 = $1 of capital controls $100
- 500:1 = $1 of capital controls $500

**What Each Means:**

**10:1 Leverage:**
- $1,000 account × 10 = $10,000 controlled
- Common for stocks, professional forex
- Moderately risky

**50:1 Leverage:**
- $1,000 account × 50 = $50,000 controlled
- Retail forex standard
- Significant risk

**100:1 Leverage:**
- $1,000 account × 100 = $100,000 controlled
- Crypto, unregulated brokers
- Very high risk

**500:1 Leverage:**
- $1,000 account × 500 = $500,000 controlled
- Extremely dangerous
- Blowup risk very high

### Margin Requirement

**Initial Margin:**
Amount of equity required to open a position

**Example (10:1 leverage):**
- To open $100,000 position
- Need $100,000 ÷ 10 = $10,000 margin requirement

**Maintenance Margin:**
- Minimum equity to hold position
- Usually 50% of initial margin
- Example: $5,000 on $10,000 position

### Calculating Risk with Margin

**Formula:**
Position Size Pip Loss = Pip Movement × Lot Size

**Example:**
- 1 standard lot EUR/USD
- Price move 50 pips against you
- Loss: 50 pips × $10/pip = $500

**With 10:1 Leverage on $5,000 Account:**
- Can control $50,000
- 50 pip loss = $500
- Account is now $4,500
- Only $4,500 ÷ 10 = $450 margin available
- Very close to margin call!

### Margin Call

**Margin Call** occurs when account equity falls below maintenance requirement

**What Happens:**
1. Broker issues warning
2. Must add funds within 24 hours
3. Or positions are closed automatically

**Example Margin Call:**
```
Initial:
- Account: $10,000
- Leverage: 10:1
- Position: $100,000
- Needed equity: $10,000

Price moves against you:
- Loss: $8,000
- Account now: $2,000
- Required equity: Still $10,000
- MARGIN CALL!
- Broker forces close of positions
```

### Margin Interest Charges

**Overnight holding charges:**
- Broker charges interest on borrowed margin
- Typically 5-10% annually
- Some brokers reduce for large accounts
- Compound daily

**Example:**
- Borrowed $50,000 at 8% annually
- Daily interest: $50,000 × 8% ÷ 365 = $10.96/day
- Over 365 days: $4,000+ just in interest!

### Safe Margin Usage

**Conservative Approach:**
- Use only 2:1 leverage max
- Never exceed 5:1 for retail traders
- Keep equity well above margin requirement
- Never mortgage your house worth of position

**Rule of Thumb:**
Keep account equity at least 10X your margin requirement

**Example:**
- Can control $100,000 (safe limit)
- Needs $10,000 margin
- Keep account at least $100,000
- This way 90% loss needed for margin call

### Leverage Multiplier Effect

**Shows how fast leverage can destroy accounts:**

**$1,000 account, 50:1 leverage:**
```
5 pip loss = $500 loss (50%)
10 pip loss = $1,000 loss (100%) - ACCOUNT BLOWN
```

**$10,000 account, 50:1 leverage:**
```
50 pip loss = $5,000 loss (50%)
100 pip loss = $10,000 loss (100%) - ACCOUNT BLOWN
```

**$10,000 account, 2:1 leverage:**
```
100 pip loss = $2,000 loss (20%) - Safe
200 pip loss = $4,000 loss (40%) - Still okay
500 pip loss = $10,000 loss (100%) - Account blown (rare on one trade)
```

### Why Most Traders Blow Accounts with Margin

**Common Scenario:**
1. Trader starts with $10,000
2. Uses 50:1 leverage (allowed)
3. Opens large position ($500,000)
4. Market moves 20 pips against
5. Loss: $20 pips × 50 × $10 = $10,000
6. Account to $0 - Blown!

**Problem:** Greed + Leverage = Disaster

### Professional Margin Usage

**Institutional traders:**
- Use massive leverage (100+:1)
- But with strict risk management
- Risk only 0.5-1% per position
- Multiple positions offset risk
- Large teams managing
- Stop losses are sacred

**Retail traders:**
- Should use minimal leverage
- 2:1 maximum recommended
- Risk 1-2% per trade
- Single position focus
- High emotional control required

### Regulations on Margin

**US (FINRA):**
- Stocks: Minimum 25% margin requirement
- Day trader minimum: $25,000 account

**Forex (NFA):**
- Retail: Max 50:1 leverage (highly regulated)
- Professional: 500:1 possible (less regulated)

**Crypto:**
- Unregulated, no leverage limits
- Some exchanges allow 100:1+
- Very high risk

**EU:**
- Retail: Limited to 30:1 or 50:1 depending on asset
- Professional: Higher limits

### When Margin is Reasonable

**Good Use Cases:**
1. Professional trader with strict risk rules
2. Small leverage (2:1 to 5:1 max)
3. Accounts $10,000+
4. Multiple years experience
5. Strict position sizing
6. Stop losses on every trade
7. Money they can afford to lose

**Poor Use Cases:**
1. Beginners
2. High leverage (50:1+)
3. Small accounts (under $1,000)
4. Emotional decision makers
5. No risk management
6. "House money" feeling
7. Revenge trading mentality

### Calculating Your Maximum Safe Leverage

**Formula:**
Max Leverage = Account Size ÷ Max Loss Per Trade

**Example:**
- Account: $10,000
- Max loss per trade: $100 (1%)
- Max leverage: $10,000 ÷ $100 = 100:1

But that's only if stop loss is always 1 pip, unrealistic.

**Better calculation:**
- Account: $10,000
- Max loss per trade: $200 (2%)
- Average stop loss: 30 pips
- Needed leverage: $200 ÷ (30 × $10) = 0.67 lots = 6.7:1 leverage

**Safer approach:**
- Don't think in leverage
- Think in position size
- Never position more than 2-5% of account at risk
- Leverage ratio will follow naturally

## Summary

**Margin Key Points:**
- Borrowed money to control larger positions
- Multiplies both gains and losses
- Margin calls force position closure
- High leverage blows accounts quickly
- Professionals use leverage with strict rules
- Retail traders should minimize leverage

**Safe Guidelines:**
- Use 2:1 leverage maximum
- Never exceed 5:1
- Risk only 1-2% per trade
- Keep large equity buffer
- Stop losses on every trade
- Never trade "house money"
- Treat leverage as dangerous tool

Leverage is the #1 reason retail traders blow accounts. Use it carefully or not at all.
