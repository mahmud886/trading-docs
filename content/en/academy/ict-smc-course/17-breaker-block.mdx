### Definition

A Breaker Block is a **failed Order Block that has been broken (traded through) and now acts as support/resistance from the opposite side**. When an Order Block fails — meaning price displaces through it — the OB "flips" its role and becomes a Breaker Block.

- **Bullish Breaker Block:** A previous BEARISH Order Block that price broke ABOVE → Now acts as SUPPORT
- **Bearish Breaker Block:** A previous BULLISH Order Block that price broke BELOW → Now acts as RESISTANCE

The concept: Institutions initially placed orders at the OB (creating it). When they allowed price to trade through it (breaking the OB), they trapped traders who traded that OB. Now when price returns to this level, the trapped traders exit (creating Liquidity) and institutions re-enter.

### Why It Matters

- Breaker Blocks represent TRAPPED traders — their exit provides Liquidity for your entry
- They often produce stronger reactions than regular Order Blocks
- They mark genuine reversals — the structure break that created the Breaker confirms direction change
- They give context to CHOCH/BOS — the candle that caused the break becomes the Breaker
- They're often found at the beginning of new trends (post-CHOCH entries)

### How It Works

**Bullish Breaker Block Formation:**
1. Bearish trend exists — Bearish OB forms (bullish candle before down displacement)
2. Price uses this Bearish OB as resistance (sells off from it)
3. Eventually, price breaks ABOVE this Bearish OB (with displacement)
4. The broken Bearish OB now = **Bullish Breaker Block**
5. When price returns DOWN to this zone → BUY opportunity (former resistance = new support)

**Bearish Breaker Block Formation:**
1. Bullish trend exists — Bullish OB forms (bearish candle before up displacement)
2. Price uses this Bullish OB as support (bounces from it)
3. Eventually, price breaks BELOW this Bullish OB (with displacement)
4. The broken Bullish OB now = **Bearish Breaker Block**
5. When price returns UP to this zone → SELL opportunity (former support = new resistance)

**Key validation:**
- The break of the OB must be with DISPLACEMENT (strong candle, FVG creation)
- This displacement should represent a BOS or CHOCH
- The Breaker is strongest on its FIRST retest

### Institutional Logic

**Why Breakers work from an institutional perspective:**

1. **Trapped traders:** Traders who used the original OB as entry are now trapped. When price returns to the Breaker, they EXIT (closing at breakeven or small loss) → Their exit orders = Liquidity for Smart Money
2. **Institutional re-loading:** Smart Money broke the OB purposefully. When price returns, they ADD to their position using the trapped traders' exit Liquidity
3. **Efficiency:** The Breaker zone has institutional memory — it's a level where the algorithm placed orders before and will again
4. **Confirmation of intent:** The fact that they BROKE the OB confirms they've CHANGED direction. The Breaker is their new "side"

### Trading Application

**Trading a Bullish Breaker Block:**
1. Identify a Bearish OB that was previously respected as resistance
2. Price breaks above it with displacement (creating CHOCH or BOS)
3. Mark the broken OB zone = Bullish Breaker
4. Wait for price to retrace back DOWN to the Breaker zone
5. Enter Buy at the Breaker zone
6. Stop Loss: Below the Breaker low (with buffer)
7. Target: BSL above / new structural high

**Trading a Bearish Breaker Block:**
1. Identify a Bullish OB that was previously respected as support
2. Price breaks below it with displacement
3. Mark the broken OB zone = Bearish Breaker
4. Wait for price to retrace UP to the Breaker zone
5. Enter Sell at the Breaker zone
6. Stop Loss: Above the Breaker high
7. Target: SSL below / new structural low

### Common Mistakes

- ❌ **Trading Breakers without displacement on the break** — Slow grinds through OBs don't create valid Breakers
- ❌ **Using Breakers that have been tested multiple times** — First retest is best
- ❌ **Confusing Breaker with Mitigation Block** — Breaker = OB that was broken and flipped. Mitigation = OB that was partially filled
- ❌ **Not aligning with HTF bias** — Bullish Breaker needs bullish HTF context
- ❌ **Trading the break itself** — Wait for the RETEST of the Breaker, not the initial break

### Advanced Notes

- **Breaker + FVG overlap** = Extremely high probability zone
- **Breaker that forms at CHOCH point** = The entry for a new trend (highest conviction)
- **Breaker failure** — If price closes through the Breaker on retest → Setup is invalid
- **Multiple Breakers** — Sometimes several Breakers form during a structural shift. The deepest one (closest to Equilibrium) is often best
- **Breaker + OTE alignment** — Breaker at 62-79% of the new impulse = Textbook entry
- **Time-based Breaker validation** — Breakers respected during Kill Zones > Random session

### Example

**USD/JPY — Bullish Breaker Block Trade:**

1. **Context:** 4H was bearish. Bearish OB at 149.50-149.80 was resistance (price sold from it twice)
2. **Break:** 15M strong bullish displacement breaks above 149.80, creates FVG → This is CHOCH
3. **Breaker formed:** The Bearish OB (149.50-149.80) is now a Bullish Breaker Block
4. **Retrace:** Price returns to 149.60 (inside the Breaker zone)
5. **Entry:** Buy at 149.60
6. **Stop Loss:** 149.40 (below Breaker low)
7. **Target:** 151.00 (BSL — swing high above)
8. **R:R:** 1:7.0, +140 pips
