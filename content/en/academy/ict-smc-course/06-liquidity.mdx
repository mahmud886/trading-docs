### Definition

In ICT/SMC methodology, **Liquidity** refers to clusters of resting orders in the market — primarily **Stop Losses** and **Pending Orders** that accumulate above Swing Highs (Buy Side Liquidity) and below Swing Lows (Sell Side Liquidity). The market-making algorithm's PRIMARY purpose is to deliver price to these Liquidity pools.

Liquidity is NOT the traditional definition (ease of buying/selling). In ICT context, Liquidity = **WHERE Stop Losses and Pending Orders are clustered** = WHERE Smart Money will drive price to fill their large orders.

Think of Liquidity as FUEL — Smart Money needs it to enter and exit large positions without slippage.

### Why It Matters

- **Every significant market move exists to capture Liquidity** — Nothing is random
- **Your Stop Loss IS Liquidity** — When your stop gets hit, you provided Liquidity for an institution
- **Liquidity tells you WHERE price is heading** — The next Liquidity pool = the next target
- **Understanding Liquidity prevents being stopped out** — Place stops where Liquidity ISN'T concentrated
- **Liquidity Sweeps create the best entries** — After a sweep, the real move begins

### How It Works

**Where Liquidity Forms:**

1. **Above Swing Highs** — Traders who sold place Stop Losses above. Breakout traders place Buy Stops above. = Buy Side Liquidity (BSL)

2. **Below Swing Lows** — Traders who bought place Stop Losses below. Breakdown traders place Sell Stops below. = Sell Side Liquidity (SSL)

3. **Equal Highs** — Multiple touches at the same level = MASSIVE cluster of stops above = Obvious BSL target

4. **Equal Lows** — Multiple touches at the same level = MASSIVE cluster of stops below = Obvious SSL target

5. **Trendlines** — Stops placed along trendlines = Liquidity that will be swept when the trendline "breaks"

6. **Session Highs/Lows** — Previous Day High/Low, Asian High/Low = Session-based Liquidity

**The Liquidity Cycle:**

```
Liquidity Builds → Algorithm Identifies → Price Sweeps → Orders Fill → Real Move Begins
```

### Institutional Logic

**Why Smart Money NEEDS Liquidity:**

Imagine a bank wants to BUY 500 million EUR/USD. If they place a market Buy order:

- Price would spike massively (slippage)
- They'd get terrible average fill price
- The move would be visible and others would front-run them

**Instead, they do this:**

1. ALLOW price to drop (or PUSH it down) to a level where many Sell Stops exist
2. When retail traders' Sell Stops get triggered → those Sell stops become MARKET SELL orders
3. The bank's algorithm BUYS against these Sell orders (perfect counterparty Liquidity)
4. They get filled at a great price with no slippage
5. Then they let price rally (their intended direction all along)

**Key Insight:** When your Stop Loss gets hit and price immediately reverses in your original direction — you just provided Liquidity for an institution.

### Trading Application

**Step 1: Identify Liquidity Pools**

- Mark equal highs/lows (clearest targets)
- Mark obvious swing points where stops accumulate
- Mark session highs/lows (PDH, PDL, Asian H/L)
- Mark trendline liquidity

**Step 2: Determine Which Pool Is Targeted**

- Use Daily Bias (where is the HTF draw?)
- If price is in Discount → SSL below has been taken → BSL above is the TARGET
- If price is in Premium → BSL above has been taken → SSL below is the TARGET

**Step 3: Wait for the Sweep**

- Price takes out the Liquidity pool (goes beyond the level briefly)
- Look for immediate reversal signs (rejection candle, MSS on LTF)

**Step 4: Enter After Sweep**

- After Liquidity Sweep + MSS confirmation → Enter in the reversal direction
- Stop Loss: Beyond the sweep extreme
- Target: The OPPOSING Liquidity pool

### Common Mistakes

- ❌ **Placing stops at obvious levels** — Where retail puts stops = Where Smart Money hunts
- ❌ **Not seeing Liquidity as a target** — Every high/low is a potential Liquidity target
- ❌ **Trading the Liquidity grab as a breakout** — The sweep IS the manipulation, not the real move
- ❌ **Ignoring session Liquidity** — PDH/PDL and Asian H/L are taken DAILY
- ❌ **Not distinguishing Internal vs External Liquidity** — Internal (minor swings within range) gets taken before External (major swing extremes)

### Advanced Notes

- **Internal Liquidity** = Minor swing points within a dealing range → Taken FIRST
- **External Liquidity** = Major swing highs/lows of the dealing range → Taken AFTER internal
- **Liquidity void** = Area with no resting orders → Price moves quickly through these
- **Draw on Liquidity** (DOL) = The Liquidity pool that price is CURRENTLY targeting
- **Raid** vs **Sweep**: Raid = quick wick beyond → immediate reversal. Sweep = candle close beyond → could be genuine break
- **Time-based Liquidity** — Liquidity resting from different sessions (Asian, London, NY) gets targeted in order
- **IPDA cycles** use 20/40/60 day Liquidity references to determine targets

### Example

**EUR/USD — Liquidity Sweep to Trade Setup:**

1. **Context:** Daily bullish. Price has been in Consolidation creating Equal Lows at 1.0850 (SSL)
2. **Above:** Equal Highs at 1.0920 (BSL) — this is the probable TARGET
3. **London Kill Zone:** Price drops below 1.0850, sweeping the Sell Side Liquidity
4. **Confirmation:** 5M Bullish MSS immediately after the sweep
5. **Entry:** Buy at 1.0855 (at Bullish OB formed after sweep)
6. **Stop Loss:** 1.0840 (below sweep extreme)
7. **Target:** 1.0920 (Buy Side Liquidity — Equal Highs above)
8. **Result:** +65 pips, R:R 1:4.3
