## What Are Futures Contracts?

A futures contract is a **legally binding agreement** to buy or sell an asset at a predetermined price on a specific future date. Unlike spot trading where you buy the actual asset, futures let you speculate on price direction with leverage.

<Callout type="info" title="Key Difference">
In spot trading, you own the asset. In futures trading, you own a contract that represents the asset. This allows trading with much less capital through leverage.
</Callout>

## How Futures Work

### Contract Specifications

Every futures contract has standardized specifications:

| Specification | Example (E-mini S&P 500) |
|--------------|--------------------------|
| **Underlying Asset** | S&P 500 Index |
| **Contract Size** | $50 x Index value |
| **Tick Size** | 0.25 points = $12.50 |
| **Expiry Months** | March, June, Sept, Dec (quarterly) |
| **Trading Hours** | Nearly 24 hours (Sun-Fri) |
| **Margin Required** | ~$12,000 per contract |

### The Two Sides

- **Long (Buy)** — Profit when the asset price rises
- **Short (Sell)** — Profit when the asset price falls
- Every futures trade has a buyer AND a seller
- Total open interest tracks total outstanding contracts

## Key Futures Concepts

### Contract Expiry

- Futures contracts have **expiration dates**
- At expiry, contracts are settled (cash or physical delivery)
- Most retail traders **roll over** — close current contract and open the next one
- Quarterly contracts: March (H), June (M), September (U), December (Z)

### Margin and Leverage

**Initial Margin** — The deposit required to open a position
**Maintenance Margin** — Minimum equity required to keep the position open

| Futures Market | Contract Value | Margin Required | Effective Leverage |
|---------------|---------------|----------------|-------------------|
| E-mini S&P 500 | ~$250,000 | ~$12,000 | ~20:1 |
| E-mini NAS100 | ~$350,000 | ~$16,000 | ~22:1 |
| Crude Oil | ~$75,000 | ~$6,000 | ~12:1 |
| Gold (GC) | ~$230,000 | ~$10,000 | ~23:1 |
| Euro FX | ~$125,000 | ~$3,000 | ~40:1 |

### Mark-to-Market (Daily Settlement)

- Futures accounts are settled **daily**
- Profits are credited and losses are debited every day
- If your account falls below maintenance margin, you receive a **margin call**
- You must deposit more funds or your position is liquidated

### Contango and Backwardation

**Contango** — Futures price is higher than the spot price
- Normal in most markets (storage costs, interest)
- Each successive contract month is more expensive

**Backwardation** — Futures price is lower than the spot price
- Indicates strong current demand
- Common during supply shortages

## Futures vs Forex vs Stocks

| Feature | Futures | Forex | Stocks |
|---------|---------|-------|--------|
| **Market Hours** | ~23 hours (Sun-Fri) | 24/5 | Limited hours |
| **Leverage** | High (regulated) | High | Low-Moderate |
| **Regulation** | CME/CBOT (highly regulated) | Varies by broker | Highly regulated |
| **Commissions** | Per contract | Spread-based | Per share/trade |
| **Short Selling** | Easy (equal to going long) | Easy | Restrictions apply |
| **Transparency** | Centralized exchange | OTC (decentralized) | Centralized exchange |

## Micro Futures for Beginners

Micro futures are **1/10th the size** of standard contracts — perfect for learning:

| Micro Contract | Symbol | Margin Required | Tick Value |
|---------------|--------|----------------|-----------|
| Micro E-mini S&P | MES | ~$1,200 | $1.25 |
| Micro E-mini NASDAQ | MNQ | ~$1,600 | $0.50 |
| Micro Gold | MGC | ~$1,000 | $1.00 |
| Micro Crude Oil | MCL | ~$600 | $1.00 |

<Callout type="info" title="Start with Micros">
If you are new to futures, always start with micro contracts. They provide the same market exposure at 1/10th the risk. Graduate to E-mini contracts only after consistent profitability.
</Callout>

## Futures Trading Strategies

### 1. Trend Following
- Identify the prevailing trend on daily/4H charts
- Enter on pullbacks to key levels
- Use trailing stops to capture large moves

### 2. Mean Reversion
- Trade reversals when price is extended from moving averages
- Use RSI or Bollinger Bands for overbought/oversold conditions
- Best in ranging markets

### 3. Spread Trading
- Trade the price difference between two related contracts
- Example: Long September crude, short December crude
- Lower risk than outright directional trades

### 4. Scalping the DOM
- Use the Depth of Market (order book) for ultra-short-term trades
- Read order flow to identify buying/selling pressure
- Requires fast execution and deep market knowledge

## Common Mistakes

<Callout type="warning" title="Futures Trading Pitfalls">

**❌ Ignoring contract expiry** — Holding through expiry can result in physical delivery obligations or forced liquidation.

**❌ Underestimating leverage** — A 1% move on a $250,000 contract = $2,500 gain or loss. Leverage amplifies everything.

**❌ Trading full-size contracts too early** — Start with micro contracts. Your ego does not need E-minis.

**❌ Not understanding tick values** — Each market has different tick sizes and values. Know them before trading.

**❌ Ignoring margin calls** — If you receive a margin call, your position is at risk. Always maintain adequate margin.

</Callout>
