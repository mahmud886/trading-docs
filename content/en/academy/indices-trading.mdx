## What Are Stock Market Indices?

A stock market index tracks the performance of a **group of stocks** that represent a specific market or sector. Instead of buying individual stocks, you trade the overall movement of the entire group.

<Callout type="info" title="Why Trade Indices">
Indices provide diversified exposure to an entire market. When you trade NAS100, you are trading the collective movement of 100 top technology companies — without needing to analyze each one individually.
</Callout>

## What Drives Index Prices?

### 1. Earnings Reports
- Quarterly earnings from major components (Apple, Microsoft, etc.)
- Better than expected → Index rises
- Worse than expected → Index falls
- Earnings season = high volatility

### 2. Economic Data
- GDP, employment, inflation data
- Consumer confidence and retail sales
- Manufacturing and services PMI

### 3. Federal Reserve / Central Bank Policy
- Interest rate decisions → Biggest single-event mover
- Quantitative easing/tightening
- Forward guidance and press conferences

### 4. Sector Rotation
- Technology sector weakness → NAS100 drops more than SPX500
- Financial sector strength → US30 outperforms
- Understanding sector weight helps predict index moves

### 5. Risk Sentiment
- Risk-on → Indices rise, especially NAS100
- Risk-off → Indices fall, gold and bonds rise
- VIX (fear index) measures expected S&P 500 volatility

## Index Trading Strategies

### 1. Gap and Go

- US indices often open with a **gap** from the previous close
- If gap is in the direction of the trend → trade the continuation
- If gap is against the trend → wait for gap fill setup

### 2. Opening Range Breakout

- Wait for the first 15-30 minutes after cash open
- Mark the high and low of this range
- Trade the breakout of this range in the direction of the daily bias
- Stop loss: opposite side of the opening range

### 3. VWAP Strategy

- VWAP (Volume Weighted Average Price) acts as a magnet for index prices
- **Buy** when price pulls back to VWAP from above (uptrend)
- **Sell** when price rallies to VWAP from below (downtrend)
- Most effective during the first 2-3 hours of the session

### 4. Sector Correlation

- If Apple, Microsoft, and Google are all bullish → NAS100 is likely bullish
- Check the top 5 weights of the index for confirmation
- Trade the index instead of individual stocks for diversification

## Position Sizing for Indices

**Example:** $10,000 account, 1% risk ($100)

| Index | Stop Loss | Point Value (0.1 lot) | Position Size |
|-------|----------|----------------------|---------------|
| NAS100 | 50 points | $1/point | $100 / (50 x $1) = **2.0 lots** |
| US30 | 50 points | $1/point | $100 / (50 x $1) = **2.0 lots** |
| SPX500 | 10 points | $10/point | $100 / (10 x $10) = **1.0 lot** |

**Always verify your broker's contract specifications** — point values differ significantly between brokers.

## Risk Management for Indices

<Callout type="warning" title="Index-Specific Risks">

1. **Gap risk** — Indices can gap 100+ points overnight. Never hold positions without stops.
2. **News sensitivity** — FOMC, NFP, CPI can move indices 200+ points in minutes.
3. **After-hours** — Lower liquidity in pre/post-market means wider spreads.
4. **Concentration risk** — NAS100 is 50%+ technology stocks. One sector event can cause massive moves.
5. **Daily drawdown** — Set a maximum daily loss limit (2-3% of account) and stop trading when hit.

</Callout>

## Common Mistakes

<Callout type="warning" title="Index Trading Pitfalls">

**❌ Not knowing your contract specifications** — A 1-point move means different dollar amounts on different brokers and lot sizes.

**❌ Trading all sessions** — US indices are best during US hours. Asian pre-market is choppy and thin.

**❌ Ignoring the macro picture** — Indices are driven by fundamentals. Pure technical trading without macro awareness leads to surprises.

**❌ Over-trading** — Index moves are fast and exciting. This tempts traders to take too many positions.

**❌ Not understanding correlation** — NAS100, US30, and SPX500 are highly correlated. Three positions on three US indices is essentially one large trade.

</Callout>
