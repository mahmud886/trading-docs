## What is the Economic Calendar?

The economic calendar is a schedule of **macroeconomic data releases** and events that affect financial markets. Professional traders check the calendar daily to know when volatility-causing events are coming and plan their trading accordingly.

<Callout type="info" title="Rule #1">
  Never be surprised by a news release. Check the economic calendar EVERY morning before your trading session. If you do
  not know what news is coming, you should not be trading.
</Callout>

## Impact Levels

Economic events are categorized by their expected impact on the market:

| Impact        | Color Code    | Effect                               | Action                                                  |
| ------------- | ------------- | ------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| 🔴 **High**   | Red           | Major price movements (50-200+ pips) | Avoid trading 15 min before/after OR trade the reaction |
| 🟡 **Medium** | Yellow/Orange | Moderate moves (20-50 pips)          | Be aware, reduce size if needed                         |
| ⚪ **Low**    | Gray/White    | Minimal impact (under 10 pips)       | Usually safe to ignore                                  |

## How to Read Economic Data

### The Three Numbers That Matter

For every economic release, you need to know:

1. **Previous** — Last month/quarter's actual number
2. **Forecast (Consensus)** — What economists expect
3. **Actual** — The real number when released

### How Markets React

| Scenario                            | Market Reaction                      |
| ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| Actual is MUCH better than forecast | Strong bullish for that currency     |
| Actual is slightly better           | Mild bullish, may fade               |
| Actual matches forecast             | Minimal reaction (already priced in) |
| Actual is slightly worse            | Mild bearish, may fade               |
| Actual is MUCH worse than forecast  | Strong bearish for that currency     |

**Key insight:** Markets move based on the **surprise factor** — how much the actual differs from the forecast — not the absolute number.

## Key Economic Indicators Explained

### Non-Farm Payrolls (NFP)

- **What:** Number of jobs added/lost in the US economy (excluding farms)
- **When:** First Friday of every month, 8:30 PM BD time
- **Why it matters:** Jobs = consumer spending = economic growth
- **Trading tip:** Avoid entering new trades 30 minutes before NFP. Wait for the initial spike to settle (15-30 min) before trading the direction

### Consumer Price Index (CPI)

- **What:** Measures inflation — the change in consumer prices
- **When:** Monthly, 8:30 PM BD time
- **Why it matters:** Inflation drives central bank decisions on interest rates
- **Higher CPI** → Expectations of rate hikes → Bullish for currency
- **Lower CPI** → Expectations of rate cuts → Bearish for currency

### Interest Rate Decisions

- **What:** Central bank sets the benchmark interest rate
- **When:** Scheduled meetings (FOMC 8x/year, ECB 8x/year)
- **Why it matters:** Higher rates attract foreign investment → Bullish for currency
- **The press conference** after the decision often moves markets more than the decision itself

### Gross Domestic Product (GDP)

- **What:** Total value of goods and services produced
- **When:** Quarterly (advance, preliminary, final)
- **Why it matters:** Overall measure of economic health
- **Rising GDP** → Economy growing → Bullish
- **Falling GDP** → Recession risk → Bearish

### PMI (Purchasing Managers Index)

- **What:** Survey of business conditions
- **When:** Monthly
- **Above 50** = expansion (economy growing)
- **Below 50** = contraction (economy shrinking)
- Manufacturing PMI and Services PMI are reported separately

## Trading Around News

### Strategy 1: Avoid News Entirely

The safest approach for beginners:

- Close all positions 15-30 minutes before high-impact news
- Wait 15-30 minutes after the release before entering new trades
- Spreads widen significantly during news — poor execution

### Strategy 2: Straddle (Pre-News)

- Place buy stop above current price and sell stop below
- One order triggers on the news move
- Cancel the other order
- **Risk:** Whipsaw — price spikes both directions, hitting both stops

### Strategy 3: Fade the Spike

- Wait for the initial news reaction (first 5-15 minutes)
- If the move is unsustainable, trade the reversal
- Requires experience and strict risk management
- Only for experienced traders

### Strategy 4: Trade the Trend After

- Wait for the full reaction (30-60 minutes post-release)
- Identify the new trend direction established by the data
- Enter on pullback in the new direction
- **This is the safest and most reliable news trading strategy**

## Weekly Calendar Routine

### Sunday Evening

1. Review the upcoming week's calendar
2. Identify all high-impact events
3. Note which days to be cautious
4. Plan trading sessions around news

### Daily (Before Trading)

1. Check today's events and times
2. Mark the high-impact events on your chart
3. Decide: trade before/after/avoid
4. Set reminders for key releases

## Common Mistakes

<Callout type="warning" title="Calendar Mistakes">

**❌ Not checking the calendar** — Getting caught in a news event unprepared is the fastest way to blow a trade.

**❌ Trading through high-impact news** — Spreads can widen to 10-20x normal. Stops may not execute at your price.

**❌ Only watching one currency** — If you trade EUR/USD, check BOTH EUR and USD events.

**❌ Ignoring the forecast vs actual** — The number itself matters less than the surprise.

**❌ Forgetting about speeches** — Central bank speeches can move markets just as much as data releases.

**❌ Not adjusting for timezone** — Always set your calendar to Bangladesh time to avoid confusion.

</Callout>
