## Why Trade Gold?

Gold (XAUUSD) is one of the most popular trading instruments worldwide. It offers **high volatility**, strong trends, and clear technical patterns — making it ideal for both day traders and swing traders.

<Callout type="info" title="Gold's Unique Appeal">
  Gold moves an average of 200-400 pips (points) per day, compared to EUR/USD's 50-80 pips. This creates significant
  profit opportunities, but also requires careful position sizing.
</Callout>

---

## Gold Price Drivers

### 1. US Dollar (Inverse Correlation)

Gold and the US Dollar typically move in **opposite directions**:

- Dollar strengthens → Gold usually falls
- Dollar weakens → Gold usually rises
- Always check DXY (Dollar Index) before trading gold

### 2. Interest Rates

- **Rising rates** → Gold tends to fall (opportunity cost of holding non-yielding gold increases)
- **Falling rates** → Gold tends to rise (gold becomes more attractive)
- Watch Fed statements, FOMC meetings, and rate decisions

### 3. Inflation

- Gold is traditionally a **hedge against inflation**
- When CPI data shows rising inflation → Gold tends to rise
- During hyperinflation fears → Gold can surge dramatically

### 4. Geopolitical Events

- Wars, conflicts, political instability → Gold rises (safe haven)
- Global uncertainty → Capital flows into gold
- This makes gold reactive to news events

### 5. Central Bank Buying

- Central banks (China, Russia, India) have been buying gold reserves
- Sustained central bank purchasing provides long-term support
- Reported quarterly — affects longer-term trends

### Position Sizing for Gold

Example: $5,000 account, 1% risk ($50), Stop Loss = 50 pips (5.0 points)

- Standard lot point value: $100/point → $50 / ($100 x 5.0) = **0.10 lots**
- For a 100-pip (10 point) stop: $50 / ($100 x 10) = **0.05 lots**

## Gold Trading Strategies

### 1. Trend Following with Moving Averages

- Use 50 EMA and 200 EMA on the 4H chart
- **Buy** when price is above both EMAs and pulls back to 50 EMA
- **Sell** when price is below both EMAs and rallies to 50 EMA
- Confirm with volume and momentum

### 2. Support and Resistance Levels

- Gold respects round numbers ($2,000, $2,050, $2,100)
- Previous day's high/low are key levels
- Weekly open price acts as a magnet
- Asian session high/low often gets swept during London

### 3. News-Based Trading

- Wait for high-impact USD news (NFP, CPI, FOMC)
- Do NOT trade during the news release (volatility is chaotic)
- Wait for the dust to settle (15-30 minutes post-release)
- Then trade the resulting trend

### 4. London Session Breakout

- Mark the Asian session range (high and low)
- When London session opens, wait for a breakout above or below
- Enter on the retest of the breakout level
- Stop loss behind the opposite side of the range

## Risk Management for Gold

<Callout type="warning" title="Gold Risk Rules">

Gold's high volatility requires special risk management:

1. **Reduce lot size** — Trade 50-75% smaller positions compared to forex
2. **Wider stops** — Gold needs 50-150 pip stops minimum (tight stops get hunted)
3. **Watch for news** — Gold can move 300+ pips on major news in minutes
4. **Check DXY first** — Always confirm dollar direction before entering gold trades
5. **Avoid Asian session** — Low liquidity = choppy price action and stop hunting

</Callout>

---

## Common Mistakes

<Callout type="warning" title="Gold Trading Pitfalls">

**❌ Using forex-sized stop losses** — A 20-pip stop on gold will get hit constantly. Gold needs wider stops.

**❌ Over-leveraging** — Gold's point value is $100 per standard lot. One wrong move can be very costly.

**❌ Ignoring the Dollar** — Trading gold without checking DXY is like driving blindfolded.

**❌ Trading during consolidation** — Gold in Asian session is choppy. Wait for London or NY.

**❌ Fighting the trend** — Gold trends can last weeks. Do not counter-trend trade without strong reasons.

**❌ Not accounting for spread** — Gold spreads are 15-30 pips compared to 0.5-1.5 pips on EUR/USD.

</Callout>
