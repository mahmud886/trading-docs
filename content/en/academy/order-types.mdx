## Order Types Explained

An order is an instruction to buy or sell a security. Understanding different order types is essential for executing your trading plan effectively.

### Basic Order Types

#### 1. **Market Order**
A market order executes immediately at the current market price.

**Characteristics:**
- Executed instantly
- Guaranteed execution
- Price not guaranteed (slippage possible)
- Best for entering/exiting positions quickly

**When to Use:**
- Need to enter/exit immediately
- During high volatility
- When price is moving fast
- Large position that needs quick execution

**Example:**
Buying 1 lot of EUR/USD at market price = execute immediately at current price

#### 2. **Limit Order**
A limit order specifies the maximum price you'll pay to buy or minimum price you'll accept to sell.

**Characteristics:**
- Not guaranteed to execute
- Fills only at specified price or better
- Useful for precise entry/exit points
- Can sit unfilled if price doesn't reach level

**Buy Limit Order:**
- Placed below current market price
- Executes when price falls to or below limit price
- Good for buying on dips

**Sell Limit Order:**
- Placed above current market price
- Executes when price rises to or above limit price
- Good for taking profits at target levels

**Example:**
Buying EUR/USD at 1.0950 limit = execute only if price reaches 1.0950 or lower

#### 3. **Stop Order (Stop-Loss)**
A stop order executes a market order when price reaches a specified level.

**Characteristics:**
- Provides risk protection
- Converts to market order at trigger price
- Execution price may differ from stop price (slippage)
- Essential for risk management

**Stop-Loss Order:**
- Below entry price for long positions
- Above entry price for short positions
- Prevents large losses

**Trailing Stop:**
- Automatically adjusts as price moves favorably
- Locks in profits while allowing for price movement
- Useful for trending markets

**Example:**
Buy at 1.0950, place stop at 1.0920 = if price falls to 1.0920, sell immediately to limit losses

#### 4. **Stop-Limit Order**
Combines stop order and limit order functionality.

**Characteristics:**
- Triggers at stop price
- Fills at limit price or better
- Provides control but no execution guarantee
- Two price points needed

**How It Works:**
1. Set stop price (trigger)
2. Set limit price (execution price)
3. When price reaches stop, limit order activates
4. Order fills only at limit price or better

**Example:**
Buy stop-limit: stop at 1.1000, limit at 1.1005
- Triggers when price touches 1.1000
- Only executes at 1.1005 or lower
- Won't execute if price jumps to 1.1010

### Advanced Order Types

#### 5. **One-Cancels-Other (OCO) Order**
Two orders where execution of one automatically cancels the other.

**Use Case:**
- Profit taking at resistance + stop loss below support
- If price rises and hits profit target, stop loss cancels automatically
- If price falls and hits stop loss, profit target cancels automatically

#### 6. **One-Triggers-Other (OTO) Order**
Executing one order automatically triggers another order.

**Use Case:**
- Enter position with OTO order
- Entry order triggers, automatically places stop loss and take profit
- Ensures risk management automatically

#### 7. **Iceberg Order**
A large order divided into smaller visible portions.

**Characteristics:**
- Only a portion visible to market
- Rest hidden until visible portion fills
- Useful for large position entries
- Reduces market impact

### Order Placement Best Practices

#### 1. **Set Stop Loss First**
- Never trade without a stop loss
- Place at logical levels based on support/resistance
- Consider volatility when setting distance

#### 2. **Risk-Reward Ratio**
- Target profit should be 2:1 or better
- Example: Risk $100 to make $200 (2:1)
- Improves long-term profitability

#### 3. **Use Limit Orders for Entries**
- More precise entry prices
- Often get better fills
- Risk missing the trade
- Good for planned setups

#### 4. **Use Market Orders for Exits**
- Guarantee execution when hitting targets
- Avoid leaving money on the table
- Accept slight slippage risk

#### 5. **Time in Force**
Different duration options for orders:
- **Day Order:** Cancels at end of trading day
- **Good-Till-Canceled (GTC):** Remains until filled or canceled
- **Immediate or Cancel (IOC):** Fills immediately or cancels
- **Fill or Kill (FOK):** All or nothing, executes immediately

### Common Order Mistakes

1. **No Stop Loss:** Never trade without one
2. **Stop Too Close:** Gets hit by normal volatility
3. **Huge Risk Per Trade:** Ruins account over time
4. **Chasing Entries:** Market orders at bad prices
5. **Moving Stops Against You:** Converts winners to losers
6. **Not Using Limits on Targets:** Leaves profits on table

### Order Management Checklist

- [ ] Stop loss placed immediately
- [ ] Risk-reward ratio favorable (2:1+)
- [ ] Position size appropriate
- [ ] Orders placed at logical levels
- [ ] Multiple timeframes confirmed
- [ ] Trade aligned with current trend

## Summary

Different order types serve different purposes:
- **Market Orders:** Quick execution
- **Limit Orders:** Precise pricing
- **Stop Orders:** Risk control
- **Stop-Limit:** Control with risk
- **Advanced Orders:** Automation and strategy execution

Master order types to execute your trading plan with precision and discipline.
