## Options Trading Basics

Options are derivative instruments that give traders the right to buy or sell an underlying asset at a specified price by a certain date.

### Understanding Options

**What is an Option?**
An option is a contract giving the holder the right, but not the obligation, to:
- **Call Option:** Buy the underlying asset at a specific price
- **Put Option:** Sell the underlying asset at a specific price

**Key Terminology:**
- **Strike Price:** The agreed-upon price to buy/sell
- **Expiration Date:** Last day the option can be exercised
- **Premium:** Price paid to buy the option
- **Underlying Asset:** The asset the option is based on (EUR/USD, S&P 500, etc.)

### Call Options

A call option gives the right to buy the underlying asset.

**When to Buy Calls:**
- Expect the price to rise
- Limited risk (premium paid)
- Unlimited profit potential
- Leverage on capital

**When to Sell Calls:**
- Expect the price to stay flat or fall
- Collect premium immediately
- Unlimited risk (in theory)
- Limited profit (premium received)

**Example:**
- EUR/USD trading at 1.0950
- Buy call option: strike 1.1000, premium 50 pips
- Cost: $500 (50 pips × $10 per pip)
- Profit if above 1.1050 at expiration
- Loss limited to $500

### Put Options

A put option gives the right to sell the underlying asset.

**When to Buy Puts:**
- Expect the price to fall
- Limited risk (premium paid)
- Profit from downward moves
- Insurance against losses

**When to Sell Puts:**
- Expect the price to stay flat or rise
- Collect premium
- Unlimited risk if falls significantly
- Limited profit (premium received)

**Example:**
- EUR/USD trading at 1.0950
- Buy put option: strike 1.0900, premium 40 pips
- Cost: $400
- Profit if below 1.0860 at expiration
- Loss limited to $400

### Option Greeks (Risk Metrics)

#### Delta
Measures how much the option price changes with each 1-pip move in the underlying.
- **Call Delta:** 0 to 1.0
- **Put Delta:** -1.0 to 0
- At-the-money (ATM) options: ~0.5 delta
- Deep in-the-money: Delta approaches 1.0
- Deep out-of-the-money: Delta approaches 0

#### Gamma
Measures how quickly delta changes.
- ATM options have highest gamma
- OTM and ITM options have lower gamma
- High gamma = fast price changes

#### Theta (Time Decay)
Measures how much value the option loses with each day passing.
- Positive theta when selling options (time works for you)
- Negative theta when buying options (time works against you)
- Accelerates closer to expiration

#### Vega
Measures sensitivity to volatility changes.
- Higher vega = more sensitive to volatility
- Positive vega for long options (profit from volatility increase)
- Negative vega for short options (profit from volatility decrease)

### Option Strategies for Different Market Views

#### 1. **Bullish Strategies**

**Long Call:**
- Buy call option
- Profit if price rises above strike + premium
- Limited risk, unlimited profit
- Best for: Expect strong directional move up

**Call Spread:**
- Buy lower strike call
- Sell higher strike call
- Limited risk and limited profit
- Lower cost than long call
- Best for: Moderate bullish view

**Covered Call:**
- Own underlying asset or long position
- Sell call option
- Collect premium, capped upside
- Best for: Generate income on owned positions

#### 2. **Bearish Strategies**

**Long Put:**
- Buy put option
- Profit if price falls below strike - premium
- Limited risk, unlimited profit
- Best for: Expect strong downward move

**Put Spread:**
- Buy higher strike put
- Sell lower strike put
- Limited risk and profit
- Lower cost than long put
- Best for: Moderate bearish view

**Protective Put:**
- Buy protective put
- Protects against downside
- Like insurance policy
- Best for: Protect long positions

#### 3. **Neutral Strategies**

**Iron Condor:**
- Sell OTM call spread
- Sell OTM put spread
- Profit from price staying in range
- Limited risk and profit
- Best for: Range-bound markets

**Short Straddle:**
- Sell ATM call and put
- Profit from no movement
- High risk, collects premium
- Best for: Low volatility environments

### Basic Option Trading Rules

#### 1. **Position Sizing**
- Risk only what you can afford to lose
- Start with small positions
- Each option contract = 100 units of underlying (typically)
- Adjust contract size for account size

#### 2. **Time Value Decay**
- Buying options loses time value daily (theta)
- Selling options gains time value daily
- Close option trades with 2-3 weeks to expiration
- Never hold options to expiration (if first trade)

#### 3. **Profit Taking**
- Close winners at 50% profit
- Let winners run if strong momentum
- Cut losses at 20-30% loss
- Don't hold short-term options for years

#### 4. **Volatility Awareness**
- Buy options during low volatility
- Sell options during high volatility
- Check implied volatility before entering
- Volatility expansion can end trades early

### Options Trading Process

**Step 1: Choose Underlying Asset**
- Stock, ETF, Index, Forex, or Cryptocurrency
- Liquid markets preferred (tight bid-ask spread)

**Step 2: Determine Market View**
- Bullish = Buy Call or Sell Put
- Bearish = Buy Put or Sell Call
- Neutral = Iron Condor or Straddle

**Step 3: Select Strike Price**
- ATM (At The Money) = Balanced risk/reward
- ITM (In The Money) = Higher probability, lower profit
- OTM (Out of The Money) = Lower probability, higher profit

**Step 4: Choose Expiration Date**
- Short-term: 7-30 days
- Medium-term: 30-60 days
- Long-term: 60+ days
- Avoid final week (fastest decay)

**Step 5: Analyze Price**
- Is premium reasonable?
- Check IV (implied volatility)
- Compare bid-ask spread
- Calculate risk-reward

**Step 6: Set Exits**
- Profit target (usually 50% gain)
- Stop loss (usually 20-30% loss)
- Expiration date (automatic exit)
- Time stop (exit if no movement)

### Greeks in Action Example

**Short Straddle Setup:**
- EUR/USD at 1.0950
- Sell call at 1.1000 (ATM): Premium 40
- Sell put at 1.0900 (ATM): Premium 40
- Total premium collected: 80 pips ($800)

**Greeks:**
- Delta: ~0 (neutral position)
- Theta: Positive (time decay helps)
- Vega: Negative (volatility hurts)

**Profit Scenario:**
- EUR/USD stays between 1.0900-1.1000
- Both options expire worthless
- Keep entire 80 pips premium

### Common Options Trading Mistakes

1. **Buying Far OTM Options:** Low probability, high loss
2. **Ignoring Time Decay:** Theta accelerates near expiration
3. **Holding to Expiration:** Creates unnecessary risk
4. **Over-Leveraging:** Options can move 10%+ daily
5. **Not Following Greeks:** Price movement ≠ profit
6. **Trading Illiquid Pairs:** Wide spreads destroy profits
7. **Selling Unlimited Risk:** Sell puts/calls only if prepared
8. **Chasing Premium:** Don't overpay for options

### Paper Trading Options

Before real money:
1. Paper trade 20-30 trades
2. Track actual costs (bid-ask spreads)
3. Monitor Greeks throughout trade
4. Test strategy in different market conditions
5. Refine timing and strike selection

## Summary

Options Trading:
- **Call:** Bet on price rising
- **Put:** Bet on price falling
- **Premium:** Cost to buy, reward for selling
- **Greeks:** Risk metrics guiding decisions
- **Strategies:** Combine options for different views
- **Key:** Understand time decay and volatility impact

Start with simple strategies (long calls/puts), master the Greeks, and progress to spreads. Options offer leverage and defined risk when used properly.
