## Perpetual Futures & Funding Rates

Perpetual futures are the most popular crypto derivatives. Understanding funding rates and leverage impacts is critical for profitability.

### What are Perpetual Futures?

**Perpetual Futures** = Leveraged contracts with no expiration date

**Characteristics:**
- **No expiry:** Hold indefinitely (vs. quarterly futures expiring)
- **Leverage:** 1x to 125x (depending on exchange)
- **Leverage margin:** Funding rate instead of carrying cost
- **Settlement:** 8-hour funding rate intervals
- **Basis:** Usually tracks spot price closely
- **Most popular:** Binance, OKX, Bybit, Kraken

### How Perpetual Futures Work

#### **Opening a Position:**

1. **Deposit Margin** (collateral, usually 1-10% of position)
2. **Select Leverage** (2x to 125x)
3. **Enter Position** (long or short)
4. **Pay Funding Rates** (every 8 hours)
5. **Manage Position** (adjust stops/targets)
6. **Close Position** (lock in P&L)

**Example:**
- Balance: $1,000
- Leverage: 10x
- Can control: $10,000 position
- In Bitcoin at $40,000: 0.25 BTC position

### Funding Rates Explained

**Funding Rates** = Interest paid between longs and shorts every 8 hours

**Purpose:**
- Keep perpetual price = spot price
- If longs are overleveraged: Longs pay shorts (negative funding)
- If shorts are overleveraged: Shorts pay longs (positive funding)
- Rebalancing mechanism

**How It Works:**

**Positive Funding Rate (Bullish):**
- Many longs, few shorts
- Price above spot (premium)
- Longs pay shorts
- Incentive to short (arbitrage)
- Shorts getting paid to open positions

**Negative Funding Rate (Bearish):**
- Many shorts, few longs
- Price below spot (discount)
- Shorts pay longs
- Incentive to long (arbitrage)
- Longs getting paid to open positions

**Typical Rates:**
- Normal: +0.02% to -0.02% per period
- Elevated: 0.05-0.20% (overleveraged market)
- Extreme: 0.30%+ (panic levels)

### Funding Rate Calculation

**Funding Payment = Position Size × Funding Rate**

**Example with Positive Rate:**
- Position: 10 Bitcoin
- Funding rate: +0.05%
- Payment due: 10 × 0.05% = 0.005 BTC
- Going LONG, you pay shorts 0.005 BTC
- Going SHORT, shorts pay you 0.005 BTC

**Annual Impact (if constant 0.05% = +1.5% annual rate):**
- Short perpetuals earning 1.5% annually (vs. 0% on spot)
- Long perpetuals paying 1.5% annually
- Compounding all 8-hour periods

### Funding Rate Strategies

#### **Strategy 1: Funding Rate Harvesting**
- **When funding extreme** (above 0.30%): Too many longs overleveraged
- **Action:** Short perpetuals
- **Benefit:** Earn fundingrate + position profit (if price falls)
- **Risk:** Price could spike higher before funding drops

**Example:**
- Fund rate: +0.50% (extreme)
- Open short position
- Earn ~0.50% every 8 hours
- Price drops: 2x profit (short profit + funding)
- If price rises: Minimal loss (funding offset decline)

#### **Strategy 2: Spot-Futures Arbitrage**
- **Mechanism:** Spot price lower than perpetual
- **Action:** Buy spot, short perpetual
- **Profit:** Funding rate harvesting + price convergence
- **Risk:** Minimal if execution perfect
- **Requires:** Quick execution, low fees

**Example:**
- Bitcoin spot: $39,900
- Perpetual funding: +0.30%
- Buy spot at $39,900
- Short perp at $40,100
- Earn funding + $200 convergence
- Total: "Riskless" profit

#### **Strategy 3: Funding Fade**
- **When:** Funding rates extreme (too many longs)
- **Act:** Short initially
- **Why:** Extreme funding unsustainable, will decrease, price may fall
- **Directional bet:** That overleveraged market will deleverage

#### **Strategy 4: Funding Ride**
- **When:** Funding high (market bullish)
- **Act:** Go long
- **Why:** Earn funding while riding trend
- **Dual profit:** Position gain + funding income

### Leverage Impact on Funding Rates

**High Leverage = Higher Risk = Higher Funding Rates:**
- 2x leverage: Less overleveraged = lower funding rates
- 10x leverage: More overleveraged = higher funding rates
- 100x leverage: Extremely overleveraged = extreme funding rates
- Vicious cycle: High rates attract shorts, creates liquidations

**Leverage Liquidation Cascade:**
1. Many traders use 50x+ leverage
2. Small price move = massive losses
3. Liquidations trigger = positions automatically closed
4. More liquidations = more selling spree
5. More funding rate extremes
6. Vicious feedback loop

### Managing Leverage Positions

**Position Sizing with Leverage:**
```
Max position risk = Account size × Risk %
Position size = Max position risk ÷ (Leverage × Stop distance)
```

**Example:**
- Account: $10,000
- Risk: 2% = $200
- Leverage: 10x
- Stop distance: 50 pips
- Position size: $200 ÷ (10 × $50) = 0.04 BTC

**Margin Ratio:**
- Healthy: Margin ratio > 30%
- Risk: Margin ratio 20-30%
- Danger: Margin ratio < 20%
- Liquidation often at 3-10% depending on exchange

**Margin Ratio Calculation:**
```
Margin Ratio = Account Equity ÷ Used Margin
- 50%: Very safe (can lose 50% before liquidation)
- 30%: Safe (can lose 70%)
- 20%: Risky (can lose 80%)
- 10%: Very risky (can lose 90%)
- < 10%: Liquidation imminent
```

### Perpetual Futures Risks

**Liquidation Risk:**
- Positions auto-closed at loss
- Liquidation price calculated by exchange
- Above leverage = closer liquidation
- Market gaps can gap through liquidation price = account wipe

**Funding Rate Risk:**
- Extended negative rate = loss for shorts
- Extended positive rate = loss for longs
- Can exceed profits

**Manipulation Risk:**
- Unregulated exchanges possible
- Sudden "updates" to liquidation engine
- Pump-and-dumps possible
- Insider manipulation

**Leverage Spiral Risk:**
- Cascade liquidations
- Market gaps
- Flash crashes
- Extreme slippage

### Safe Perpetual Futures Trading

**Conservative Approach:**
- Use 2:1 leverage maximum
- Position sizing: Risk 1% absolute max
- Stop losses mandatory
- Take profits regularly
- Manage funding rate costs
- Monitor liquidation price
- Never go all-in

**Professional Approach:**
- Use moderate leverage (5-10x)
- But with strict position sizing
- Diversified positions
- Comprehensive risk management
- Automated trading
- Always under-leverage true account capacity

### Perpetual vs Spot

| Factor | Spot | Perpetual |
|--------|------|-----------|
| **Leverage** | None | 1-125x |
| **Funding** | None | Yes, every 8h |
| **Expiration** | None | None |
| **Collateral** | 100% | 1-100% |
| **Risk** | Limited to capital | Liquidation possible |
| **Complexity** | Simple | Advanced |
| **Yield** | Capital gains only | Funding income possible |

## Summary

**Perpetual Futures:**
- Leveraged crypto derivatives
- No expiration date
- Funding rates facilitate price pegging
- Trading funding rates possible
- Leverage extremely risky

**Key Takeaways:**
- Leverage amplifies risk AND reward
- Funding rates can be harvested
- Liquidation risk critical
- Proper position sizing essential
- Most leverage traders blow accounts

Perpetual futures are powerful tools but require discipline, risk management, and understanding of unique mechanics.
