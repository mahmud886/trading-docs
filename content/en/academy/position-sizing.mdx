## What is Position Sizing?

Position sizing determines **how many units** (lots, shares, contracts) you trade on each position. It is the single most important factor in determining whether you survive long enough to become profitable.

<Callout type="info" title="The Golden Rule">
Position sizing is NOT about how much you can make — it is about how much you can afford to lose. Every trade should risk a fixed percentage of your account, regardless of how confident you feel.
</Callout>

---

## Why Position Sizing Matters

Consider two traders with the same strategy (60% win rate, 1:2 risk-reward):

| Metric | Trader A (Random Sizing) | Trader B (Fixed 1% Risk) |
|--------|--------------------------|--------------------------|
| Starting Balance | $10,000 | $10,000 |
| After 100 Trades | $3,200 (blown) | $14,800 (profitable) |
| Max Drawdown | 78% | 12% |
| Survival | ❌ Account blown | ✅ Consistently growing |

The **only difference** is position sizing. Same strategy, same market — completely different outcomes.

## The Position Sizing Formula

The universal formula for calculating position size:

**Position Size = Risk Amount / Stop Loss Distance**

Where:
- **Risk Amount** = Account Balance x Risk Percentage
- **Stop Loss Distance** = Entry Price - Stop Loss Price (in pips, points, or dollars)

### Example Calculation

- Account Balance: $10,000
- Risk per trade: 1% = $100
- Entry: 1.1050 (EUR/USD)
- Stop Loss: 1.1020 (30 pips away)
- Pip value for 1 standard lot: $10/pip

**Position Size** = $100 / (30 pips x $10) = $100 / $300 = **0.33 lots**

## Position Sizing Methods

### 1. Fixed Percentage Method (Recommended)

Risk a fixed percentage of your current account balance on every trade.

| Account Size | Risk % | Risk Amount | 30-pip SL | Lot Size |
|-------------|--------|-------------|-----------|----------|
| $1,000 | 1% | $10 | 30 pips | 0.03 |
| $5,000 | 1% | $50 | 30 pips | 0.17 |
| $10,000 | 1% | $100 | 30 pips | 0.33 |
| $50,000 | 1% | $500 | 30 pips | 1.67 |

**Advantages:**
- Automatically adjusts as account grows or shrinks
- Protects during drawdowns (smaller sizes when losing)
- Compounds gains during winning streaks

### 2. Fixed Dollar Amount

Risk the same dollar amount on every trade regardless of account size.

- Example: Always risk $50 per trade
- **Problem:** Does not scale with account growth or drawdown
- **Use case:** Very small accounts where percentage calculation is impractical

### 3. Kelly Criterion

A mathematical formula for optimal bet sizing:

**Kelly % = W - ((1 - W) / R)**

Where:
- W = Win rate (decimal)
- R = Average win / Average loss ratio

**Example:** Win rate 55%, avg win $200, avg loss $100
- Kelly % = 0.55 - ((1 - 0.55) / 2) = 0.55 - 0.225 = **32.5%**

<Callout type="warning" title="Kelly Criterion Warning">
Full Kelly is extremely aggressive. Most professional traders use **Quarter Kelly** (divide by 4) or **Half Kelly** (divide by 2) to reduce volatility. In this example, Quarter Kelly would be about 8% — still aggressive for most traders.
</Callout>

### 4. Fixed Ratio Method (Ryan Jones)

Increases position size based on accumulated profits rather than account balance:
- Start with 1 lot
- Add 1 lot for every $X in profits (delta)
- Reduces risk of giving back profits during drawdowns

## Position Sizing for Different Markets

### Forex

- Pip value varies by pair and lot size
- Cross pairs (EUR/GBP) have different pip values than USD pairs
- Always check pip value before calculating

**Formula:** Lot Size = Risk Amount / (SL in pips x Pip Value per lot)

### Gold (XAUUSD)

- Gold moves in **points**, not pips
- 1 standard lot = 100 oz, 1 point = $1 per oz = **$100 per point**
- Gold is highly volatile — use smaller position sizes

**Example:** $10,000 account, 1% risk ($100), Stop Loss = 5 points
- Position Size = $100 / (5 x $100) = **0.20 lots**

### Indices (NAS100, US30)

- Each index has different point values
- NAS100: 1 standard lot = $1 per point (varies by broker)
- US30: 1 standard lot = $1 per point (varies by broker)
- Always verify contract specifications with your broker

### Crypto (BTC, ETH)

- Position size in units of the coin
- Risk Amount / (Entry - Stop Loss) = Units to buy
- Example: $5,000 account, 1% risk ($50), BTC entry $60,000, SL $59,000
- Units = $50 / $1,000 = **0.05 BTC**

## Risk Percentage Guidelines

| Trader Type | Risk per Trade | Max Open Risk |
|------------|---------------|---------------|
| Conservative | 0.5% | 2-3% |
| Standard | 1% | 3-5% |
| Moderate | 1.5% | 5-7% |
| Aggressive | 2% | 6-10% |

<Callout type="warning" title="Never Exceed 2%">
Professional traders rarely risk more than 2% per trade. Risking 5% per trade means just 6 consecutive losses will draw your account down by 26%. At 10% risk, 6 losses = 47% drawdown.
</Callout>

## The Math of Drawdowns

Understanding how drawdowns compound:

| Drawdown | Gain Needed to Recover |
|----------|----------------------|
| 10% | 11.1% |
| 20% | 25% |
| 30% | 42.9% |
| 50% | 100% |
| 70% | 233% |
| 90% | 900% |

This is why position sizing and risk management are **survival tools**, not optional extras.

## Scaling Strategies

### Scaling In (Adding to Winners)

1. Enter with 50% of planned position size
2. Add 25% when price moves in your favor and confirms
3. Add final 25% at the next confirmation
4. Move stop loss to breakeven on first position

### Scaling Out (Partial Profits)

1. Take 50% profit at 1R (1x your risk)
2. Move stop to breakeven
3. Let remaining 50% run to 2R or 3R
4. Trail stop behind structure

## Common Position Sizing Mistakes

<Callout type="warning" title="Avoid These Errors">

**❌ Risking too much per trade** — Even the best strategy will blow your account with 5-10% risk per trade.

**❌ Not adjusting for volatility** — A 30-pip stop on EUR/USD is very different from a 30-pip stop on GBP/JPY.

**❌ Ignoring correlation** — 3 trades on EUR/USD, GBP/USD, and AUD/USD are essentially 3 trades against USD. Your real risk is 3x what you think.

**❌ Increasing size after losses** — This is the Martingale trap. Never double down after a loss.

**❌ Calculating lot size based on feeling** — Always use the formula. No exceptions.

**❌ Not accounting for gaps** — Weekend gaps or news events can blow past your stop loss.

</Callout>

## Position Size Calculator Checklist

Before every trade, answer these 5 questions:

1. **What is my account balance right now?** (Not what it was yesterday)
2. **What percentage am I risking?** (Should be 0.5-2%)
3. **Where is my stop loss?** (Must be placed BEFORE calculating size)
4. **What is the pip/point value for this instrument?** (Check broker specs)
5. **What is the calculated position size?** (Use the formula, not feelings)
