## Elliott Wave Theory

Elliott Wave Theory is a method of analyzing market cycles based on the observation that prices move in identifiable patterns or waves.

### The Basics of Elliott Waves

Elliott identified that markets move in repetitive cycles:
- **Impulse waves:** 5 waves in the direction of the trend (1, 2, 3, 4, 5)
- **Corrective waves:** 3 waves against the trend (A, B, C)

### The 5-Wave Impulse Pattern

An impulse wave consists of 5 waves: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5

**Wave 1: Initial Move**
- First move off a low
- Often meets resistance
- Many traders miss this wave

**Wave 2: Pullback/Retracement**
- Corrects wave 1
- Retraces 38.2%, 50%, or 61.8% of wave 1
- Never goes below wave 1 start (never breaks below the low)

**Wave 3: The Strongest Wave**
- Typically the longest and strongest
- Goes beyond wave 1 high
- Often 161.8% of wave 1
- Attracts most traders

**Wave 4: Pullback**
- Retraces wave 3 (typically 23.6%-50%)
- Stays above wave 1 high
- Complex structure

**Wave 5: Final Wave**
- Smaller than wave 3, usually
- Reaches toward 161.8% of wave 3
- Often shows declining momentum entering

**Fibonacci in Elliott Waves:**
- Wave 3 often = 1.618 × wave 1
- Wave 5 often = 0.618 × wave 3
- Targets calculated using Fibonacci ratios

### The 3-Wave Corrective Pattern

Corrections come in three waves: A, B, C

**Types of Corrections:**

**Zigzag (5-3-5):**
- Wave A: 5 waves down (in a downtrend)
- Wave B: 3 waves up (retracement of A)
- Wave C: 5 waves down (2X wave A)

**Flat (3-3-5):**
- Wave A: 3 waves down
- Wave B: 3 waves up (starts near start of wave A)
- Wave C: 5 waves down

**Triangle (3-3-3-3-3):**
- Five three-wave moves
- Progressively smaller
- Breakout at E wave

**Complex Correction:**
- Combinations of the above
- Could be zigzag + flat
- Extends corrections longer

### Elliott Wave Rules (Don't Break These)

**Rule 1: Wave 2 Cannot Retrace Below Wave 1**
- If price goes below wave 1 low, the count is wrong
- Start over with wave counting

**Rule 2: Wave 3 Cannot Be the Shortest of Waves 1, 3, 5**
- Wave 3 must be longer than at least one of 1 and 5
- Usually wave 3 is the longest

**Rule 3: Wave 4 Cannot Overlap Wave 1**
- Wave 4 low must stay above wave 1 high
- Exception in expanding diagonals

### Wave Degrees (Time Scales)

Elliott Waves exist at all timeframes:
- **Grand Supercycle:** Years-decades
- **Supercycle:** Several years
- **Cycle:** 1-3 years
- **Primary:** Months
- **Intermediate:** Weeks
- **Minor:** Days
- **Minute:** Hours
- **Minuette:** Minutes

**Strategy:** Count on multiple timeframes simultaneously.

### Elliott Wave Trading Strategy

#### 1. **Wave 3 Trading (Safest)**
- Wait for wave 1 to complete
- Enter on the beginning of wave 3 (confirmed)
- Stop loss: Below wave 2 low
- Target: 1.618 × wave 1 distance
- Most reliable wave to trade

#### 2. **Wave 1/5 Trading**
- Less aggressive than wave 3
- Can short on wave completion (before correction)
- Target = Previous impulse height

#### 3. **Corrective Wave Trading**
- Trade bounces in corrections (shorter term)
- Less predictable than impulses
- Use support/resistance for entries

### Practical Elliott Wave Application

**Step 1: Identify the Trend**
- Is price in an impulse (5 waves) or correction (3 waves)?
- What timeframe are you analyzing?

**Step 2: Count the Waves**
- Form = 5 waves up = impulse uptrend
- Label: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5
- Calculate using Fibonacci ratios

**Step 3: Assess Wave Position**
- Where are we in the pattern?
- What's the next expected move?
- What's the target for this wave?

**Step 4: Trade the Setup**
- Enter: Confirmed beginning of impulsive wave
- Stop: Below/above previous wave appropriately
- Target: Fibonacci level expected

### Common Elliott Wave Mistakes

1. **Miscounting:** Count waves carefully, use Fibonacci
2. **Wrong timeframe:** Count on multiple timeframes
3. **Rule breaking:** If rules broken, recount immediately
4. **Ignoring volume:** Volume should increase in impulse waves
5. **Trading corrections:** Corrections are less profitable; stick to impulses
6. **Rigid expectation:** Market doesn't always follow perfect Elliott patterns
7. **Incomplete analysis:** Always have plan for waves 4 & 5 before entering wave 3

### Elliott Wave + Other Analysis

**Elliott + Fibonacci:**
- Wave targets calculated via Fibonacci
- Combined gives high accuracy
- Use 1.618, 0.618 multiplied by wave size

**Elliott + Support/Resistance:**
- Wave targets align with key resistance/support
- Confluence = higher probability
- Wave 4 often pulls back to previous support

**Elliott + Volume:**
- Impulse waves: Volume increases
- Corrective waves: Volume decreases
- Divergence warns of wave completion

### Realistic Expectations

Elliott Wave Theory:
- **Best for:** Identifying major trend direction
- **Better for:** Setting profit targets with Fibonacci
- **Predicting exact tops/bottoms:** Very difficult
- **Mechanical trading:** Requires confirmation from other factors

The best use: Elliott to identify structure and targets, then use price action/confluence for entries.

## Summary

Elliott Wave Theory:
- **5-wave impulse:** In direction of main trend
- **3-wave correction:** Against the trend
- **Fibonacci ratios:** Generate targets
- **Multiple timeframes:** Confirm patterns
- **Wave 3 trading:** Most reliable opportunity

Elliott Waves provide a framework for understanding market structure. Combined with Fibonacci and other confirmation, you have a powerful system for identifying high-probability moves.
