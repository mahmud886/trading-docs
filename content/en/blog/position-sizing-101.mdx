## The #1 Reason Trading Accounts Blow Up

You know the feeling. You had a great strategy. Your backtests looked perfect. You were so confident...

Then one bad trade wiped out your entire account.

**How did it happen?**

Not because your strategy was bad. But because **you sized your position wrong**.

Position sizing is the single most important factor in trading — more important than entry signals, more important than indicators, more important than anything else. Yet most traders ignore it completely.

---

## The 1% Rule (The Foundation)

The **1% Rule** is the most common position sizing method used by professional traders:

**Risk only 1% of your total account on any single trade.**

### How it works:

Let's say you have a **$10,000 account**.

1% of $10,000 = **$100**

So you risk a maximum of **$100 per trade**.

If your stop loss is **50 pips away**, and you're trading EUR/USD (each pip = $10 on a standard lot):
- **$100 ÷ $10 per pip ÷ 50 pips = 0.2 standard lots** (or 2 micro lots)

That's your position size.

### Why 1%?

**Draw-downs are INEVITABLE.** Even pros have losing streaks. If you risk too much, one bad streak will wipe you out.

With 1% risk, here's what happens after a losing streak:

| Scenario | Position Size | 5 Losses in a Row | 10 Losses | Recovery Needed |
|----------|---|---|---|---|
| 1% Risk | Your account after: | -$500 (from $10K to $9,500) | -$1,000 (from $10K to $9,000) | 5-10 wins to recover |
| 5% Risk | Your account after: | -$2,500 (from $10K to $7,500) | -$5,000 (from $10K to $5,000) | **50+ wins needed** |
| 10% Risk | Your account after: | -$5,000 (from $10K to $5,000) | -$10,000 (ACCOUNT BLOWN) | **Starting over** |

**See the difference?**

With 1% risk, a 10-loss streak hurts but doesn't kill you. With 10% risk, you're done.

## The Kelly Criterion (For Advanced Traders)

The Kelly Criterion is a mathematical formula to calculate the **optimal position size** based on your win rate and reward-to-risk ratio.

**Formula:**
```
Position Size % = (Win % × Average Win) - (Loss % × Average Loss)
                  ÷ Average Win
```

### Example:

If your strategy has:
- **Win rate:** 60%
- **Average winner:** 100 pips
- **Average loser:** 50 pips

```
Position Size % = (0.60 × 100) - (0.40 × 50)
                  ÷ 100
                = (60 - 20)
                  ÷ 100
                = 40%
```

This means you can risk **40% per trade** and still grow your account optimally.

<Callout type="warning" title="Kelly Criterion Warning">
This assumes:
1. Your statistics are ACCURATE (from 100+ real trades, not backtests)
2. Your past performance continues into the future
3. You're willing to risk larger positions

Most traders use **Half-Kelly (20%)** for safety, since real trading has slippage and fees that reduce effectiveness.
</Callout>

## Real-World Position Sizing Examples

### Example 1: Conservative Day Trader

- **Account:** $5,000
- **Risk per trade:** 1% = $50
- **Trading:** EUR/USD, micro lots
- **Stop loss:** 40 pips

Position size = $50 ÷ $10 per 100 pips ÷ 40 pips = **0.125 micro lots (12.5 pips per pip value)**

Actually, simplest: Use a **1 micro lot** (10,000 units) = $0.10 per pip
- Risk = $0.10 × 40 pips = $4 per stop (well under 1%)

---

### Example 2: Gold Trader (Larger Account)

- **Account:** $50,000
- **Risk per trade:** 1% = $500
- **Trading:** Gold (XAU/USD), 1 lot = 100 oz
- **Stop loss:** 20 pips ($20 per pip for 1 lot)

Position size = $500 ÷ $20 per pip per lot = **25 lots** (25 × 100 oz = 2,500 oz)

Verify: 25 lots × $20 per pip × 20 pips = $10,000... **Wait, that's 20% of account!**

Let's recalculate: We want $500 risk
- $500 ÷ $20 per pip = 25 pips
- So our stop should be 25 pips, not 20

OR reduce lots: $500 ÷ $20 per pip ÷ 20 pips = 1.25 lots ✅

## Common Position Sizing Mistakes

### Mistake #1: Ignoring Account Size Swings

"I risked 2% on that last trade and made $200. So I can risk 2% again."

**Wrong.** Your 1% risk should always be calculated from your NEW account balance after wins and losses.

- Account: $10,000 → 1% = $100
- After winning: $10,500 → 1% = $105
- After losing $500: $10,000 → 1% = $100

Recalculate your position size after EVERY trade.

### Mistake #2: Increasing Risk After Wins

"I just made 3 wins. I'm hot. I'll risk 3% this time."

This is how drawdowns turn into account blowups. **Stick to 1% regardless of win streaks.**

### Mistake #3: Adjusting Stop Loss to "Fit" Position Size

"My strategy says stop at 50 pips, but I sized for a 30-pip stop to get more pips. Let me move the stop."

**NEVER.** Position size is determined by your stop loss, not the other way around. If a setup requires a 50-pip stop, adjust your position size downward, don't move the stop.

## The Position Sizing Formula Cheat Sheet

**Quick reference for common scenarios:**

```
Position Size = Risk Amount ÷ (Stop Loss in Pips × Pip Value)
```

### Standard Lot (100,000 units):
- EUR/USD: 1 pip = $10
- GBP/USD: 1 pip = $10
- USD/JPY: 1 pip = $10 (approximately)

### Micro Lot (10,000 units):
- EUR/USD: 1 pip = $1
- GBP/USD: 1 pip = $1

### Example Calculation:
- Account: $5,000 (1% risk = $50)
- Stop loss: 50 pips
- Pair: EUR/USD (standard lot, $10 per pip)

Position size = $50 ÷ ($10 × 50 pips) = **0.1 standard lots** (1 micro lot)

## The Bottom Line

**Position sizing is your shield against disaster.**

Rules:
1. ✅ Risk 1% of your account per trade (1%)
2. ✅ Recalculate after every trade
3. ✅ Never adjust your stop loss to "fit" your position
4. ✅ Never increase risk after winning streaks
5. ✅ Use micro lots when starting out (less pressure)

A trader with a mediocre strategy but **excellent position sizing** will out-earn a trader with a great strategy but **poor position sizing** 100% of the time.

Boring? Yes. Effective? Absolutely.

Master position sizing and you'll never blow an account again.
