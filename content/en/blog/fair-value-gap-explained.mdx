## What is a Fair Value Gap?

A **Fair Value Gap (FVG)** is a price imbalance created when the market moves so aggressively that it leaves behind an inefficient area with little or no overlap between candles.

In simple terms, price moved too fast for both sides of the market to be fully matched. That imbalance often becomes a magnet for future retracement.

<Callout type="tip" title="Why FVGs Matter">
FVGs help traders identify where price may return to rebalance before continuing in the original direction. They are one of the cleanest tools for precision entries in ICT and SMC.
</Callout>

---

## How to Identify a Valid FVG

A valid FVG usually appears after **strong displacement**.

### Bullish FVG
- Candle 1 high is below Candle 3 low
- The middle candle shows strong impulsive buying
- Price often retraces into the gap before continuing up

### Bearish FVG
- Candle 1 low is above Candle 3 high
- The middle candle shows strong impulsive selling
- Price often retests the gap before continuing down

A simple way to remember it:
- **Bullish imbalance** = buy-side efficiency is still missing
- **Bearish imbalance** = sell-side efficiency is still missing

## FVG vs Order Block

FVGs and Order Blocks often work together, but they are not the same.

| Concept | What it Represents | Best Use |
|---------|--------------------|----------|
| **FVG** | Price inefficiency / imbalance | Precision retracement zone |
| **Order Block** | Institutional origin candle | Broader POI and confluence |

The strongest setups often happen when an FVG forms **inside or near an Order Block**.

## A Simple FVG Trading Model

1. Identify higher-timeframe direction
2. Wait for displacement that leaves an FVG
3. Check whether the gap aligns with premium/discount
4. Look for confluence with liquidity, BOS, or CHOCH
5. Enter on the retracement into the gap
6. Place the stop beyond the invalidation point
7. Target the next liquidity pool

### Example

- Daily trend: bullish
- London session sweeps a local low
- Price displaces upward and forms a bullish FVG on the 15M
- Price retraces into the gap during New York
- Entry is taken after confirmation
- Target is the previous day high

This is the kind of setup that gives traders a repeatable framework instead of random entries.

## Common Mistakes Traders Make

<Callout type="warning" title="Avoid These Mistakes">

**1. Trading every tiny gap** — Not every gap is meaningful. Look for displacement.

**2. Ignoring context** — A bullish FVG in a strong downtrend is lower probability.

**3. Entering too early** — Wait for price to reach the gap, then confirm reaction.

**4. No invalidation plan** — Always define the level that proves your idea wrong.

</Callout>

## Key Takeaways

- FVGs are market inefficiencies created by aggressive displacement
- They work best with trend, liquidity, and session context
- The strongest FVGs usually appear near an Order Block or after a sweep
- Use them as precision entry tools, not standalone signals
