## What is Price Action Trading?

Price Action trading is the art of making trading decisions based on **raw price movement** — no indicators, no oscillators, just the price chart itself. It's the purest form of technical analysis and has been used by professional traders for centuries.

While most beginners load their charts with dozens of indicators hoping for a "magic signal," experienced traders know that **price itself is the ultimate indicator**. Every indicator is derived from price anyway — so why not go straight to the source?

<Callout type="info" title="Why This Matters">
Understanding Price Action is the foundation for ALL other trading methodologies — including Smart Money Concepts (SMC) and ICT. Master this first, and everything else becomes easier.
</Callout>

---

## The Problem with Indicator-Based Trading

Most beginners start with indicators like RSI, MACD, and Moving Averages. While these tools have their place, relying on them exclusively creates several problems:

1. **Lagging Signals** — Indicators are calculated from past price data, so they always lag behind the current market
2. **Conflicting Signals** — RSI says buy, MACD says sell — which do you follow?
3. **False Sense of Security** — Indicators can't predict the future; they only describe the past
4. **Analysis Paralysis** — Too many indicators create confusion and indecision
5. **Missing Context** — Indicators don't tell you WHY price moved, only that it did

**Price Action solves all of these problems** by teaching you to read what price is actually doing RIGHT NOW.

## The Three Pillars of Price Action

### 1. Candlestick Analysis

Every candle tells a story about the battle between buyers and sellers:

- **Large green body** = Strong buying pressure, buyers dominated
- **Large red body** = Strong selling pressure, sellers dominated
- **Long upper wick** = Sellers rejected higher prices
- **Long lower wick** = Buyers rejected lower prices
- **Small body (Doji)** = Indecision, neither side won

The KEY is not memorizing 100 candlestick patterns — it's understanding the **logic** behind each candle shape.

### 2. Market Structure

Market structure is the **skeleton of price movement**:

- **Uptrend:** Higher Highs (HH) and Higher Lows (HL)
- **Downtrend:** Lower Highs (LH) and Lower Lows (LL)
- **Range:** Price bouncing between support and resistance

```
    Uptrend:                Downtrend:

         HH                LH
        /  \              /  \
       /    \            /    \
      HL     \          /      LL
     /        HH       LH        \
    /        /  \      /  \        \
   HL       /    \    /    \        LL
           HL     \  /      LL
```

When you can identify market structure, you know:
- **Which direction** to trade
- **Where** to look for entries (pullbacks in trends)
- **When** the trend might be ending (structure breaks)

### 3. Key Levels (Support & Resistance)

Key levels are price zones where **buying or selling pressure has historically been strong**:

- **Support:** A level where buying pressure enters, preventing further decline
- **Resistance:** A level where selling pressure enters, preventing further rise
- **Flip Zones:** Former support becomes resistance (and vice versa)

<Callout type="tip" title="Pro Tip">
Don't draw support and resistance as exact lines — they're ZONES. Price rarely respects an exact pip level. Mark them as areas (boxes) on your chart for more realistic expectations.
</Callout>

## Your First Price Action Analysis

Here's how to analyze any chart using Price Action:

### Step 1: Identify the Trend (Higher Timeframe)

Open the Daily or 4H chart and ask:
- Is price making HH and HL? → **Uptrend** → Look for buys
- Is price making LH and LL? → **Downtrend** → Look for sells
- Is price stuck in a range? → **Wait** or trade the range boundaries

### Step 2: Mark Key Levels

Draw horizontal zones at:
- Previous swing highs and lows
- Areas where price has bounced multiple times
- Round numbers (1.1000, 1.0500, etc.)

### Step 3: Wait for Price to Reach a Key Level

Don't chase price in the middle of nowhere. Wait for it to arrive at one of your marked levels.

### Step 4: Read the Candle at the Level

When price reaches your level, examine the candle:
- **Rejection candle (pin bar)** at support → Potential buy signal
- **Engulfing candle** at resistance → Potential sell signal
- **Doji** at a level → Wait for more confirmation

### Step 5: Enter with a Plan

Before entering, define:
- **Entry price:** Where exactly you'll enter
- **Stop loss:** Where the setup is invalidated
- **Take profit:** Where you expect price to reach
- **Position size:** Based on your risk (1-2% of account)

## Real Example: EUR/USD Daily Chart

Let me walk through a real scenario:

1. **Daily chart shows uptrend** — Clear HH and HL pattern
2. **Previous resistance at 1.0900** now acting as support (flip zone)
3. **Price pulls back to 1.0900** area
4. **Bullish engulfing candle** forms at 1.0900 — strong buying pressure
5. **Entry:** 1.0905 (above the engulfing candle close)
6. **Stop loss:** 1.0860 (below the swing low, 45 pips)
7. **Take profit:** 1.1000 (next resistance level, 95 pips)
8. **R:R = 1:2.1** — Favorable risk-reward
9. **Position size:** Based on 1% risk and 45-pip stop

## Common Beginner Mistakes

<Callout type="warning" title="Avoid These Errors">

**❌ Over-analyzing every single candle** — Not every candle is meaningful. Focus on candles at KEY LEVELS only.

**❌ Drawing too many support/resistance lines** — If your chart looks like a barcode, you have too many lines. Use 3-5 major levels per chart.

**❌ Trading without a stop loss** — Every Price Action setup can fail. Protect your capital.

**❌ Ignoring the higher timeframe** — A bullish pin bar on the 5-minute chart is meaningless if the Daily is in a strong downtrend.

**❌ Expecting 100% accuracy** — Even the best setups fail 30-40% of the time. That's normal. Your edge comes from risk management and R:R.

</Callout>

| Concept | Summary |
|---------|---------|
