## Why Scale In and Out?

Scaling lets you participate in a move while managing risk and improving average entry price. Instead of one large position, break entries into smaller pieces to adapt to market uncertainty. This is how **institutional traders and hedge funds** build positions — never all at once.

<Callout type="tip" title="Professional Standard">
  George Soros famously said: "It's not whether you're right or wrong, but how much money you make when you're right."
  Scaling maximizes gains on winners while minimizing losses on losers.
</Callout>

### Scaling Benefits

| Benefit                  | How It Helps                               |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------ |
| **Reduced Initial Risk** | Smaller first entry = Less capital at risk |
| **Better Average Price** | Add at confirmation = Improved average     |
| **Psychological Ease**   | Smaller size = Less emotional pressure     |
| **Locked Profits**       | Partial exits = Guaranteed gains           |
| **Runner Potential**     | Keep portion for extended moves            |
| **Drawdown Control**     | Only full size when market confirms        |

## Scaling In: Building Positions Like Institutions

### Method 1: Confirmation-Based Scaling (Recommended)

```
Entry Structure:
- 1st Entry (50%): At initial POI (OB/FVG) with LTF confirmation
- 2nd Entry (30%): After BOS confirms direction
- 3rd Entry (20%): At next OB/FVG in direction (with trailing stop on earlier entries)

Example:
Account: $10,000, Total Risk: 1% ($100)
- 1st Entry: 0.50% risk at 4H OB → $50 risk
  - If wrong: Only -$50 (0.5%)
- Price confirms with 15M BOS
- 2nd Entry: 0.30% risk at new 15M OB → $30 risk
  - Move 1st entry stop to breakeven
- Price makes new BOS
- 3rd Entry: 0.20% risk at pullback → $20 risk
  - Trail both previous stops

Maximum Risk at any time: $50 (initial)
But Position Size has GROWN with trend
If right: 3× the profit potential vs single entry
```

### Method 2: Time-Based Scaling

```
When to add:
- Entry 1: Kill Zone open (London/NY)
- Entry 2: After 30-60 minutes IF direction confirmed
- Entry 3: At next session (if trend intact)

Rules:
- Never add to LOSING positions
- Only add after CONFIRMATION (BOS, new structure)
- Always move previous stops to breakeven/profit before adding
```

### Method 3: Price-Level Scaling (For Swing Trades)

```
Swing Trade Example (EUR/USD Long):
- Level 1 Entry: 1.0800 (Major OB) → 40% of total position
- Level 2 Entry: 1.0820 (After BOS) → 35% of total position
- Level 3 Entry: 1.0840 (Breakout Confirmed) → 25% of total position

Stops Trail:
- After Level 2: Level 1 stop moves to breakeven
- After Level 3: Level 1 moves to profit, Level 2 to breakeven
- Maximum open risk: Never exceeds 1%
```

### Scaling In Rules (Non-Negotiable)

```
✅ DO:
- Only add to WINNING positions
- Move previous stops to breakeven BEFORE adding
- Calculate total exposure before each add
- Use smaller size on each subsequent add
- Require new confirmation for each add

❌ DON'T:
- Never add to losing positions (averaging down = death)
- Never let total risk exceed 1.5% at any point
- Never add without new structural confirmation
- Never add more than 3 times to one trade
- Never add in the same direction as a losing trade
```

## Scaling Out: Locking Profits Like a Professional

### Method 1: The 40/30/30 Model (Most Popular)

```
Full Position Entered at OB/FVG:

TP1 (1:2 R:R): Take 40% profit
- Lock in guaranteed return
- Move stop to breakeven on remaining 60%
- Worst case now: Breakeven (not a loss!)

TP2 (1:3 R:R): Take 30% profit
- Trail stop to TP1 level on remaining 30%
- Already a winning trade regardless

Runner (30%): Trail with structure
- Move stop below each new HL (bullish) or above each new LH (bearish)
- Target: Next major liquidity pool or 1:5+ R:R
- Let the market tell you when it's done
```

### Method 2: The 50/50 Simple Model

```
Entry: Full size at POI
TP1 (1:2 R:R): Take 50%, move stop to breakeven
Runner (50%): Trail to major target (1:4-1:5+)

Pros: Simple, effective, psychological comfort
Cons: May leave money on table at TP1
Best for: Beginners and intermediate traders
```

### Method 3: The 33/33/34 Equal Model

```
TP1 (1:1.5 R:R): Take 33%
TP2 (1:3 R:R): Take 33%
TP3 (1:5+ R:R): Take 34% (runner)

Pros: Maximizes potential on big moves
Cons: Lower guaranteed profit
Best for: Trending markets with clear targets
```

### Scaling Out Decision Framework

```
When to Take Profits:
- Price reaches key liquidity level (PDH/PDL)
- Volume exhaustion signals (decreasing volume on push)
- Session ending (London close, NY close)
- Opposite CHOCH on entry timeframe
- Pre-news (high impact within 30 min)

When to Let It Run:
- Trend clearly intact (BOS continuing)
- No key levels nearby
- Volume supporting direction
- Session has time remaining
- HTF aligns strongly
```

## Real-World Scaling Examples

### Example 1: EUR/USD Scaling In (Long)

```
Context: 4H Bullish, Liquidity Sweep Completed
Risk Budget: 1% ($100 on $10,000)

Entry 1: Long at 1.0805 (4H OB + FVG)
- Size: 0.06 lots
- Stop: 1.0785 (20 pips = $48 risk)
- Status: Watching for confirmation

15M BOS occurs at 1.0825:
Entry 2: Long at 1.0820 (15M OB after BOS)
- Size: 0.04 lots
- Stop: 1.0808 (12 pips = $32 risk)
- Move Entry 1 stop to 1.0805 (breakeven)
- New max risk: $32 (Entry 2 only)

Price continues, 15M BOS at 1.0850:
Entry 3: Long at 1.0845 (pullback)
- Size: 0.02 lots
- Stop: 1.0830 (15 pips = $20 risk)
- Move Entry 1 stop to 1.0820 (+15 pips locked)
- Move Entry 2 stop to 1.0820 (breakeven)

Total Position: 0.12 lots
Maximum risk: $20 (only Entry 3)
Locked profit: $9 minimum (Entry 1 in profit)
Target: 1.0900 = +$114 total (+95 on E1, +32 on E2, +11 on E3)
```

### Example 2: Gold Scaling Out (Short)

```
Entry: Short $2,440 at Bearish OB (Full 0.10 lots, $7 stop = 1%)

Price drops to $2,425 (1:2 R:R):
- Take 40%: Close 0.04 lots at $2,425 = +$60 profit
- Move stop to $2,440 (breakeven on remaining 0.06)

Price drops to $2,415 (1:3 R:R):
- Take 30%: Close 0.03 lots at $2,415 = +$75 profit
- Trail stop to $2,430 on final 0.03 lots

Price drops to $2,395 (1:6+ R:R):
- Final 30%: Close at $2,395 = +$135 profit
- OR continue trailing if structure supports

Total Profit: $60 + $75 + $135 = $270 (3.9R blended)
Even if runner gets stopped at $2,430: Still $60 + $75 + $30 = $165 (2.4R)
```

## When NOT to Scale

### Use Full Single Entry When:

```
1. High-Confidence A+ Setup (All confluence aligned)
2. Tight Stop Available (Small risk relative to target)
3. News Catalyst Incoming (Need full exposure for spike)
4. HTF Trend Very Strong (BOS continuation)
5. R:R Already Excellent (1:4+ with single entry)
```

### Use Scaling When:

```
1. Moderate Confidence (Not sure market will reach target)
2. Wide Stop Required (Needs to be far from entry)
3. Uncertain Session (Pre-news, low liquidity)
4. Counter-Trend Element (Reversal trade)
5. Large Target Distance (Swing trade, multiple days)
```

## Common Mistakes

<Callout type="warning" title="Avoid These">

**1. Averaging down** — Adding to losers is the #1 account killer with scaling. NEVER add to a losing trade.

**2. Not moving stops** — Before adding new entries, ALWAYS move previous stops to breakeven or profit.

**3. Over-sizing** — Total risk across all entries should never exceed 1.5%.

**4. Taking profits too early** — Many traders close 100% at TP1 and miss the big move. Keep a runner.

**5. Not having a plan** — Decide your scaling levels BEFORE the trade, not during.

**6. Adding without confirmation** — Each new entry needs fresh structural evidence (BOS/CHOCH).

</Callout>

## Key Takeaways

| Concept            | Rule                                 |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------ |
| **Scale In**       | Only to winners, after confirmation  |
| **Scale Out**      | 40/30/30 or 50/50 model              |
| **Max Adds**       | 3 entries maximum per trade          |
| **Stop Rule**      | Move to BE before adding             |
| **Total Risk**     | Never exceed 1.5% across all entries |
| **Runner**         | Always keep 25-30% for big move      |
| **Averaging Down** | NEVER — absolute rule                |
| **Planning**       | Decide levels BEFORE the trade       |
