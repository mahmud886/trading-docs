## What Are Breaker Blocks?

A **Breaker Block** is a failed Order Block that gets violated and then acts as support/resistance from the opposite direction. When an Order Block fails (price closes through it with displacement), it doesn't become worthless — it transforms into a Breaker Block.

<Callout type="info" title="The Transformation">
Order Block FAILS → Becomes Breaker Block → Now works from the OPPOSITE direction. This is one of the most powerful concepts in SMC because it shows you exactly where institutions shifted their positions.
</Callout>

---

## Why Do Breaker Blocks Form?

The logic behind Breaker Blocks is rooted in institutional order flow:

1. **Institutions create an Order Block** — They place initial orders, pushing price in one direction
2. **Price moves as expected** — Other traders join the move
3. **Counter-institutions step in** — Bigger players or opposing flow overwhelms the original OB
4. **The OB gets violated** — Price closes through the OB with displacement
5. **Original orders are now underwater** — The institutions who created the OB are now in losing positions
6. **Price returns to the broken OB** — When price comes back, those underwater traders close at breakeven or small loss
7. **Their closing creates reaction** — This exit order flow causes price to bounce off the Breaker Block

## Identifying Breaker Blocks

### Bullish Breaker Block:

```
Step 1: Bearish OB forms (last green candle before drop)
Step 2: Price drops, then REVERSES and breaks ABOVE the Bearish OB
Step 3: The violated Bearish OB = NOW a Bullish Breaker Block
Step 4: When price returns to this zone, it acts as SUPPORT

    Price breaks above OB ↑
    ─────────────────────
    │  Former Bearish OB  │  ← Now Bullish Breaker (support)
    ─────────────────────
    Price dropped from here initially
```

### Bearish Breaker Block:

```
Step 1: Bullish OB forms (last red candle before rally)
Step 2: Price rallies, then REVERSES and breaks BELOW the Bullish OB
Step 3: The violated Bullish OB = NOW a Bearish Breaker Block
Step 4: When price returns to this zone, it acts as RESISTANCE

    Price initially rallied from here
    ─────────────────────
    │  Former Bullish OB  │  ← Now Bearish Breaker (resistance)
    ─────────────────────
    Price breaks below OB ↓
```

## Breaker Block Validation Criteria

Not every failed OB becomes a valid Breaker Block. Check these criteria:

| Criteria | Required | Why |
|----------|----------|-----|
| **Displacement through OB** | ✅ Yes | Candle body must close through, not just wick |
| **FVG created during break** | ✅ Preferred | Shows institutional commitment to the new direction |
| **Trend alignment** | ✅ Yes | Breaker must align with HTF direction |
| **Premium/Discount** | ✅ Yes | Bullish Breaker in discount, Bearish in premium |
| **First touch** | ✅ Preferred | First retest is strongest |

## What Are Mitigation Blocks?

A **Mitigation Block** is the zone where institutions return to "mitigate" (close out) their losing positions from a previous failed move. It's similar to a Breaker Block but specifically refers to the zone where trapped traders exit.

### Key Difference:
- **Breaker Block** = The failed OB zone itself (structural)
- **Mitigation Block** = The specific area where institutions will close losing trades (functional)

In practice, many traders use these terms interchangeably, but understanding the nuance helps with precision:

```
Original move creates OB at Zone A
↓
Price violates Zone A (OB fails)
↓
Zone A becomes Breaker/Mitigation Block
↓
When price RETURNS to Zone A:
  - Trapped longs EXIT (mitigation) = selling pressure
  - New shorts ENTER (breaker reaction) = more selling
  - Combined effect = strong rejection from the zone
```

## Trading Strategy: Breaker Block Entries

### Step-by-Step Process:

1. **Identify a valid Order Block on HTF (4H/1H)**
2. **Wait for the OB to be VIOLATED** — Price must close through it with a strong candle
3. **Mark the violated OB as a Breaker Block**
4. **Confirm direction** — The break direction is your new bias
5. **Wait for price to retrace** back to the Breaker Block zone
6. **Enter on LTF confirmation** — 15M/5M CHOCH at the Breaker zone
7. **Stop loss** — Beyond the Breaker Block zone (the far side)
8. **Target** — Next liquidity pool in the direction of the break

### Entry Refinement on Lower Timeframe:

When price approaches the Breaker Block:
1. Drop to 15M or 5M chart
2. Wait for a lower-timeframe CHOCH/MSS confirming the Breaker reaction
3. Enter at the LTF OB created during the CHOCH
4. This gives you a much tighter stop and better R:R

## Real Trade Examples

### Trade 1: EUR/USD Bullish Breaker (4H)

**Context:** Daily uptrend, 4H pullback violated a Bearish OB

1. **4H Bearish OB** at 1.0880-1.0895 (last green candle before the drop)
2. Price dropped to 1.0820, then reversed with strong displacement
3. **OB violated** — 4H candle closed above 1.0895 with an FVG
4. **Breaker Block marked** at 1.0880-1.0895 (now bullish support)
5. Price pulled back to 1.0888 (middle of Breaker)
6. **15M bullish CHOCH** at the Breaker zone
7. **Entry:** 1.0885 | **Stop:** 1.0870 (15 pips) | **Target:** 1.0960 (75 pips)
8. **Result:** TP hit → **+75 pips, 5.0R** ✅

### Trade 2: Gold Bearish Breaker (1H)

**Context:** Gold failed to hold above $2,450, previous Bullish OB violated

1. **1H Bullish OB** at $2,435-$2,445 (last red candle before previous rally)
2. Gold rallied to $2,480, then crashed back through $2,435 with displacement
3. **OB violated** — 1H candle closed below $2,435 with massive FVG
4. **Breaker Block marked** at $2,435-$2,445 (now bearish resistance)
5. Price retraced up to $2,440 (into the Breaker zone)
6. **5M bearish CHOCH** with rejection wick
7. **Entry:** $2,441 | **Stop:** $2,448 ($7) | **Target:** $2,400 ($41)
8. **Result:** TP hit → **+$41, 5.9R** ✅

## Mitigation Block Trading

### When to Use Mitigation Blocks:

Mitigation Blocks are particularly useful after:
- **Failed breakouts** — Price breaks a level, traps traders, then reverses
- **Liquidity sweeps** — Price sweeps above/below a level, creating trapped traders
- **Trend reversals** — Major shifts where previous positions become underwater

### Mitigation Block Strategy:

1. Identify where traders got trapped (the failed move's origin)
2. Wait for price to return to that zone
3. Enter in the direction of the NEW trend when price reaches the mitigation zone
4. Those trapped traders exiting add fuel to your trade direction

## Common Mistakes

<Callout type="warning" title="Avoid These Breaker Block Errors">

**❌ Trading every failed OB as a Breaker** — The failure must have displacement and ideally create an FVG. Weak violations don't create valid Breakers.

**❌ Ignoring trend context** — A Bullish Breaker in a Daily downtrend is low probability. Always align with HTF.

**❌ Entering without LTF confirmation** — Don't blindly limit order at the Breaker. Wait for price action confirmation on lower timeframes.

**❌ Trading the 3rd+ touch** — Like Order Blocks, first touch of a Breaker is strongest. Multiple touches weaken it.

**❌ Confusing with regular S/R** — Breaker Blocks have specific criteria (failed OB + displacement). Not every support/resistance flip is a Breaker.

</Callout>

## Breaker vs Order Block: When to Use Which

| Scenario | Use |
|----------|-----|
| Fresh OB, first touch, trend aligned | **Order Block** entry |
| OB has been violated with displacement | **Breaker Block** entry (opposite direction) |
| Previous move trapped traders | **Mitigation Block** entry |
| Multiple confluences at same zone | **Highest probability** — combine all |
