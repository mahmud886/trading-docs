A checklist does not make a trade good; it makes the **process repeatable** and stops you skipping steps when you are excited or tired. Pilots and surgeons use checklists for the same reason. Keep yours short enough to use every time.

<Callout type="warning" title="Corrected and toned down">
  The original called a long checklist the mark of "professional traders" and required fixed confluence counts. The
  items below are a template to adapt; a checklist is only worth what you actually follow, so remove anything you don't
  use.
</Callout>

---

## Before the Session

| Item                    | Done when…                                                        |
| ----------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Daily bias written      | You have a direction (or "no bias") and the level that cancels it |
| Levels marked           | Previous day's high/low, session extremes, key zones              |
| News checked in BD time | Releases noted (US data at 6:30 PM BD in summer)                  |
| Trading window set      | You know which hours you will trade and when you stop             |
| Risk limits set         | Risk per trade, trade cap and daily loss limit written down       |

## The Pre-Entry Gate

| #   | Gate             | Question                                                                                                 | If no            |
| --- | ---------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------- |
| 1   | **Bias**         | Is the trade in the direction of the bias?                                                               | Skip             |
| 2   | **Location**     | Is price at a planned level or zone (and in the right half of the range)?                                | Skip             |
| 3   | **Timing**       | Is it inside my trading window and away from a release?                                                  | Skip             |
| 4   | **Confirmation** | Has a lower-timeframe reaction or shift appeared? (see [Entry Confirmation](/en/smc/entry-confirmation)) | Wait             |
| 5   | **Risk**         | Is the stop at a structural point, with a reward of at least 1.5R?                                       | Skip or adjust   |
| 6   | **Size**         | Is the size computed from the stop for my fixed risk?                                                    | Recompute        |
| 7   | **Limits**       | Am I inside my trade cap and daily loss limit?                                                           | Stop for the day |

One "no" is enough to skip the trade. If you find yourself arguing for an exception, that is the signal to skip.

## During and After the Trade

| Phase  | Do                                                                                                          | Don't                               |
| ------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| During | Follow the management plan written before entry (see [Trade Management](/en/price-action/trade-management)) | Move the stop away, add to a loser  |
| After  | Log: setup, entry, stop, target, result in R, followed the plan? (yes/no), emotion                          | Judge the trade by the result alone |
| Weekly | Review plan-trades against off-plan trades; trim the checklist                                              | Change rules mid-week               |

Keep the log in your [trading journal](/en/blog/trading-journal-template).

## Example (no chart)

London open, EUR/USD. Gates: bias bullish ✓; price in a discount order block **1.0826 – 1.0832** ✓; 1:40 PM BD, no release ✓; a 5-minute close above the last lower high ✓; stop **1.0820** (below the zone and sweep), target **1.0870** with entry **1.0832**: risk 12 pips, reward 38, about **1 : 3.2**; size 100 ÷ (12 × 10) = **0.83 standard lot** at 1% on 10,000; trades today: 1 of 3 ✓. All gates pass: enter. If any had failed, skip.

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake                              | Fix                                         |
| ------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------- |
| A checklist so long that you skip it | Keep seven to ten items                     |
| Making exceptions when excited       | One "no" means skip                         |
| Filling it in after the trade        | Fill it in before clicking                  |
| Never reviewing it                   | Trim items that don't change your decisions |

---

## Practice

1. Write your own checklist with no more than ten items.
2. For ten past trades, tick which gates were met at entry.
3. Compare the R of trades that passed all gates with those that did not.
4. Log it in your [trading journal](/en/tools/journal-template).

## Summary

| Idea    | Detail                                                   |
| ------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| Purpose | Repeatable process, not a trade generator                |
| Gate    | Bias, location, timing, confirmation, risk, size, limits |
| Rule    | One "no" means skip                                      |
| Review  | Log every trade and trim the list monthly                |
