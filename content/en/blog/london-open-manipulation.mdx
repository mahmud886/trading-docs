## Why the London Open Matters

The London open is a high-liquidity session where institutional order flow often creates false moves to hunt stops and collect liquidity. Traders who recognize manipulation can avoid traps and trade the real move.

<Callout type="tip" title="The Judas Swing">
A Judas Swing is a short, sharp move designed to lure retail traders into the wrong side before the true directional move follows. It often occurs around the London open.
</Callout>

---

## Typical London Manipulation Sequence

1. Sweep the Asian range high/low to collect resting liquidity
2. Create a quick reversal (Judas swing) that traps breakout traders
3. Resume the higher-timeframe bias with displacement

Recognizing this sequence helps you plan entries rather than react emotionally.

---

## How to Trade It

1. Mark the Asian range and session highs/lows
2. Wait for the London open reaction — look for sudden wick removals
3. If a Judas Swing occurs, wait for the return to the area and look for HTF confluence (OB/FVG)
4. Enter on lower-timeframe confirmation (CHOCH/MSS) with a clear stop

---

## Example

- Asian range: 1.0840-1.0870
- London spike pushes price to 1.0885 (sweep)
- Quick reversal to 1.0850 (traps breakout buyers)
- Later, price resumes bullish displacement — entry at retrace into FVG

---

## Key Takeaways

- London manipulation is common and exploitable
- Always trade after liquidity has been taken, not during the trap
- Use HTF context plus OB/FVG confluence for safer entries
