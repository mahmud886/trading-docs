## What Is the Initial Balance?

The **Initial Balance (IB)** is the high-to-low range of the **first hour** of a session — in Market Profile, periods A and B. It is the market's first attempt to find a fair price for the day. Traders mark it and watch what price does when it leaves.

<Callout type="warning" title="Toned down">
  The original said institutions "use the IB as a reference" and implied fixed odds for breakouts and failures. We found
  no source for any hit-rate, and the IB is only a **reference range**. A break is information; it isn't a forecast.
  Measure the behaviour on your own market and journal.
</Callout>

| Outcome                | What price does                                                   | What you do                                   |
| ---------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| **A · Break and hold** | Closes above the IB high; the retest holds from above             | Trade with the break on the retest            |
| **B · Failed break**   | Closes above, then closes back inside, then breaks the other side | Trade the failure after the close back inside |
| **C · Stays inside**   | No close outside the IB                                           | No IB trade; treat the day as balanced so far |

The IB here is **10 pips** (1.0856 – 1.0866). One IB width above and below gives extension levels at **1.0876** and **1.0846**. In outcome A price reaches the upper extension; in B it falls toward but doesn't reach the lower one.

## Marking It

1. Choose the session whose first hour you will use, and keep it fixed.
2. Mark the **IB high** and **IB low** (wicks included) after the hour ends.
3. Note the width in pips.
4. Add extension levels at 1× the width above and below if you want reference targets.

---

## Forex Adaptation

Forex has no single opening bell, so you must **choose** the IB session:

| Choice                 | First hour (BD time, US/UK summer)        |
| ---------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| London open            | 1:00 – 2:00 PM                            |
| New York open (forex)  | 6:00 – 7:00 PM                            |
| Futures cash open (6E) | 7:30 – 8:30 PM (exchange 9:30 ET, summer) |

Add one hour in winter for the London and futures rows (see [Best Trading Times in Bangladesh](/en/forex-sessions/best-trading-times-bd)). The IB is a convention you apply consistently, not a market fact.

## Trading the Outcomes

### Break and hold

Close outside the IB, then a retest that holds the IB edge from outside. Stop inside the IB or beyond the retest low; targets at the extension levels.

#### Example (no chart) — outcome A

IB **1.0856 – 1.0866**. A 15-minute candle closes at **1.0868**, the next candle retests **1.0865** and holds. Plan: buy **1.0867**, stop **1.0863** (inside the IB), target **1.0876** (1× extension): risk 4 pips, reward 9, **1 : 2.3** planned. If price closes back below **1.0865**, treat it as a failed break.

### Failed break

A close outside the IB followed by a close back inside means the first break found no follow-through.

#### Example (no chart) — outcome B

After a close at **1.0868**, the next candle closes at **1.0864**, back inside. Plan: sell **1.0863**, stop **1.0871** (above the break high), target **1.0846** (the 1× extension below): risk 8 pips, reward 17, **1 : 2.1** planned. In the chart price falls to 1.0850 and the target is not reached. If price closes above **1.0870**, the failure idea is wrong.

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake                                      | Fix                                                         |
| -------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| Trading the first touch of the IB edge       | Wait for the close                                          |
| Moving the IB after the hour ends            | Mark it once and leave it                                   |
| Changing the IB session day to day           | Fix the session for each pair                               |
| Ignoring IB width                            | Very narrow or very wide IBs behave differently; journal it |
| Treating extension levels as certain targets | They are references; take partial profits and use a stop    |

---

## Practice

1. For 20 sessions, mark the IB on one chart and classify the day: break and hold, failed break, or stays inside.
2. Record the IB width and whether the extension level was reached.
3. For failed-break days, note where the entry and stop would have been.
4. Log it in your [trading journal](/en/tools/journal-template).
