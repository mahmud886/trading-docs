## Two Kinds of Extreme

On a [Market Profile](/en/auction-market/market-profile) chart, the day's high and low can look very different:

| Extreme             | Shape on the chart                                         | The idea                                             |
| ------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| **Excess**          | A thin **tail** of single prints (one letter per row)      | Price pushed out, found no interest and left quickly |
| **Poor high / low** | A **flat** top or bottom: several letters stacked, no tail | Price stopped there without a clear rejection        |

In auction terms, an excess looks like a **finished** auction at that end, and a poor extreme an **unfinished** one.

<Callout type="warning" title="Corrected and toned down">
  The original said poor extremes "are revisited" and described trading them with "institutional logic". Some poor
  extremes are retested and many aren't, and we found no source for a reliable rate. A profile shape is a **clue**, not
  a promise, and says nothing about who traded.
</Callout>

## How to Recognise Them

| Check                                  | Excess                             | Poor extreme            |
| -------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- | ----------------------- |
| Number of single prints at the extreme | Three or more (a common guideline) | None, or one            |
| Letters at the extreme row             | One                                | Several (stacked)       |
| How price left                         | Quickly, in one or two periods     | Gradually, over several |
| Volume at the extreme (if you have it) | Often thin                         | Often heavier           |

"Three or more" is a rule of thumb, not a standard; other practitioners use two. State your rule and keep it.

## Using Them

### The excess

An excess tail can act as a reference for where price was rejected. Traders sometimes fade a return to the tail or use the tail's far end as a stop level.

#### Example (no chart) — fading a return to the excess

The next session rallies back to **1.0866**, just under the tail. Plan: sell **1.0866**, stop **1.0873** (above the tail's high), target **1.0856** (the value-area middle): risk 7 pips, reward 10, **1 : 1.4** planned. If price closes above **1.0872**, the rejection has failed and the plan is off.

### The poor low

A poor extreme is sometimes treated as a level price may come back to test, because the auction there looks unfinished. That is a hypothesis to test, not a rule.

#### What a plan can look like

Mark the poor low (**1.0850**) and wait to see how price behaves if it returns: a quick reversal with single prints would make it look more like an excess; a clean close below with follow-through suggests the low is being accepted lower. In both cases the stop belongs beyond the extreme.

## Where the Ideas Fail

| Failure                                | What it looks like                                |
| -------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| An excess that gets broken             | Price returns and closes beyond the tail          |
| A poor extreme that is never revisited | Price leaves and doesn't come back for days       |
| Trend days                             | Extremes keep extending; "excess" is just a pause |
| News                                   | A release can create a tail or erase one          |

---

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake                                     | Fix                                                     |
| ------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| Calling any wick an "excess"                | Count single prints on a TPO chart                      |
| Assuming a poor extreme will be revisited   | It is a hypothesis; use a stop and journal the outcomes |
| Treating a tail as a wall                   | It's a reference; plan for it to break                  |
| Changing the single-print rule between days | Fix one rule and apply it consistently                  |
| Ignoring the trend                          | In a strong trend, "excess" highs get exceeded          |

---

## Practice

1. On ten TPO days, mark every excess (your rule) and every poor extreme.
2. For each, record what price did over the next one to three days: returned, broke, or ignored.
3. Compare the tallies for excess and poor extremes. Don't assume they match your expectations.
4. Log it in your [trading journal](/en/tools/journal-template).

## Summary

| Idea         | Detail                                                      |
| ------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| Excess       | A tail of single prints: a quick rejection                  |
| Poor extreme | A flat top or bottom with stacked letters and no tail       |
| Use          | Clues about where price was or wasn't rejected, with a stop |
| Limits       | Neither is a promise; trends and news break both            |
