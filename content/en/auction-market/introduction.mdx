## What Is Auction Market Theory?

**Auction Market Theory (AMT)** describes a market as a continuous auction. Price moves to find the levels where trade happens: up if buyers need more sellers, down if sellers need more buyers. Where both sides trade easily, price **stays** and volume builds. Where they don't, price **moves on quickly**.

<Callout type="warning" title="Toned down">
  The original said AMT shows "who is driving" the market and described a "complete institutional framework" together
  with ICT/SMC. AMT is a **vocabulary for describing** what price and volume did. It doesn't identify participants, and
  it doesn't predict. Treat its levels as references, as in [Volume Profile](/en/order-flow/volume-profile).
</Callout>

## A Simple Workflow

| Step | Question                                | Reading                                                              |
| ---- | --------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1    | Is price balanced, moving, or settling? | Rotation in a range / a run with little overlap / a new range        |
| 2    | Where did it accept price?              | High-volume areas, previous POC, value-area edges                    |
| 3    | Where did it move through quickly?      | Thin zones and single prints: price may move quickly there again     |
| 4    | What does your plan say?                | Fade the edges in balance, follow a break that holds, or stand aside |

## Reference Levels AMT Gives You

- Previous session's POC, VAH and VAL ([Value Area](/en/auction-market/value-area)).
- The opening range, as the [Initial Balance](/en/auction-market/initial-balance).
- Thin areas and [single prints](/en/auction-market/single-prints).
- Failed extremes: [excess and poor highs/lows](/en/auction-market/excess-poor-highs-lows).

### Example (no chart) — a test of the new balance

After the drive, price holds the new area's low at **1.0874** twice. Plan: buy a hold at **1.0875**, stop **1.0871** (below the area), target **1.0882** (the top of the area): risk 4 pips, reward 7, **1 : 1.8** planned. If price closes below **1.0873** and stays there, the new balance has failed and the thin zone below is open.

## Comparing With ICT/SMC Vocabulary

| AMT term       | Rough ICT/SMC analogue |
| -------------- | ---------------------- |
| Value-area top | Premium side           |
| Value-area low | Discount side          |
| POC            | Equilibrium            |
| Single prints  | Imbalance / FVG        |

These are loose analogues for orientation, not equivalences: the two systems define their levels differently and use different data (volume versus candle structure).

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake                                         | Fix                                                 |
| ----------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| Treating the POC or value-area edges as walls   | They are references; plan for them to fail          |
| Calling every range "balance" and fading it     | Wait for evidence that price is accepted there      |
| Using tick volume as if it were exchange volume | Prefer futures or real-volume data for profiles     |
| Assuming a drive must continue                  | A drive can end in a new balance, or reverse        |
| Mixing profile periods                          | State whether you mean session, weekly or composite |

## Practice

1. On a futures chart, mark yesterday's balance areas and the move between them.
2. Label today's first two hours: balance, imbalance or settling?
3. Mark one thin zone and note how price behaved when it crossed it.
4. Log it in your [trading journal](/en/tools/journal-template).

## Summary

| Idea        | Detail                                                             |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| AMT         | A way to describe price as a search for levels where trade happens |
| Balance     | Rotation and volume build-up                                       |
| Imbalance   | A fast, one-directional move with little volume per price          |
| New balance | Price settles in a new area                                        |
| Use         | Orientation and reference levels, not prediction                   |
