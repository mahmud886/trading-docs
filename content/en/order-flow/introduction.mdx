## What Is Order Flow?

**Order flow** is the study of the orders actually executing in the market — **who** was aggressive, **at what price**, and **how much**. A candle shows only the open, high, low and close; order flow shows the trades inside it.

<Callout type="warning" title="Toned down">
  The original said order flow reveals "institutional activity invisible on regular charts" and is "the foundation of
  professional trading". It shows executed volume and, on some platforms, resting orders — not who is trading or why.
  Treat it as extra information about **how** price moved, not as a view into anyone's intentions.
</Callout>

---

## How a Trade Happens

Every trade has two sides:

| Side                  | Order type              | Role                                         |
| --------------------- | ----------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| **Aggressive buyer**  | Market buy              | Buys at the **ask**, taking liquidity        |
| **Passive seller**    | Limit sell (at the ask) | Waits in the order book, providing liquidity |
| **Aggressive seller** | Market sell             | Sells at the **bid**, taking liquidity       |
| **Passive buyer**     | Limit buy (at the bid)  | Waits in the order book, providing liquidity |

A trade is classified as a **buy** when it happens at the ask and a **sell** when it happens at the bid. Passive participants can be large or small; large participants often use limit orders to avoid moving price, but nothing in the data says who is who.

## What Data Do You Need?

| Market                    | Order-flow data                                                                                                                                 |
| ------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Futures (e.g. 6E, ES, GC) | Centralised exchange data: real volume, trade-by-trade bid/ask, a single order book                                                             |
| Stocks                    | Exchange data with real volume; fragmented across venues                                                                                        |
| **Spot forex**            | No central exchange: "volume" from a broker is **tick volume** or that broker's own flow; order-book tools show only that broker/liquidity pool |

If you trade spot forex, many order-flow tools are best used on the **currency futures** (for example 6E for EUR/USD) as a proxy — and even then they reflect one venue.

## How to Start

1. Learn the vocabulary: bid, ask, aggressive, passive, delta, POC.
2. Start with the [volume profile](/en/order-flow/volume-profile) and [VWAP](/en/order-flow/vwap) — the simplest tools.
3. Add [delta](/en/order-flow/delta-analysis) once you can read volume at price.
4. Use footprint charts and the DOM last; they take the most screen time.
5. Always combine with context: structure, levels and your plan. Order flow **confirms**; it doesn't replace analysis.

### Example (no chart) — using order flow as confirmation

Price pulls back to a daily support at **1.0850** on 6E. At the level, a 5-minute candle shows heavy selling at the bid (**1,800** contracts) but the low holds, and the next candle's delta is **+620** with a close above **1.0854**. Plan: buy **1.0855**, stop **1.0846** (below the support), target **1.0888**: risk 9 pips, reward 33, **1 : 3.7** planned. The order-flow evidence (selling absorbed, then buying) only adds to a level you had already marked.

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake                                         | Fix                                                    |
| ----------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| Using tick volume as if it were exchange volume | Know what your data is                                 |
| Trading order flow without context              | Mark levels and structure first                        |
| Reading too many tools at once                  | One or two tools for a month before adding more        |
| Over-trusting one candle's delta                | Look at several candles and the level                  |
| Assuming you can see "institutions"             | You see executed volume and (sometimes) resting orders |

## Practice

1. On a 5-minute futures chart (or a replay), pick one candle and write its volume by price, POC and delta.
2. Compare the candle's colour with its delta: do they agree?
3. Note the level the candle formed at and what the next candle did.
4. Log it in your [trading journal](/en/tools/journal-template).

## Summary

| Idea       | Detail                                                                       |
| ---------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Order flow | The executed trades inside the candles — who was aggressive, where, how much |
| Delta      | Volume bought at the ask minus volume sold at the bid                        |
| POC        | The price with the most volume                                               |
| Limits     | Needs real, classified data; weaker for spot forex                           |
