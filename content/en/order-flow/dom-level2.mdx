## What Is the DOM?

The **depth of market (DOM)** — also called **Level 2**, the **order book** or the **ladder** — lists the resting **limit orders** at each price: bids (buy limits) below the market and asks (sell limits) above it.

| Level       | What it shows                                 |
| ----------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| **Level 1** | Best bid, best ask, last price                |
| **Level 2** | Resting orders at several prices on each side |

The DOM shows what's **resting**; the **tape** (time and sales) shows what's **executed**. You need both: a resting order isn't a trade, and it can be removed at any time.

<Callout type="warning" title="Toned down">
  The original called the DOM "the closest you can get to seeing institutional intent". A resting order shows what
  someone is **willing** to do right now, not what they will do, and it can be cancelled in milliseconds. Large
  participants also often hide size. Read the DOM as a *snapshot of offers*, not as intent.
</Callout>

The lesson isn't "walls are resistance". It's that **a wall is a hypothesis**: it matters only if it holds when it's traded into. When it's consumed, the book behind it decides how fast price moves.

## What to Watch

| Observation                           | Possible reading                                                  | Caution                                                     |
| ------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Thick level**                       | Possible support or resistance                                    | It can disappear or be hidden                               |
| **Thin levels**                       | Price can move through quickly                                    | Thin books also gap on news                                 |
| **Orders refilling**                  | Someone is reloading at the same price                            | See [Iceberg and Spoofing](/en/order-flow/iceberg-spoofing) |
| **Orders vanishing before being hit** | They may have been cancelled for many reasons, including spoofing | Don't assume manipulation                                   |
| **Bid/ask imbalance**                 | One side is heavier right now                                     | Imbalance changes constantly; check the tape                |

### Absorption in the DOM

An **absorbing** level takes aggressive volume without price moving through it: the tape shows many contracts executing at one price while the displayed size stays about the same or refills. In the chart, 320 contracts hit the wall and price stalled — until the rest of the wall went.

### Example (no chart) — using the DOM as one input

Price rallies to **1.0954** and meets an ask wall at **1.0955**. Instead of selling "because there's a wall", the plan: _if_ the wall is consumed (size falls below 100 with buyers still lifting) and price trades at **1.0956**, buy **1.0957**, stop **1.0951** (below the last bid cluster), target **1.0975**: risk 6, reward 18, **1 : 3.0** planned. If instead the wall holds and the tape shows buyers drying up, there's no trade.

## DOM Across Markets

| Market            | What the DOM shows                                                                        |
| ----------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Futures (e.g. 6E) | One exchange's central limit order book — the full resting depth                          |
| Stocks            | One venue's book; several others trade the same stock                                     |
| **Spot forex**    | No central book: a broker's DOM shows only its own liquidity providers or its own clients |

Spot-forex traders who want order-book information often use the **futures** book as a proxy.

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake                                                | Fix                                                  |
| ------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| Trading a wall as if it were a guarantee               | Wait to see how it behaves when traded into          |
| Ignoring the tape                                      | Watch executed volume next to the book               |
| Calling every disappearing order "spoofing"            | Cancellations are normal; look for repeated patterns |
| Using a broker's "DOM" for spot forex like a real book | Know what liquidity it represents                    |
| Scalping the DOM without a stop                        | Size and stop first; speed second                    |

## Practice

1. On a futures DOM (or a replay), watch one large resting order for 10 minutes. Does it hold, refill, vanish or get consumed?
2. Write the tape volume traded at that price during that time.
3. Note what price did after the wall changed.
4. Log it in your [trading journal](/en/tools/journal-template).
