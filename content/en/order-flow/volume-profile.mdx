## What Is a Volume Profile?

A **volume profile** turns volume sideways: instead of volume per **time** bar, it shows the volume that traded at each **price** over a period. It answers _where was trading accepted, and where did price move through quickly?_

<Callout type="warning" title="Toned down">
  The original said institutions "don't care about time-based volume" and that the profile "shows exactly" where they
  filled orders. A profile shows **where volume traded**, not who traded it. And the point of control doesn't "act as a
  magnet" by rule — price often returns to it, and often doesn't.
</Callout>

<Callout type="info" title="About this chart">
  The session is **synthetic**. The profile is built by spreading each candle's volume evenly across the ticks it
  covers; real profiles use trade-by-trade volume at price.
</Callout>

| Component                | Definition                                                   | In the chart                      |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------- |
| **POC** point of control | The price with the most volume                               | 1.0874                            |
| **Value area (VA)**      | The contiguous area around the POC holding 70% of the volume | 1.0858 – 1.0879                   |
| **VAH / VAL**            | The top and bottom of the value area                         | 1.0879 / 1.0858                   |
| **HVN** high-volume node | A cluster of volume: price spent time and traded there       | 1.0858 and 1.0874                 |
| **LVN** low-volume node  | A thin area: price moved through quickly                     | 1.0867 (≈ 5% of the POC's volume) |

The 70% value area is built **outward from the POC**, adding whichever neighbouring price has more volume. In a session with two nodes like this one, the area spans both — and the LVN sits in the middle of it. That's why the LVN and HVNs matter alongside the value area.

## What the Shapes Suggest

| Profile shape                            | Often describes                       | Common idea                                         |
| ---------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| **One bulge (D-shape)**                  | A balanced session                    | Price rotates around the POC                        |
| **Two bulges (b / double distribution)** | A move to a new area, like this chart | The LVN is a "fast" area between two accepted areas |
| **Thin tail at one end**                 | A rejection of those prices           | Price moved there and left                          |

These are descriptions of what happened, and shapes only suggest what may happen next.

## Using Profile Levels

| Level         | Typical use                                                            | Caution                                       |
| ------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| **POC**       | A fair-value reference; targets for mean reversion                     | Price can trend away from the POC all day     |
| **VAH / VAL** | Edges of accepted value; entries on tests and holds, or breakouts      | Test them with the tape; don't treat as walls |
| **HVN**       | Price tends to slow, rotate or stall around it                         | A strong move can run through an HVN          |
| **LVN**       | Price tends to move through quickly; useful as a stop/target reference | A fast move can reverse in an LVN too         |

### Example (no chart) — an LVN as a target and a stop reference

In the chart, price has rotated around the upper HVN near **1.0874** and falls back to **1.0872**. Plan: buy a hold of the POC at **1.0873**, stop **1.0866** (just below the LVN, since losing the area means price may drop through the thin zone), target **1.0885** (above the session high): risk 7, reward 12, **1 : 1.7** planned. If price closes below **1.0867**, the upper area has been rejected and the thin zone below is open.

## Which Profile?

| Profile                | Period                | Use                                 |
| ---------------------- | --------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| **Session profile**    | One trading day       | Today's POC, VA and nodes           |
| **Composite / weekly** | Several days or weeks | Longer-term value and nodes         |
| **Fixed range**        | From a chosen swing   | Where trading accepted after a move |

Previous-day POC, VAH and VAL are popular references the next day. Which profile to trust is something to test on your own market.

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake                                         | Fix                                                        |
| ----------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| Treating the POC as a magnet                    | It's a reference; plan for it not to hold                  |
| Using tick volume as if it were exchange volume | Prefer futures or real-volume markets                      |
| Ignoring the period                             | The same price has different profiles on different periods |
| Trading every node                              | Combine with structure, session and a stop                 |
| Forgetting the value area is contiguous         | Two-node sessions have a thin zone inside the VA           |

## Practice

1. Build a profile for yesterday's session on a futures chart. Mark POC, VAH, VAL and the main nodes.
2. Note where today's price opened relative to the value area and what it did at VAH/VAL.
3. Mark one LVN and see how price behaved when it crossed it.
4. Log it in your [trading journal](/en/tools/journal-template).
