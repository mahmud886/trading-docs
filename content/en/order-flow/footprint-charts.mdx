## What Is a Footprint Chart?

A **footprint chart** replaces each candle's body with a grid of numbers: at **each price** inside the candle, the volume **sold at the bid** (left) and **bought at the ask** (right). It's the single-candle view from the [introduction](/en/order-flow/introduction), repeated across the chart.

From one footprint you can read the candle's **delta**, its **point of control** (the price with the most volume) and **imbalances** between neighbouring prices.

<Callout type="warning" title="Toned down">
  The original treated imbalances and stacked cells as institutional footprints ("institutions buying/selling
  aggressively"). Footprints show **executed aggressive volume by price**, nothing about who is trading. They can also
  look very different on different data feeds and tick sizes. Use them as detail about how a candle formed.
</Callout>

## How to Read a Cell Grid

| Element                | How to read it                                                         |
| ---------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Bid × ask**          | Sold at the bid × bought at the ask, at that price                     |
| **Delta**              | Σ ask − Σ bid across the candle                                        |
| **POC**                | The price with the largest total — a candle-level "fair price"         |
| **Buy imbalance**      | Ask at price _p_ ≥ 3 × bid at price _p − 1 tick_ (diagonal comparison) |
| **Sell imbalance**     | Bid at price _p_ ≥ 3 × ask at price _p + 1 tick_                       |
| **Stacked imbalances** | Three or more imbalances at adjacent prices in the same direction      |

The diagonal comparison matters: aggressive buyers at price _p_ are matched against sellers resting at _p_, so the comparison is made against the neighbouring level to see whether the next level was also lifted.

## What Footprints Are Used For

| Pattern                                       | Possible reading                                                                                 | Caution                             |
| --------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------- |
| **Stacked buy imbalances**                    | Aggressive buying through many prices; some traders use the zone as support on a retest          | They don't guarantee a hold         |
| **Stacked sell imbalances**                   | Aggressive selling through many prices; zone as resistance on a retest                           | Same                                |
| **Heavy volume at an extreme, price rejects** | Absorption or exhaustion (see [Absorption and Exhaustion](/en/order-flow/absorption-exhaustion)) | Needs the next candles to confirm   |
| **POC at the extreme of a candle**            | One-sided candle: most volume traded at the end                                                  | Compare with delta and the next bar |
| **Thin prices inside a candle**               | Price moved through quickly                                                                      | Can be a gap in data too            |

### Example (no chart) — retest of a stacked-imbalance zone

After candle **B** (stacked buy imbalances from 1.0854 to 1.0859), price pulls back into the zone at **1.0855** and shows **sell-side volume drying up** (bid volume under 100 at each price). Plan: buy **1.0856**, stop **1.0851** (below the imbalance zone's start), target **1.0869** (one-and-a-half times the candle's height above its high): risk 5, reward 13, **1 : 2.6** planned. If price trades back through **1.0853** on sell-side volume, the zone has failed and the idea is off.

## Limits

| Limit                             | Why it matters                                                             |
| --------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Data quality**                  | Needs trade-by-trade data with correct bid/ask tagging                     |
| **Tick size and imbalance ratio** | The same candle shows different imbalances with 2:1, 3:1 or 4:1 thresholds |
| **One venue**                     | A broker's footprint in spot forex covers only its own flow                |
| **Screen clutter**                | Too many candles of numbers slows decisions                                |
| **No context**                    | A stacked imbalance mid-range means little without a level                 |

---

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake                                         | Fix                                                      |
| ----------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| Treating every imbalance as a signal            | Only those at levels with a plan                         |
| Changing the imbalance ratio to fit an idea     | Pick a ratio and keep it                                 |
| Trusting tick-volume "footprints" in spot forex | Use futures or real-volume data                          |
| Ignoring what the next candle did               | Footprints describe; the follow-through confirms         |
| No stop                                         | Put it beyond the imbalance zone or the absorbed extreme |

## Practice

1. On a futures footprint (or a replay), find three candles with stacked imbalances and mark the zone.
2. Note whether price came back to the zone and what the footprint showed on the retest.
3. Find one candle with its largest cell at the extreme and record what the next candles did.
4. Log it in your [trading journal](/en/tools/journal-template).

| --------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| Footprint | Bid × ask volume at every price in each candle            |
| Reads     | Delta, POC, imbalances, stacked imbalances                |
