## What Is Accumulation?

**Accumulation** is a range at the end of a decline in which selling is gradually absorbed and demand takes over. Wyckoff described it with a sequence of **events** grouped into five **phases (A – E)**. The sequence is a **template**, not a promise — real ranges skip events, reorder them, or fail.

For the SMC version of the same idea see [SMC Accumulation](/en/smc/accumulation).

| Phase | Event                         | What happens                                             | Volume (idealised)                |
| ----- | ----------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| **A** | **PS** preliminary support    | First sign of buying; the decline continues              | Volume rises                      |
|       | **SC** selling climax         | Wide-spread low, price closes off the low (19.6)         | The highest volume of the decline |
|       | **AR** automatic rally        | Sharp rally; sets the range top (26.0)                   | High                              |
|       | **ST** secondary test         | Retest of the low (20.0), higher than the SC             | Less than the SC                  |
| **B** | Building the range            | Up-and-down swings between 20 and 26                     | Declining on tests of the low     |
| **C** | **Spring**                    | Dip below support (18.9) that closes back inside         | Moderate or low                   |
|       | **Test**                      | Retest of the spring area (20.3), a higher low           | Very low                          |
| **D** | **SOS** sign of strength      | Close above resistance (26.9)                            | Rising, the heaviest since the SC |
|       | **LPS** last point of support | Quiet pullback holding near the broken resistance (24.9) | Low                               |
| **E** | Markup                        | Price leaves the range                                   | Rises with rallies                |

### The planned trade

Buy the **LPS** at **26.2**, stop **24.4** (below the LPS low), target **32.4** — the range width (26.0 − 19.6 = 6.4) projected from the breakout level. Risk **1.8**, reward **6.2**, **1 : 3.4** planned.

## Schematic #2 — Without a Spring

Some accumulations never dip below support. Instead the last test of the range makes a **higher low** (an LPS above the SC low), and price breaks out from there.

| Feature | Schematic #1                      | Schematic #2                                  |
| ------- | --------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| Phase C | Spring below support, then a Test | A higher low at/above the middle of the range |
| Entry   | Test, or LPS after the SOS        | The higher low, or the pullback after the SOS |
| Stop    | Below the spring                  | Below the higher low                          |
| Risk    | Often smaller (the stop is near)  | Similar                                       |

See [Phases and Events](/en/wyckoff/phases-events) for how to identify the phase when the events are unclear.

<Callout type="warning" title="Toned down">
  The original taught the spring as "smart money shakes out weak holders". A spring is a **pattern**: price dips below
  support, then reverses on low supply. It is a useful clue, not proof of who is selling or buying. A dip below support
  on heavy volume that doesn't recover is a **failure**, not a spring.
</Callout>

## Trading It

1. **Identify the prior decline** and the first climax (SC, with the highest volume).
2. **Mark the range:** support (SC low) and resistance (AR high).
3. **Look for the test** of the low on less volume (ST, then Spring/Test).
4. **Enter** on the Test (aggressive) or the LPS after an SOS (more confirmed).
5. **Stop** beyond the invalidating point: below the spring/test, or below the LPS.
6. **Target:** the range width projected up, with partial targets near resistance levels.

### Example (no chart) — the no-spring variant

A stock falls from 48 to 30, makes a climax at **29.6** with 3.5× volume, then a range between **29.6 and 34.0**. The last swing low holds at **31.2** on light volume, then price closes above **34.0** on rising volume. Buy the pullback at **34.4**, stop **32.8** (below the pullback low), target **38.4** (range width 4.4 projected): risk 1.6, reward 4.0, **1 : 2.5** planned. If price closes below **31.0** on heavy volume the accumulation story is dropped.

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake                                       | Fix                                                                             |
| --------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Calling any range after a fall "accumulation" | Need a climax, a test on less volume and no new lows                            |
| Buying a break of support as a "spring"       | A spring closes back inside; a heavy-volume break that stays below is a failure |
| Labelling events to fit a schematic           | Identify the phase first; events are clues                                      |
| Entering without a stop                       | The stop goes beyond the invalidating event                                     |
| Expecting the target to be hit                | It is a plan; scale out near levels                                             |

## Practice

1. On three daily charts, mark the climax, the range top and bottom, and any test of the low.
2. For each, note the volume at the climax, the test and the breakout.
3. Decide whether it looks like schematic #1, schematic #2 or neither, and why.
4. Log it in your [trading journal](/en/tools/journal-template).

## Summary

| Idea         | Detail                                                                                |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Phases       | A stop the fall · B build the range · C test (spring) · D sign of strength · E markup |
| Key evidence | Less volume on tests; heavy volume on the breakout                                    |
| Entry        | Test or LPS; stop beyond it; target from the range width                              |
| Failure      | A heavy-volume break below support that doesn't recover                               |
