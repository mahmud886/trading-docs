## What Is Trade Management?

**Trade management** is everything between entry and exit: moving the stop, taking partial profit, trailing, and deciding what to do when something changes. The same entry can produce very different results depending on how it's managed.

---

## The Four Phases

| Phase              | What to do                                                                                |
| ------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **1. Initial**     | Hands off. Don't touch the stop for the first candle or two                               |
| **2. Protect**     | Optional: move the stop to break-even once the trade is about +1R and structure allows it |
| **3. Lock profit** | Take part of the position; trail the stop behind new swings                               |
| **4. Exit**        | Target reached, trailed stop hit, or the setup is invalidated                             |

Long **1.0850**, stop **1.0830** (1R = 20 pips), targets at **1R 1.0870**, **2R 1.0890**, **3R 1.0910**. Price climbs to about **1.8R**, pulls back to **1.0849**, then runs to **3R**.

| Style                         | What happens                                                               | Result    |
| ----------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------- |
| **A · Hold to 3R**            | Rides the pullback; reaches the target                                     | **+3R**   |
| **B · Break-even at 1R**      | Stop moved to 1.0850; the pullback hits it                                 | **0R**    |
| **C · Partials 50 / 30 / 20** | 50% closed at 1R (+0.5R), stop to break-even; the rest is stopped at entry | **+0.5R** |

On a path that reverses from 1.8R to the original stop, the order flips: A loses 1R, B scratches at 0R, and C keeps +0.5R. **No management style wins on every path**; each trades upside for protection.

<Callout type="info" title="Clarified: 'risk-free' and 'guaranteed'">
  The original called a break-even trade "risk-free" and said partials "guarantee some profit". A break-even stop can
  still lose on slippage or a gap, and a partial only locks in what was actually closed. Both reduce risk; neither
  removes it.
</Callout>

## Partial Profits

The original's plan for a 1.0-lot position:

| At  | Close         | Stop          |
| --- | ------------- | ------------- |
| 1R  | 0.5 lot (50%) | To break-even |
| 2R  | 0.3 lot (30%) | Trail         |
| 3R  | 0.2 lot (20%) | —             |

If every level is hit, this plan makes **1.7R** (0.5 + 0.6 + 0.6) against **3R** for holding the whole position. You give up part of the best case in exchange for a smoother equity curve. Test both on your own trades.

## When to Move the Stop

| Move it when…                                    | Move it to…                     |
| ------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------- |
| The trade reaches about +1R and structure allows | Entry (break-even)              |
| A new swing forms in your direction              | Just beyond that swing          |
| Price makes a new BOS                            | Beyond the swing behind the BOS |
| Price nears a major opposing level               | Tighter, to lock more profit    |

### Never move it

- In the first candle or two.
- Because of fear or hope.
- **Away** from price (widening the stop).
- Against your written plan.

## Scenarios

| Scenario                               | Action                                  |
| -------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| Price goes straight to target          | Take the profit                         |
| Price chops around the entry           | Give it time, within your time limit    |
| Price goes against you                 | Let the stop do its job; don't widen it |
| Price reaches 1R, then stalls          | Take a partial; trail the rest          |
| High-impact news is coming             | Reduce, tighten or close before it      |
| The higher timeframe turns against you | Close or reduce                         |

### Example (no chart) — GBP/USD with a time stop

Long **1.2650**, stop **1.2630**, target **1.2710** (**R:R 1 : 3.0**, planned). Rule: if price hasn't reached +1R within six 1H candles, close at market. After six candles price is at 1.2656; the trade is closed for a small gain and the capital is free for the next setup.

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake                        | Fix                                          |
| ------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------- |
| Moving to break-even too early | Wait for +1R and a new swing                 |
| Widening the stop              | Never; close instead if the idea has changed |
| Managing by feel               | Write the management rules before entry      |
| Changing style trade to trade  | Pick one plan and test it over many trades   |

## Practice

1. Take your last 20 trades and re-run each with styles A, B and C.
2. Total the R for each style.
3. Pick the style that suits your results and your nerves, and write it down.
4. Log it in your [trading journal](/en/tools/journal-template).

## Summary

| Aspect         | Key point                                         |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| **Phases**     | Initial → protect → lock profit → exit            |
| **Break-even** | Protects, but gets stopped by normal pullbacks    |
| **Partials**   | Smoother results; smaller best case               |
| **Stops**      | Move only toward profit, after structure confirms |
| **Rule**       | Decide management before entering                 |
