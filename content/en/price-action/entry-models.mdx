## What Is an Entry Model?

An **entry model** is the rule for **when and where** you enter once a setup is valid. The setup says "short at resistance"; the entry model says "with a limit order in the zone", "after a bearish engulfing closes", or "after structure breaks and retests".

---

## Three Core Models

| Model            | How                                                           | Trade-off                              |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| **Risk entry**   | A limit order in the zone; no confirmation                    | Best price; no proof the zone holds    |
| **Confirmation** | Wait for a rejection or engulfing candle to close at the zone | Some proof; worse price                |
| **Break-retest** | Wait for a lower-timeframe CHOCH, then enter on the retest    | Most proof; worst price; may be missed |

<Callout type="warning" title="Removed: win rates per model">
  The original gave each model a win rate ("~45–55%", "~55–65%", "~60–75%") and R:R ranges. Those numbers have no source
  and depend entirely on the trader, market and rules. What *can* be calculated is the win rate each model **needs**,
  shown below.
</Callout>

All three use the same stop (**2,428**, above resistance) and target (**2,388**):

| Model                                                | Entry | Risk | Reward | **R:R**     | Break-even win rate |
| ---------------------------------------------------- | ----- | ---- | ------ | ----------- | ------------------- |
| **A · Risk entry**                                   | 2,420 | $8   | $32    | **1 : 4.0** | **20%**             |
| **B · Confirmation** (engulfing close)               | 2,414 | $14  | $26    | **1 : 1.9** | **35%**             |
| **C · Break-retest** (after the CHOCH below 2,409.5) | 2,412 | $16  | $24    | **1 : 1.5** | **40%**             |

**Break-even win rate = 1 ÷ (1 + R:R).** A risk entry at 1 : 4 is profitable if more than 1 in 5 trades work; a break-retest entry at 1 : 1.5 needs 2 in 5. The question isn't which model is "best", but **which one you can win often enough with**.

Many traders using model C tighten the stop to just above the retest's swing instead of above the zone; that improves its R:R (see [Advanced Execution Models](/en/smc/advanced-execution-models)).

## Choosing a Model

| Situation                                    | Model                        | Why                           |
| -------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| Strong higher-timeframe zone, with the trend | Risk entry                   | The context carries the trade |
| Ordinary setup                               | Confirmation                 | Balance of price and proof    |
| Counter-trend                                | Break-retest                 | Needs the most proof          |
| Fast markets / scalping                      | Risk or confirmation         | The retest may never come     |
| Swing trading                                | Confirmation or break-retest | Time allows patience          |

### Example (no chart) — EUR/USD confirmation entry

EUR/USD pulls back to daily support at **1.0850**. A 4H bullish engulfing closes at **1.0862**. Buy **1.0862**, stop **1.0838** below the zone, target **1.0930**: risk 24 pips, reward 68 pips, **R:R 1 : 2.8** (planned), break-even win rate **26%**.

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake                          | Fix                                    |
| -------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| Switching models mid-setup       | Choose before price reaches the zone   |
| Judging a model by its R:R alone | Compare R:R with the win rate it needs |
| Risk entries against the trend   | Use them only with strong context      |
| Chasing a missed break-retest    | No retest, no trade                    |

## Practice

1. For 20 past setups, record where entries A, B and C would have been, with the same stop and target.
2. Note which setups reached the target and which hit the stop for each model.
3. Compute each model's win rate and compare it with its break-even win rate.
4. Log it in your [trading journal](/en/tools/journal-template).
