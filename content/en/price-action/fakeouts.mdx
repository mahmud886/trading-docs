## What is a Fakeout?

A fakeout (false breakout) occurs when price **temporarily breaks** beyond a support/resistance level but **quickly reverses** back inside, trapping traders who entered on the breakout. Fakeouts are one of the most common traps in trading and are often **engineered by institutions** to collect liquidity.

<Callout type="info" title="Fakeout = Opportunity">
Most retail traders fear fakeouts. Smart traders LOVE them. A confirmed fakeout gives you a high-probability reversal entry with a tight stop and large target. Learning to trade fakeouts transforms your biggest frustration into your greatest edge.
</Callout>

---

## Why Fakeouts Happen

### 1. Institutional Liquidity Collection
Banks and hedge funds need large amounts of liquidity to fill orders. Retail traders place stops just beyond S/R levels. Institutions push price past these levels to trigger stops and collect liquidity.

```
Fakeout Anatomy:
                    ╱╲ Fakeout spike
═══ Resistance ════╱══╲════════════
  ╱╲    ╱╲   ╱╲  ╱    ╲
 ╱  ╲  ╱  ╲ ╱  ╲╱      ╲
╱    ╲╱    ╲╱            ╲ ← Reversal after
                          ╲   collecting stops
                           ╲╱

Retail traders: Buy stops above resistance → triggered
Institutions: USE those buy orders as liquidity to SELL
```

### 2. Stop Loss Hunting
When many traders have stops at similar levels, market makers push price to those levels to trigger them, then reverse.

### 3. News-Driven Spikes
High-impact news (NFP, CPI, FOMC) causes volatile spikes that break levels but reverse quickly as the initial panic subsides.

## How to Identify a Fakeout

### Key Characteristics:
1. **Long wick beyond the level** — Price pokes through but closes back inside
2. **Quick reversal** — Within 1-3 candles, price is back inside the range
3. **No follow-through** — The candle after the break doesn't continue
4. **Volume divergence** — The break has less conviction than expected
5. **Context mismatch** — Break is AGAINST the HTF trend

### Fakeout vs. True Breakout Comparison:

| Feature | Fakeout ❌ | True Breakout ✅ |
|---------|-----------|-----------------|
| **Candle close** | Inside the range | Beyond the level |
| **Wick** | Long (rejection) | Short or none |
| **Follow-through** | Reverses immediately | Continues next candle |
| **Momentum** | Weak, indecisive | Strong displacement |
| **HTF alignment** | Against HTF trend | With HTF trend |
| **Retest** | Doesn't retest | Clean retest holds |

## Types of Fakeouts

### 1. Single-Candle Fakeout (Pin Bar / Hammer)
A single candle pokes beyond the level with a long wick, then closes back inside.
- **Most common** type
- High reliability when combined with HTF trend

### 2. Multi-Candle Fakeout (Trap Pattern)
2-3 candles close slightly beyond the level, then price reverses sharply.
- More deceptive — retail traders are convinced the breakout is real
- Often seen around high-impact news releases

### 3. Stop Hunt Fakeout (Liquidity Sweep)
Price spikes sharply beyond the level (often by 10-20 pips), then reverses aggressively. This is the institutional liquidity grab.
- Identified by a **long wick** beyond obvious stop-loss clusters
- Often preceded by equal highs/lows (where stops accumulate)

### 4. Session Fakeout
One trading session breaks a level, but the next session reverses. Common at:
- **Asian session highs/lows** swept by London
- **London session** levels swept by NY

## Fakeout Trading Strategies

### Strategy 1: Pin Bar Reversal at S/R ⭐
1. Identify a strong S/R level with 2+ touches
2. Price approaches and forms a candle with a long wick beyond the level
3. The candle body closes INSIDE the range
4. Enter in the direction of the rejection (opposite to the wick)
5. Stop: Beyond the wick extreme (+ 5-10 pip buffer)
6. Target: Opposite S/R level or midpoint

### Strategy 2: Sweep and Reverse
1. Identify equal highs/lows (liquidity pool)
2. Wait for price to sweep beyond those equal levels
3. Look for an aggressive reversal candle after the sweep
4. Enter on the reversal candle
5. Stop: Beyond the sweep high/low
6. Target: The range opposite side or further

### Strategy 3: Session Sweep Entry (BD Time)
1. Mark the Asian session range (2:00 AM - 10:00 AM BD)
2. During London open (1:00 PM BD), watch for a sweep of Asian high/low
3. If price sweeps and immediately reverses, enter the reversal
4. Stop: Beyond the sweep wick
5. Target: Opposite side of Asian range + expansion

## Real Trading Examples

### Example 1: EUR/USD Resistance Fakeout
- **Level**: 1.0950 resistance (tested 3 times on Daily)
- **Fakeout**: Price spikes to 1.0962, long upper wick, closes at 1.0940
- **Signal**: Bearish pin bar with wick above resistance
- **Entry**: Sell at 1.0938 | **Stop**: 1.0968 | **Target**: 1.0870
- **R:R**: 2.3:1 | **Result**: -68 pips, +2.3R ✅

- **Sweep**: Price spikes to $2,412 (clears all equal highs)

- **Entry**: Sell at $2,390 | **Stop**: $2,415 | **Target**: $2,350
- **R:R**: 1.6:1 | **Result**: +$40, TP hit ✅

- **Entry**: Buy at 18,235 | **Stop**: 18,170 | **Target**: 18,350

## When NOT to Trade Fakeouts

<Callout type="warning" title="Avoid These Scenarios">

1. **Strong momentum breakout** — If the breakout candle is a massive displacement candle with tiny wick, it's probably NOT a fakeout.
2. **HTF trend aligned** — Breakouts WITH the HTF trend are more likely to be real.
3. **No clear S/R level** — Don't assume every reversal is a fakeout. You need a CLEAR level that was broken.
4. **During major news** — The first candle of a news event is unpredictable. Wait for the dust to settle.
5. **Low liquidity hours** — Fakeouts in low-liquidity periods (Asian session for EUR pairs) can extend further than expected.

</Callout>

## Common Mistakes

<Callout type="warning" title="Fakeout Trading Errors">

**❌ Calling every reversal a fakeout** — A fakeout requires a CLEAR level that was broken. Random reversals are not fakeouts.

**❌ Entering too early** — Wait for the reversal candle to CLOSE before entering. The spike might extend further.

**❌ Stop too tight** — Place your stop beyond the fakeout wick with buffer. If price returns to the fakeout level, the trade is invalid.

**❌ Ignoring the HTF** — Fakeouts against the HTF trend are the highest probability. Fakeouts WITH the HTF trend might not be fakeouts at all.

**❌ Over-sizing** — Even high-probability fakeout trades can fail. Use standard 1-2% risk.

</Callout>
