## What Is Candlestick Psychology?

A candle is a summary of a fight. **Candlestick psychology** means reading that summary as a story: who pushed first, how far they got, who pushed back, and who was in control when the period ended.

Once you can read the story, you don't need to memorise patterns. Any candle can be read with the same questions.

## Reading the Parts

| Part               | Question                     | Reading                                                 |
| ------------------ | ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Body**           | Who won the period?          | Large body: one side won clearly. Small body: no winner |
| **Upper wick**     | Were higher prices accepted? | Long: price went up and was pushed back down            |
| **Lower wick**     | Were lower prices accepted?  | Long: price went down and was pushed back up            |
| **Close position** | Who had control at the end?  | Close near the high: buyers. Near the low: sellers      |

| Wick-to-body ratio          | Usual reading                                          |
| --------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| Body much larger than wicks | The winner had conviction; continuation is more likely |
| Wicks larger than the body  | Both sides fought; indecision or a possible turn       |
| No wicks (marubozu)         | One side controlled the whole period                   |

## Three Candle Stories

### The hammer

Sellers drive price down, buyers step in at the low, and price closes near the high. **At support** it means the support held under pressure. In the middle of a move, it means much less.

### The bearish engulfing

Candle 1: buyers in control, a bullish body. Candle 2: sellers erase all of candle 1's body and more. The control has changed hands within two periods. **At resistance after a rally**, that's a warning. In the middle of a strong uptrend, it's often just a pullback.

### The doji

Open and close are almost equal: both sides pushed, neither held. A doji is a pause, not a direction. **At a key level**, wait for the next candle to show who wins.

## Location Decides Everything

| Candle            | At a key level                             | In the middle of nowhere         |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------- |
| Hammer            | At support: a buy signal                   | Noise                            |
| Bearish engulfing | At resistance after a rally: a sell signal | In an uptrend: likely a pullback |
| Doji              | Pause; wait for the next candle            | Noise                            |

Always ask **where** the candle formed before asking **what** it is.

  lesson describes what the candle shows, not who made it.
</Callout>

## Examples (No Chart)

### 1. EUR/USD hammer at daily support

Support **1.0850**. A daily hammer: lower wick 45 pips, body 10 pips. Buy **1.0855**, stop **1.0800** below the wick, target **1.0920**: risk 55 pips, reward 65 pips, **R:R 1 : 1.2** (planned). Low R:R because the wick is long; a 4H entry closer to the level would improve it.

### 2. Gold bearish engulfing at 4H resistance

Resistance **2,450**. Candle 1 is a small bullish candle ($5 body); candle 2 is a bearish candle with an $18 body that engulfs it. Sell **2,445**, stop **2,460**, target **2,400**: risk $15, reward $45, **R:R 1 : 3.0** (planned).

### 3. NAS100 doji at support, then confirmation

At **9:00 PM BD** (NY session), a 15m doji forms at **18,200** support. The next candle is a bullish engulfing. Buy **18,215**, stop **18,185**, target **18,300**: risk 30 points, reward 85 points, **R:R 1 : 2.8** (planned).

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake                               | Fix                                                    |
| ------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| Reading a candle without its location | Key level first, candle second                         |
| Deciding on one candle                | Let the next candle confirm or cancel it               |
| Memorising shapes without the story   | Ask: who pushed, how far, who won the close?           |
| Treating every timeframe the same     | A daily doji at a level matters; a 1m doji rarely does |
| Acting before the candle closes       | The story isn't finished until the close               |

## Practice

1. Pick five hammers or pin bars on a 1H chart.
2. Open the 5m chart for each hour and mark the three phases: push, absorption, take-back.
3. Note which ones formed at a key level and what followed.
4. Log it in your [trading journal](/en/tools/journal-template).
