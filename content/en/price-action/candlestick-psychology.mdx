## What is Candlestick Psychology?

Candlestick psychology is the study of **what each candle reveals about the battle between buyers and sellers**. Every candle tells a story — the body shows who won, the wicks show who tried and failed, and the size shows the intensity of the fight.

<Callout type="info" title="Beyond Patterns">
Most traders memorize candlestick patterns. Professional traders understand the PSYCHOLOGY behind each candle. When you understand WHY a candle formed the way it did, you can read the market like an open book — even with patterns you've never seen before.
</Callout>

---

## Anatomy of Psychology

### The Body — Who Won the Battle
- **Large bullish body** → Buyers dominated the entire session. Strong conviction.
- **Large bearish body** → Sellers dominated. Strong selling pressure.
- **Small body (doji-like)** → Neither side won. Indecision, balance, potential turning point.

### The Upper Wick — Seller Rejection
- **Long upper wick** → Buyers pushed price up, but sellers REJECTED it and pushed back down
- The longer the upper wick, the **stronger the selling pressure** at those higher prices
- At resistance = very bearish signal

### The Lower Wick — Buyer Rejection
- **Long lower wick** → Sellers pushed price down, but buyers REJECTED it and pushed back up
- The longer the lower wick, the **stronger the buying pressure** at those lower prices
- At support = very bullish signal

### Wick-to-Body Ratio
- **Body > Wicks** → The winning side had conviction (continuation likely)
- **Wicks > Body** → Both sides fought hard (reversal or indecision likely)
- **No wicks (marubozu)** → Complete domination by one side

## Reading Candle Stories

### Story 1: The Bullish Hammer
```
     │
     │ ← Long lower wick
     │
    ┌┤
    │█│ ← Small body at top
    └┤
```
**Psychology**: Sellers pushed price down aggressively during the session. But buyers stepped in at the low and pushed price all the way back to the open. The sellers' effort was completely rejected. **Buyers showed dominant strength**.

At support → Strong buy signal.
In a downtrend randomly → Less meaningful.

### Story 2: The Bearish Engulfing
```
    Candle 1    Candle 2
    ┌──┐       ┌────┐
    │  │       │████│
    │██│  →    │████│
    │██│       │████│
    └──┘       │████│
               └────┘
```
**Psychology**: Day 1, buyers were in control (small green candle). Day 2, sellers completely overwhelmed buyers — the entire previous day's gains were erased AND more. The shift from buyer control to seller control is dramatic and decisive.

At resistance after an uptrend → Strong sell signal.

### Story 3: The Doji
```
        │
       ─┼─
        │
```
**Psychology**: The session opened and closed at nearly the same price. During the session, both buyers and sellers pushed price in their direction, but NEITHER could maintain control. This represents **perfect indecision** — a potential turning point.

At a key level → Watch for the NEXT candle to determine direction.

## Context is Everything

<Callout type="warning" title="The Location Rule">
The SAME candle pattern means COMPLETELY different things depending on WHERE it appears:

- A **hammer at strong support** = High-probability buy signal ✅
- A **hammer in the middle of nowhere** = Meaningless noise ❌
- A **bearish engulfing at all-time highs** = Potential reversal ✅
- A **bearish engulfing in a strong uptrend middle** = Likely just a pullback ⚠️

**Always ask: WHERE is this candle forming?**
</Callout>

## Institutional Candle Signatures

### Displacement Candles
Large-bodied candles with minimal wicks that indicate **institutional order flow**:
- Body covers 80%+ of the total range
- Often creates Fair Value Gaps (FVGs)
- Shows aggressive buying or selling
- Usually followed by continuation

### Absorption Candles
Candles where one side absorbs the other's effort:
- Price moves in one direction, then the candle closes in the opposite direction
- Shows institutional orders absorbing retail flow
- Common at key S/R levels and order blocks

### Rejection Candles
Long-wicked candles at key levels showing institutional rejection:
- Wick is 2x+ the body size
- Body closes in the direction of expected reversal
- Often seen at supply/demand zones and liquidity sweeps

## Real Trading Examples

### Example 1: EUR/USD Hammer at Support
- **Level**: 1.0850 support (Daily chart)
- **Candle**: Hammer with lower wick of 45 pips, body of 10 pips
- **Psychology**: Sellers tried hard (-45 pips) but buyers rejected completely
- **Entry**: Buy at 1.0855 | **Stop**: 1.0800 | **Target**: 1.0920
- **R:R**: 1.2:1 | **Result**: +65 pips ✅

### Example 2: Gold Bearish Engulfing at Resistance
- **Level**: $2,450 resistance (4H chart)
- **Candle 1**: Small bullish candle (body $5)
- **Candle 2**: Large bearish candle (body $18) completely engulfing candle 1
- **Entry**: Sell at $2,445 | **Stop**: $2,460 | **Target**: $2,400
- **R:R**: 3.0:1 | **Result**: +$42 ✅

### Example 3: NAS100 Doji at Support (BD Time)
- **Time**: 9:00 PM BD (NY session active)
- **Level**: 18,200 intraday support
- **Candle**: Doji with equal wicks at 18,200
- **Next candle**: Bullish engulfing → Confirmed direction
- **Entry**: Buy at 18,215 | **Stop**: 18,185 | **Target**: 18,300
- **Result**: +85 points, 2.8R ✅

## Common Mistakes

<Callout type="warning" title="Psychology Errors">

**❌ Ignoring context** — A pattern without context (key level, trend, session) is meaningless noise.

**❌ Single candle decisions** — Wait for the NEXT candle to confirm. A hammer means nothing if the next candle breaks its low.

**❌ Memorizing patterns without understanding** — Learn WHY patterns work, not just what they look like.

**❌ Not considering the timeframe** — A doji on the 1M chart is noise. A doji on the Daily chart at a key level is significant.

</Callout>

| Element | Psychology |

| **Doji** | Indecision — potential turning point |
