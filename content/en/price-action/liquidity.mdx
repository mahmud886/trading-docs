## What Is Liquidity?

In trading, **liquidity** means orders waiting to be filled. To buy a large amount, someone needs a lot of sell orders to buy from, and the reverse.

Some of the biggest clusters of orders are **stop losses**. They sit in predictable places: just beyond obvious highs and lows. When price reaches them, they turn into market orders, which is why price often runs just past an obvious level.

<Callout type="info" title="Clarified: 'retail stops' and 'hunting'">
  The original said retail stop losses provide the liquidity institutions need, and that price "hunts" them. Stops from
  all kinds of traders cluster beyond obvious levels, and price often trades through those levels. Whether any one move
  was a deliberate hunt can't be seen on a chart; the pattern can, and that's what this lesson trades.
</Callout>

---

## Buy-Side and Sell-Side

| Type                          | Where       | Whose orders                                      |
| ----------------------------- | ----------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| **Buy-side liquidity (BSL)**  | Above highs | Stops of short sellers, buy-stop breakout entries |
| **Sell-side liquidity (SSL)** | Below lows  | Stops of buyers, sell-stop breakdown entries      |

## Where Liquidity Pools Form

| Pool                                 | Why stops gather there                         | Strength         |
| ------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------- | ---------------- |
| **Equal highs / lows**               | Obvious double tops/bottoms; stops just beyond | Strong           |
| **Swing highs / lows**               | The natural stop for anyone trading the swing  | Strong if recent |
| **Trendlines**                       | Stops sit just beyond the line                 | Medium           |
| **Previous day / week high and low** | Watched by many traders                        | Strong           |
| **Session highs / lows**             | The Asian range high and low before London     | Medium–strong    |
| **Round numbers**                    | Stops placed at or just beyond them            | Medium           |

Weaker pools: levels already swept, single-touch levels, and very old levels.

## Anatomy of a Sweep

1. An obvious level forms (equal lows at 1.1000).
2. Traders buy there and place stops just below.
3. Price trades below, triggering those stops.
4. The stops (sell orders) are absorbed by buyers.
5. Price **closes back above** the level and moves away.

A sweep needs the **close back inside**. If price closes beyond the level and holds, it's a breakout.

---

## Trading a Sweep

| Step | Rule                                                               |
| ---- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| 1    | Mark the pool (equal highs/lows, swing, session level)             |
| 2    | Wait for price to trade beyond it                                  |
| 3    | Wait for a close back inside (and ideally a lower-timeframe CHOCH) |
| 4    | Enter on the close or on a retrace into the order block / FVG      |
| 5    | Stop beyond the sweep's extreme                                    |
| 6    | Target the opposite pool                                           |

## Internal vs External Liquidity

| Type         | Where                                      | Role                       |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------------ | -------------------------- |
| **External** | Beyond the range's or swing's high and low | The main targets           |
| **Internal** | Inside the range: minor highs/lows, FVGs   | Stepping stones on the way |

**The liquidity cycle:** price often moves from one pool to the next, taking one side (a sweep), then travelling to the opposite side. See [Internal & External Liquidity](/en/smc/internal-external-liquidity).

### Example (no chart) — gold, previous day's high

Gold's previous day's high is **2,420**. In the London session it runs to **2,427** and closes back below. Sell **2,418**, stop **2,430**, target the previous day's low at **2,385**: risk $12, reward $33, **R:R 1 : 2.75** (planned).

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake                                    | Fix                                               |
| ------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------- |
| Placing your own stop at the obvious level | Put it beyond structure, not exactly at the level |
| Trading the first push through a level     | Wait for the close back inside                    |
| Calling a breakout a sweep                 | A close beyond that holds is a breakout           |
| Targeting pools already taken              | Target untouched pools                            |
| Ignoring the higher-timeframe draw         | Sweeps against a strong HTF trend often fail      |

## Practice

1. On a 1H chart, mark every equal high and low and the previous day's high and low.
2. Note which ones were swept and whether price closed back inside.
3. After each sweep, check if price reached the opposite pool.
4. Log it in your [trading journal](/en/tools/journal-template).
