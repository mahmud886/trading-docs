## How News Affects Price Action

Scheduled economic data — inflation, jobs, rate decisions — can move price more in one minute than in the previous hour. At the release:

- **Spreads widen** and liquidity thins.
- **Stops can slip**: a stop order fills at the next available price, which can be well past your level.
- Price often **spikes both ways** before it picks a direction.

A technical setup that was valid at 6:25 PM can be meaningless at 6:31 PM.

## Key Releases in Bangladesh Time

| Release                        | Local time        | **BD (US summer)**  | **BD (winter)** |
| ------------------------------ | ----------------- | ------------------- | --------------- |
| US CPI, NFP, retail sales, GDP | 8:30 AM New York  | 6:30 PM             | 7:30 PM         |
| FOMC rate decision             | 2:00 PM New York  | 12:00 AM (midnight) | 1:00 AM         |
| ECB rate decision              | 2:15 PM Frankfurt | 6:15 PM             | 7:15 PM         |
| Bank of England rate decision  | 12:00 PM London   | 5:00 PM             | 6:00 PM         |

Check an economic calendar every week and convert to BD time; dates move, and Europe and the US change clocks on different weekends in March and October/November.

## How News Candles Behave

| Pattern                | What you see                          | What to do                               |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| **Spike and continue** | One direction, holds, extends         | Trade the first pullback, with HTF bias  |
| **Spike and reverse**  | Big move, fully retraced              | Wait for a structure break the other way |
| **Two-way spike**      | Wicks on both sides (the chart above) | Wait for a close outside the news candle |
| **No reaction**        | Data close to forecast                | Normal conditions resume                 |

## Rules Around News

1. **Know the calendar** for your pair each week, in BD time.
2. **Don't open** new trades in the 15–30 minutes before a major release.
3. Decide in advance for open trades: **close, reduce, or hold** with the risk of slippage accepted.
4. **Wait** at least one or two candles on your timeframe after the release.
5. Use the **news candle's high and low** as levels.

### Example (no chart) — gold after NFP

Gold ranges **2,398 – 2,405** before NFP (6:30 PM BD in summer). The release candle spikes to **2,418** and back to **2,400**. At 7:00 PM a 15m candle closes at **2,421**, above the news-candle high. Buy the retest at **2,419**, stop **2,411**, target **2,440**: risk $8, reward $21, **1 : 2.6** planned.

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake                                         | Fix                                          |
| ----------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| Entering just before the release "for the move" | No new entries in the pre-release window     |
| Tight stops through the release                 | Close or reduce; stops can slip              |
| Chasing the first spike                         | Wait for a close outside the news candle     |
| Forgetting the time change                      | Convert every release to BD time, every week |

## Practice

2. Note which pattern each formed and where the first close outside the news candle came.
3. Record the spread at your broker at the release, if you can.
4. Log it in your [trading journal](/en/tools/journal-template).

## Summary

| Rule                              | Why                                   |
| --------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| No new positions into the release | Spreads widen, stops slip             |
| Wait, then read the structure     | The first spike is often not the move |
| News-candle high/low = new levels | Clear reference points                |
| Times in BD, checked weekly       | Releases and clocks move              |
