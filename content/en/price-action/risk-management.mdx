## Risk Management in Two Steps

1. **Stop at structure:** put the stop beyond the swing, zone or level that would prove the trade wrong, not at a fixed number of pips.
2. **Size from the stop:** choose the position size so that, if the stop is hit, you lose a fixed percentage of the account.

A wider stop means a smaller position. The amount at risk stays the same.

---

## Where the Stop Goes

| Setup              | Stop                                      |
| ------------------ | ----------------------------------------- |
| Order block entry  | Beyond the OB's full range, plus a buffer |
| Fibonacci pullback | Beyond 78.6% or the swing point           |
| S/R bounce         | Beyond the zone, plus a buffer            |
| Trendline bounce   | Beyond the trendline and the last swing   |
| CHOCH entry        | Beyond the swing that made the CHOCH      |
| Liquidity sweep    | Beyond the sweep's wick                   |

<Callout type="info" title="Clarified: OB stops">
  The original said to place OB stops "beyond the OB (body)". The wick is part of the zone and retests often reach it,
  so this site uses the full range. See [Risk Models](/en/smc/risk-models) for a chart comparing the two.
</Callout>

## Position Size

```
Lot size = (Account × Risk %) ÷ (Stop in pips × Pip value per lot)
```

Account $10,000, risk 1% = $100, structural stop 35 pips, pip value $10 per standard lot:

$100 ÷ (35 × $10) = 0.286 → **0.28 lots**

<Callout type="info" title="Corrected: rounding">
  The original rounded 0.286 up to 0.29 lots. Always round **down**: rounding up risks more than planned.
</Callout>

## Drawdown Recovery

| Drawdown | Gain needed to recover |
| -------- | ---------------------- |
| 10%      | 11%                    |
| 20%      | 25%                    |
| 30%      | 43%                    |
| 50%      | 100%                   |

Losses hurt more than equal gains help. Small risk per trade keeps drawdowns in the range you can recover from.

## Risk Rules

1. **0.5–1% per trade** while learning; **2% at most**.
2. **Stop beyond structure**, with a buffer, never exactly on it.
3. **Wider stop = smaller position**, never "no stop".
4. **Correlated trades count together:** EUR/USD and GBP/USD longs are close to one bigger trade; limit the combined risk (for example 2%).
5. **Daily loss limit 3%:** stop for the day.
6. **Weekly loss limit 5%:** cut size the next week.
7. **Drawdown 10%:** stop and review the strategy.

## Structural vs Fixed vs ATR Stops

| Method         | How                  | For                             | Against                  |
| -------------- | -------------------- | ------------------------------- | ------------------------ |
| **Structural** | Beyond swing/OB/zone | Logical; fewer random stop-outs | Sometimes wide           |
| **Fixed pips** | Always X pips        | Simple                          | May sit inside structure |
| **ATR-based**  | 1.5–2× ATR           | Adjusts to volatility           | Ignores structure        |

Best of both: place the stop at structure, then check it isn't absurdly small or large compared with ATR, then size the position to fit.

### Example (no chart) — gold

Gold long at **2,355**, structural stop **2,338** below the swing low ($17). Account $8,000, risk 1% = $80. A $1 move is about $100 per standard lot: $80 ÷ (17 × $100) = 0.047 → **0.04 lots**. Target **2,400**: reward $45, **R:R 1 : 2.6** (planned).

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake                                    | Fix                                          |
| ------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------- |
| Choosing the lot size first                | Stop first, then size                        |
| Rounding the size up                       | Round down                                   |
| Raising risk after losses to "win it back" | Keep risk fixed; cut it after a limit is hit |
| Ignoring correlation                       | Count correlated positions together          |
| No daily limit                             | Set one before the session                   |

## Practice

1. Recalculate the position size of your last 20 trades with the formula.
2. Find your longest losing streak and work out the drawdown it would cause at 1%, 2% and 5%.
3. Write your daily, weekly and drawdown limits down.
4. Log it in your [trading journal](/en/tools/journal-template).

## Summary

| Rule       | Detail                                                          |
| ---------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Stop**   | Beyond structure, with a buffer                                 |
| **Size**   | (Account × risk %) ÷ (stop pips × pip value), rounded down      |
| **Risk**   | 0.5–1% per trade; 2% maximum                                    |
| **Limits** | −3% daily, −5% weekly, −10% review                              |
| **Why**    | Drawdowns grow fast with risk; recovery is harder than the loss |
