## What "Advanced" Means Here

Advanced price action isn't new patterns. It is reading the **sequence**: where the range is, where liquidity sits, which side has been taken, and whether price is cheap or expensive within the range. Each idea has its own lesson; this one connects them.

<Callout type="warning" title="Toned down">
  The original said equal highs and lows are "deliberately created by institutions" and that "retail does the opposite".
  Nobody can see who placed which orders, and those claims can't be checked. This lesson treats the ideas as a **way to
  read the chart**, not as knowledge of anyone's intentions.
</Callout>

## The Building Blocks

| Concept                | What it is                                                  | Lesson                                                |
| ---------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| **Dealing range**      | The latest clear swing low to swing high                    | [Market Structure](/en/price-action/market-structure) |
| **Premium / discount** | Above / below the range's 50% line                          | [Premium & Discount](/en/smc/premium-discount)        |
| **Liquidity**          | Stops clustered beyond equal highs/lows and obvious swings  | [Equal Highs & Lows](/en/smc/equal-highs-lows)        |
| **Sweep**              | A wick beyond that liquidity, then a close back inside      | [Liquidity](/en/price-action/liquidity)               |
| **Displacement**       | Strong candles in one direction that leave a fair value gap | [Fair Value Gap](/en/smc/fair-value-gap)              |
| **Fresh vs used**      | A level not yet revisited vs one already tested             | [Fresh vs Tested](/en/smc/fresh-vs-tested)            |

Each step answers one question: _where is the liquidity, which side was taken, did price commit, and is the entry cheap within the range?_ If any answer is missing, there is no trade.

## Top-Down Routine

| Timeframe   | Question                                             | Output                                |
| ----------- | ---------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| **Weekly**  | Where is the obvious liquidity (draw)?               | A target area                         |
| **Daily**   | Which way is structure delivering?                   | Bias: long, short or none             |
| **H4 / H1** | Where is the dealing range and its 50%? Fresh zones? | Areas of interest in discount/premium |
| **H1**      | Has one side of liquidity been swept?                | The narrative for today               |
| **M15**     | Displacement and FVG/structure break at the area?    | Entry, stop, target                   |

Add **time**: London and New York hours (BD time) are when sweeps and displacement most often show up — see [Session Analysis](/en/price-action/session-analysis).

### Example (no chart) — gold short from premium

Gold's daily structure is bearish. The H4 dealing range is **2,380 – 2,440**, 50% at **2,410**. Equal highs sit at **2,432**. In London, price sweeps them to **2,435**, closes back below, and displaces down, leaving an FVG at **2,424 – 2,428** — in premium. Sell **2,426**, stop **2,438**, target **2,388** (just above the range low): risk $12, reward $38, **1 : 3.2** planned.

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake                                  | Fix                                                          |
| ---------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Labelling everything "manipulation"      | Describe what price did; skip guesses about who did it       |
| Buying in premium because "it's bullish" | Wait for discount, or skip                                   |
| Calling any big candle "displacement"    | It must break structure and leave an FVG                     |
| Drawing a new dealing range every hour   | Use the obvious swing on the timeframe you analyse           |
| Too many concepts on one chart           | Range, liquidity, sweep, displacement, entry — that's enough |

## Practice

1. On 10 past H1 charts, mark the dealing range, the 50% line and equal highs/lows.
2. Note each sweep and whether a displacement followed.
3. Record where an FVG entry in discount/premium would have been, with stop and target.
4. Log it in your [trading journal](/en/tools/journal-template).
