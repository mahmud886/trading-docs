## What are Swing Highs and Swing Lows?

Swing points are the **building blocks of market structure**. A swing high is a price peak surrounded by lower highs. A swing low is a price trough surrounded by higher lows. They mark where price changed direction and are the foundation for identifying trends, support/resistance, and structure breaks.

<Callout type="info" title="Foundation of Everything">
  Without correctly identifying swing highs and lows, you cannot determine trends, draw trendlines, identify BOS/CHOCH,
  or trade any price action strategy. Master this concept FIRST — everything else builds on it.
</Callout>

---

## How to Identify Swing Points

### The 3-Candle Rule (Standard)

- **Swing High**: A candle whose HIGH is higher than the high of the candle before AND after it
- **Swing Low**: A candle whose LOW is lower than the low of the candle before AND after it

```
Swing High (3-candle):
      ┃ ← This candle's high is the highest
    ┃ ┃ ┃
    ┃ ┃ ┃ ← Candles on both sides have lower highs

Swing Low (3-candle):
    ┃ ┃ ┃ ← Candles on both sides have higher lows
    ┃ ┃ ┃
      ┃ ← This candle's low is the lowest
```

### The 5-Candle Rule (Strong Swing)

For stronger, more significant swing points, require **2 candles** on each side with lower highs (for SH) or higher lows (for SL).

### Fractals

A fractal is essentially a swing point. The term comes from Bill Williams:

- **Fractal High** = Swing High (bearish fractal)
- **Fractal Low** = Swing Low (bullish fractal)

## Types of Swing Points

### Major Swings (External Structure)

Visible on higher timeframes (Daily/4H). These define the overall trend:

- Create the HH/HL or LH/LL pattern
- Form strong S/R levels
- Take more candles and time to form

### Minor Swings (Internal Structure)

Visible within each leg of the major structure:

- Create internal sub-waves
- Used for entry timing on lower timeframes
- Less significant for overall trend direction

```
MAJOR (Daily):     HH ────────── New HH
                  ╱               ╱
                 ╱     HL        ╱
                ╱                ╱
               HL               ╱

MINOR (1H):        ╱╲    ╱╲   ╱╲
within Daily →    ╱  ╲  ╱  ╲ ╱  ╲
               HL╱    ╲╱ m  ╲╱ m  → HH
                      SL       SL
```

## Swing Points as Decision Points

Every swing high and low represents a **decision**: the market decided to change direction at that price. This makes swing points powerful levels for:

1. **Support/Resistance** — Previous swing highs become resistance; previous swing lows become support
2. **Stop Placement** — Place stops beyond swing points (beyond the decision)
3. **Target Setting** — Previous swings are natural targets
4. **Trend Identification** — The pattern of swings defines the trend
5. **Liquidity** — Stop losses cluster below swing lows and above swing highs

## Swing Failure Pattern (SFP)

A **Swing Failure Pattern** occurs when price tests a previous swing high or low but fails to close beyond it, then reverses. It's a powerful reversal signal.

### Bullish SFP (at Swing Low):

```
Previous SL: $2,380
                ╲
                 ╲
Previous SL ─── $2,380 ────────────
                  ╲  ╱ ← Price goes below
                   ╲╱      but CLOSES above
                   SFP at $2,375
                   (wick below, close above)
                   → BUY signal
```

### Bearish SFP (at Swing High):

```
Previous SH ─── 1.0950 ────────────
                  ╱╲ ← Price goes above
                 ╱  ╲    but CLOSES below
                ╱    ╲
                     SFP at 1.0955
                     (wick above, close below)
                     → SELL signal
```

### Why SFP Works:

- Price sweeps the liquidity (stops) beyond the swing point
- But fails to hold → indicates the opposite side has more strength
- Essentially a **stop hunt** + rejection in one candle

## Real Trading Examples

### Example 1: NAS100 Swing High Identification

- **4H chart**: Mark the obvious peaks
- Swing High 1: 18,450 (July 5)
- Swing High 2: 18,520 (July 8) → Higher High confirmed
- Swing High 3: 18,580 (July 12) → Uptrend confirmed
- **Trade**: Buy at pullback to the most recent swing low (HL)

### Example 2: EUR/USD Swing Failure Pattern

- **Previous swing high**: 1.0950 (tested 2 times)
- **SFP candle**: Price wicks to 1.0958, closes at 1.0942
- **Entry**: Sell at 1.0940 | **Stop**: 1.0965 | **Target**: 1.0880
- **R:R**: 2.4:1 | **Result**: +58 pips ✅

### Example 3: Gold Swing Low as Support

- **Previous swing low**: $2,350
- **Price returns**: Drops to $2,352 (2 points from previous SL)
- **Signal**: Bullish hammer at $2,352
- **Entry**: $2,355 | **Stop**: $2,338 | **Target**: $2,400
- **R:R**: 2.6:1 | **Result**: +$45 ✅

### Example 4: NAS100 Multi-Swing Analysis (BD Time)

- **NY Open** (7:30 PM BD): Identify 15M swing structure
- **Pre-market swing low**: 18,220 (from London session)
- **NY sweeps**: Price dips to 18,210 (SFP below London SL)
- **Entry**: Buy at 18,225 | **Stop**: 18,200 | **Target**: 18,320
- **Session**: 8:00 PM BD entry → 10:30 PM BD target hit
- **Result**: +95 points, 3.8R ✅

## Swing Point Rules for Structure

### Rule 1: Most Recent Swing Matters Most

The most recent swing high/low is the most relevant for structure analysis. Older swings lose significance as new ones form.

### Rule 2: Broken Swings Change Role

- A broken swing high (BOS) → that high is no longer resistance
- A broken swing low (BOS) → that low is no longer support
- Focus on UNBROKEN swing points for S/R

### Rule 3: Equal Highs/Lows = Liquidity

When price creates equal (or nearly equal) swing highs or lows:

- **Equal highs** = Buy-side liquidity (stops above)
- **Equal lows** = Sell-side liquidity (stops below)
- These are prime targets for institutional sweeps

## Common Mistakes

<Callout type="warning" title="Swing Point Errors">

**❌ Marking every tiny swing** — Focus on OBVIOUS swings visible from a distance. Too many marks create confusion.

**❌ Using wrong timeframe** — Match your swing analysis to your trading style. Scalpers use 15M swings; swing traders use Daily swings.

**❌ Ignoring the SFP** — Swing Failure Patterns are among the highest probability reversals. Don't miss them.

**❌ Not updating swings** — As new swings form, update your chart. Old swings become less relevant.

**❌ Confusing wick for body** — For structure breaks, a BODY close beyond the swing is required, not just a wick.

</Callout>
