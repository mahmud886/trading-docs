## What Are Swing Highs and Lows?

A **swing high** is a peak: a candle whose high is above the candles around it. A **swing low** is a trough: a candle whose low is below the candles around it.

Swings are the building blocks of everything else in price action: trend, structure, support and resistance, stops and targets.

---

## Two Rules for Finding Swings

| Rule                 | Swing high                              | Swing low                              | Finds                        |
| -------------------- | --------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| **3-candle** (minor) | High above the candle on each side      | Low below the candle on each side      | Many small swings            |
| **5-candle** (major) | High above **two** candles on each side | Low below **two** candles on each side | Fewer, more important swings |

The 5-candle rule is the same as the classic **fractal** indicator. Note that a swing is only confirmed once the candles after it have closed: two candles later for the 5-candle rule.

On the same 31 candles, the 3-candle rule marks **10 swings**; the 5-candle rule keeps only **4**. Every major swing is also a minor one, but not the other way round.

| Type      | Also called        | Use                                         |
| --------- | ------------------ | ------------------------------------------- |
| **Major** | External structure | Trend direction, key levels, main targets   |
| **Minor** | Internal structure | Entry timing, early warnings, tighter stops |

## What Swings Are Used For

| Use           | How                                               |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| **Structure** | HH/HL or LH/LL sequences define the trend         |
| **Stops**     | Beyond the swing that would prove the trade wrong |
| **Targets**   | The previous swing high (long) or low (short)     |
| **Levels**    | Old swings become support and resistance          |
| **Liquidity** | Stops gather just beyond obvious swings           |

---

## Swing Failure Pattern (SFP)

A **swing failure** happens when price trades **beyond** a swing point but **closes back inside** it, often in one candle.

| SFP         | What happens                                 | Bias |
| ----------- | -------------------------------------------- | ---- |
| **Bearish** | Wick above a swing high, close back below it | Down |
| **Bullish** | Wick below a swing low, close back above it  | Up   |

Why it works: the stops beyond the swing are triggered, but no follow-through comes; the traders who broke out are now trapped. It's a **sweep** in SMC terms; see [Liquidity Sweep](/en/ict/liquidity-sweep).

**The trade in the chart:** the major swing high is **1.0950**. A candle wicks to **1.0958** and closes at **1.0942**.

| Level   | Price       | Note                         |
| ------- | ----------- | ---------------------------- |
| Entry   | 1.0940      | After the SFP candle closes  |
| Stop    | 1.0965      | Above the 1.0958 wick        |
| Target  | 1.0880      | The next swing-low area      |
| **R:R** | **1 : 2.4** | Risk 25 pips, reward 60 pips |

---

## Rules That Help

1. **The most recent swing matters most.** Older swings matter less unless they're also major higher-timeframe levels.
2. **Broken swings change role.** A broken swing high often becomes support (see [Support & Resistance](/en/price-action/support-resistance)).
3. **Equal highs and lows are liquidity.** Two swings at the same price attract stops on the other side.

## Examples (No Chart)

### 1. Gold, a swing low as support

Gold pulls back to its last major 4H swing low at **2,350** and forms a bullish pin bar. Buy **2,355**, stop **2,338** below the swing low, target the last swing high at **2,400**: risk $17, reward $45, **R:R 1 : 2.6** (planned).

### 2. NAS100, 15m swings in the NY session (BD time)

From the NY open (**7:30 PM BD** in US summer), mark the 15m swings. NAS100 makes a higher low at **18,210** and breaks the last minor high. Buy **18,225** at about **8:00 PM BD**, stop **18,200** below the higher low, target the session's major high at **18,320**: risk 25 points, reward 95 points, **R:R 1 : 3.8** (planned).

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake                                  | Fix                                                   |
| ---------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| Marking a swing before it's confirmed    | Wait for the candles after it to close                |
| Treating minor swings as major levels    | Use the 5-candle rule on the analysis timeframe       |
| Stop exactly at the swing                | A few pips beyond it, where a sweep would end         |
| Calling every wick beyond a swing an SFP | It needs a close back inside, and ideally a key level |
| Ignoring the higher timeframe            | A 1H SFP against a strong daily trend often fails     |
