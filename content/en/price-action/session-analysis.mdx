## Why Sessions Matter

Forex trades 24 hours a day, five days a week, but activity isn't even. As each financial centre opens, different participants arrive, volume changes and price behaves differently. Session analysis means using **time** as well as price.

---

## Sessions in Bangladesh Time

| Session                     | Local hours                | **BD time (US/UK summer)** | **BD time (winter)** | Typical character                 |
| --------------------------- | -------------------------- | -------------------------- | -------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| **Tokyo / Asia**            | 9:00 AM – 6:00 PM Tokyo    | 6:00 AM – 3:00 PM          | 6:00 AM – 3:00 PM    | Quieter; ranges are common        |
| **London**                  | 8:00 AM – 5:00 PM London   | 1:00 PM – 10:00 PM         | 2:00 PM – 11:00 PM   | Volume rises; directional moves   |
| **New York**                | 8:00 AM – 5:00 PM New York | 6:00 PM – 3:00 AM          | 7:00 PM – 4:00 AM    | US data; continuation or reversal |
| **London–New York overlap** | —                          | 6:00 PM – 10:00 PM         | 7:00 PM – 11:00 PM   | Usually the busiest hours         |

Japan doesn't change its clocks, so Tokyo stays at 6:00 AM BD all year. London and New York move **one hour later** in BD time in winter. In this lesson the "Asian range" means **6:00 AM – 12:00 PM BD** — the hours before London.

<Callout type="warning" title="Corrected">
  The original table gave times in US Eastern time only and called the Asian session "low volume, tight spreads".
  Spreads are often **wider** in quiet hours, not tighter. Check your broker's spreads by session.
</Callout>

| Step | Time (BD)          | What happens                                                                            |
| ---- | ------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 0    | 6:00 AM – 12:00 PM | Asia builds a **20-pip** range: high **1.0872**, low **1.0852**                         |
| ①    | 1:00 PM            | London's first push sweeps the Asian high to 1.0880, **closes back inside** — a fakeout |
| ②    | 2:30 PM            | The second move **closes below** the Asian low                                          |
| ③    | 3:30 PM            | Sell the retest at **1.0850**; stop **1.0861** above the retest high                    |
| —    | from 6:00 PM       | New York continues lower                                                                |

Target: **2× the Asian range** below the low = 1.0852 − 40 pips = **1.0812**. Risk **11 pips**, reward **38 pips**, **1 : 3.5** planned.

The rule isn't "fade the first break". It is: **don't trust a break until a candle closes outside the range and holds** — whichever side that turns out to be.

## London → New York

| Scenario         | What you see                                | How to use it                               |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| **Continuation** | NY pulls back into London's move and holds  | Buy/sell the pullback in London's direction |
| **Reversal**     | NY breaks London's last swing after US data | Wait for a structure break before switching |
| **Range**        | London's high and low hold all evening      | Range-trade or stand aside                  |

US data is usually released at **8:30 AM New York = 6:30 PM BD (summer) / 7:30 PM BD (winter)**. Avoid entering in the minutes before it — see [News Impact](/en/price-action/news-impact).

### Example (no chart) — gold, London to New York

Gold's Asian range is **2,398 – 2,408**. At 1:30 PM BD London breaks above and closes at **2,412**, then pulls back to 2,409 and holds. Buy **2,410**, stop **2,403** (inside the range), target **2,428** (previous-day high): risk $7, reward $18, **1 : 2.6** planned. Exit before 6:30 PM BD if US data is due.

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake                                        | Fix                                                 |
| ---------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| Using session times without converting to BD   | Write BD times on your chart; change them in winter |
| Trading the first spike out of the range       | Wait for a close outside and a hold                 |
| Trading Asia's range with London-sized targets | Targets follow the session's activity               |
| Holding through US data without a plan         | Close, reduce, or accept the gap risk in advance    |

## Practice

1. For 20 days, mark the Asian range (6:00 AM – 12:00 PM BD) on EUR/USD or gold.
2. Note whether London's first break held or failed, and which side the day's move went.
3. Record the time of the day's high and low in BD time.
4. Log it in your [trading journal](/en/tools/journal-template).
