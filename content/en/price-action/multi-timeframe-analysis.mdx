## What Is Multi-Timeframe Analysis?

**Multi-timeframe analysis** (MTA) means reading the same market on several timeframes, from the highest to the lowest:

- The **higher timeframe** gives the **direction**.
- The **middle timeframe** shows the **setup**: where price is likely to react.
- The **lower timeframe** gives the **entry**: an exact trigger and a tight stop.

<Callout type="info" title="Clarified: 'professional traders never…'">
  The original said professional traders "never" trade from one timeframe and that this habit "separates profitable
  traders from struggling ones". Many traders do work top-down, and it's a sound method, but there's no evidence it's
  universal or the deciding factor. Its value is consistency: you always know which way you should be trading.
</Callout>

## The Three-Timeframe Framework

| Role            | Timeframe examples | Question                         |
| --------------- | ------------------ | -------------------------------- |
| **HTF — bias**  | Weekly / daily     | Which way is the trend?          |
| **MTF — setup** | 4H / 1H            | Where is the zone to trade from? |
| **LTF — entry** | 15m / 5m           | Has price turned at the zone?    |

| Trading style | HTF     | MTF     | LTF      |
| ------------- | ------- | ------- | -------- |
| **Position**  | Monthly | Weekly  | Daily    |
| **Swing**     | Weekly  | Daily   | 4H       |
| **Day trade** | Daily   | 4H / 1H | 15m / 5m |
| **Scalp**     | 4H      | 1H      | 5m / 1m  |

A ratio of about 4–6× between timeframes keeps each one meaningful.

## Step by Step

1. **HTF:** decide the trend (swings, see [Trend Analysis](/en/price-action/trend-analysis)), mark the major levels, and note whether price is in premium or discount.
2. **MTF:** find the nearest zone in the direction of the HTF trend: a support level, demand zone or order block. Wait for price to reach it.
3. **LTF:** at the zone, wait for structure to turn in the HTF direction (a CHOCH), then enter at the order block or FVG it leaves.
4. **Plan:** stop beyond the LTF swing; target the next HTF level.

## Examples (No Chart)

### 1. Gold swing trade

**Weekly:** uptrend, price in the discount half near **2,350**. **Daily:** a bullish engulfing at the **2,355** demand zone. **4H:** a bullish CHOCH and an order block at **2,360**. Buy **2,362**, stop **2,340**, target **2,430**: risk $22, reward $68, **R:R 1 : 3.1** (planned).

### 2. NAS100 scalp (BD time)

**4H:** bullish structure since the London session. **1H:** price pulls back to 1H demand at **18,280**. **5m:** at about **9:00 PM BD**, a bullish CHOCH at **18,285**. Buy **18,288**, stop **18,270**, target **18,330**: risk 18 points, reward 42 points, **R:R 1 : 2.3** (planned).

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake                                    | Fix                                                |
| ------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------- |
| Trading LTF signals against the HTF        | If the daily is bearish, skip the 15m longs        |
| Using too many timeframes                  | Three is enough                                    |
| Starting on the LTF                        | Always start from the top                          |
| Letting LTF noise override the HTF         | A 5m signal doesn't cancel a daily structure break |
| Entering at the MTF zone without a trigger | Wait for the LTF shift                             |

## Practice

1. Pick one pair. Each day for two weeks, write the daily bias, the 4H zone and whether a 15m trigger came.
2. Note which trades lined up on all three timeframes and how they went.
3. Compare them with trades taken on one timeframe only.
4. Log it in your [trading journal](/en/tools/journal-template).
