## The Formula

**Expectancy** is the average result of one trade, measured in **R** (multiples of the amount risked):

> **Expectancy = (win rate × average win) − (loss rate × average loss)**

If you always risk 1R and your target is _RR_ times your risk, this simplifies to **W × RR − (1 − W)**, where _W_ is the win rate. Positive means the average trade earns money **before costs**; zero is break-even.

<Callout type="warning" title="Corrected and toned down">
  The original called expectancy "the mathematical foundation of every profitable trading system" and said a "100+
  trade" sample is enough. Expectancy is a useful summary, but it is an **estimate**, and a positive estimate from a
  small sample can easily be luck. This lesson shows how to put a rough error bar on it.
</Callout>

---

## Break-Even Win Rates

The expectancy is zero when **W = 1 / (1 + RR)**.

| Reward-to-risk | Break-even win rate | Win rate for +0.25R |
| -------------- | ------------------- | ------------------- |
| 1 : 1          | 50%                 | 62.5%               |
| 1 : 1.5        | 40%                 | 50%                 |
| 1 : 2          | 33.3%               | 41.7%               |
| 1 : 3          | 25%                 | 31.3%               |

(Win rate for +0.25R: W = (0.25 + 1) / (RR + 1).) A high reward-to-risk ratio lowers the win rate you need, but you still have to hit it.

<Callout type="info" title="About this chart">
  The 40 trades are **synthetic**, drawn at random with about a 42% chance of a +2R win. The interval uses the simple
  normal approximation (mean ± 1.96 standard errors), which is rough for small samples.
</Callout>

| Quantity                 | Value in the sample                                 |
| ------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------- |
| Trades                   | 40 (17 winners, 20 losers, 3 break-even or partial) |
| Win rate                 | 17 ÷ 40 = 42.5%                                     |
| Average win              | +2.00R                                              |
| Average loss             | −0.95R                                              |
| **Expectancy**           | 0.425 × 2.00 − 0.50 × 0.95 = **+0.375R** (≈ +0.38R) |
| Standard deviation of R  | 1.44R                                               |
| Standard error           | 1.44 ÷ √40 = 0.23R                                  |
| **Approx. 95% interval** | **−0.07R to +0.82R**                                |

The average is positive, but the interval **includes zero**: with only 40 trades this sample cannot tell a real edge from luck. The uncertainty shrinks with the square root of the sample size: to halve the interval you need about four times as many trades.

## Costs

Costs reduce every trade, winners and losers alike. Expressed in R: **cost in R = cost in pips ÷ stop distance in pips**.

### Example (no chart) — a plan with costs

Entry **1.0850**, stop **1.0830** (20 pips), target **1.0890** (40 pips): **1 : 2.0** planned. If you expect to win 40%, the expectancy before costs is 0.4 × 2 − 0.6 = **+0.20R**. A round-trip cost of 1.5 pips is 1.5 ÷ 20 = 0.075R on every trade, so the expectancy after costs is about **+0.125R**. With a 35% win rate it would be 0.35 × 2 − 0.65 = +0.05R before costs and **−0.025R** after: costs turn it negative.

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake                                  | Fix                                                    |
| ---------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| Ignoring costs                           | Subtract cost in R from every trade                    |
| Reporting expectancy with no uncertainty | Give the interval or standard error                    |
| Mixing different risk sizes              | Convert every result to R first                        |
| Using win rate alone                     | Win rate means little without the average win and loss |
| Re-estimating after changing rules       | Treat each rule change as a new sample                 |

## Practice

1. Convert your last 30 trades to R (result ÷ amount risked).
2. Compute win rate, average win, average loss and expectancy; then the standard error and a rough 95% interval.
3. Subtract your typical cost in R. Is the interval still above zero?
4. Log it in your [trading journal](/en/tools/journal-template).

## Summary

| Idea        | Detail                                                      |
| ----------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| Formula     | Win rate × average win − loss rate × average loss, in R     |
| Break-even  | W = 1 / (1 + RR)                                            |
| Costs       | Cost in R = pips of cost ÷ pips of stop                     |
| Uncertainty | Standard error = SD ÷ √n; small samples give wide intervals |
