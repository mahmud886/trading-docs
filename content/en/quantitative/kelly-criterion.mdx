## The Kelly Criterion

The **Kelly criterion** finds the fraction of capital to stake on a repeated bet so that wealth grows as fast as possible in the long run. For a trade that wins _b_ times the amount risked with probability _p_ and loses the amount risked with probability _q = 1 − p_:

> **f\* = p − q / b** (equivalently (b·p − q) / b)

Here \*f\*\* is the **fraction of capital risked per trade** (the distance to the stop, not the position size).

<Callout type="warning" title="Corrected and toned down">
  The original said "most institutional traders use 25–50% Kelly". We found no source for that. Many practitioners do
  bet a fraction of Kelly, and the reason is well understood: the inputs are estimated, and full Kelly is very volatile.
  Treat the fractions below as illustrations to test, not as industry standards.
</Callout>

## An Example in Trading Terms

A system wins **45%** of trades for **+1.5R** and loses **55%** for **−1R**: _p_ = 0.45, _b_ = 1.5, expectancy +0.125R.

> f\* = 0.45 − 0.55 / 1.5 = **0.0833** → risk about **8.3%** of capital per trade at full Kelly

| Stake         | Risk per trade | Expected log growth per trade |
| ------------- | -------------- | ----------------------------- |
| Full Kelly    | 8.3%           | +0.51%                        |
| Half Kelly    | 4.2%           | +0.39%                        |
| Quarter Kelly | 2.1%           | +0.23%                        |

Half Kelly keeps about **75%** of the growth of full Kelly while risking half as much per trade.

| Stake (even-money, 55% wins) | Fraction | Growth per bet | Final multiple (one run) | Max drawdown (one run) |
| ---------------------------- | -------- | -------------- | ------------------------ | ---------------------- |
| ¼ Kelly                      | 2.5%     | +0.22%         | ×2.3                     | 34%                    |
| ½ Kelly                      | 5%       | +0.38%         | ×4.1                     | 57%                    |
| Full Kelly                   | 10%      | +0.50%         | ×6.1                     | 83%                    |
| 2× Kelly                     | 20%      | −0.01%         | ×0.6                     | 99%                    |

Three things to take from it. **Growth peaks at Kelly** and falls on both sides, but falls much faster on the over-betting side: double Kelly has no growth and a near-total drawdown. **Full Kelly's drawdown is severe** even though its growth is the best. And in this one run full Kelly ended highest, but that is one run: across runs, the ordering of final results varies much more than the ordering of drawdowns.

## Why Use a Fraction

| Reason               | Detail                                                                     |
| -------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Estimation error     | You never know _p_ and _b_; an overestimate makes full Kelly an over-bet   |
| Drawdowns            | Half Kelly cuts the typical drawdown a lot for about a quarter less growth |
| Changing conditions  | Edges fade; a fractional stake leaves room                                 |
| Psychological limits | A drawdown you can't stand won't be sat through                            |

The estimation point in numbers: if the true win rate is **40%** but you believe **45%** and bet full Kelly (8.3%), the true expected growth is about **−0.51%** per trade. Betting half Kelly in the same situation gives about −0.13% and quarter Kelly about −0.03%: still negative, but far less damaging.

### Example (no chart) — sizing a trade

Account **10,000**, quarter Kelly: risk **2.1%** = **210** per trade. Entry **1.0850**, stop **1.0830** (20 pips), target **1.0880** (30 pips): **1 : 1.5** planned. Each pip is worth 210 ÷ 20 = **10.5** for this trade, i.e. about 1.05 standard lots on a USD-quoted pair. If your estimated win rate falls to 38%, \*f\*\* = 0.38 − 0.62 / 1.5 = −0.033: the formula says do not trade it.

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake                                        | Fix                                                        |
| ---------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| Betting full Kelly on estimated numbers        | Use a fraction; stress the inputs                          |
| Using position size instead of risk per trade  | \*f\*\* is the fraction of capital lost if the stop is hit |
| Applying Kelly to correlated positions at once | Combined risk is larger than each trade's                  |
| Ignoring costs in _p_ and _b_                  | Use after-cost win rate and reward-to-risk                 |
| Using it when expectancy is negative           | Kelly says stake zero                                      |

## Practice

1. Take your own estimated win rate and average reward-to-risk and compute \*f\*\*.
2. Recompute with the win rate 5 points lower. How fast does \*f\*\* fall?
3. Decide what fraction of Kelly you would actually risk and write down your maximum drawdown tolerance.
4. Log it in your [trading journal](/en/tools/journal-template).

## Summary

| Idea         | Detail                                                     |
| ------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| Formula      | f\* = p − q / b (fraction of capital risked per trade)     |
| Growth curve | Peaks at f\*, falls slowly below it and fast above it      |
| Fractions    | Half Kelly ≈ 75% of the growth with much smaller drawdowns |
| Main reason  | Win rate and payoff are estimated with error               |
