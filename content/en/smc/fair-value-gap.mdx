## What is a Fair Value Gap (FVG)?

A Fair Value Gap (FVG) is a **three-candle pattern** that represents an area of **price imbalance** created by aggressive institutional order flow. It occurs when the market moves so quickly in one direction that it leaves behind a gap between the wicks of the first and third candles — an area where **no two-way price auction** took place.

In simple terms: when big banks and institutions place massive orders, price moves so fast that it "skips" certain price levels. These skipped areas are Fair Value Gaps.

<Callout type="info" title="Why FVGs Matter">
  Fair Value Gaps represent areas of **unfinished business** in the market. Price has a natural tendency to return to
  these areas to "fill" the gap — creating high-probability trading opportunities for Smart Money traders.
</Callout>

## The Anatomy of a Fair Value Gap

An FVG consists of **exactly three consecutive candles**:

| Candle       | Role                | Description                                                                         |
| ------------ | ------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Candle 1** | Setup candle        | Establishes the starting point; its high (bullish) or low (bearish) is one boundary |
| **Candle 2** | Impulse candle      | The large candle that creates the displacement; body shows aggression               |
| **Candle 3** | Confirmation candle | Its low (bullish) or high (bearish) is the other boundary                           |

**The gap exists between:**

- **Bullish FVG**: The high of Candle 1 and the low of Candle 3
- **Bearish FVG**: The low of Candle 1 and the high of Candle 3

---

## Bullish Fair Value Gap

A **Bullish FVG** forms during a strong upward move:

```
        ┌─────┐
        │  3  │  ← Candle 3 (low is upper boundary)
        └──┬──┘
           │
    ░░░░░░░░░░░░░  ← THE GAP (Bullish FVG)
           │
     ┌─────┤
     │  1  │     ← Candle 1 (high is lower boundary)
     └─────┘
```

**How to identify:**

1. Candle 2 has a **large bullish body** (displacement candle)
2. The **high of Candle 1** does NOT overlap with the **low of Candle 3**
3. The space between them is the FVG

**What it means:** Institutions aggressively bought, pushing price up so fast that sellers couldn't participate. Price is likely to return to this zone to allow buyers to re-enter at a "fair value."

### Bullish FVG Trading Setup

**Entry conditions:**

1. Identify a bullish FVG on your timeframe
2. Wait for price to retrace back into the FVG zone
3. Look for a **bullish reaction** (rejection candle, engulfing pattern) at the FVG
4. Enter long at the **midpoint (50% level) of the FVG** or at the bottom of the FVG
5. Stop loss below the FVG (below Candle 1's high)
6. Target the next liquidity level or swing high

**Real example — EUR/USD:**

> During the London session, EUR/USD drops to 1.0850 and then explodes upward to 1.0920 in three candles, creating a bullish FVG between 1.0870 and 1.0885. Two hours later, during the NY session, price retraces to 1.0878 (the 50% level of the FVG), shows a bullish engulfing candle on the 15-minute chart, and rallies to 1.0950. The FVG served as the "launch pad" for the continuation move.

## Bearish Fair Value Gap

A **Bearish FVG** forms during a strong downward move:

```
     ┌─────┐
     │  1  │     ← Candle 1 (low is upper boundary)
     └──┬──┘
        │
    ░░░░░░░░░░░░░  ← THE GAP (Bearish FVG)
        │
        ┌─┴───┐
        │  3  │  ← Candle 3 (high is lower boundary)
        └─────┘
```

**How to identify:**

1. Candle 2 has a **large bearish body** (displacement candle)
2. The **low of Candle 1** does NOT overlap with the **high of Candle 3**
3. The space between them is the bearish FVG

**What it means:** Institutions aggressively sold, and price will likely return to this zone to allow sellers to re-enter.

### Bearish FVG Trading Setup

**Entry conditions:**

1. Identify a bearish FVG on your timeframe
2. Wait for price to retrace UP into the FVG zone
3. Look for a **bearish reaction** at the FVG
4. Enter short at the **midpoint (50%)** or at the top of the FVG
5. Stop loss above the FVG (above Candle 1's low)
6. Target the next liquidity level or swing low

## Why Do Fair Value Gaps Form?

FVGs form due to **institutional order imbalance**. Here's the deeper logic:

### 1. Aggressive Institutional Entry

When large players (banks, hedge funds, central banks) need to fill massive orders, they do so aggressively, causing displacement. The resulting gap shows where only one-sided order flow existed.

### 2. News Events and Economic Releases

Major data releases (NFP, CPI, FOMC) create sudden volatility where price moves without pause, leaving FVGs at multiple levels.

### 3. Stop Loss Cascades

When institutional algorithms trigger stop losses at key levels, the cascade of orders pushes price rapidly, creating FVGs.

### 4. Market Structure Shifts

During a BOS or CHOCH, the displacement candle often creates an FVG that becomes a key reference point for re-entry.

<Callout type="tip" title="Institutional Logic">
  Smart money doesn't fill entire positions at once. They often enter partially during the initial displacement, then
  use the FVG as a re-entry zone to add to their position. This is why price "respects" FVGs — institutions have pending
  orders there.
</Callout>

## How Price Reacts to Fair Value Gaps

Understanding price behavior at FVGs is critical for execution:

### 1. Full Fill (Complete Mitigation)

Price returns and fills the entire FVG, often continuing in the original direction:

- Most common in **trending markets**
- Provides the best entries at the bottom/top of the FVG
- Wait for a reaction candle before entering

### 2. Partial Fill (50% Rule)

Price returns to approximately the **50% (midpoint)** of the FVG and reverses:

- The most common reaction type
- ICT calls this the "consequent encroachment"
- Optimal for aggressive entries

### 3. Rejection at the Edge

Price barely touches the FVG boundary and reverses:

- Happens in **strong trends** where institutions are eager to re-enter
- Requires aggressive entry or limit orders
- Lower risk but might miss the move

### 4. FVG Invalidation

Price passes **completely through** the FVG and closes beyond it:

- Indicates the FVG has been **invalidated**
- The original institutional bias may have changed
- Do NOT trade an invalidated FVG

<Callout type="danger" title="Common Mistake">
  Never assume every FVG will hold. If price closes a full candle body beyond the FVG (not just a wick), the gap is
  invalidated. Move on to find fresh FVGs.
</Callout>

## FVG Entry Models

### Model 1: Limit Order Entry at FVG

The most precise method:

1. **Identify** a clear FVG on 15min or 1H chart
2. **Calculate** the 50% level (midpoint) of the FVG
3. **Place** a limit order at the 50% level
4. **Stop loss** 5-10 pips beyond the full FVG
5. **Take profit** at the next swing high/low or liquidity target

**Pros:** Precise entry, great R:R
**Cons:** Price might not reach your limit; requires patience

### Model 2: Market Order Entry on Reaction

Wait for price to enter the FVG and show a reaction:

1. **Watch** as price enters the FVG zone
2. **Wait** for a confirmation candle (engulfing, pin bar, or displacement in the opposite direction)
3. **Enter** at market after the confirmation candle closes
4. **Stop loss** below the FVG
5. **Take profit** at the next liquidity level

**Pros:** Higher confirmation, more confident
**Cons:** Slightly worse entry price, potentially lower R:R

### Model 3: FVG + Order Block Confluence

The highest-probability setup:

1. **Find** an FVG that overlaps with an Order Block
2. This creates a **confluence zone** — double institutional interest
3. **Enter** when price reaches this zone with confirmation
4. **Stop loss** beyond the Order Block
5. **Target** is usually 1:3 R:R or better

## FVG with Other SMC Concepts

### FVG + Liquidity Sweep

The best FVG entries come after a **liquidity sweep**:

1. Price sweeps a swing high/low (takes out stops)
2. A displacement candle creates an FVG
3. Price returns to the FVG for re-entry
4. This sequence has the highest probability

### FVG + Market Structure Shift (MSS)

When a CHOCH or BOS is confirmed:

1. The displacement candle that breaks structure usually creates an FVG
2. This FVG becomes your **entry zone** for the new trend direction
3. This is the core ICT entry model

### FVG + Premium/Discount

- Trade **bullish FVGs** in the **discount zone** (below 50% of the range)
- Trade **bearish FVGs** in the **premium zone** (above 50% of the range)
- Avoid FVGs that form in neutral territory

## Multi-Timeframe FVG Analysis

### Higher Timeframe FVGs (Daily, 4H)

- Act as **major demand/supply zones**
- Price often reacts strongly (50-200 pip reactions)
- Use for **directional bias** and identifying key levels

### Lower Timeframe FVGs (15min, 5min, 1min)

- Used for **precision entries** within the higher timeframe FVG
- Provide tight stop losses and excellent R:R ratios
- Best for scalping and intraday execution

### The Multi-TF Process

1. **Daily/4H**: Identify the direction and the HTF FVG
2. **1H/15min**: Find the LTF FVG within the HTF FVG zone
3. **5min/1min**: Time your exact entry using a reaction at the LTF FVG
4. This **top-down approach** dramatically increases win rate

<Callout type="tip" title="Pro Tip: Nested FVGs">
  When a lower timeframe FVG exists inside a higher timeframe FVG, this is called a "nested FVG." These have **extremely
  high probability** because two levels of institutional interest converge at the same price zone.
</Callout>

## Types of FVGs

### 1. Standard FVG

The basic three-candle pattern described above. Most common and most reliable.

### 2. Inversion Fair Value Gap (IFVG)

When a previously respected FVG gets **violated** (price closes through it), the FVG "inverts" its role:

- A **bullish FVG** that gets broken becomes **resistance** (bearish)
- A **bearish FVG** that gets broken becomes **support** (bullish)
- IFVGs are advanced concepts used by experienced traders

### 3. Consequent Encroachment (CE)

The **50% midpoint** of an FVG. ICT specifically teaches that this level is where institutions often place their limit orders. Many traders use the CE as the primary entry level rather than the full FVG.

### 4. Opening Gap (Weekly/Daily)

Gaps that form between Friday's close and Sunday's open, or between daily sessions:

- These act as large FVGs
- Price tends to fill these gaps within the week
- Useful for swing trading

## Common Mistakes with FVGs

### ❌ Mistake 1: Trading Every FVG

Not all FVGs are valid. Only trade FVGs that:

- Form during a clear market structure break
- Are on your higher timeframe key level
- Have not been previously tested/filled

### ❌ Mistake 2: Ignoring the Trend

A bullish FVG in a **bearish trend** is likely to get invalidated. Always trade FVGs in the direction of the dominant trend.

### ❌ Mistake 3: No Stop Loss Beyond the FVG

If your stop is inside the FVG, you'll get stopped out during the fill. Place your stop **beyond the entire FVG zone**.

### ❌ Mistake 4: Trading FVGs in Low-Volume Sessions

FVGs created during the Asian session (low volume) are less reliable than those created during London or New York sessions.

### ❌ Mistake 5: Not Waiting for Confirmation

Placing limit orders without any context. Always have confluence — don't trade an FVG just because it exists.

## Advanced FVG Concepts

### FVG Stacking

When multiple FVGs form in the same price area during a move, it creates a **stacked FVG zone**. These are extremely powerful because multiple levels of institutional interest overlap.

### FVG as Profit Target

FVGs above/below current price can be used as take-profit targets because price is drawn to fill gaps.

### Time-Based FVG Validity

FVGs are most powerful within **24-48 hours** of formation. After that, their relevance decreases as the institutional position may have already been filled.

### Session-Specific FVGs

- **London session FVGs**: Most reliable; highest institutional volume
- **NY Kill Zone FVGs**: Strong for intraday; created during peak overlap
- **Asian session FVGs**: Generally weaker; avoid trading these
- **News event FVGs**: Very powerful but volatile; require wider stops

| Component           | Guideline                                 |
| ------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| **Risk per trade**  | 1-2% of account                           |

## Pro Tips

<Callout type="tip" title="Pro Tip 1: The Displacement Filter">
  Only trade FVGs where Candle 2 (the impulse candle) has a body that is **at least 2x the average candle size**. This
  ensures genuine institutional involvement, not just random noise.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Pro Tip 2: Fresh vs. Tested FVGs">
  A "fresh" FVG (never been returned to) is far more reliable than one that has already been partially filled. The first
  test of an FVG has the highest probability of holding.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="Pro Tip 3: Kill Zone Timing">
  FVG entries during Kill Zones (London: 1:00-4:00 PM BD, NY: 7:00-10:00 PM BD) have significantly higher win rates than
  entries during off-hours.
</Callout>

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What timeframe is best for FVG trading?

The **15-minute and 1-hour charts** are ideal for identifying FVGs. Use the **5-minute chart** for precision entries. Higher timeframes (4H, Daily) are for identifying direction.

### How do I know if an FVG is still valid?

An FVG is valid as long as price has not **closed a full candle body** beyond the far side of the FVG. Wicks passing through are acceptable.

### Can FVGs form on any instrument?

Yes — FVGs work on **forex, indices, crypto, commodities, and stocks**. The concept is universal because all markets have institutional participants.

### How many FVGs should I trade per day?

Quality over quantity. Focus on **1-3 high-probability FVG setups** per day that align with higher timeframe bias and Kill Zone timing.

### Do FVGs work in ranging markets?

FVGs are less reliable in ranges. They work best in **trending or transitioning markets** where clear displacement occurs.
