## What Is Manipulation in SMC?

In SMC, **manipulation** is the name for a **false move**: price runs through an obvious level, triggers the stop and breakout orders resting there, and then reverses. It is the middle leg of accumulation → manipulation → distribution ([AMD](/en/ict/amd)).

The usual explanation is liquidity: a large order needs a lot of opposite orders to fill against, and the stops clustered beyond an obvious high or low provide them.

<Callout type="info" title="Reframed: observable, not intent">
  The original lesson called manipulation the "deliberate engineering of price moves by institutions". You can't see
  anyone's intent on a chart, and not every false break is planned by someone. What you **can** see is the pattern: a
  run beyond a level, a close back inside, and a shift in structure. This lesson trades that pattern. (Deliberately
  rigging prices is illegal market manipulation; that's not what SMC traders mean by the word.)
</Callout>

## Three Common Forms

| Form                  | What you see                                                                        | The trapped side                          |
| --------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| **Stop hunt (sweep)** | A wick beyond equal highs/lows or a swing point, then a close back inside           | Stops of traders positioned the other way |
| **Failed breakout**   | A candle _closes_ beyond support/resistance, then the next candles fall back inside | Breakout traders who entered on the close |
| **News spike**        | A fast spike, often in both directions, around a high-impact release                | Stops on one or both sides                |

<Callout type="warning" title="Clarified: news">
  The original said the real direction "emerges 15–30 minutes after" the news. That's a common tendency, not a rule:
  sometimes the first spike is the real move and keeps going. Treat the first minutes as untradeable and wait for a
  structure shift, as with any other sweep.
</Callout>

## Where and When It Happens

| Location                           | Likely false move          |
| ---------------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| Equal highs / equal lows           | Sweep beyond, then reverse |
| Previous day / session high or low | Session open runs it first |
| Obvious support / resistance       | Failed breakout            |
| Round numbers                      | Wick through, close back   |

By time (Bangladesh time, US summer; add one hour in US winter):

| Window               | BD time         | Why                                                 |
| -------------------- | --------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| London open          | 12:00 – 3:00 PM | London often runs the Asian range high or low first |
| New York AM          | 5:00 – 8:00 PM  | NY often runs the London high or low                |
| US data (8:30 AM NY) | 6:30 PM         | News spikes                                         |

See [Kill Zones](/en/smc/kill-zones) for the full session map.

## Trading It, Step by Step

1. **Mark the obvious liquidity:** equal highs/lows, the previous day's and session's high and low.
2. **Have a bias** from the higher timeframe. A sweep against your bias is a setup; one with your bias may be a real breakout.
3. **Wait for the sweep** and a **close back inside** the level. Don't trade the first break.
4. **Wait for the shift:** a CHOCH or MSS on the lower timeframe, ideally with displacement.
5. **Enter** at the FVG or OB left by the shift.
6. **Stop** beyond the sweep's extreme; **target** the liquidity on the opposite side.

### Second example (no chart) — GBP/USD, failed breakout

Bias bearish (daily in premium). In the NY session, GBP/USD closes a 15m candle above resistance at **1.2750**, then the next two candles close back below it and break the last higher low. Sell the retrace into the FVG at **1.2742**, stop **1.2762** above the breakout high, target **1.2690** at the session low: risk 20 pips, reward 52 pips, **R:R 1 : 2.6** (planned).

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake                                    | Fix                                                                                           |
| ------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Calling every break "manipulation"         | Without a close back inside and a shift, it may be a real breakout                            |
| Entering on the sweep candle itself        | Wait for the CHOCH; sweeps can extend further                                                 |
| Stop exactly at the sweep high/low         | Put it beyond, with a buffer for the spread                                                   |
| Trading the first minutes of news          | Spreads widen and spikes reverse; wait for structure                                          |
| No higher-timeframe bias                   | Every move then looks like manipulation                                                       |
| Placing your own stop at the obvious level | Put it beyond the structure that would prove you wrong, not at the round number everyone uses |

## Practice

1. On 20 charts, mark every equal high/low and swing point that was swept.
2. For each, note whether price closed back inside and whether a CHOCH followed.
3. Count how many became reversals and how many became continuation breakouts.
4. Log it in your [trading journal](/en/tools/journal-template).
