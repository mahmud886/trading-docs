## What Are RBR and DBD?

They're the **continuation** patterns of supply and demand:

- **Rally-base-rally (RBR):** price rallies, pauses in a small base, then rallies again. The base becomes a **demand** zone inside an uptrend.
- **Drop-base-drop (DBD):** price drops, pauses, then drops again. The base becomes a **supply** zone inside a downtrend.

Each pause is where the trend reloaded. When price pulls back to it, the trend often resumes from there.

| Pattern | Trend | Zone   | Reversal cousin       |
| ------- | ----- | ------ | --------------------- |
| **RBR** | Up    | Demand | DBR (drop-base-rally) |
| **DBD** | Down  | Supply | RBD (rally-base-drop) |

## Recognising the Pattern

| Part        | What to look for                                                                             |
| ----------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Leg-in**  | One or more candles in the trend direction                                                   |
| **Base**    | 1–3 small candles; bodies no more than about half their range                                |
| **Leg-out** | Large candles in the same direction, ideally breaking the last swing high (RBR) or low (DBD) |

<Callout type="info" title="RBR vs DBR">
  A **DBR** demand forms after a *drop*, so it marks a possible reversal. An **RBR** demand forms after a *rally*, so
  it's a continuation zone. RBRs are usually traded with the trend; DBRs need more confirmation because they bet on a
  turn.
</Callout>

## Trading It, Step by Step

1. **Confirm the trend** on the higher timeframe: higher highs and higher lows for RBR, the opposite for DBD.
2. **Find the base** whose leg-out broke the last swing.
3. **Draw** the proximal line (highest body for RBR, lowest body for DBD) and the distal line (the far wick).
4. **Wait** for the first pullback to the zone.
5. **Enter** at the proximal line, or on a lower-timeframe CHOCH inside the zone.
6. **Stop** beyond the distal line; **target** the last swing high (RBR) or low (DBD), or the next liquidity beyond it.

### Second example (no chart) — DBD in a downtrend

GBP/USD is falling. It drops to **1.2680**, bases for two hours between **1.2672 and 1.2685**, then drops 40 pips and breaks the last swing low. On the first pullback, sell at the proximal **1.2672**, stop **1.2690** above the distal, target the next sell-side liquidity at **1.2600**: risk 18 pips, reward 72 pips, **R:R 1 : 4.0** (planned).

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake                                        | Fix                                                          |
| ---------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Taking RBRs against the higher-timeframe trend | They're continuation zones; trade them with the trend        |
| Marking every pause as a base                  | The leg-out must be strong and break structure               |
| Buying the third touch of an RBR               | First return; see [Fresh vs Tested](/en/smc/fresh-vs-tested) |
| Buying an RBR high in premium                  | Prefer zones in the discount half of the leg                 |
| Ignoring a close below the distal line         | That ends the zone, and maybe the trend                      |

## Summary

| Aspect   | RBR                  | DBD                   |
| -------- | -------------------- | --------------------- |
| Trend    | Up                   | Down                  |
| Zone     | Demand               | Supply                |
| Proximal | Highest base body    | Lowest base body      |
| Distal   | Lowest base wick     | Highest base wick     |
| Trade    | Buy the first return | Sell the first return |
