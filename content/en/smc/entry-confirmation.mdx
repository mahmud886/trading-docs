## What Is Entry Confirmation?

Entry confirmation is the **final trigger** before you enter at your point of interest (POI): an order block, FVG or other zone you marked on the higher timeframe.

Price touching a POI doesn't mean the zone will hold. Confirmation is evidence, on the lower timeframe, that price is actually reacting there. The trade-off: **the more evidence you wait for, the worse your entry price, and the lower your R:R.**

| Entry | Trigger                                                            | Price  | Risk    | Reward  | **R:R**     |
| ----- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------ | ------- | ------- | ----------- |
| **A** | Risk entry: limit order at the OB's top                            | 1.0850 | 12 pips | 50 pips | **1 : 4.2** |

| **C** | Structure: CHOCH above the 1.0866 lower high, entry in the new FVG | 1.0861 | 23 pips | 39 pips | **1 : 1.7** |

Entry A gets the best price but has no evidence the zone will hold; it's also filled on every setup that fails. Entry C has the most evidence but the smallest reward. Many traders using C move the stop up to just below the swing low that made the CHOCH (here 1.0843, so a stop near 1.0840), which brings its R:R back toward 1 : 2.

## Confirmation Methods

### 1. Lower-timeframe CHOCH (strongest)

1. Price reaches your HTF POI.
2. Drop to the 5m or 15m chart.
3. Wait for a CHOCH in your trade direction: a close beyond the last lower high (for a long) or higher low (for a short).
4. Enter on the retrace into the FVG or OB the CHOCH left behind.
5. Stop beyond the swing that made the CHOCH, or beyond the POI.

### 2. Displacement + new FVG

A strong, large-bodied candle away from the POI that leaves an FVG. It usually _is_ the CHOCH candle, so this and method 1 often arrive together. Enter on the retrace into the new FVG.

### 3. Candle pattern

An engulfing candle, a pin bar or a strong close at the POI. It's the earliest and weakest form of confirmation, so use it with other confluence.

<Callout type="info" title="Clarified: the hierarchy">
  The original ranked "displacement + FVG" as having higher R:R than a CHOCH entry. The two are usually the same move:
  the displacement candle is what breaks structure. What changes R:R is where you enter and where you put the stop, not
  the name of the trigger.
</Callout>

| Rank | Method                       | Evidence | Entry price |
| ---- | ---------------------------- | -------- | ----------- |
| 1    | LTF CHOCH + FVG retrace      | Most     | Latest      |
| 2    | Displacement candle close    | High     | Late        |
| 3    | Engulfing / pin bar at POI   | Some     | Early       |
| 4    | Risk entry (no confirmation) | None     | Best        |

## Grading the Setup

Score each setup before you take it:

| Criterion                                    | ✓   |
| -------------------------------------------- | --- |
| Higher-timeframe trend agrees                |     |
| POI is unmitigated (first touch)             |     |
| POI is in discount (long) or premium (short) |     |
| LTF confirmation (CHOCH) present             |     |
| Inside a [kill zone](/en/smc/kill-zones)     |     |

| Score | Grade | Action       |
| ----- | ----- | ------------ |
| 5 / 5 | A+    | Normal risk  |
| 3–4   | B     | Reduced risk |
| 0–2   | C     | Skip         |

This is a checklist for consistency, not a measured win rate. Track your own results by grade in your journal to see whether A+ setups really do better for you.

### Second example (no chart) — Gold, short at a 4H OB

A 4H bearish OB at **2,418 – 2,425**. Price taps it in the NY kill zone. On 5m, a CHOCH below **2,412** leaves an FVG; sell the retrace at **2,414**, stop **2,427** above the OB, target **2,385**: risk $13, reward $29, **R:R 1 : 2.2** (planned). A risk entry at the OB's bottom (2,418) with the same stop and target would have been 1 : 3.7, with no evidence.

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake                                         | Fix                                                       |
| ----------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| Entering on the touch with no plan              | Decide in advance: risk entry or confirmation             |
| Waiting for confirmation, then using a POI stop | The R:R gets poor; consider the CHOCH-swing stop          |
| Calling any small candle "confirmation"         | Use structure (a CHOCH), not a single candle, when unsure |
| Chasing after confirmation                      | If you missed the FVG retrace, the trade is gone          |
| Taking C-grade setups                           | Skip them; there will be others                           |

## Practice

1. Find 20 HTF POI touches.
2. For each, record where A, B and C entries would have been, with the same stop and target.
3. Note which ones failed before a CHOCH ever formed: those are losses that only the risk entry took.
4. Log it in your [trading journal](/en/tools/journal-template).
