## What Is SMT Divergence?

**SMT divergence** happens when two markets that usually move together **disagree at a swing point**: one makes a new high or low, the other doesn't.

| Type            | What you see                                                                 | Suggests                                               |
| --------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Bullish SMT** | One pair makes a **lower low**; the correlated pair makes a **higher low**   | The sell-off isn't confirmed; a turn up is more likely |
| **Bearish SMT** | One pair makes a **higher high**; the correlated pair makes a **lower high** | The rally isn't confirmed; a turn down is more likely  |

"SMT" is usually expanded as "smart money technique". The idea is simple: if the move were broad, both markets would confirm it.

<Callout type="info" title="Corrected: what it proves">
  The original said a divergence shows "smart money is not bearish". It doesn't show anyone's intent. It shows the move
  isn't confirmed across related markets, which makes a reversal **more likely**, not certain.
</Callout>

5. That disagreement is a **bullish SMT**. Both pairs then rally.

## Which Markets to Compare

| Market 1     | Market 2    | Correlation | Divergence to look for                                                                   |
| ------------ | ----------- | ----------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| EUR/USD      | GBP/USD     | Positive    | One makes a new extreme, the other doesn't                                               |
| EUR/USD      | DXY         | **Inverse** | DXY makes a new **high** while EUR/USD does **not** make a new low (bullish for EUR/USD) |
| Gold         | DXY         | Inverse     | Same logic as EUR/USD vs DXY                                                             |
| ES (S&P 500) | NQ (Nasdaq) | Positive    | One index makes a new extreme, the other doesn't                                         |
| US30         | US100       | Positive    | Same                                                                                     |

Correlations change over time. Check that the two markets have been moving together recently before reading a divergence.

## Trading It

1. Watch both markets on the **same timeframe**, at a **key level** (a session high/low, a previous day's high/low, an HTF zone).
2. Spot the divergence: a new extreme on one, not on the other.
3. Wait for a **CHOCH** in the expected direction.
4. Enter at the OB or FVG the reversal leaves.
5. **Stop** beyond the sweep extreme on the market you trade. **Target** the opposite liquidity.

**Which pair to trade?** Two common choices: the one that **swept** (it took liquidity and often reverses hardest), or the **stronger** one that didn't make the new extreme. Pick one rule and keep it in your journal.

## Limits

| Limit                        | What it means                                               |
| ---------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| Divergences happen often     | Most are noise; only those at key levels matter             |
| Correlation breaks down      | Pair-specific news (e.g. UK data for GBP) can cause the gap |
| Timeframe matters            | A 1m divergence means little against a 4H trend             |
| It's a filter, not a trigger | Combine it with a sweep and a CHOCH                         |

---

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake                        | Fix                                           |
| ------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------- |
| Trading SMT on its own         | It's confluence, not an entry signal          |
| Comparing different timeframes | Same timeframe, same swing                    |
| Ignoring news on one pair      | Divergence from news isn't SMT                |
| Forgetting inverse correlation | With DXY, compare its high with EUR/USD's low |

## Practice

1. Put EUR/USD and GBP/USD side by side on 15m for 20 sessions.
2. At each London or NY session high or low, note whether both made new extremes.
3. When they didn't, record what happened next. Log it in your [trading journal](/en/tools/journal-template).
