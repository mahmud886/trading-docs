## What is a Breaker Block?

A Breaker Block is an **Order Block that FAILED** — price traded completely through the OB, invalidating it. But instead of being useless, this failed OB now acts as support/resistance from the OTHER side. The OB "breaks" and becomes a new zone.

<Callout type="info" title="From Failure to Opportunity">
A bullish OB that fails (price drops through it) becomes a BEARISH Breaker Block — now acting as resistance from above. The trapped traders who bought at the OB create the selling pressure when price returns.
</Callout>

---

## How Breaker Blocks Form

### Bearish Breaker (from failed Bullish OB)
1. Bullish OB forms (last bearish candle before up move)
2. Price initially uses it as support (respects it once)
3. Price then breaks DOWN through the OB aggressively
4. The failed bullish OB is now a **Bearish Breaker Block**
5. When price retraces UP to this level — SELL opportunity

### Bullish Breaker (from failed Bearish OB)
1. Bearish OB forms (last bullish candle before down move)
2. Price initially uses it as resistance (respects it once)
3. Price then breaks UP through the OB aggressively
4. The failed bearish OB is now a **Bullish Breaker Block**
5. When price retraces DOWN to this level — BUY opportunity

---

## Why Breakers Work

1. **Trapped traders** — Traders who entered at the original OB are now underwater
2. **Breakeven exits** — When price returns to the breaker, trapped traders exit
3. **Their exits fuel your entry** — Their selling (at a bullish breaker) provides YOUR buy liquidity
4. **Institutional logic** — Smart money uses breakers as re-entry points

## Real Example: Gold Bearish Breaker

- **Bullish OB** at 2,035-2,040 was acting as support
- Buyers entered long at 2,037 expecting bounce
- **Price breaks through** 2,035 with displacement (OB fails)
- Price drops to 2,015, then retraces
- **Bearish Breaker** at 2,035-2,040 (now resistance)
- **Entry:** Sell at 2,037 (breaker level)
- **SL:** 2,042 | **TP:** 2,005
- **R:R:** 1:6.4
- Trapped buyers exit at breakeven → fuels the next leg down

## Breaker vs Mitigation Block

| Feature | Breaker Block | Mitigation Block |
|---------|--------------|------------------|
| **OB Status** | Fully failed (broken through) | Partially filled |
| **Role Change** | Support → Resistance (or vice versa) | Same direction, refined zone |
| **Entry Direction** | OPPOSITE to original OB | SAME as original OB |

## Common Mistakes

<Callout type="warning" title="Breaker Errors">

**❌ Confusing with mitigation** — Breaker = OB fully failed. Mitigation = OB partially filled

**❌ No displacement through the OB** — The break must be aggressive to create a valid breaker

**❌ Trading breakers against HTF** — Breakers still need HTF alignment

</Callout>
