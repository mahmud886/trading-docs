## What Is an Imbalance?

An **imbalance** (or **inefficiency**) is a price range that was traded **only one way, quickly**. Buyers (or sellers) were so much stronger that the other side barely got filled there. On the chart it shows up as gaps between candles or as a run of large candles with little overlap.

Imbalances matter because price **often** comes back to them, and when it does, the original side often defends them.

<Callout type="warning" title="Corrected: 'price will almost always return'">
  The original said price returns to imbalances "almost always" and called them magnets. Many imbalances are revisited,
  but in strong trends many stay open for a long time. Use them as likely reaction zones in the trend direction, not as
  certainties.
</Callout>

| Type                 | How to spot it                                                                                       | Notes                                                  |
| -------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Fair value gap**   | Candle 1's high and candle 3's low don't overlap (or the reverse)                                    | The most used; see [FVG](/en/smc/fair-value-gap)       |
| **Volume imbalance** | One candle's **body** ends and the next one's starts at a different price, but the **wicks overlap** | Common at session opens; smaller, often filled quickly |
| **Liquidity void**   | Several large candles in one direction with little or no overlap                                     | A whole leg; price may retrace into it in steps        |

The chart uses conceptual candles to show each shape.

## Why Price Revisits Them

| Common explanation            | What you can actually observe                       |
| ----------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| Unfilled orders in the gap    | Little trading happened there                       |
| "Seeking fair value"          | Price often rotates back through fast moves         |
| Re-entry by the original side | The first retest often holds in the trend direction |

The "institutions re-enter" story is common but can't be seen on a chart. The observable part is enough to trade.

## Trading an Imbalance Retest

1. Find an imbalance created by **displacement** in the higher-timeframe trend direction.
2. Wait for price to come back into it.
3. Enter in the **original direction**: at the near edge, the 50% level, or after a lower-timeframe CHOCH inside it.
4. **Stop** beyond the imbalance (or beyond the swing it came from).
5. **Target** the next structure level or liquidity.

**Example (bullish FVG):** a rally leaves a gap at **1.1000 – 1.1020**. Price returns to **1.1010** (the 50% level). Buy 1.1010, stop below **1.0990**. Risk 20 pips; the target is the next swing high, so R:R depends on where that is.

## When Imbalances Don't Fill

| Situation                                      | Expect                                 |
| ---------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| Strong trend, imbalance in the trend direction | May not be revisited for days or weeks |
| Counter-trend imbalance in a strong trend      | Often runs straight through            |
| News spike                                     | Retests are messy; wait for structure  |
| Thin session (late NY, holidays)               | Gaps mean less                         |

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake                                       | Fix                                      |
| --------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| Treating every gap as a target                | Only imbalances from displacement matter |
| Fading the trend at an imbalance              | Trade retests in the trend direction     |
| Calling a body gap an FVG                     | Check whether the wicks overlap          |
| Entering on the first touch with no stop plan | Stop beyond the imbalance                |
