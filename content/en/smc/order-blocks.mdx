## What Is an Order Block?

An **Order Block (OB)** is the **last opposing candle before a displacement**: a strong, impulsive move that breaks market structure.

- A **bullish OB** is the last **bearish** candle before a bullish displacement.
- A **bearish OB** is the last **bullish** candle before a bearish displacement.

SMC traders read this candle as the footprint of large orders. Price often comes back to it before continuing in the direction of the displacement, which makes the zone a potential entry area.

<Callout type="info" title="Why the zone can matter">
  Large participants cannot fill a full position at one price without moving the market against themselves. The idea
  behind an OB is that part of the position is filled before the displacement, and price later returns to the same area
  where the rest can be filled. Treat this as a working model that explains the pattern, not as a guarantee that price
  will react.
</Callout>

### Key terms

| Term                   | Meaning                                                                       |
| ---------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Displacement**       | A strong, one-sided move, usually one or more large-bodied candles            |
| **Structure break**    | A close beyond a swing point: **BOS** (with the trend) or **CHOCH** (against) |
| **Mean threshold**     | The 50% level of the OB candle's body                                         |
| **Mitigated OB**       | An OB that price has already returned to                                      |
| **Premium / Discount** | Above / below the 50% level (equilibrium) of the current dealing range        |

## Bullish Order Block

![Bullish Order Block — EUR/USD 15m retest entry after displacement](/images/smc/order-blocks/bullish-order-block-eurusd-dark.png)

**How to identify it**

1. Find a strong **bullish displacement** that closes above a recent swing high (a CHOCH or BOS).
2. Go back to the **last bearish candle** before that displacement.
3. Mark its range. Use **high to low** for the standard OB, or **open to close** (the body) for a refined OB.
4. Confirm that the zone sits in **discount**, below the 50% level of the new swing.

**How to trade it**

- Wait for price to **retrace into the zone**. Do not chase the displacement.
- Look for a bullish reaction: a lower-timeframe (LTF) BOS, an engulfing candle or a rejection wick.
- Place the stop loss **below the OB low**, not inside the zone.
- Target the next **buy-side liquidity**, such as a prior swing high or equal highs.

## Bearish Order Block

![Bearish Order Block — NAS100 15m premium retest](/images/smc/order-blocks/bearish-order-block-nas100-dark.png)

**How to identify it**

1. Find a strong **bearish displacement** that closes below a recent swing low.
2. Go back to the **last bullish candle** before that displacement.
3. Mark its range, high to low or body only.
4. Confirm that the zone sits in **premium**, above the 50% level of the new swing.

**How to trade it**

- Wait for price to **retrace up** into the zone.
- Look for a bearish reaction.
- Place the stop loss **above the OB high**.
- Target the next **sell-side liquidity**, such as a prior swing low or equal lows.

## Validation Checklist

Score every OB before you trade it. The more boxes it ticks, the stronger the zone.

| #   | Check                                                                                     | Why it matters                               |
| --- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | **Displacement** after the candle; body at least **2× the average candle**                | No displacement means no proof of intent     |
| 2   | Displacement **closes** beyond structure (**BOS or CHOCH**); a wick is not enough         | Confirms a real shift in order flow          |
| 3   | Zone is in **discount** (bullish) or **premium** (bearish)                                | You buy low and sell high within the range   |
| 4   | **Fresh**: price has not returned to it yet                                               | Orders at a mitigated OB may already be used |
| 5   | Aligned with the **higher-timeframe bias**                                                | Counter-trend OBs fail more often            |
| 6   | Formed during the **London or New York** session                                          | More participation than the Asian session    |
| 7   | Bonus: an **FVG** formed by the displacement, or a **liquidity sweep** just before the OB | Extra confluence                             |

<Callout type="warning" title="The #1 OB mistake">
  Do not mark every swing as an Order Block. Without displacement and a structure break, the candle is just a candle.
</Callout>

## Three Entry Models

### Model 1 — Aggressive: limit order at the OB

1. After a valid OB forms, place a **limit order at the mean threshold** (50% of the body) or at the proximal edge.
2. Stop loss beyond the OB's distal edge.
3. Target the next liquidity level.

**Trade-off:** you get the best price and need no screen time. But there is no confirmation, so you take every failure.

### Model 2 — Conservative: confirmation inside the OB

1. Wait for price to **enter** the zone.
2. Drop to a lower timeframe (5m or 1m).
3. Enter after a **LTF BOS** or a clear reversal candle in your direction.
4. Keep the stop beyond the OB.

**Trade-off:** fewer failed trades. The entry is worse, some moves leave without you, and the R:R is lower.

### Model 3 — Confluence: OB + FVG

1. Find an OB whose displacement left a **Fair Value Gap**.
2. Use the overlap, or the FVG's 50% level, as the entry area.
3. Stop beyond the OB.

**Trade-off:** fewer setups, but each one has more reasons to hold.

## Invalidation — When the OB Is Wrong

Decide this **before** you enter:

- **Bullish OB:** a candle **closes below the OB low** on your execution timeframe.
- **Bearish OB:** a candle **closes above the OB high**.
- Price reaches the zone but **no confirmation forms** (Model 2). No trade is a valid outcome.
- The higher-timeframe bias flips before price returns.

A failed OB often becomes a **Breaker Block** in the opposite direction, so the failure itself is useful information.

## Multi-Timeframe Process

| Timeframe  | Job                                                |
| ---------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| Daily / 4H | Bias and the major OBs that define where you trade |
| 1H / 15m   | The OB that sits inside the higher-timeframe zone  |
| 5m / 1m    | Confirmation and a precise entry inside the 15m OB |

Work **top-down**. Only trade a lower-timeframe OB that sits inside a higher-timeframe zone pointing the same way.

## Worked Examples

<Callout type="info" title="About these examples">
  The charts use **synthetic candles** built around the levels below. They show how the method works and are not records
  of real trades. R:R values are the planned risk-to-reward, not results.
</Callout>

### Example 1 — EUR/USD, bullish OB (15m, London)

**Observations**

- The sell-off makes a last lower high at **1.0896**.
- The last bearish candle before the rally spans **1.0855 – 1.0862**. This is the OB.
- The displacement candle has a **45-pip body** against a 5.6-pip average. It closes above 1.0896, which is a CHOCH, and the move peaks at 1.0910.
- Two hours later price returns to **1.0860**, inside the OB, and a 5m bullish engulfing forms.

**Trade plan**

| Level  | Price   | Note                                  |
| ------ | ------- | ------------------------------------- |
| Entry  | 1.0861  | After the 5m confirmation             |
| Stop   | 1.0850  | 5 pips below the OB                   |
| Target | 1.0920  | Prior swing high (buy-side liquidity) |
| R:R    | 1 : 5.4 | Risk 11 pips, reward 59 pips          |

**Invalidation:** a 15m close below 1.0850.

### Example 2 — NAS100, bearish OB (15m, New York)

**Observations**

- After the New York open, price rallies into a last bullish candle at **18,380 – 18,420**. This is the OB, and it sits in premium.
- A 90-point bearish displacement follows. The next candle closes below the 18,288 higher low, which is a CHOCH, and prints 18,250.
- Price retraces to **18,400** and a 15m bearish engulfing forms.

**Trade plan**

| Level  | Price   | Note                                                 |
| ------ | ------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| Entry  | 18,395  | Sell limit after the engulfing, filled on the retest |
| Stop   | 18,430  | 10 points above the OB                               |
| Target | 18,280  | Placed just ahead of the 18,250 low                  |
| R:R    | 1 : 3.3 | Risk 35 points, reward 115 points                    |

**Invalidation:** a 15m close above 18,430.

### Example 3 — Gold (XAU/USD), bullish OB

**Setup:** gold drops to $2,315 in London. The last bearish candle at **$2,318 – $2,322** is followed by a displacement to $2,350 that breaks structure. Price later returns to $2,320, the middle of the OB, and a bullish pin bar forms on the 5m chart.

| Level  | Price   | Note                |
| ------ | ------- | ------------------- |
| Entry  | $2,320  | OB mean threshold   |
| Stop   | $2,312  | Below the OB        |
| Target | $2,355  | Next liquidity      |
| R:R    | 1 : 4.4 | Risk $8, reward $35 |

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake                               | Fix                                                             |
| ------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Marking OBs without displacement      | Require a body ≥ 2× average and a structure close               |
| Trading an OB that was already tested | Trade the first return only                                     |
| Ignoring the higher-timeframe bias    | Only take OBs in the direction of the HTF trend                 |
| Stop loss inside the zone             | Stop beyond the full OB, plus a small buffer                    |
| Chasing the displacement              | Wait for the retrace; a missed trade costs nothing              |
| Judging a setup by its R:R alone      | Wide targets are reached less often; check the conditions first |

## Risk Management

| Component            | Guideline                                        |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| **Risk per trade**   | 1–2% of the account (start lower while learning) |
| **Position size**    | Account risk ÷ stop distance                     |
| **Stop loss**        | 5–15 pips (or equivalent) beyond the OB boundary |
| **Take profit 1**    | Next structural level (around 1 : 2)             |
| **Take profit 2**    | Next liquidity pool (1 : 3 or more)              |
| **Max daily trades** | 2–3 quality OB setups                            |

<Callout type="tip" title="Size from the stop, not from the target">
  For example, with a $1,000 account, 1% risk ($10) and an 11-pip stop, the position should lose $10 if the stop is hit.
  Use the [Position Size Calculator](/en/tools/position-calculator).
</Callout>

## Practice

1. Open a 15m chart and mark the last **five** displacements. For each one, find the OB and score it with the checklist.
2. For each OB, write down the invalidation level **before** looking at what price did next.
3. Log the result of each first return in your [trading journal](/en/tools/journal-template). After 50 or more examples you will have your own statistics, which are worth more than any number in a guide.

## FAQ

### What is the difference between an Order Block and a supply/demand zone?

An OB is a specific candle that must be followed by displacement and a structure break. A supply/demand zone is usually a wider base area. Every valid OB lies inside a supply or demand zone, but not every zone contains a valid OB.

### Should I use the full candle or only the body?

Use the full range when you want more fills and a safer stop. Use the body (refined OB) when you want a tighter stop and a better R:R, and can accept missing some trades.

### How long does an OB stay valid?

Until price mitigates it or closes through it. Lower-timeframe OBs tend to matter for hours to a few days. Higher-timeframe OBs can matter for weeks.
