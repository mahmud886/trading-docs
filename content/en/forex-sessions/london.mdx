## Market Behavior

### The London Manipulation Sequence

London doesn't just "trade" — it **manipulates** the market systematically:

1. **1:00 PM BD — Session Open**: Institutional algorithms activate
2. **1:00 PM - 2:00 PM BD**: Asian liquidity sweep — London takes out Asian session highs OR lows
3. **2:00 PM - 4:00 PM BD**: True directional move begins after the sweep
4. **4:00 PM - 6:00 PM BD**: Continuation of trend, European data releases
5. **6:00 PM BD**: New York opens — London + NY overlap begins
6. **6:00 PM - 10:00 PM BD**: Peak volatility, massive moves

<Callout type="tip" title="ICT London Kill Zone 🇧🇩">
The **ICT London Kill Zone** in Bangladesh time is **1:00 PM to 4:00 PM BD**. This is when smart money engineers liquidity sweeps of the Asian range to establish the daily trend direction.
</Callout>

## London Session Strategies

### 1. Asian Range Sweep Strategy
- Mark Asian session high and low (5:00 AM - 1:00 PM BD)
- Wait for London to break one side (1:00 PM - 2:00 PM BD)
- Look for rejection / reversal candle after the sweep
- Enter in the opposite direction of the sweep
- Target the other side of the Asian range

### 2. London Breakout
- Wait for the first 30 minutes (1:00 - 1:30 PM BD)
- Mark the high and low of this opening range
- Enter on the breakout of this range with momentum confirmation
- Stop loss below/above the range
- Target 1:2 or 1:3 R:R

### 3. Order Block Entry (SMC)
- Identify the last bearish candle before a bullish move (or vice versa)
- Wait for price to return to this order block during London session
- Enter with a tight stop below the OB
- Target the next liquidity level

### 4. London + NY Overlap Power Trades
- Wait for the 6:00 PM BD convergence
- Look for FVGs (Fair Value Gaps) created during London
- Enter on mitigation of these gaps during NY
- Use the London session high/low as targets

<Callout type="danger" title="Risk Warning">
London session volatility can create **100+ pip moves in minutes** on GBP/USD and Gold. Always use proper position sizing and stop losses. Never over-leverage during London.
</Callout>
