## Tokyo Session Overview

The Tokyo session — often called the **Asian session** — is the BD morning. It's usually calmer than London or New York, and the range it builds is a reference for the rest of the day.

| Detail             | Info                                                           |
| ------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 🕐 **BD time**     | **6:00 AM – 3:00 PM**, all year (Japan has no daylight saving) |
| 🌐 **Local / UTC** | 9:00 AM – 6:00 PM Tokyo · 00:00 – 09:00 UTC                    |
| 📊 **Activity**    | Moderate; most active in JPY, AUD and NZD pairs                |
| 💱 **Main pairs**  | USD/JPY, EUR/JPY, GBP/JPY, AUD/JPY, AUD/USD, NZD/USD           |

<Callout type="warning" title="Corrected">
  The original gave Tokyo as 5:00 AM – 2:00 PM BD. Tokyo's 9:00 AM – 6:00 PM is **6:00 AM – 3:00 PM BD**, and it doesn't
  change with the seasons.
</Callout>

## Scheduled Events in BD Time

| Event                                    | Local time                                | **BD time**                                       |
| ---------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| Japanese data (e.g. Tankan, GDP)         | 8:50 AM Tokyo                             | **5:50 AM**                                       |
| **Tokyo fix** (reference rate for banks) | 9:55 AM Tokyo                             | **6:55 AM**                                       |
| Australian data                          | 11:30 AM Sydney                           | **7:30 AM** (US summer) · **6:30 AM** (US winter) |
| Chinese data                             | 9:30 – 10:00 AM Beijing                   | **7:30 – 8:00 AM**                                |
| Bank of Japan decision                   | No fixed time; usually late morning Tokyo | Usually **8:00 – 10:00 AM**                       |

Around the 9:55 AM fix, USD/JPY can jump briefly as banks fill orders at the reference rate.

Planned: risk **10 pips**, reward **19 pips**, **1 : 1.9**. On this path the target wasn't hit; the time rule closed the trade at **+13 pips**. The rule exists because London often breaks the Asian range — and a range trade is wrong once the range breaks.

## How Tokyo Behaves

| Phase (BD)         | Typical behaviour                               |
| ------------------ | ----------------------------------------------- |
| 6:00 – 7:00 AM     | Opening moves, data at 5:50, the fix at 6:55    |
| 7:00 – 11:00 AM    | Range forms on many pairs                       |
| 11:00 AM – 1:00 PM | Quieter; positioning before London              |
| 1:00 – 3:00 PM     | Tokyo–London overlap; the range is often tested |

The Asian range is a **reference**, not a promise: some days London breaks it, sometimes it sweeps one side and reverses, sometimes it holds. See [Asian Range](/en/forex-sessions/asian-range).

### Example (no chart) — AUD/USD after Australian data

Australian jobs data at **7:30 AM BD** (US summer) pushes AUD/USD from 0.6650 to **0.6678**. By 8:00 AM it pulls back to **0.6668** and holds above the pre-release high 0.6660. Buy **0.6668**, stop **0.6656**, target **0.6690**: risk 12 pips, reward 22, **1 : 1.8** planned; flat by 1:00 PM BD if not at target.

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake                                           | Fix                                       |
| ------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| Using 5:00 AM as Tokyo's open                     | It's 6:00 AM BD, all year                 |
| Trading EUR/USD or GBP/USD for big moves in Tokyo | Use JPY/AUD/NZD pairs, or wait for London |
| Holding a range trade into London                 | Close or protect it before 1:00 PM BD     |
| Entering right at the fix                         | Let the 6:55 AM spike pass                |

## Practice

1. For 10 days, mark the USD/JPY range from 7:00 to 9:00 AM BD.
2. Note what happened at 6:55 AM BD each day.
3. Record whether London broke the range, swept one side, or left it.
4. Log it in your [trading journal](/en/tools/journal-template).
