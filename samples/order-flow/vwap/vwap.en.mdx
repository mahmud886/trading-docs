## What Is VWAP?

**VWAP** is the session's **volume-weighted average price**: each bar's typical price (high + low + close ÷ 3) is weighted by that bar's volume and averaged since the session began.

> VWAP = Σ (typical price × volume) ÷ Σ volume

Because heavy-volume bars count more, VWAP moves toward where most of the trading happened. It **resets each session**, so it's always about *today's* average.

<Callout type="warning" title="Toned down">
  The original called VWAP "the institutional benchmark". VWAP *is* used as an execution benchmark by some desks, and
  many traders watch it, which is part of why it's useful. But it isn't a floor or ceiling, and price can run away from
  it all day. It's a reference, not a wall.
</Callout>

## Using VWAP

| Use                      | Idea                                                                       | Caution                                         |
| ------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| **Trend filter**         | Above VWAP = buyers have been in control on average; below = sellers       | Early in the session VWAP is noisy              |
| **Pullback reference**   | In an uptrend, a quiet pullback to VWAP and a hold can be a long entry     | Needs the bounce, not just the touch            |
| **Mean reversion**       | A move well beyond the ±1σ / ±2σ bands may snap back toward VWAP           | In a strong trend price can ride the outer band |
| **Execution benchmark**  | Fills better than VWAP (buying below, selling above) are "good"            | Not a trading signal                            |

### Standard-deviation bands

The bands are VWAP ± *k* volume-weighted standard deviations of price. About two-thirds of volume trades inside ±1σ if the distribution were normal — but real intraday volume isn't normal, so treat the bands as **stretch markers**, not probabilities.

### Example (no chart) — a failed pullback

In a similar session price falls back to VWAP at **1.0864**, but the candle that touches it closes **below** VWAP at **1.0861** and the next candle closes lower. The long idea is off: no entry. A close back below VWAP on rising volume suggests sellers have taken control for now, and the opposite trade needs its own plan (sell a retest of VWAP from below, stop above the band, target the lower band).

## Where VWAP Fails

| Situation                              | Why                                                       |
| -------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| First 30 minutes                       | Little data; VWAP is close to price and noisy             |
| Strong one-way trend                   | Price may never return to VWAP                            |
| Low-volume sessions                    | VWAP is dominated by a few bars                           |
| Spot forex without real volume         | Tick-volume VWAP is a weak proxy; use futures if possible |
| Multi-day comparisons                  | VWAP resets daily; use anchored VWAP for longer anchors   |

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake                                         | Fix                                                    |
| ----------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| Treating VWAP as support/resistance             | Wait for the bounce and a close                        |
| Buying every touch                              | Need the trend and the quiet pullback                  |
| Stop at VWAP exactly                            | Give it room beyond the band                           |
| Using VWAP on instruments with no real volume   | Use futures or exchange data                           |
| Ignoring the time of day                        | VWAP is less reliable early in the session             |
