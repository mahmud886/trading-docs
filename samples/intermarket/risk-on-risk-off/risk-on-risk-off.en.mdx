## What Is Risk-On / Risk-Off?

**Risk-on** describes periods when investors are willing to hold riskier assets; **risk-off** describes periods when they cut risk and move toward assets seen as safer. Many markets respond to the same shift, so grouping moves this way gives context for a single chart.

<Callout type="warning" title="Corrected and toned down">
  The original called this an "institutional risk regime framework" and told readers how to position "your portfolio".
  It is a way of describing **what markets have been doing together**, not an official model, and not a forecast. The
  assets below are *usually* grouped this way; they don't always behave that way.
</Callout>

---

## Who Usually Goes Where

| Group               | Often grouped as risk-on                               | Often grouped as risk-off                        |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------ |
| Stocks              | Equity indices, especially cyclical and growth stocks  | —                                                |
| Currencies          | AUD, NZD (and sometimes CAD) and emerging-market currencies | JPY, CHF (and often USD)                     |
| Bonds               | Lower-rated or longer-risk credit                       | Government bonds from major economies            |
| Commodities         | Industrial metals, oil                                  | Gold                                             |
| Volatility          | Low                                                     | High (a VIX-style index rises)                   |

Why the yen and franc: both countries are net creditors and the yen has long been a **funding currency** for carry trades, which are often unwound in risk-off periods, pushing it up. The dollar's role varies: it often rises in risk-off, but not always.

| Asset                | Average daily return, risk-on days | In the risk-off episode | Reading                              |
| -------------------- | ---------------------------------- | ----------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| Stocks (SPX)         | +0.06%                             | −0.57%                  | The anchor of the regime             |
| AUD/USD              | 0.00%                              | −0.18%                  | A risk currency weakens              |
| USD/JPY              | +0.07%                             | −0.20%                  | Falling pair = **yen strengthens**   |

| Volatility index     | about 10–16                        | peaks near 34           | Fear measure spikes                  |

Note the direction rule: USD/JPY and USD/CHF **fall** when the yen and franc strengthen against the dollar.

## Reading the Mood

| Evidence                                 | Leans risk-off                           | Leans risk-on                         |
| ---------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| Stock index                              | Falling, large down days                 | Rising, low volatility                |
| Volatility index                         | Rising sharply                           | Low and stable                        |
| AUD/JPY (a popular gauge)                | Falling                                  | Rising                                |
| Government bond prices                   | Rising (yields falling)                  | Falling (yields rising)               |
| Gold                                     | Often rising                             | Mixed                                 |

AUD/JPY combines a risk currency with a safe-haven one, so it works as a quick gauge. No single reading settles the regime.

## Using It in Trading

| Situation                                      | Idea to test                                                          |
| ---------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Stocks and volatility signal risk-off          | Favour shorts in AUD/JPY or AUD/USD; avoid longs in risk currencies   |
| Mood turns risk-on                             | The reverse, and risk currencies often recover                        |
| Signals disagree                               | Smaller size or no trade                                              |
| Event days                                     | Regimes can flip within hours                                         |

### Example (no chart) — a short on a risk currency

Stocks have fallen for a week and the volatility index has jumped. AUD/USD rallies to **0.6700** into resistance. Plan: sell **0.6700**, stop **0.6730**, target **0.6620**: risk 30 pips, reward 80, **1 : 2.7** planned. If stocks reverse up and the volatility index falls back, the risk-off reason weakens; exit or tighten.

## Where It Fails

| Failure                                         | What it looks like                                          |
| ----------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| Yen or franc do not strengthen                  | Rates or domestic news dominate                             |
| Stocks and bonds fall together                  | Inflation or rate shocks (bonds are not a safe haven then)  |
| The dollar rallies and risk currencies still rise | Local drivers override the global mood                    |
| Fast reversals                                  | A regime read in the morning can be wrong by the afternoon  |

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake                                         | Fix                                                        |
| ----------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| Assuming JPY and CHF always strengthen in a sell-off | Check; it is a tendency                                |
| Mixing up pair direction                        | USD/JPY falls when the yen strengthens                     |
| Trading the regime without a stop               | Regimes change; use a stop                                 |
| Using one gauge                                 | Combine stocks, volatility and one or two currency gauges  |
| Ignoring event risk                             | Check the calendar                                         |

## Summary

| Idea             | Detail                                                                    |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Regimes          | Risk-on and risk-off describe shared moves across markets                 |
| Typical groups   | Stocks, AUD, NZD vs JPY, CHF, government bonds, gold                      |
| Mood gauges      | Stocks, volatility, AUD/JPY                                               |
| Limits           | Tendencies, not rules; they can flip quickly                              |
