## What Is Cross-Asset Momentum?

**Time-series momentum** is the idea that an asset which has been rising tends to keep rising for a while, and one that has been falling tends to keep falling. Academic work has documented it across stocks, bonds, commodities and currencies over long samples. **Cross-asset** momentum means looking at that trend in **several** markets and using it as context for the one you trade.

<Callout type="warning" title="Corrected and toned down">
  The original said momentum in equities, bonds and commodities creates "leading signals for forex". Trends in one
  market do not reliably lead another, and we found no source for a fixed lead time. What is documented is momentum
  **within** each market over long periods, with weak periods and sharp reversals. Here it is treated as a hypothesis
  to test, not an established edge for any pair.
</Callout>

## A Simple Test on Four Assets

The rule: each day, look at the return over the past **60 days**. If positive, hold a long position for the next day; if negative, hold a short. No costs.

![Synthetic daily data for four assets over 300 days with a 60-day momentum rule: the equity index gains 36% (buy-and-hold 35%), gold gains 25% (buy-and-hold 22%), the bond index returns −1% (buy-and-hold −2%) and the FX pair −2% (buy-and-hold +5%)](/images/intermarket/cross-asset-momentum/cross-asset-momentum-dark.png)

<Callout type="info" title="About this chart">
  The data is **synthetic**: a slow trend cycle was built into the equity index and gold, and the bond index and FX pair
  are close to random walks. It illustrates the method; it says nothing about real markets.
</Callout>

| Asset         | Momentum rule | Buy-and-hold | Reading                                                   |
| ------------- | ------------- | ------------ | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| Equity index  | +36%          | +35%         | Gains, but only about equal to holding the index          |
| Gold          | +25%          | +22%         | Slightly ahead of holding                                 |
| Bond index    | −1%           | −2%          | Nothing useful: no persistent trend to follow             |
| FX pair       | −2%           | +5%          | Worse than holding: moves were close to random            |

Three honest points from the table. Where trends persisted the rule **made money**, but it mostly matched buy-and-hold in rising markets. Where moves were noise it **gained nothing**. And this is **gross** of costs: every sign flip would pay spread and slippage.

## Using Momentum as Context for a Pair

| Idea                                              | How to use it                                                       |
| ------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Check the trend of related markets                | Stocks, yields, the dollar index, gold — do they agree with your pair's trend? |
| Agreement across markets                          | Raises confidence in a trade that follows the shared direction      |
| Disagreement                                      | Reduce size or wait; the trend may be ending or local               |
| Lookback choice                                   | Use the same horizon you trade, and test two (for example 20 and 60 days) |

### Example (no chart) — agreement as context

EUR/USD has risen for 60 days, the dollar index has fallen for 60 days and US 2-year yields have fallen. A pullback to **1.0880** holds. Plan: buy **1.0885**, stop **1.0860**, target **1.0950**: risk 25 pips, reward 65, **1 : 2.6** planned. If the dollar index turns up and closes above its 60-day high, the agreement is gone and the plan is off.

## Traps

| Trap                              | What goes wrong                                                          |
| --------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Costs                             | Frequent flips can turn a gross gain into a loss                         |
| Overfitting the lookback          | A window picked because it looked best in the past is likely to fail     |
| Regime changes                    | Momentum can reverse sharply after long runs                             |
| One market only                   | A rule that worked on equities may not work on FX                        |
| Testing on the data that built it | Test on a separate period                                                |

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake                                         | Fix                                                        |
| ----------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| Treating another market's trend as a signal     | Use it as context and require your own setup               |
| Ignoring costs in a test                        | Include spread and slippage                                |
| Choosing the lookback after seeing the results  | Fix it first; test neighbours (not just the best one)      |
| Trusting a short sample                         | Use as many market regimes as you can                      |
| Assuming momentum works in every market         | Test each separately                                       |

## Practice

1. For one pair you trade, compute the 20-day and 60-day trailing returns and note the sign each day for a year.
2. Count how often the sign matched the next day's direction. Is it better than a coin flip?
3. Repeat for a related market (stocks or the dollar index). Do the signs agree with your pair's?
4. Log it in your [trading journal](/en/tools/journal-template).
