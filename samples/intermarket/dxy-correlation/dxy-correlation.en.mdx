## What Is the Dollar Index?

The **US Dollar Index (DXY)** measures the dollar against a fixed basket of six currencies. Its weights have not changed since the euro's introduction in 1999:

| Currency        | Weight |
| --------------- | ------ |
| Euro (EUR)      | 57.6%  |
| Japanese yen    | 13.6%  |
| British pound   | 11.9%  |
| Canadian dollar | 9.1%   |
| Swedish krona   | 4.2%   |
| Swiss franc     | 3.6%   |

Two consequences follow straight from the table: the index is **mostly a euro-versus-dollar measure**, and currencies that aren't in it (AUD, NZD, NOK, most emerging-market currencies) are related to it only indirectly.

<Callout type="warning" title="Corrected and toned down">
  The original said DXY movements "predict" EUR/USD, GBP/USD and commodity currencies "with institutional precision".
  DXY and the pairs are largely **the same information**: the euro is over half the index, so EUR/USD and DXY are close
  to mirror images by construction, not one forecasting the other. Treat DXY as confirmation and context.
</Callout>

## How Pairs Relate to DXY

<Callout type="info" title="About this chart">
  The weights are the published DXY weights. The correlations are **synthetic** (120 days built to show the pattern);
  real values vary with the window and the period.
</Callout>

| Pair      | Dollar is… | Direction vs DXY | Synthetic correlation | Why                                              |
| --------- | ---------- | ---------------- | --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| EUR/USD   | quote      | Against          | −0.94                 | Euro is 57.6% of the basket                      |
| GBP/USD   | quote      | Against          | −0.70                 | Pound is 11.9%, with its own drivers             |
| AUD/USD   | quote      | Against          | −0.41                 | Not in the basket; also trades with risk and commodities |
| USD/JPY   | base       | With             | +0.32                 | Yen is 13.6%, but also a risk and rates currency |
| USD/CAD   | base       | With             | +0.60                 | CAD is 9.1%; oil is a separate driver            |
| USD/CHF   | base       | With             | +0.76                 | Franc is 3.6% but often moves with the euro      |

The pattern: **dollar as quote currency** (EUR/USD, GBP/USD, AUD/USD) → pair moves against DXY; **dollar as base currency** (USD/JPY, USD/CAD, USD/CHF) → moves with it. How tight the link is depends on how much of the basket the other currency is — and on whatever else drives that currency.

## Using DXY as Context

| Situation                                   | Reading                                                                 |
| ------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| DXY and EUR/USD moving in opposite directions | Normal; DXY adds little new information for EUR/USD                   |
| DXY rises, GBP/USD or AUD/USD **doesn't fall** | The pair has its own driver (UK data, commodities, risk); look for it |
| DXY breaks a level, your pair hasn't yet    | A pair move may follow — or the pair may be the one that's right        |
| USD/JPY ignores DXY                         | Rates and risk sentiment often dominate the yen                         |

### Example (no chart) — DXY as confirmation

DXY has broken above its previous day's high and EUR/USD is falling toward **1.0870**. Plan: sell a break of the day's low at **1.0860** only once EUR/USD itself confirms; stop **1.0885**, target **1.0820**: risk 25 pips, reward 40, **1 : 1.6** planned. If DXY falls back below its breakout level, the dollar-strength reason is gone and the plan is off.

## Limits

| Limit                                         | Detail                                                                   |
| --------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| The index is euro-heavy                       | DXY is a poor guide to currencies that aren't in it                      |
| Correlations change                           | See [the introduction](/en/intermarket/introduction); re-measure on your window |
| DXY is not tradable directly everywhere       | Futures (DX) exist; spot-forex brokers use their own indices or none     |
| Event days                                    | Pairs can diverge sharply on national data or central-bank news          |

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake                                           | Fix                                                        |
| ------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| Treating DXY as a forecast of EUR/USD             | They are close to the same information                     |
| Using DXY for AUD or NZD as if they were in it    | They aren't; expect looser links                           |
| Trading a DXY signal with no confirmation in the pair | Wait for the pair to show it                           |
| Forgetting the base/quote direction               | Dollar as quote → inverse; dollar as base → direct         |
| Ignoring news that moves one currency only        | Check the calendar                                         |

## Summary

| Idea             | Detail                                                                    |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| DXY              | Six-currency basket; euro 57.6%                                           |
| Direction        | Dollar as quote → pair moves against DXY; as base → with it               |
| Strength         | Follows the other currency's weight and its own drivers                   |
| Use              | Context and confirmation, not a forecast                                  |
