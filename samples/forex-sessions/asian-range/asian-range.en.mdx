## What Is the Asian Range?

The **Asian range** is the high and low price makes during the quiet hours before London opens. It's a **reference**: traders mark it, because London's first big move often tests one side of it.

In this site's convention the Asian range runs **6:00 AM to 12:00 PM BD** in US/UK summer (7:00 AM to 1:00 PM in winter) — the hours after Tokyo opens and before London.

<Callout type="warning" title="Corrected and toned down">
  The original put the range at 5:00 AM – 1:00 PM BD and said London targets it "approximately 75% of trading days".
  Tokyo opens at 6:00 AM BD all year, and we found no source for the 75% figure. London tests the Asian range **often**,
  but on some days it breaks out and keeps going, and on others it ignores it.
</Callout>

---

## How to Mark It

1. Before London opens (12:00 PM BD in summer), look at the 6:00 AM – 12:00 PM BD candles.
2. Mark the **highest high** and **lowest low**. Use the same timeframe every day (15m or 1H).
3. Note the range width in pips. Very narrow or very wide ranges behave differently (see below).
4. Note the daily bias before London opens.

| Outcome                   | What London does                                                                  | What you do                                                  |
| ------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **A · Sweep and reverse** | Drops below the low, then closes back inside and goes through the high            | Trade the reversal after a close back inside (and a CHOCH)   |
| **B · Break and hold**    | Closes above the high; the retest holds from above                                | Trade the break's direction on the retest                    |
| **C · Range holds**       | No close outside the range                                                        | No Asian-range trade; trade the edges small or stand aside   |

The same 20-pip range, the same Asian candles: only London's close decides which day it is. Don't decide before the close.

## Trading the Sweep

| Step | Rule                                                                   |
| ---- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1    | Mark the range and the daily bias before London                        |
| 2    | London takes out the high or low (a wick or a brief close beyond it)   |
| 3    | A candle closes **back inside** the range                              |
| 4    | A CHOCH on the 5m/15m confirms the turn                                |
| 5    | Enter on the retest or the FVG; stop beyond the sweep's extreme        |
| 6    | Targets: the other side of the range, then the previous day's high/low |

See [London Open Manipulation](/en/forex-sessions/london-open-manipulation) for a worked chart.

### Example (no chart) — outcome A on EUR/USD

Range **1.0852 – 1.0872**, bias bullish. At 1:30 PM BD an hourly candle dips to **1.0844** and the next one closes at **1.0862**, back inside. A 15m close above the last lower high confirms the CHOCH. Buy the retest at **1.0858**, stop **1.0841**, target **1.0895** (previous day's high): risk 17 pips, reward 37, **1 : 2.2** planned.

## Range Width

| Width       | What to expect                                              |
| ----------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| Very narrow | Often breaks with force — but direction is unknown          |
| Typical     | Sweep or break equally plausible; wait for closes           |
| Very wide   | Much of the day's move may already be done; targets are far |

The original's "wider range = bigger London move" isn't a reliable rule; measure it in your own journal.

## Daily Routine (BD time, summer)

| Time (BD)      | Action                                           |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| 6:00 AM        | Tokyo opens — range starts forming               |
| 12:00 PM       | Review: are the high and low clean? Mark them    |
| 1:00 PM        | London opens — watch, don't trade the first move |
| 1:00 – 3:00 PM | Sweep or break; wait for the close and a CHOCH   |
| 3:00 PM        | If neither side has closed outside, stand aside  |

Add one hour in winter.

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake                                      | Fix                                                  |
| -------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| Trading the first touch of the high/low      | Wait for the close                                   |
| Treating every break as a sweep              | Check whether the retest holds (outcome B)           |
| Moving the range after the fact              | Mark it before London and leave it                   |
| Using winter times in summer, or the reverse | London opens 1:00 PM BD in summer, 2:00 PM in winter |

## Practice

1. For 20 days, mark EUR/USD's Asian range (6:00 AM – 12:00 PM BD) before London.
2. Classify each day: sweep and reverse, break and hold, or range holds.
3. For sweep days, note whether a CHOCH appeared and what the retest entry would have been.
4. Log it in your [trading journal](/en/tools/journal-template).
