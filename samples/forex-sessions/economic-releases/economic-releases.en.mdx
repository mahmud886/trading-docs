## Why Release Times Matter

Scheduled data and central-bank decisions can move price more in a minute than in the previous hour, and spreads often widen around them. Knowing **when** each release lands in your own clock lets you decide ahead of time: trade it, avoid it, or just reduce size.

<Callout type="warning" title="Corrected">
  The original table put RBA at 7:30 AM BD, UK data at 1:00 PM BD and BoJ at "6:00 – 8:00 AM BD". Converted from local
  time, RBA is **10:30 AM BD** (Sydney winter) or 9:30 AM, UK data is **12:00 PM BD** in summer, and the Bank of Japan has
  no fixed time (usually between about 8:00 and 10:00 AM BD). We also removed "50 – 200 pips" style reaction sizes.
</Callout>

NFP is released on the first Friday of the month; US jobless claims every Thursday at the same 8:30 AM New York time.

<Callout type="info" title="Clock-change weeks">
  The US and UK/Europe change clocks on different weekends in March and in October/November, so for about two to three
  weeks a year the gap between them is one hour different. Check the calendar's own BD-time column in those weeks.
</Callout>

## Reading the Day

For a Bangladesh trader in summer, the day has **four** natural clusters:

| Cluster               | BD time (summer)    | Releases                              | Affects            |
| --------------------- | ------------------- | ------------------------------------- | ------------------ |
| Asian morning         | 5:50 – 10:30 AM     | Japan, Australia, China, RBA          | JPY, AUD, NZD      |
| London noon           | 12:00 PM            | UK data                               | GBP                |
| London–New York evening | 5:00 – 7:00 PM    | BoE, ECB, US data (6:30 PM)           | GBP, EUR, USD      |
| Late night            | 12:00 AM            | FOMC (on decision days)               | USD and everything |

The evening cluster is the busiest: BoE, ECB and US data can land within 90 minutes on certain days.

## Trading Around Releases

| Approach              | How                                                                 | Notes                                     |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| **Stand aside**       | No new entries 15–30 minutes before; close or reduce positions       | The simplest; recommended while learning  |
| **Trade the aftermath** | Wait for the news candle to close; trade a close outside its range with a defined stop | See [News Impact](/en/price-action/news-impact) |
| **Hold through**      | Smaller size, wider stop, accept possible slippage                  | Only with a plan and a loss limit you can afford |

During the release, spreads can widen and stop orders can fill at a worse price than set.

### Example (no chart) — a Thursday evening

At 5:00 PM BD the BoE decision moves GBP/USD; at 6:30 PM US jobless claims land on the same pair. A trader with a planned long from 3:00 PM (entry 1.2640, stop 1.2615, target 1.2700) decides in the morning: move the stop to break-even at 1.2660 before 4:45 PM or close at 4:45 PM, and take **no** new entries between 4:30 and 5:15 PM or between 6:15 and 6:50 PM. The rule is set when calm, not at the spike.

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake                                      | Fix                                                       |
| -------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| Using the same BD times all year             | UK/US events shift one hour in winter; Australia the other way |
| Entering a minute before a release           | Stand aside for at least 15 minutes                       |
| Trusting an old BD-time table                | Check the calendar's BD-time column each week             |
| Ignoring Australia's opposite clock change   | RBA and Australian data move a different way             |
