## What Are Session Liquidity Sweeps?

When a session ends, its **high and low** are left on the chart with stop orders resting just beyond them. The next session often trades to those levels — and sometimes reverses straight back. That reversal after taking the level is a **sweep**.

It's a way to read a day, not a law: price can also close beyond a level and keep going. That's a **breakout**, and the difference is what you do next.

## The Sweep Chain (BD time, summer)

| Session   | BD time            | Level it leaves            | What the next session may do          |
| --------- | ------------------ | -------------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| Asia      | 6:00 AM – 1:00 PM  | Asian high and low         | London tests one side                 |
| London    | 1:00 PM – 10:00 PM | London high and low        | New York tests one side               |
| New York  | 6:00 PM – 3:00 AM  | NY high and low            | Next day's Asia / London              |
| Prior day | —                  | Previous day's high / low  | Strongest reference levels            |

Add one hour to London and New York times in winter.

## Sweep or Breakout?

| Clue                           | Sweep                                       | Breakout                                     |
| ------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| Candle close                   | Back inside the old range                   | Beyond the level, and it holds                |
| Retest                         | Fails to hold beyond the level              | Holds from the other side                    |
| Structure                      | CHOCH back the other way                    | Trend continues                              |
| Context                        | Against the bias, or into a big level       | With the bias                                |

If the next candle after the touch doesn't close back inside, it's not a sweep yet.

---

## Rules

1. Mark every session's high and low before the next session opens.
2. Trade the **close back inside** and a CHOCH — not the touch.
3. Prefer sweeps that go **against** the higher-timeframe bias first, with the bias second target.
4. Stops go beyond the sweep's extreme; targets: the other side of the old range, then older levels.
5. Previous-day high/low are the most watched; a sweep of them matters more than a sweep of an Asian level, but they are not guaranteed to hold.

### Example (no chart) — a gold sweep of the previous day's high

Gold's previous-day high is **2,438**. At 1:40 PM BD London pushes to **2,441** and a 15m candle closes at **2,435**. A close below the last higher low **2,433** confirms. Sell the retest at **2,434**, stop **2,443**, target **2,410**: risk $9, reward $24, **1 : 2.7** planned.

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake                                    | Fix                                               |
| ------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------- |
| Fading every touch of a level              | Wait for the close back inside                    |
| Stop inside the sweep                      | Beyond the extreme                                |
| Forgetting New York can sweep London       | Mark London's high/low before 6:00 PM BD          |
| Using summer times in winter               | Add one hour to London and New York               |

## Practice

1. For 15 days, mark Asia's and London's highs and lows (BD time).
2. For each, note whether the next session swept, broke, or ignored it.
3. For sweeps, note the close back inside, the CHOCH and where the retest entry would have been.
4. Log it in your [trading journal](/en/tools/journal-template).
