## What Is London Open Manipulation?

The **Judas swing** is an ICT name for a first move at the London open that **runs one way and then fails**, before the day's real move goes the other way. A common version: London pushes through the Asian low, triggers sell stops, then reverses up.

It's a **pattern to recognise**, not a daily event. Some days London's first move is the real move.

<Callout type="warning" title="Toned down">
  The original called this "the DAILY manipulation pattern of London session" and said the false move "traps retail
  traders" while "smart money filled their longs". Whether a first move fails can only be seen **after** it fails, and
  who was on which side isn't visible on a chart. The rules below wait for that evidence.
</Callout>

## Judas Swing or Real Breakout?

| Clue                        | Judas swing                                  | Real breakout                              |
| --------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| Direction vs daily bias     | Against the bias                             | With the bias                              |
| After the break             | Closes back inside the Asian range           | Holds outside; retests from the other side |
| Structure                   | CHOCH back in the bias direction             | No CHOCH; lower-timeframe trend continues  |
| What you do                 | Trade the reversal after the CHOCH           | Trade the retest in the break's direction  |

If you can't tell, there's no trade.

## Rules

1. Mark the Asian range (6:00 AM – 12:00 PM BD in summer, 7:00 AM – 1:00 PM in winter) and the daily bias **before** London.
2. Don't enter during London's first move.
3. Require: a sweep of the Asian high/low **against** the bias → a close back inside → a CHOCH.
4. Enter on the retest (or an FVG left by the CHOCH); stop beyond the sweep's extreme.
5. Targets: the other side of the Asian range, then the previous day's high/low.

### Timing in BD time

| Time (BD, summer) | Typical                                    |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| 1:00 – 1:30 PM    | First move — watch, don't trade            |
| 1:30 – 2:30 PM    | Close back inside, CHOCH, retest           |
| 2:30 – 6:00 PM    | The day's move develops (or doesn't)       |

Add one hour in winter.

### Example (no chart) — a bearish Judas swing on GBP/USD

Daily bias bearish. Asian range **1.2720 – 1.2745**. At 1:10 PM BD London lifts GBP/USD to **1.2753**, then a 5m candle closes back at **1.2741**. At 1:30 PM a close below the last higher low **1.2736** confirms the CHOCH. Sell the retest at **1.2739**, stop **1.2756**, target the previous day's low **1.2700**: risk 17 pips, reward 39, **1 : 2.3** planned.

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake                                         | Fix                                                    |
| ----------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| Fading every first move                         | Only against the bias, and only after a close back inside |
| Entering on the sweep wick                      | Wait for the CHOCH                                     |
| Stop inside the sweep                           | Stop beyond the extreme                                |
| Using summer times in winter                    | London opens 2:00 PM BD in winter                      |

## Practice
