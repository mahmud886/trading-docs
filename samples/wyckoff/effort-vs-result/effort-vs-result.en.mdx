## The Third Law

Wyckoff's third law says that **effort** (volume) should lead to a matching **result** (price progress). When more volume than usual produces less progress than usual — or a big move happens on little volume — something is out of balance, and that is the **clue**.

<Callout type="warning" title="Toned down">
  The original said a divergence "= institutional manipulation" and "smart money at work". A mismatch tells you only
  that supply and demand are not behaving as they have been; it doesn't tell you who is doing it, and it isn't a signal
  to trade on its own. In spot forex, volume is **tick volume**, a weaker proxy than exchange volume.
</Callout>

Across the 20 ordinary bars, **spread rises about 1.4 points for every 1,000 of volume** (spread ≈ −0.65 + 0.0014 × volume). Two bars sit far outside the band:

| Bar                | Volume | Spread | What the usual line predicts       | Reading                                              |
| ------------------ | ------ | ------ | ---------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| **Ease of movement** (bar 6) | 900   | 3.0  | about 0.6                         | Price moved easily — little resistance on the way up |
| **Effort without result** (bar 11) | 3,500 | 1.0 | about 4.2                  | Heavy trading made almost no progress — the other side is absorbing |

After the absorbed bar, price fell more than 4 points within five bars. On this synthetic chart the mismatch was followed by a turn — in real markets it sometimes isn't.

## Two Directions of Mismatch

| Mismatch                         | Meaning                                              | Where it matters                                 |
| -------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| **High effort, low result**      | Absorption: one side is meeting the other's push     | Near highs (supply absorbing demand) or lows (demand absorbing supply) |
| **Low effort, high result**      | Ease of movement: little opposition                  | Early in a move; or an exhausted move that is easy to reverse |

The same high-effort bar means different things in different places: after a decline near a low it can be **stopping volume** (see [VSA](/en/wyckoff/volume-spread-analysis)); after a rally near a high it can be **distribution**.

## How to Use It

1. **Measure** volume and spread against recent averages (or fit a line as above).
2. **Locate** the bar: trend, range edge, climax area.
3. **Name the possibilities:** absorption of demand, absorption of supply, an easy move.
4. **Wait for the next bar(s)** to confirm: a close in the direction the mismatch implies, or a failure at the level.
5. **Plan with a stop** beyond the mismatch bar's extreme.

### Example (no chart) — an absorbed high

A stock rallies 50 → 61 in nine days on normal volume (1,500 – 2,000). On day 11, volume jumps to 3,500 but the bar's spread is only 1.0 and it closes at **61.0**, near the open. Day 12 closes lower at **60.4**. Plan: sell a close below the absorbed bar's low (**60.4**) with a stop above its high (**61.6**) and a target at the last pullback low **57.0**: risk 1.2, reward 3.4, **1 : 2.8** planned. If price closes above **61.6** on rising volume, the absorption idea is wrong.

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake                                      | Fix                                                    |
| -------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| Reading "high volume" without checking the spread | Always compare volume **and** spread                 |
| Treating the mismatch as a signal            | It's a clue; wait for confirmation                      |
| Ignoring location                            | Same bar, different meaning at a high vs a low          |
| Comparing different sessions or time frames  | Compare like with like                                  |
| No stop beyond the bar's extreme             | The mismatch bar's high/low is the natural invalidation |

## Practice

1. On three charts, mark the highest-volume bar of the last 40 bars; measure its spread against the average.
2. Find one low-volume, wide-spread bar. What came next?
3. For each mismatch bar, write the location (trend, edge, climax) and the possibility you'd name.
4. Log it in your [trading journal](/en/tools/journal-template).

## Summary

| Idea             | Detail                                                       |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Law              | Effort (volume) should match result (progress)               |
| Two mismatches   | High effort/low result: absorption · low effort/high result: ease |

| Limit            | A clue, not a signal; weaker with tick volume                |
