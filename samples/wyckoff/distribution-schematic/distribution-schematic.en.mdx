## What Is Distribution?

**Distribution** is a range at the end of an advance in which buying is gradually met by supply and demand weakens. It is the mirror image of [accumulation](/en/wyckoff/accumulation-schematic). Wyckoff described it with a sequence of **events** in five **phases (A – E)**. As with accumulation, the sequence is a **template**: real ranges skip events or fail.

The SMC version is in [SMC Distribution](/en/smc/distribution).

| Phase | Event                               | What happens                                               | Volume (idealised)                    |
| ----- | ----------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| **A** | **PSY** preliminary supply          | First sign of supply; the advance continues                | Volume rises                          |
|       | **BC** buying climax                | Wide-spread high (33.0)                                    | The highest volume of the advance     |
|       | **AR** automatic reaction           | Sharp drop; sets the range bottom (27.5)                   | High                                  |
|       | **ST** secondary test               | Retest of the high (32.8), lower than the BC               | Less than the BC                      |
| **B** | Building the range                  | Swings between 27.5 and 33                                 | Declining on tests of the high        |
| **C** | **UTAD**                            | Push above the range (34.4) that closes back inside        | Heavy: big effort, no progress        |
| **D** | **SOW** sign of weakness            | Close below support (26.0)                                 | Heavy                                 |
|       | **LPSY** last point of supply       | Quiet rally back to the broken support (27.6)              | Low                                   |
| **E** | Markdown                            | Price leaves the range downward                            | Rises with declines                   |

### The planned trade

Sell the **LPSY** at **27.4**, stop **29.2** (above the LPSY high), target **22.0** — the range width (33.0 − 27.5 = 5.5) projected down from the breakdown level. Risk **1.8**, reward **5.4**, **1 : 3.0** planned.

## With and Without an Upthrust

| Feature            | With a UTAD                             | Without a UTAD                              |
| ------------------ | --------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| Phase C            | A push above the range that fails       | A lower high that doesn't reach the range top |
| Entry              | The LPSY after the SOW                  | The LPSY after the SOW, or the lower high   |
| Stop               | Above the UTAD or LPSY high             | Above the lower high                        |

An **upthrust** is a pattern, not a verdict: a close back inside the range on heavy volume shows that the push above found no follow-through. A push above the range that **holds** with a rising close is a breakout, and the distribution story is wrong.

<Callout type="warning" title="Toned down">
  The original described distribution as institutions "unloading onto the crowd". Price and volume can't show who is on
  which side. Describe what's visible: **heavy volume with no upside progress near a high**.
</Callout>

## Trading It

1. **Identify the prior advance** and the buying climax (the highest volume near the high).
2. **Mark the range:** resistance (BC high) and support (AR low).
3. **Watch the tests of the high** — less volume, lower highs — and the UTAD.
4. **Enter** on the LPSY after a SOW (more confirmed), or on a failed UTAD (aggressive).
5. **Stop** above the LPSY or UTAD high.
6. **Target:** the range width projected down, with partial targets at earlier lows.

### Example (no chart) — a distribution without a UTAD

A stock rallies from 40 to 62, prints a climax at **63.0** on 3.5× volume, then ranges between **63.0 and 57.0**. The next tests of the top stop short at **62.2** and **61.4** on lighter volume. Price then closes below **57.0** on rising volume, and a quiet rally back reaches **57.4** and fails. Sell **57.2**, stop **58.8**, target **51.0** (range width 6.0 projected down): risk 1.6, reward 6.2, **1 : 3.9** planned. A close back above **58.8** on heavy volume ends the idea.

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake                                       | Fix                                                      |
| --------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| Shorting every high                           | Need a climax, lower highs on less volume, and a break   |
| Calling any push above a range an upthrust    | An upthrust closes back inside; a holding push is a breakout |
| Shorting the SOW candle itself                | Wait for the LPSY or a retest                            |
| Stop below the LPSY                           | The stop goes above the supply point                     |
| Ignoring the uptrend                          | A range in a strong uptrend may be a pause, not a top    |

## Practice

1. On three daily charts at highs, mark the climax, the range, and any test of the high.
2. Note the volume at the climax, the tests and the breakdown.
3. Decide whether each looks like distribution with a UTAD, without, or neither.
4. Log it in your [trading journal](/en/tools/journal-template).

## Summary

| Idea           | Detail                                                                    |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Phases         | A stop the rise · B build the range · C test (UTAD) · D sign of weakness · E markdown |
| Key evidence   | Heavy volume without upside progress; lower highs on less volume          |
| Entry          | LPSY after the SOW; stop above it; target from the range width            |
| Failure        | A heavy-volume close back above the range top                             |
