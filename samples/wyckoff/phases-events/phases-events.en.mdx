## Phase First, Events Second

The most common Wyckoff mistake is hunting for event labels (SC, ST, spring …) before knowing **what phase the market is in**. Events are clues inside a phase. Real markets skip events, repeat them and arrive in a different order — but the **phase question** is almost always answerable: *is the decline/advance stopping, is the range being built, is it being tested, is the break confirmed, is the new trend running?*

| Phase | Purpose                           | Question to ask                      | Typical events (accumulation)      | Evidence you want                          |
| ----- | --------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| **A** | Stop the previous trend           | Is the decline stopping?             | PS, SC, AR, ST                     | A climax on the heaviest volume, then a test on less |
| **B** | Build the range (the "cause")     | Is supply drying up?                 | Swings between support and resistance | Volume fading on tests of the low       |
| **C** | Test the opposite side            | Does a test hold?                    | Spring, or a higher-low test       | A quick recovery on low supply             |
| **D** | Confirm the new direction         | Is demand taking over?               | SOS, then LPS                      | A close beyond the range on heavy volume, then a quiet pullback |
| **E** | The new trend                     | Is the trend running?                | Higher highs, shallow pullbacks    | Volume rises with rallies                  |

## Event Glossary — Both Sides

| Accumulation (after a decline) | Distribution (after an advance)  | Meaning                                                        |
| ------------------------------ | -------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **PS** preliminary support     | **PSY** preliminary supply       | First sign that the trend is being resisted                    |
| **SC** selling climax          | **BC** buying climax             | The extreme of the trend on the heaviest volume                |
| **AR** automatic rally         | **AR** automatic reaction        | The sharp reversal that defines the other side of the range    |
| **ST** secondary test          | **ST** secondary test            | A retest of the climax on less volume                          |
| **Spring**                     | **UT / UTAD** upthrust           | A push beyond the range that closes back inside                |
| **Test**                       | **Test**                         | A quiet retest of the spring/upthrust area                     |
| **SOS** sign of strength       | **SOW** sign of weakness         | A close beyond the range on heavy volume                       |
| **LPS** last point of support  | **LPSY** last point of supply    | A quiet pullback to the broken range edge                      |
| **BU / backup**                | **BU / backup**                  | A pullback that tests the broken edge from the other side      |

Not every range shows all of them. A range with only an SC, a test and an SOS can still be an accumulation.

## Identifying the Phase

1. **What came before?** A decline (look for accumulation) or an advance (look for distribution).
2. **Is there a climax?** The heaviest volume near the extreme, followed by a sharp reversal (AR).
3. **Has the range held?** Support (or resistance) not broken on heavy volume.
4. **Is there a test?** A move to the edge on less volume that reverses.
5. **Is there a break?** A close beyond the opposite edge on heavy volume — the sign of strength or weakness.

If you can answer 1, 2 and 3, you're probably in phase B; with 4 you're in C; with 5 you're in D. If the range's edge breaks on heavy volume **against** your idea, you were wrong — drop the idea.

### Example (no chart) — the example on the chart

Price falls from 50 to 40.6; the SC at **39.4** has the heaviest volume, the AR rallies to **45.8**, the ST holds at **40.0** on less volume. In phase B, price swings between 41.8 and 45.7 with fading volume. In phase C there's **no spring**: a quiet low at **41.9** (a higher low) on very low volume. Phase D: a close at **46.9** on 2.5× volume (SOS), then a quiet pullback to **44.6** (LPS). Buy **46.0**, stop **44.2**, target **52.2** (range width 6.4 projected up): risk 1.8, reward 6.2, **1 : 3.4** planned.

## What to Do in Each Phase

| Phase | Action                                                                         |
| ----- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| A     | Watch. Mark the climax, the range top and the first test. No trade                 |
| B     | Wait. Mark the range edges; note volume on each test                           |
| C     | Prepare. Aggressive entries here (spring/test) need a tight stop                |
| D     | Enter on the LPS/backup after the sign of strength, with a stop below it       |
| E     | Manage. Trail behind pullback lows; don't add into a stretched move            |

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake                                         | Fix                                                  |
| ----------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| Naming events before the phase                  | Answer the five-step list first                      |
| Requiring every event                           | Phase B, C and D can look very different             |
| Treating phase B as a trade                     | It's a waiting phase; most of the range is noise     |
| Entering at the first SOS candle                | Wait for the pullback (LPS/backup) unless you accept more risk |
| Not defining invalidation                       | A heavy-volume close through the edge cancels the idea |

## Practice

1. Take three daily charts at lows (or highs) and write the phase of each, with the evidence for your answer.
2. For each, list the events you can identify and the ones that are missing.
3. State the level and volume condition that would prove you wrong.
4. Log it in your [trading journal](/en/tools/journal-template).

| Phases                  | A stop · B build · C test · D confirm · E trend                    |
