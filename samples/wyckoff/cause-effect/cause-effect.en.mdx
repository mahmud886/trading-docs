## The Second Law

Wyckoff's law of **cause and effect** says that a move (the **effect**) is related to the preparation before it (the **cause**). In practice the cause is a **range**: the longer and wider it is, the more positions were built or sold there, and the more fuel the next trend may have.

This is a **rule of thumb**, not an equation. A large range can produce a small move, and many ranges end in failure rather than a measured move.

<Callout type="warning" title="Toned down">
  The original said "the size of the cause determines the size of the effect" and "accumulation range → markup".
  "Determines" is too strong: a range is a rough guide to **how far a move could go**, not a guarantee of how far it
  will. Every objective below is a *first target*, to be planned with a stop.
</Callout>

## Measuring the Cause

1. **Define the range** by its clear high and low (not a single outlier wick).
2. **Height:** high − low.
3. **Width:** count the bars (or days) between the first test of the edges and the breakout.
4. **Measured move:** *objective = range high + range height* (for a breakout up); *range low − range height* (down). This is a minimum objective; extended objectives use 1.5× or 2× as stretch targets.

### The point-and-figure count (simplified)

Wyckoff traders often use a point-and-figure chart because it counts **columns** in the range rather than time:

> objective = count line ± (number of columns in the range × box size × reversal amount)

For a chart with **box size 0.5** and a **3-box reversal**, a range of **8 columns** gives 8 × 0.5 × 3 = **12 points**. Added to a count line at the range's middle (here 50.5), the objective would be about **62.5**. The method is a **heuristic** — treat it as a rough target, not a forecast.

### Example (no chart) — a planned breakout

A stock ranges between **38.1 and 43.9** (height 5.8) for 28 days after a decline. A close above **43.9** on rising volume, and a pullback that holds near **44.0**: buy **44.2**, stop **42.6** (below the pullback low), first objective **49.7** (1× measured move, 43.9 + 5.8), stretch **52.6** (1.5×): risk 1.6, reward to 1× **5.5**, **1 : 3.4** planned. If price closes back below **43.0** on heavy volume, the breakout has failed.

## When the Idea Fails

| Situation                                      | What it means                                           |
| ---------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| A long range breaks the *other* way            | The range was distribution, not accumulation            |
| A wide range with no volume clues              | Just a range; no reason to expect a measured move       |
| A breakout reaches 0.5× then fails             | Many don't reach the full objective; scale out          |
| A range inside a strong opposing trend         | Often a pause, with the trend resuming                  |

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake                                      | Fix                                                    |
| -------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| Treating the objective as a promise          | It's a first target; use a stop and scale out          |
| Measuring from an outlier wick               | Use the clear boundaries of the range                  |
| Using the same multiple for every market     | Test your own market and time frame                    |
| Ignoring volume and phase                    | The range must show the evidence of [accumulation](/en/wyckoff/accumulation-schematic) or [distribution](/en/wyckoff/distribution-schematic) |
| Entering before the breakout is confirmed    | Wait for the close and the pullback                    |

## Practice

1. On five daily charts, measure the height and width of a range that was followed by a breakout.
2. Compute the 1× measured move and note whether, and when, it was reached.
3. Compare the follow-through with the range's width and height.
4. Log it in your [trading journal](/en/tools/journal-template).

## Summary

| Idea                   | Detail                                                             |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Law                    | A move is related to the preparation (the range) before it         |
| Cause                  | Range height × width                                               |
| Objective              | Range high/low ± height (1×); extended 1.5–2×; or a point-and-figure count |
| Caution                | A rule of thumb; ranges fail; always plan with a stop              |
