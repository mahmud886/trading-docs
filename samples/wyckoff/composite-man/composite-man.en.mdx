## What Is the Composite Man?

Wyckoff suggested a mental trick: when you study a chart, **imagine all the large participants as one person** — the **Composite Man** — who plans a campaign. That person buys quietly, lifts price, sells into the excitement, and lets price fall.

It is a **model for asking questions**: *If one big participant needed to build a large position without moving price, what would that look like on the chart? What would selling it look like?*

<Callout type="warning" title="A tool, not a theory of manipulation">
  The original called the Composite Man "smart money" that "manipulates" the market. Wyckoff's own framing was a way
  of thinking; no single actor controls a market, and positions can't be seen from a chart. Use the idea to organise
  what price and volume **can** show — never to claim to know who is doing what.
</Callout>

## The Campaign, Stage by Stage

<Callout type="info" title="About this chart">
  The price and volume are **synthetic and idealised**. The inventory line is a **model** — it can't be observed in
  real markets.
</Callout>

| Stage (the model)   | What the imagined participant does            | What you could observe (idealised)                                |
| ------------------- | --------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Accumulate**      | Buys over time without pushing price up       | A range after a decline; heavy volume, little price progress       |
| **Mark up**         | Lets price rise with a full position          | Rallies on rising volume; shallow pullbacks on lighter volume      |
| **Distribute**      | Sells over time without pushing price down    | A range after a rally; heavy volume, little progress               |
| **Mark down**       | Has nothing left to support price             | Declines with weak rallies                                         |

In the chart, price moves only **~5 points** during each 22-day range even though the imagined inventory changes by 100% — while during the markup price moves **over 14 points** with the inventory flat. That mismatch between **effort** (volume and inventory) and **result** (price progress) is what Wyckoff asked you to look for. See [Effort vs Result](/en/wyckoff/effort-vs-result).

## Using the Idea

1. **Describe, don't accuse.** "Heavy volume with little progress near a low is consistent with buying being absorbed" — not "they are buying".
2. **Ask what would prove you wrong.** If the range's low breaks on heavy volume and doesn't recover, the accumulation story fails.
3. **Match the phase to evidence**: [Accumulation](/en/wyckoff/accumulation-schematic) needs a prior decline and signs that selling is exhausted; [Distribution](/en/wyckoff/distribution-schematic) needs a prior rally and signs that buying is exhausted.
4. **Plan with a stop**, because the model can be wrong.

### Example (no chart) — reading a range

A stock falls from 60 to 38 over two months, then moves between **36.5 and 41.5** for 25 sessions. Volume in the range averages 1,900 versus 1,000 during the fall's early part, but price makes no new low beyond 36.2. The story fitting this is "selling is being absorbed". Plan: wait for a close above **41.5** on rising volume and a quiet pullback; buy **41.3**, stop **39.8**, target **47.0**: risk 1.5, reward 5.7, **1 : 3.8** planned. If price closes below **36.0** on heavy volume, the story is dropped.

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake                                           | Fix                                                    |
| ------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| Believing a single hidden operator exists         | It's a model for asking questions                      |
| "They are accumulating" with no evidence          | State the evidence: volume vs progress, prior decline  |
| No invalidation level                             | State what would drop the story                        |
| Seeing a Composite Man in every sideways move     | Needs the preceding trend and the volume behaviour     |

## Practice

1. Pick five ranges on daily charts. For each, write one sentence: "if a large participant were X, I'd expect Y", and check Y.
2. Note the volume inside the range compared with the trend before it.
3. Write the level that would drop your story.
4. Log it in your [trading journal](/en/tools/journal-template).

## Summary

| Idea             | Detail                                                       |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| What it is       | A model: one imagined big participant's campaign             |
| What you see     | Ranges where effort (volume) and result (progress) don't match |
| How to use       | Describe, ask what proves you wrong, plan with a stop        |
| What it isn't    | Proof that someone is manipulating the market                |
