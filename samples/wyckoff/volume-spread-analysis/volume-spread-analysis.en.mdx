## What Is VSA?

**Volume spread analysis** reads three things on every bar together:

| Element       | What it is                                          | How to measure it                       |
| ------------- | --------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| **Volume**    | The effort — how much traded                        | Compare with the recent average         |
| **Spread**    | The result — the bar's high-to-low range            | Compare with the recent average spread  |
| **Close**     | Who finished the bar in control                     | Close position in the bar (0 = low, 1 = high) |

The method grew from Wyckoff's [effort versus result](/en/wyckoff/effort-vs-result) idea and was popularised by Tom Williams. Its premise: *when effort (volume) and result (spread) disagree, one side is being overwhelmed.*

<Callout type="warning" title="Toned down">
  The original lessons in this series assumed volume shows "smart money". Volume tells you how much traded, not by
  whom. In spot forex, "volume" is **tick volume** (the number of price updates), a weaker proxy than the exchange
  volume VSA was built for. Use VSA mainly on stocks, futures and indices, and treat forex signals with more caution.
</Callout>

| --------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Stopping volume**   | Volume 2.1× average, wide spread, close in the upper 68% after a decline | Selling is being absorbed — the decline may be ending |
| **Test**              | Volume 0.4× average, narrow spread, a higher low           | Little supply left near the climax low                     |
| **No demand**         | Up bar, narrow spread, volume 0.4× average and below the two bars before | The rally lacks buying interest; a pause or pullback is more likely |
| **No supply**         | Down bar, narrow spread, volume 0.4× average and below the two bars before | The pullback lacks selling interest; an upward resumption is more likely |

## How to Read a Bar

1. **Trend and location:** where is the bar — after a decline, at a high, at a range edge? The same bar means different things in different places.
2. **Volume vs average:** high (≥ ~1.5×), average, or low (≤ ~0.6×)?
3. **Spread vs average:** wide, average or narrow?
4. **Close position:** near the high, mid or near the low?
5. **Compare with the neighbours:** volume against the two previous bars; spread against the last few.

| Pattern                                  | Reading (in context)                                           |
| ---------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| High volume, wide spread, closes near high | Strong buying (after a decline: possible stopping action)    |
| High volume, wide spread, closes near low  | Strong selling (after a rally: possible climax)              |
| High volume, narrow spread               | Effort without result: the other side is absorbing            |
| Low volume, narrow spread                | Lack of interest: a test or a pause                           |
| Up bar, narrow spread, low volume        | No demand                                                     |
| Down bar, narrow spread, low volume      | No supply                                                     |

### Example (no chart) — a stopping-volume long

After a six-bar decline from 105 to 96, a bar with 2.1× average volume and a 4.4-point spread closes at **95.0**, 68% up from its low of **92.0**. Two bars later a quiet test holds at **95.3** on 0.4× average volume. Plan: buy a close above the test bar's high at **96.4**, stop **91.8** (below the climax low), target **103.0**: risk 4.6, reward 6.6, **1 : 1.4** planned — modest, so many traders scale in or wait for a no-supply pullback to improve the entry. If price closes below **92.0** on heavy volume, the signal has failed.

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake                                        | Fix                                                    |
| ---------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| Reading a bar out of context                   | Note the trend and location first                      |
| Trusting forex tick volume like exchange volume | Use VSA mainly where volume is real; be cautious in spot forex |
| Treating one signal as a trade                 | Need confirmation (a follow-through bar, a break)      |
| Comparing volume across very different sessions | Compare against the same-session average               |
| No stop                                        | Place it beyond the climax/test extreme                |

## Practice

1. On three daily charts of a liquid stock or index, mark the highest-volume bar of each decline and rally.
2. Measure volume and spread against the 20-bar averages and note the close position.
3. Find one test (low volume, narrow spread) near each climax and note what price did next.
4. Log it in your [trading journal](/en/tools/journal-template).
