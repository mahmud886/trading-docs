## What Is a Supply or Demand Zone?

A **demand zone** is a price area where buying clearly beat selling: price paused in a small **base**, then rallied away fast. A **supply zone** is the opposite: a base, then a fast drop.

The idea is that large orders were only partly filled in the base. When price returns, the remaining orders may meet it again and cause a reaction.

<Callout type="info" title="Clarified: 'unfilled orders'">
  The original said unfilled institutional orders "remain" in the zone, so "price returns to fill them". That's the
  model's explanation, and it can't be checked from a chart. What you can check is the footprint: a tight base and a
  fast departure. Treat the zone as a likely reaction area, not a guaranteed one.
</Callout>

---

## The Four Patterns

| Pattern   | Before the base | After the base | Zone     | Type          |
| --------- | --------------- | -------------- | -------- | ------------- |
| **DBR**   | Drop            | Rally          | Demand   | Reversal      |
| **RBR**   | Rally           | Rally          | Demand   | Continuation  |
| **RBD**   | Rally           | Drop           | Supply   | Reversal      |
| **DBD**   | Drop            | Drop           | Supply   | Continuation  |

A **base** is one to a few small candles (bodies no more than about half their range). The **leg-out** is one or more large-bodied candles leaving the base.

| Line          | Demand zone                    | Supply zone                    |
| ------------- | ------------------------------ | ------------------------------ |
| **Proximal**  | Highest **body** in the base   | Lowest **body** in the base    |
| **Distal**    | Lowest **wick** in the base    | Highest **wick** in the base   |

In the chart:

- **Supply (RBD):** a rally, two base candles, then a fast drop. Proximal **1.0963**, distal **1.0975**.
- **Demand (DBR):** a drop, two base candles, then a fast rally. Proximal **1.0891**, distal **1.0880**.

## The Trade in the Chart

Price rallies from demand, then comes back for the first time. Buy at the proximal line; target the next opposing zone.

| Level   | Price  | Note                                 |
| ------- | ------ | ------------------------------------ |
| Entry   | 1.0891 | Demand proximal, first return        |
| Stop    | 1.0875 | 5 pips below the 1.0880 distal       |
| Target  | 1.0963 | Supply proximal (the opposing zone)  |
| **R:R** | **1 : 4.5** | Risk 16 pips, reward 72 pips    |

## Zones vs Support/Resistance vs Order Blocks

| Tool                    | Built from                               | Width                 |
| ----------------------- | ---------------------------------------- | --------------------- |
| Support / resistance    | Repeated turning points at a price       | A line or thin band   |
| Supply / demand zone    | The base before a fast move              | The whole base        |
| [Order block](/en/smc/order-blocks) | The last opposite candle before displacement | One candle    |

An order block is usually the last candle of a base, so it sits **inside** the S&D zone. Many traders use the zone for context and the OB inside it for a tighter entry.

## Zone Quality

| Factor           | Stronger                          | Weaker                           |
| ---------------- | --------------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| Leg-out          | Fast, large candles, leaves an FVG | Slow, overlapping candles       |
| Time in the base | 1–3 candles                       | Many candles                     |
| Freshness        | Not yet revisited                 | Revisited several times          |
| Result of leg-out | Broke structure                  | Stayed inside the range          |
| Location         | Demand in discount, supply in premium | The opposite                 |

<Callout type="info" title="Clarified: volume and return speed">
  The original also listed "low volume in the zone" and a "slow approach" back to it as signs of strength. Both are
  commonly taught, but spot forex has no central volume, and evidence on approach speed is mixed. Use them as minor
  factors at most. [Zone Strength Scoring](/en/smc/zone-strength-scoring) turns the table into a score.
</Callout>

## Trading It, Step by Step

1. **Find the move:** a fast leg on your trading timeframe, ideally one that broke structure.
2. **Find the base** just before it and draw the proximal and distal lines.
3. **Check the context:** the higher-timeframe trend, and premium/discount.
4. **Wait** for the first return to the zone.
5. **Enter** at the proximal line (or wait for a lower-timeframe CHOCH inside the zone).
6. **Stop** a few pips beyond the distal line; **target** the next opposing zone or a liquidity level.

<Callout type="warning" title="When a zone is finished">
  A **close beyond the distal line** invalidates the zone. Each revisit also uses up some of it; see
  [Fresh vs Tested Zones](/en/smc/fresh-vs-tested).
</Callout>

### Second example (no chart) — Gold, supply (RBD)

Gold rallies to a 4H base at **2,412 – 2,420** (proximal 2,412, distal 2,420), then drops $35. On the first return, sell **2,412**, stop **2,423**, target the demand zone proximal at **2,370**: risk $11, reward $42, **R:R 1 : 3.8** (planned).

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake                                  | Fix                                                  |
| ---------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| Drawing a zone without a fast leg-out    | No fast move, no zone                                |
| Drawing the zone across many candles     | Only the base; keep it tight                         |
| Trading a zone that's been revisited many times | Prefer the first return                       |
| Stop exactly on the distal line          | Add a buffer for spread and wicks                    |
| Ignoring higher-timeframe direction      | Trade demand in uptrends, supply in downtrends first |

## Practice

1. On 10 charts, mark every DBR, RBR, RBD and DBD you can find.
2. For each zone, record whether the first return reacted, and by how many pips.
3. Note which quality factors the zones that worked had in common.
4. Log it in your [trading journal](/en/tools/journal-template).

## Summary

| ----------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| **Zone**    | The base before a fast move                         |
| **Patterns** | DBR, RBR (demand); RBD, DBD (supply)               |
| **Lines**   | Proximal = nearest body; distal = farthest wick     |
