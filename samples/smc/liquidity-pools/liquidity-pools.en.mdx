## What Is Liquidity?

In SMC, **liquidity** means clusters of **resting orders**, mostly stop-losses and breakout entries, at predictable prices. Large traders need someone to trade against: a big buy order fills most easily where many sell orders sit, and vice versa. So price tends to move **from one pool of resting orders to the next**.

| Pool                       | Where                 | What sits there                                      |
| -------------------------- | --------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| **Buy-side (BSL)**         | Above swing highs     | Shorts' stop-losses (buy stops), breakout buy orders |
| **Sell-side (SSL)**        | Below swing lows      | Longs' stop-losses (sell stops), breakdown sell orders |

<Callout type="info" title="A model, not a guarantee">
  The original said price is "attracted to liquidity like a magnet" and that liquidity shows "where price will go
  next". Pools are likely targets, not certainties. Use them to plan targets and to avoid putting your own stop in the
  obvious place.
</Callout>

## Reading Pools on a Chart

![Liquidity pools: buy-side above swing highs, sell-side below swing lows, a sweep of 1.0925, a run through 1.0868 and 1.0862, and 1.0822 still resting](/images/smc/liquidity-pools/liquidity-pools-map-dark.png)

<Callout type="info" title="About this chart">
  The candles are **synthetic**, built to show the concept. They are not real prices.
</Callout>

1. Every swing high has **buy-side** liquidity above it; every swing low has **sell-side** below it.
2. In the uptrend, each new high takes the buy-side above the last one. That's normal trend behaviour.
3. At hour 28, price runs above 1.0925 to 1.0930 and **closes back below**. That's a **sweep**: stops taken, move rejected.
4. The drop then **runs** the sell-side under 1.0900, 1.0868 and 1.0862 without stopping.
5. The sell-side under **1.0822** is still resting: the next obvious target.

## Sweep vs Run

| Type      | What happens                                   | What it suggests                    |
| --------- | ---------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| **Sweep** | Price trades through the level and **closes back** inside | The move was for the stops; possible reversal |
| **Run**   | Price **closes beyond** and keeps going        | The move is real; continuation      |

You only know which one it was after the candle closes. See [Liquidity Sweep](/en/ict/liquidity-sweep).

## Internal vs External Liquidity

| Type          | What it is                                       | Examples                    |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------- |
| **External**  | The range's swing highs and lows                 | BSL above, SSL below        |
| **Internal**  | Inefficiencies inside the range                  | FVGs, order blocks          |

A common rule of thumb: price alternates between the two, running external liquidity, retracing into internal liquidity, then running the next external pool. See [Internal & External Liquidity](/en/smc/internal-external-liquidity).

## Using Liquidity in Your Own Trading

| Use                       | How                                                        |
| ------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Targets**               | The next resting pool in your direction                    |
| **Entries**               | After a sweep and a CHOCH, not before                      |
| **Your own stop**         | Not exactly at the obvious level: a little beyond, or beyond the sweep that already happened |

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake                              | Fix                                                     |
| ------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| Fading every break of a high         | In a trend, highs are taken and held (runs)             |
| Calling a sweep before the close     | Wait for the candle to close back inside                |
| Marking every tiny swing             | Mark obvious levels on your timeframe                   |
| Stop right at the obvious level      | That's where the pool is                                |

## Practice

1. On a 1H chart, mark the obvious BSL and SSL for one week.
2. For each one price reached, note whether it was a sweep or a run.
3. Record what price did next. Log it in your [trading journal](/en/tools/journal-template).
