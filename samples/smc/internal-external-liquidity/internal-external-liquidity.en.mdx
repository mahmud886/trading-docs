## Two Kinds of Liquidity

| Type         | Where                                  | Examples                            | Use            |
| ------------ | -------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- | -------------- |
| **External** | Beyond the range's swing highs and lows | Swing highs/lows, equal highs/lows, session highs/lows | **Targets** |
| **Internal** | Inside the range                       | FVGs, order blocks, breakers        | **Entries** and pauses |

External liquidity is where stops rest. Internal liquidity is where price left inefficiencies on its way through the range.

---

## The Cycle

A common way to read price is as a loop:

1. Price **takes external liquidity** (a swing high or low).
2. It **retraces into internal liquidity** (an FVG or OB inside the range).
3. From there it heads to the **next external pool**.

## Trading With It

| Step                       | What to do                                                     |
| -------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Target**                 | The next untouched external pool in your direction             |
| **Wait**                   | For the opposite external pool to be taken (a sweep)           |
| **Entry**                  | At internal liquidity after that sweep: an FVG or OB from the reversal |
| **Stop**                   | Beyond the sweep                                               |
| **Bullish gaps on the way down?** | In a downtrend, old bullish FVGs are places price passes through or pauses at, not buys on their own |

<Callout type="info" title="Clarified: the original's example">
  The original's step 3 read "price reacts from FVG bullishly? No → reversal", which isn't a rule. A simpler way to say
  it: if price fills an internal gap and **doesn't** react in the old direction, that's a sign the next external pool
  on the other side is the target.
</Callout>

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake                                | Fix                                                   |
| -------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| Targeting internal levels              | Targets are external; internal levels are where price pauses |
| Entering at internal liquidity before external is taken | Wait for the sweep first                  |
| Treating every old gap as support      | Old gaps against the new direction often get run through |
| Mixing timeframes                      | Define "range" on one timeframe                       |

---

## Practice

1. On 1H charts, mark the range's external highs and lows and every FVG/OB inside.
2. After each external sweep, record which internal level price went to next, and whether it reached the opposite external pool.
3. Log it in your [trading journal](/en/tools/journal-template).
