## What Are Kill Zones?

Kill zones are **time windows** in the trading day when the big sessions open and trading volume rises. More volume means larger, cleaner moves, so SMC setups (sweeps, OB and FVG entries) tend to work better in these windows than in quiet hours.

<Callout type="info" title="Clarified: 'success rate'">
  The original said setups have "significantly higher success rates" in kill zones. That's a widely held view, and it
  fits how volume moves through the day, but no figure supports it here. Test it on your own trades: log the time of
  each one and compare.
</Callout>

## The Windows in Bangladesh Time

The windows are fixed in **New York time**. New York changes its clocks twice a year and Bangladesh doesn't, so the BD times shift by one hour.

| Window           | New York time    | BD — US summer (mid-Mar → early Nov) | BD — US winter (early Nov → mid-Mar) |
| ---------------- | ---------------- | ------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------ |
| Asian range      | 8 PM – 2 AM      | 6:00 AM – 12:00 PM                   | 7:00 AM – 1:00 PM                    |
| **London KZ**    | 2:00 – 5:00 AM   | **12:00 – 3:00 PM**                  | **1:00 – 4:00 PM**                   |
| **NY AM KZ**     | 7:00 – 10:00 AM  | **5:00 – 8:00 PM**                   | **6:00 – 9:00 PM**                   |
| **London close** | 10:00 AM – 12:00 PM | **8:00 – 10:00 PM**               | **9:00 – 11:00 PM**                  |
| NY lunch (avoid) | 12:00 – 2:00 PM  | 10:00 PM – 12:00 AM                  | 11:00 PM – 1:00 AM                   |

<Callout type="warning" title="Corrected: EST">
  The original labelled these times "EST". EST is only New York's **winter** time. The windows follow New York's clock
  all year, so in summer (EDT) they're the same New York times but one hour earlier in Bangladesh. Use the column that
  matches the date.
</Callout>

## One Day Through the Kill Zones

![One EUR/USD day in Bangladesh time: Asian range 1.0850–1.0870, London sweeps the Asian low, NY AM runs to the previous day's high, London close pulls back](/images/smc/kill-zones/smc-kill-zones-day-dark.png)

<Callout type="info" title="About this chart">

  follow it.

1. **Asian range (6 AM – 12 PM):** a narrow range, **1.0850 – 1.0870**. Mark its high and low.
2. **London KZ (12 – 3 PM):** price sweeps the Asian low to **1.0842**, then a strong candle displaces up. That sets the day's direction.
3. **Between sessions (3 – 5 PM):** a pullback, no new direction.
4. **NY AM KZ (5 – 8 PM):** the move continues to the **previous day's high (1.0900)**, the buy-side liquidity.
5. **London close (8 – 10 PM):** profit-taking pulls price back. After it, the range narrows for the night.

## What Usually Happens in Each Window

| Window          | Typical behaviour                                          | SMC use                                        |
| --------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| **Asian range** | Narrow; builds liquidity above and below                   | Preparation: mark levels, don't trade the middle |
| **London**      | Often sweeps one side of the Asian range, then trends      | Sweep → CHOCH → OB/FVG entry                   |
| **NY AM**       | Continues London's move, or reverses it if London reached a target | OB/FVG entries with the bias; PDH/PDL sweeps |
| **London close** | Profit-taking; the day's move often stalls or pulls back  | Manage or close trades; counter-trend scalps only if experienced |
| **NY lunch**    | Thin, choppy                                               | Avoid                                          |

## Planning Your Day (BD, US summer)

| BD time          | What to do                                                  |
| ---------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| Morning          | Higher-timeframe bias; mark PDH/PDL and key OBs/FVGs        |
| 6 AM – 12 PM     | Watch the Asian range form; mark its high and low at 12 PM  |
| 12 – 3 PM        | London KZ: look for a sweep of the Asian range and a CHOCH  |
| 5 – 8 PM         | NY AM KZ: continuation or reversal setups                   |
| 8 – 10 PM        | London close: manage open trades                            |
| After 10 PM      | Done for the day unless you trade the NY PM session         |

Add one hour to every time from early November to mid-March.

### Second example (no chart) — a bearish day

Bias bearish (daily in premium). The Asian range is **1.2700 – 1.2725** on GBP/USD. At **12:30 PM BD** London rallies to **1.2733**, taking the Asian high, then closes back inside and breaks the last 15m higher low. Sell the FVG at **1.2722**, stop **1.2738**, target the previous day's low at **1.2668**: risk 16 pips, reward 54 pips, **R:R 1 : 3.4** (planned). If London ends without reaching it, NY AM (5 – 8 PM BD) is where the target is most likely to be tested.

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake                                       | Fix                                                     |
| --------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| Using summer BD times in winter               | Add one hour from early November to mid-March           |
| Trading the Asian range middle                | It's for marking levels                                 |
| Taking any setup because it's in a kill zone  | Time is a filter, not a signal; the setup must be there |
| Entering new trades at London close           | It's for managing trades; reversals are common          |
| Trading through NY lunch                      | Thin and choppy; wait or stop for the day               |

## Practice

1. For two weeks, mark the Asian range each day.
2. Note which side London took first, and whether the day then went the other way.
3. Log the BD time of every trade you take, and compare results inside and outside the kill zones.
4. Use your [trading journal](/en/tools/journal-template).
