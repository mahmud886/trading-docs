## The Two Steps

SMC risk management comes down to two steps, in this order:

1. **Put the stop where the setup is wrong:** beyond the structure that would invalidate the idea, not a fixed number of pips.
2. **Size the position from that stop**, so that if it's hit you lose a fixed percentage of the account.

A wider stop means a smaller position; a tighter stop, a larger one. The money at risk stays the same.

## Structural Stop vs Tight Stop

Both stops risk **1% of a $10,000 account = $100**:

| Stop        | Price  | Distance | Size          | What happened          |
| ----------- | ------ | -------- | ------------- | ---------------------- |
| Structural  | 1.0845 | 25 pips  | **0.40 lots** | Held; plan 1 : 2.0 to 1.0920 |
| Tight       | 1.0862 | 8 pips   | 1.25 lots     | Taken by a wick to 1.0855: −$100 |

The tight stop looks better on paper (1 : 6.3 to the same target), but it sat **inside the order block**, where a normal retest can reach. The structural stop sat beyond the OB's full range, where price going would actually prove the setup wrong.

## Where the Stop Goes

| Entry type     | Stop                                         | Why                                   |
| -------------- | -------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| Order block    | Beyond the OB's **full range** (wick), plus a buffer | Trading through the whole OB invalidates it |
| FVG            | Beyond the FVG, or beyond the candle that created it | A full close through the gap invalidates it |
| Liquidity sweep | Beyond the sweep's wick                     | A new extreme means it wasn't a sweep  |
| CHOCH entry    | Beyond the swing that made the CHOCH         | Taking it means structure didn't shift |
| Breaker block  | Beyond the breaker zone                      | The breaker failed                     |

<Callout type="info" title="Clarified: body or wick">
  The original said to put an OB stop "beyond the OB (body, not wick)". Using the body gives a tighter stop, but the
  wick is part of the zone, and retests often reach it, as in the chart. This lesson uses the full range plus a small
  buffer for spread. If you use the body, accept that more stops will be hit.
</Callout>

## Position Sizing

```
Position size (lots) = (Account × Risk %) ÷ (Stop distance in pips × Pip value per lot)
```

**EUR/USD** (pip value about $10 per standard lot): account $10,000, risk 1% = $100, stop 25 pips:

$100 ÷ (25 × $10) = **0.40 lots**

**Gold** (a $1 move is about $100 per standard lot of 100 oz): account $5,000, risk 1% = $50, stop $6:

$50 ÷ (6 × $100) = **0.083 lots**, rounded **down** to 0.08.

Always round down. Pip values for pairs that don't end in USD change with the exchange rate; check them in your platform.

## Risk by Setup Grade

Using the grading from [Entry Confirmation](/en/smc/entry-confirmation):

| Grade | Score | Risk per trade      |
| ----- | ----- | ------------------- |
| A+    | 5 / 5 | 1% (up to 2% if experienced) |
| B     | 3–4   | 0.5%                |
| C     | 0–2   | Skip                |

<Callout type="info" title="Clarified: grade ranges">
  The original's ranges overlapped (B was "3–4 confluences", C was "2–3"). They're separated here so each score has
  one grade.
</Callout>

## Account Rules

| Rule                         | Guideline                                            |
| ---------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| Risk per trade               | 0.5–1% for most traders; never more than 2%          |
| Daily loss limit             | Stop for the day at −3%                              |
| Weekly loss limit            | At −5%, cut position size in half the next week      |
| Correlated trades            | EUR/USD and GBP/USD longs together count as one risk |
| Moving the stop              | Only toward profit, never away                       |
| Break-even                   | Optional after +1R; it removes risk but also gets stopped by normal pullbacks |

These are common guidelines, not fixed rules. Pick your numbers in advance and write them down.

### Second example (no chart) — short at a sweep

GBP/USD sweeps equal highs at **1.2750** to **1.2758**, then forms a 15m CHOCH. Sell **1.2744**, stop **1.2762** (beyond the sweep wick plus a 4-pip buffer), target **1.2690**: risk 18 pips, reward 54 pips, **R:R 1 : 3.0** (planned). Account $8,000, risk 0.5% (B grade) = $40: $40 ÷ (18 × $10) = **0.22 lots**.

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake                                 | Fix                                                |
| --------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| Choosing the lot size first             | Stop first, then size                              |
| Tightening the stop to improve R:R      | Put it at invalidation; reduce size instead        |
| Stop inside the zone you're trading     | Beyond the zone's full range                       |
| Same lot size on every trade            | The stop distance changes; the risk shouldn't      |
| Ignoring correlation                    | Two correlated trades are one bigger risk          |
| Rounding the size up                    | Always round down                                  |

## Practice

1. Take your last 20 trades and note where the stop was relative to the zone.
2. Recalculate each one's position size with the formula.
3. Count how many stops sat inside the zone, and how many of those were hit before the trade worked.
4. Log it in your [trading journal](/en/tools/journal-template).

## Summary

| Step       | Rule                                             |
| ---------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| **Stop**   | Beyond the structure that invalidates the setup  |
| **Size**   | (Account × risk %) ÷ (stop pips × pip value)     |
| **Grade**  | A+ 1%, B 0.5%, C skip                            |
| **Limits** | −3% daily, −5% weekly                            |
