## What Are Equal Highs and Lows?

**Equal highs (EQH)** and **equal lows (EQL)** are two or more swing points at **about the same price**. Because they look like strong resistance or support, many traders put stops just beyond them and breakout orders there too. That makes them **concentrated liquidity**.

| Level            | Liquidity                | Stops of…                     |
| ---------------- | ------------------------ | ----------------------------- |
| **Equal highs**  | Buy-side, just above     | Shorts; plus breakout buyers  |
| **Equal lows**   | Sell-side, just below    | Longs; plus breakdown sellers |

**How equal is equal?** There's no fixed rule. A practical one: within about **0.1 × ATR** of the timeframe, or a couple of pips on a 1H forex chart. Exact matches are rare.

1. Highs at **1.0950** and **1.0949**, lows at **1.0910** and **1.0911**: a range with obvious stops on both sides.
2. **Sweep:** hour 15 trades to **1.0957**, above the equal highs, and closes back below at 1.0946.
3. **Entry:** sell **1.0942** as price falls back; stop **1.0960** above the sweep; target **1.0905**, just beyond the opposite pool.
4. **Run:** the drop closes **through** the equal lows. That pool wasn't a reversal; it was the target.

| Level   | Price  | Note                              |
| ------- | ------ | --------------------------------- |
| Entry   | 1.0942 | Back below the equal highs        |
| Stop    | 1.0960 | Above the 1.0957 sweep            |
| Target  | 1.0905 | Beyond the equal lows             |
| **R:R** | **1 : 2.1** | Risk 18 pips, reward 37 pips |

<Callout type="warning" title="Corrected: 'expect price to sweep them'">
  The original said to expect equal levels to be swept and called the area after a sweep a "high-probability
  reversal zone". Equal levels are **targets**, but price can also **run** them and keep going, as the equal lows do
  here. Only a close back inside, plus a shift in structure, makes it a reversal setup.
</Callout>

## Trading Equal Levels

| Step | Action                                                         |
| ---- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1    | Mark equal highs/lows (2+ touches) on your timeframe           |
| 2    | Don't buy the break of EQH or sell the break of EQL blindly    |
| 3    | Wait: sweep (close back inside) or run (close beyond and hold)? |
| 4    | After a sweep: look for a lower-timeframe CHOCH, then enter the other way |
| 5    | Stop beyond the sweep's wick                                   |
| 6    | Target the opposite pool: EQH sweep → EQL, and vice versa      |

After a **run** with displacement, the old level often becomes support (for EQH) or resistance (for EQL) instead.

---

## More Touches: Stronger or Weaker?

The original said "more touches = more liquidity = better sweep". More touches do mean more stops. But each touch also absorbs orders on the other side, so a level tested many times is also **more likely to break and run**. Treat three or more touches as a sign that a big move through the level is coming, and let the close tell you which way.

## Where Not to Put Your Stop

| Your trade      | Avoid                         | Better                                  |
| --------------- | ----------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| Short near EQH  | Stop 1–2 pips above EQH       | Beyond a likely sweep, or skip the trade |
| Long near EQL   | Stop 1–2 pips below EQL       | Beyond a likely sweep, or enter after the sweep |

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake                              | Fix                                                   |
| ------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| Treating every EQH/EQL as a reversal | Wait for the close: sweep or run                      |
| Stops right at the equal level       | That's the pool                                       |
| Calling 10-pip-apart highs "equal"   | Keep the tolerance small for your timeframe           |
| Ignoring the higher-timeframe trend  | In a trend, the pool in the trend direction is usually run |

## Practice
