## The Two Lines

Every supply or demand zone has two edges:

- **Proximal line:** the edge **nearest** to current price. Price reaches it first.
- **Distal line:** the edge **farthest** from current price. A close beyond it invalidates the zone.

| Zone       | Proximal (standard)            | Distal                        |
| ---------- | ------------------------------ | ----------------------------- |
| **Demand** | Highest **body** in the base   | Lowest **wick** in the base   |
| **Supply** | Lowest **body** in the base    | Highest **wick** in the base  |

---

## Body-to-Wick or Wick-to-Wick?

| Method          | Proximal from    | Zone      | Effect                                     |
| --------------- | ---------------- | --------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| **Body-to-wick** | The base's bodies | Tighter  | Better R:R; price may turn before reaching it |
| **Wick-to-wick** | The base's wicks  | Wider    | Fills more often; worse R:R                |

The distal line is the far wick in both. Pick one method and use it every time, so your results are comparable.

## Choosing the Entry

| Entry          | Use when                                                   |
| -------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Proximal**   | Strong, fresh zone; fast markets; you don't want to miss it |
| **50%**        | Wide zones; a balance of fill rate and R:R                 |
| **Deep**       | Only with a lower-timeframe CHOCH inside the zone, or as a small extra order |
| **Split**      | Half at the proximal, half at 50% (same total risk)        |

<Callout type="info" title="Clarified: stops and targets">
  The original said to use "which boundary" for entries, stops and targets "for maximum precision". The usual roles
  are: **enter** at or inside the proximal line, **stop** a few pips beyond the distal line (not on it, where wicks
  reach), and **target** the proximal line of the next opposing zone, which price reaches before that zone's distal.
</Callout>

### Second example (no chart) — a supply zone, split entry

GBP/USD supply **1.2742 – 1.2756** (proximal 1.2742, distal 1.2756). Split a 1% risk: half at the proximal **1.2742**, half at 50% **1.2749**; stop **1.2761**; target the next demand's proximal at **1.2680**. Leg 1: risk 19 pips, reward 62 pips (**1 : 3.3**). Leg 2: risk 12 pips, reward 69 pips (**1 : 5.8**). Both planned. If price turns at 1.2745, only leg 1 fills.

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake                                    | Fix                                                  |
| ------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| Mixing body-to-wick and wick-to-wick       | Pick one method                                      |
| Stop exactly on the distal line            | Add a few pips for spread and wicks                  |
| Always entering deep for the best R:R      | Count the trades you miss; check the fill rate       |
| Targeting the opposing zone's distal line  | Target its proximal line; price reaches that first   |
| Proximal and distal far apart (very wide zone) | Use 50% or a lower-timeframe refinement          |

## Practice

1. Draw 20 zones both ways (body-to-wick and wick-to-wick).
2. For each, record whether price reached the proximal, the 50% level, and the deep level.
3. Calculate the fill rate and average R:R for each entry.
4. Log it in your [trading journal](/en/tools/journal-template).

## Summary

| Line / entry  | Key point                                         |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| **Proximal**  | Nearest edge; standard entry; fills most often    |
| **50%**       | Better R:R; fills less often                      |
| **Distal**    | Far edge; the stop goes just beyond it            |
| **Trade-off** | Deeper entry = better R:R, more missed trades     |
