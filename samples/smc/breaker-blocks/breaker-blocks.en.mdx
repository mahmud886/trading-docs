## What Is a Breaker Block?

A **breaker block** is an [order block](/en/smc/order-blocks) that **failed**: price closed straight through it. The zone doesn't disappear. Traders who entered there are now trapped, and when price comes back to it, the zone often acts **from the other side**.

| Original OB         | It fails when…                        | It becomes…            | Trade on the retest |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------------- | ---------------------- | ------------------- |
| **Bullish OB** (last down-close candle before a rally) | Price closes **below** it | **Bearish breaker** | Sell |
| **Bearish OB** (last up-close candle before a drop) | Price closes **above** it | **Bullish breaker** | Buy |

## A Bearish Breaker, Step by Step

<Callout type="info" title="About this example">
  The candles are **synthetic**, built to show the concept. They are not real prices or a real trade. R:R is the
  planned risk-to-reward.
</Callout>

1. **Bullish OB:** the last down-close candle before the rally, **1.0900 – 1.0906**.
2. **Sweep:** the rally runs above the 1.0925 high to 1.0930, taking buy-side liquidity, then turns.
3. **Failure:** a candle closes at 1.0898, **below the whole OB**. Buyers who used it are now underwater.
4. **Breaker:** the zone is now a **bearish breaker**.
5. **Retest:** price climbs back to 1.0905, into the breaker, and fails again. Sell **1.0903**.

| Level   | Price  | Note                              |
| ------- | ------ | --------------------------------- |
| Entry   | 1.0903 | Inside the breaker                |
| Stop    | 1.0914 | Above the OB candle's high        |
| Target  | 1.0850 | Sell-side liquidity below         |
| **R:R** | **1 : 4.8** | Risk 11 pips, reward 53 pips |

A wider, safer stop above the 1.0930 sweep would give risk 29 and **1 : 1.8**. Which stop you use is a trade-off between hit rate and R:R.

## Rules for a Valid Breaker

| Rule          | Requirement                                                    |
| ------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Origin**    | It was a real order block: the move from it broke structure    |
| **Sweep**     | The move from it took liquidity (a higher high or lower low) before failing |
| **Failure**   | A candle **closes** beyond the whole zone, ideally with displacement |
| **Retest**    | Price returns to the zone from the other side                  |
| **Context**   | The new direction agrees with the higher timeframe, or with a CHOCH |

<Callout type="info" title="Added: the sweep rule">
  The original rules didn't mention liquidity. The sweep is what separates a breaker from a
  [mitigation block](/en/smc/mitigation-blocks): if the move from the OB **failed to make** a new high or low before
  breaking, it's a mitigation block instead.
</Callout>

## Breaker vs Order Block vs Mitigation Block

| Feature          | Order block           | Breaker block                   | Mitigation block               |
| ---------------- | --------------------- | ------------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| **Status**       | Unbroken              | Broken after a sweep            | Broken without a new extreme   |
| **Trade**        | Original direction    | Opposite direction              | Opposite direction             |
| **Trapped side** | —                     | Traders who bought/sold the OB  | Same                           |

## Trading the Breaker

1. Find an OB whose move **swept liquidity** and then **failed** with a close through the zone.
2. Mark the zone (the OB candle's body and wick on the near side).
3. Wait for the retest. Look for rejection or a lower-timeframe CHOCH in the zone.
4. **Stop** beyond the zone (tight) or beyond the sweep (wide).
5. **Target** the next liquidity in the new direction.

The [Unicorn model](/en/ict/unicorn-model) adds one more condition: a fair value gap overlapping the breaker. In this example the drop's FVG (1.0905 – 1.0909) touches the breaker at 1.0905.

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake                                 | Fix                                                    |
| --------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| Calling any broken zone a breaker       | It must have been a real OB, with a sweep              |
| Wick through the OB counted as failure  | Only a close beyond the zone                           |
| Entering on the break                   | Wait for the retest                                    |
| Trading against the higher timeframe    | Breakers work best in the HTF direction                |

## Practice

1. Find 10 failed order blocks on 1H charts.
2. Sort them: swept liquidity first (breaker) or not (mitigation).
3. Record how each retest reacted. Log it in your [trading journal](/en/tools/journal-template).

## Summary

| Element      | Rule                                            |
| ------------ | ----------------------------------------------- |
| **Breaker**  | An OB that failed after its move swept liquidity |
| **Flip**     | Bullish OB → bearish breaker, and vice versa     |
| **Entry**    | On the retest from the other side               |
| **Stop**     | Beyond the zone or beyond the sweep             |
