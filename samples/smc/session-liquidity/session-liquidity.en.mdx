## What Is Session Liquidity?

Every session leaves a **high and a low**. Stops and breakout orders collect just beyond them, so they become liquidity: buy-side above the high, sell-side below the low.

The next session often runs one or both of those levels. Reading the day as a **chain**, where each session targets the one before, tells you where price is likely to go next.

<Callout type="info" title="Clarified: 'seeks'">
  The original said each session "seeks to take the liquidity left by the previous one". It's a tendency, not a rule:
  some sessions stay inside the previous range. Use the levels as targets and reference points, and let structure
  confirm the direction.
</Callout>

---

## The Levels

| Level                  | Short | Role                                  |
| ---------------------- | ----- | ------------------------------------- |
| Previous day's high    | PDH   | Buy-side target for the day           |
| Previous day's low     | PDL   | Sell-side target for the day          |
| Previous week's high   | PWH   | Bigger buy-side target                |
| Previous week's low    | PWL   | Bigger sell-side target               |
| Asian session high     | ASH   | Often London's first target           |
| Asian session low      | ASL   | Often London's first target           |
| London high / low      | —     | New York's targets                    |

## Trading It, Step by Step

1. **Before the day:** mark the PDH, PDL, PWH and PWL. Decide the bias from the higher timeframe.
2. **Pick the draw:** PDH (or PWH) if bullish, PDL (or PWL) if bearish.
3. **After the Asian session:** mark the ASH and ASL.
4. **London:** watch for a sweep of the Asian level *against* your bias, then a CHOCH.
5. **Enter** at the OB or FVG, in discount for longs or premium for shorts, in London or on the NY retest.
6. **Target** the draw. Use the London high or low as a partial target on the way.

### Second example (no chart) — a bearish day

Bias bearish; the PDL is **1.2650** on GBP/USD. The Asian range is **1.2700 – 1.2720**. London first takes the Asian high to **1.2728**, then breaks down and makes the London low at **1.2688**. In NY, price retraces into London's bearish FVG. Sell **1.2712**, stop **1.2732** above the London high, target the PDL **1.2650**: risk 20 pips, reward 62 pips, **R:R 1 : 3.1** (planned).

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake                                     | Fix                                                   |
| ------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| Treating every session high/low as a target | Pick the one your bias points to                      |
| Trading the first sweep of the Asian range as a breakout | It's often the false move; wait for the CHOCH |
| Ignoring the weekly levels                  | A nearby PWH/PWL can stop the day's move              |
| No stop in the plan                         | Below/above the sweep low/high that set up the trade  |
| Forgetting US winter time                   | Add one hour to the BD times from early November to mid-March |

## Practice

1. For 20 days, mark the PDH, PDL, ASH, ASL and the London high and low.
2. Note which ones each session took, in order.
3. Count how often London took one side of the Asian range before the day's real move.
4. Log it in your [trading journal](/en/tools/journal-template).
