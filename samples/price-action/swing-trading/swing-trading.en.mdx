## What Is Swing Trading?

**Swing trading** captures moves that last **days to a few weeks**, using the daily and 4H charts. Targets are larger (often 100–500 pips on major pairs), stops are wider, and you need far less screen time than for day trading or scalping.

<Callout type="info" title="Clarified: 'best style for most traders'">
  The original called swing trading the best style for most traders. It does suit people with jobs, because it needs
  little screen time and costs matter less, but "best" depends on the person. Its trade-offs are fewer trades, wider
  stops, and holding through overnight costs and weekend gaps.
</Callout>

---

## The Framework

| Purpose               | Timeframe               |
| --------------------- | ----------------------- |
| Bias / direction      | Weekly                  |
| Setup and levels      | Daily                   |
| Entry timing          | 4H                      |
| Precision (optional)  | 1H                      |

1. **Weekly:** trend and major levels.
2. **Daily:** structure, key levels, the setup.
3. **4H:** wait for a confirmation candle or CHOCH at the daily level.
4. **Enter** with the stop beyond daily structure.
5. **Target** the next daily swing or level.
6. **Manage** twice a day.

1. **Daily resistance** at **1.2580 – 1.2600** rejects price twice.
2. A daily candle **closes above it**: the zone becomes support.
3. A few days later, price **retests** the zone and closes above it. The 4H chart confirms.

| Level   | Price  | Note                                        |
| ------- | ------ | ------------------------------------------- |
| Entry   | 1.2615 | After the retest and 4H confirmation        |
| Stop    | 1.2555 | Below the zone (60 pips)                    |
| Target  | 1.2800 | The next daily level                        |
| **R:R** | **1 : 3.1** | Risk 60 pips, reward 185 pips          |

**Size:** 1% of a $10,000 account = $100. $100 ÷ (60 × $10) = 0.167 lots → **0.16** (round down).

## Three Entry Techniques

| Technique              | Setup                                                    | Stop                    | Target               |
| ---------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------- | -------------------- |
| **Daily order block**  | A daily BOS; mark the OB; 4H confirmation when price returns | Beyond the daily OB  | The next daily swing |
| **Daily Fibonacci pullback** | Clear daily trend; pullback to 50–61.8%; 4H rejection | Below 78.6%         | A new daily swing    |
| **Daily S/R flip**     | Resistance breaks; retest holds; 4H rejection            | Below the zone          | The next daily level |

## Managing a Swing Trade

### Sizing for wider stops

Swing stops are often 50–150 pips, so positions are smaller. Example from the original: $10,000 account, 1% risk ($100), 80-pip stop → $100 ÷ (80 × $10) = **0.125 lots → 0.12**.

### Holding

- Give the trade at least a couple of days; don't exit on intraday noise.
- Stay in until the target is reached or the setup is invalidated (a daily close beyond the stop level).
- Check twice a day.

### Trailing

- Move the stop to break-even after about **+1R**, or after the next daily swing forms.
- Close half at **1 : 2** and trail the rest behind daily swings to the major target.

### Overnight costs and gaps

- **Swap:** holding past the daily rollover charges or pays interest; on long holds it adds up.
- **Weekend gaps:** price can open Monday beyond your stop. Size so a gap doesn't break your risk rules.

## A Routine From Bangladesh

| When (BD, US summer)     | What                                                     |
| ------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| Morning (after the daily close at about 3:00 AM BD; 4:00 AM in winter) | Read the new daily candle, update levels, set alerts |
| Evening                  | Check the 4H for confirmations at alerted levels; manage open trades |

### Second example (no chart) — gold daily pullback

Gold's daily trend is up. It pulls back 50% to **2,350**, where a 4H bullish engulfing forms. Buy **2,356**, stop **2,328** below 78.6%, target the daily high at **2,440**: risk $28, reward $84, **R:R 1 : 3.0** (planned).

## Why It Suits Part-Time Traders

| Factor             | Benefit                                  |
| ------------------ | ---------------------------------------- |
| Screen time        | 30 minutes – 1 hour a day                |
| Decision speed     | Time to analyse calmly                   |
| Costs              | Fewer trades; spread is small vs target  |
| Compatibility      | Works around a full-time job             |

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake                                  | Fix                                                  |
| ---------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| Using intraday-sized stops on daily setups | Stop beyond daily structure; size down             |
| Watching every 5-minute candle           | Check twice a day                                    |
| Ignoring swap on long holds              | Check the swap rate before entering                  |
| Moving the stop to break-even too early  | Wait for +1R or a new daily swing                    |

## Practice

1. Each weekend, mark the weekly bias and three daily levels on four pairs.
2. Set alerts at those levels and only look at 4H when an alert fires.
3. Plan each trade with entry, stop, target, size and swap.
4. Log it in your [trading journal](/en/tools/journal-template).
