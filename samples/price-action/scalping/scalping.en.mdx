## What Is Scalping?

**Scalping** means taking short trades, usually minutes long, on the 1- and 5-minute charts, aiming for small moves of about 5–20 pips. With price action, the tools are the same as on higher timeframes: structure, levels and candles, just smaller.

<Callout type="warning" title="Hardest style to make work">
  Small targets mean costs (spread, commission, slippage) take a large share of every trade, and decisions must be
  fast. Most traders are better off learning on higher timeframes first and moving down only with a tested plan.
</Callout>

## Requirements

| Requirement   | Why                                                        |
| ------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Low costs** | A raw/ECN account with spreads well under 1 pip on majors  |
| **Fast execution** | One-click trading; a stable, low-latency connection    |
| **The right hours** | Active sessions only (see below)                      |
| **Liquid pairs** | EUR/USD, GBP/USD, USD/JPY; gold only with tight spreads |
| **Focus**     | 2–3 hours of uninterrupted attention                       |

<Callout type="info" title="Removed: '$5,000+ account'">
  The original said scalping needs an account of $5,000 or more. Account size doesn't change the percentage costs of a
  scalp; position size does. What matters is that the spread is small compared with your stop and target.
</Callout>

## The Method: M5 Structure, M1 Entry

![EUR/USD 1-minute chart, 7:05–7:26 PM BD: an M1 candle wicks to 1.0848 below M5 support at 1.0850 and closes back above; buy 1.0853, stop 1.0847, target 1.0865; 1:2.0 before costs, about 1:1.6 with a 1-pip spread](/images/price-action/scalping/m1-scalp-spread-dark.png)

<video controls muted playsInline loop preload="metadata" src="/videos/price-action/scalping-explainer-dark.mp4" className="mt-6 w-full rounded-xl border border-border" />

<Callout type="info" title="About this chart">
  The candles are **synthetic**, built around the levels in this lesson. They are not real prices. R:R is the planned
  risk-to-reward, not a result.
</Callout>

1. **M5:** decide the short-term structure and mark the levels: support **1.0850**, the last swing high **1.0865**.
2. **Wait** for price to reach an M5 level.
3. **M1:** look for a rejection: here a candle wicks to **1.0848** and closes back above 1.0850 (**7:17 PM BD**).
4. **Enter** just above; stop just beyond the wick; target the next M5 swing.

| Level   | Price  | Note                                        |
| ------- | ------ | ------------------------------------------- |
| Entry   | 1.0853 | After the M1 rejection                      |
| Stop    | 1.0847 | Below the 1.0848 wick (6 pips)              |
| Target  | 1.0865 | The M5 swing high (12 pips)                 |
| **R:R** | **1 : 2.0** before costs | **about 1 : 1.6** with a 1-pip spread |

## Why Costs Matter So Much

The spread is paid on every trade. On a 6-pip stop and 12-pip target, a **1-pip** spread effectively makes it 7 pips risked for 11 gained.

| Spread  | Effective risk | Effective reward | R:R     |
| ------- | -------------- | ---------------- | ------- |
| 0.2 pip | 6.2            | 11.8             | 1 : 1.9 |
| 0.5 pip | 6.5            | 11.5             | 1 : 1.8 |
| 1.0 pip | 7.0            | 11.0             | 1 : 1.6 |
| 2.0 pip | 8.0            | 10.0             | 1 : 1.25 |

The same spread on a 50-pip swing trade barely matters.

## Session-Open Scalps

1. Mark the **Asian range** high and low (about 6 AM – 12 PM BD).
2. At the **London open** (about 1 PM BD in summer) or the **NY open**, the first break of the range often fails.
3. Trade the **second push**, after a close back inside or a retest that holds.
4. Target 1–2× the Asian range width, or the next level.

## When to Scalp (Bangladesh Time)

| Window                      | BD time (US summer) | BD time (US winter) |
| --------------------------- | ------------------- | ------------------- |
| London open                 | 1:00 – 3:00 PM      | 2:00 – 4:00 PM      |
| **London/New York overlap** | **6:00 – 9:00 PM**  | **7:00 – 10:00 PM** |

<Callout type="warning" title="Corrected: overlap times">
  The original gave the overlap as "8:00–11:00 EST" with no BD time. New York's 8–11 AM is **6–9 PM BD** in US summer
  and **7–10 PM BD** in US winter. Avoid the 15 minutes either side of major news, such as US data at **6:30 PM BD**
  in summer.
</Callout>

## Scalping Rules

1. **Stops of about 5–10 pips** on EUR/USD, placed beyond M5 structure, not at a fixed number.
2. **Risk 0.25–0.5% per trade**: more trades mean more chances of a losing streak.
3. **Stop after three losses in a row** and review.
4. **Only in active sessions.**
5. **No trades in the 15 minutes around high-impact news.**
6. **A daily trade cap** (for example 10) and a **daily loss limit** (2–3%).
7. **Flat before the session ends.**

<Callout type="info" title="Clarified: stop size">
  The original said "stop 5–10 pips" in the method and "maximum 3–5 pips stop" in the rules. A 3-pip stop on EUR/USD
  is inside normal 1-minute noise and spread; 5–10 pips beyond structure is the more workable range.
</Callout>

## Scalping vs Other Styles

| Factor         | Scalping          | Day trading       | Swing trading   |
| -------------- | ----------------- | ----------------- | --------------- |
| Holding time   | 1–15 minutes      | 30 minutes – 8 hours | 2–20 days    |
| Trades per day | 5–20              | 2–5               | 0–1             |
| Target         | 5–20 pips         | 30–80 pips        | 100–500 pips    |
| Stop           | 5–10 pips         | 15–40 pips        | 50–150 pips     |
| Screen time    | 2–4 intense hours | 3–6 hours         | ~30 minutes     |
| Cost impact    | Very high         | Moderate          | Low             |

## Practice

1. On a demo account, scalp only the London/NY overlap for two weeks.
2. Log each trade's spread, slippage and R:R before and after costs.
3. Compare your net result with the same setups on the 15-minute chart.
4. Log it in your [trading journal](/en/tools/journal-template).
