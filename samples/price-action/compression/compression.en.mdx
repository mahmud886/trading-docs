## What Is Compression?

**Compression** is price squeezing into a smaller and smaller area: each swing is shorter than the last, often against a flat level. Volatility falls, and the market is coiling before a move.

Compression doesn't tell you the direction on its own. It tells you **a move is likely**, and the shape and context usually hint at which way.

---

## How It Forms

| Type                          | Shape                                     | Usual lean                     |
| ----------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| **Into resistance** (bullish) | Flat highs, rising lows                   | Break up                       |
| **Into support** (bearish)    | Flat lows, falling highs                  | Break down                     |
| **Symmetrical**               | Falling highs and rising lows             | With the prior trend           |
| **Internal**                  | Smaller swings inside a larger range      | Toward the range edge it's pressing |

1. **Flat highs:** price stops at **1.0950** four times.
2. **Rising lows:** **1.0900 → 1.0915 → 1.0928 → 1.0938**. The swings from the highs shrink: **50 → 35 → 22 → 12 pips**. Buyers step in earlier each time.
3. **The break:** a candle closes above 1.0950.
4. **The retest and trade:**

| Level   | Price  | Note                                       |
| ------- | ------ | ------------------------------------------ |
| Entry   | 1.0953 | Retest of 1.0950 from above                |
| Stop    | 1.0935 | Below the last higher low (1.0938)         |
| Target  | 1.1000 | 1.0950 + the 50-pip triangle height        |
| **R:R** | **1 : 2.6** | Risk 18 pips, reward 47 pips          |

## Why It Works

- **Pressure builds on one side:** in an ascending triangle, buyers keep pushing at a level sellers are defending; the rising lows show the sellers are giving ground.
- **Stops collect beyond the flat level:** short sellers place stops just above the flat highs, so a break adds fuel.
- **Volatility comes back:** quiet periods tend to be followed by larger moves.

---

## Trading Compression

1. **Identify** at least two higher lows (or lower highs) against a flat level, with shrinking swings.
2. **Lean** with the shape and the higher-timeframe trend: ascending → up, descending → down.
3. **Wait for a close beyond** the flat level (not a wick).
4. **Enter** on the close or the retest.
5. **Stop** beyond the last compression point (the last higher low in an ascending triangle).
6. **Target** the pattern's height from the breakout, or the next level; the height is usually at least 1 : 1.

### When it fails

A break that **closes back inside** the triangle is a warning: the stops beyond the level may have been the point. Often price then runs to the **opposite** side. See [Fakeouts](/en/price-action/fakeouts).

---

## Compression and Liquidity

Each higher low in an ascending triangle leaves sell-side stops just below it, and the flat highs leave buy-side stops above. So compression builds liquidity on **both** sides. Price may sweep one side first: a quick dip under the last higher low before the real break up is common.

### Example (no chart) — gold, descending triangle

Gold has flat lows at **2,380** and falling highs at 2,410, 2,400 and 2,392. A 1H candle closes below 2,380. Sell the retest at **2,378**, stop **2,394** above the last lower high, target **2,350** (2,380 − 30-point height): risk $16, reward $28, **R:R 1 : 1.75** (planned).

## Quality Check

| Strong compression                              | Weak compression                        |
| ----------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| Clear flat level with 3+ touches                | Messy level, no clear line              |
| Each swing clearly smaller                      | Swings the same size                    |
| With the higher-timeframe trend                 | Against it                              |
| Forms in an active session                      | Drifts through the Asian session        |

---

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake                                | Fix                                                   |
| -------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| Entering inside the triangle           | Wait for the close beyond the flat level              |
| Ignoring a failed break                | A close back inside often means the other side next   |
| Stop beyond the far side of the triangle | Beyond the last compression point is enough         |
| Expecting the full target every time   | Take partial profit at the nearest level              |
