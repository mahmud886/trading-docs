## What Is Range Trading?

**Range trading** means buying near support and selling near resistance while price moves sideways between them. It's the opposite of trend trading: you fade the edges instead of following breaks.

<Callout type="info" title="Clarified: 'markets range 60–70% of the time'">
  The original said markets spend "approximately 60–70% of their time" in ranges. It's a popular figure without a
  clear source, and the share depends on the market, the timeframe and how a range is defined. What matters is
  recognising a range when you're in one.
</Callout>

---

## Identifying a Range

- At least **two touches** of a clear high and **two** of a clear low.
- **No new highs or lows**: structure is flat.
- Candles **overlap**; moves from edge to edge are similar in size.
- The **midpoint** often acts as a minor pivot.

## The Strategy

| Step | Buy at support                                  | Sell at resistance                               |
| ---- | ----------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| 1    | Confirm the range (2+ touches each side)        | Same                                             |
| 2    | Wait for price to reach support                 | Wait for price to reach resistance               |
| 3    | Look for a rejection (pin bar, engulfing, close back inside) | Same, at resistance                  |
| 4    | Stop below support + 5–15 pips                  | Stop above resistance + 5–15 pips                |
| 5    | TP1 at the midpoint, TP2 near resistance        | TP1 at the midpoint, TP2 near support            |

Aim for at least 1 : 2 on the full position. If the range is too narrow for that after the spread and buffer, skip it.

## When Ranges Break

### Breakout signs

- Touches of one edge get closer together (compression against it — see [Compression](/en/price-action/compression)).
- A **close beyond** the edge with a large body.
- Higher-than-usual volume on the break.

### After a break

1. **Stop trading the range.** Your fade stop should already be beyond the edge.
2. Wait for the **retest** of the broken edge from the other side.
3. Enter on the retest; stop back inside the range.
4. Target the **range height** projected from the breakout. See [Breakouts](/en/price-action/breakouts).

## Examples (No Chart)

### 1. EUR/USD sell at resistance

EUR/USD ranges **1.0850 – 1.0910**. A 1H shooting star forms at **1.0908**. Sell **1.0903**, stop **1.0918**, TP1 at the midpoint **1.0880** (**1 : 1.5**), TP2 at **1.0858** (**1 : 3.0**), both planned.

### 2. Gold range break

Gold ranges **2,380 – 2,410** for two days, then a 4H candle closes at **2,418**. The retest holds at **2,412**. Buy **2,413**, stop **2,402** back inside, target **2,440** (2,410 + 30): risk $11, reward $27, **R:R 1 : 2.5** (planned).

## Range Rules

1. Trade only clear ranges with **2+ touches** on each side.
2. **Fade the edges**, not the middle.
3. Wait for a **rejection** at the edge; don't place blind limit orders in a range that's being pressed.
4. Use the **midpoint** for partial profits.
5. Keep the stop **beyond** the boundary, with a buffer.
6. **Don't hold through a range break.** A close beyond the edge ends the range trade.

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake                                 | Fix                                                     |
| --------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| Trading the middle of the range         | Only near the edges                                     |
| Calling a pullback in a trend a range   | Check for flat structure first                          |
| Stop exactly at the edge                | Beyond it, with a buffer for sweeps                     |
| Fading an edge that's being compressed  | Compression often precedes the break                    |
| Ignoring the spread on narrow ranges    | Skip ranges where the R:R doesn't work after costs      |

## Practice

1. Find five ranges on the 1H chart with two or more touches on each side.
2. For each, mark the rejection candles at the edges and plan entry, stop, TP1 and TP2.
3. Note how each range ended: which side broke and whether the retest held.
4. Log it in your [trading journal](/en/tools/journal-template).
