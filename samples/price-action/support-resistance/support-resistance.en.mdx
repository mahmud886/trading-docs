## What Are Support and Resistance?

- **Support** is a price area where falling price has stopped and turned up before: buyers outweighed sellers there.
- **Resistance** is a price area where rising price has stopped and turned down before: sellers outweighed buyers.

Traders watch these areas because orders tend to gather around them: buy orders and short-sellers' stops near support, sell orders and buyers' stops near resistance.

Price rarely turns at exactly the same tick, so draw a **zone**:

1. Find the area where price turned at least **two or three times**.
2. Draw one edge at the **bodies** (where candles closed or opened) and the other at the **wicks**.
3. Ignore one-off wicks that poke far beyond the rest. In the chart, the fourth test wicks to 1.0901 but the zone stays at **1.0893 – 1.0900**.

## What Makes a Level Strong

| Factor                 | Stronger                               | Weaker                          |
| ---------------------- | -------------------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| **Timeframe**          | Weekly / daily                         | 15m / 5m                        |
| **Touches**            | 2–4 clear reactions                    | One touch                       |
| **Reaction size**      | Big moves away from it                 | Small bounces                   |
| **Age**                | Recent                                 | Months old with no recent test  |
| **Confluence**         | Also a round number, a swing point or a flip | Stands alone              |

<Callout type="info" title="Clarified: many touches">
  More touches show a level matters, but each touch also uses up orders there and builds stops just beyond it. After
  many touches, a break becomes more likely, not less. See [Fresh vs Tested Zones](/en/smc/fresh-vs-tested).
</Callout>

## Types of Levels

| Type                 | Examples                                        | Notes                                   |
| -------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| **Horizontal**       | Previous swing highs and lows, range edges      | The most important                      |
| **Dynamic**          | Moving averages (20/50/200 EMA), trendlines     | Move with price; use as a guide         |
| **Psychological**    | Round numbers: 1.1000, 150.00, 2,400            | Orders cluster around them              |
| **Session / time**   | Previous day's high/low (PDH/PDL), weekly high/low, Asian range | Watched by intraday traders |

## The S/R Flip

When price **closes** through a level, the level often changes role: broken resistance becomes support, and broken support becomes resistance.

In the chart:

1. **Resistance** at 1.0893 – 1.0900 rejects price four times.
2. A candle **closes above 1.0900**: the break.
3. Price comes back, dips into the zone, and **closes above it**: the old resistance holds as support.
4. **The trade:**

| Level   | Price  | Note                                 |
| ------- | ------ | ------------------------------------ |
| Entry   | 1.0905 | After the retest candle holds the zone |
| Stop    | 1.0878 | Below the zone and the retest low    |
| Target  | 1.0960 | The next resistance                  |
| **R:R** | **1 : 2.0** | Risk 27 pips, reward 55 pips    |

Why it works: traders who sold at resistance are now losing and may buy back near their entry; traders who missed the breakout get a second chance.

---

## Three Strategies

| Strategy        | Setup                                                | Stop                     | Target                     |
| --------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------ | -------------------------- |
| **Bounce**      | Price reaches a strong level; a rejection candle forms | Beyond the zone + buffer | The next level or the range middle |
| **Flip**        | A close through the level, then a retest that holds  | Beyond the retested zone | The next structural level  |
| **Range fade**  | A clear range; sell the top, buy the bottom          | Beyond the range edge    | The opposite edge          |

## Examples (No Chart)

### 1. Gold bounce at daily support

Support **2,350**, tested three times on the daily chart. A 4H rejection candle forms. Buy **2,363**, stop **2,340**, target **2,400**: risk $23, reward $37, **R:R 1 : 1.6** (planned).

### 2. USD/JPY at a round number

**150.00** is a major round number and the top of the recent range. A 1H shooting star forms just above it. Sell **150.05**, stop **150.30**, target **149.30**: risk 25 pips, reward 75 pips, **R:R 1 : 3.0** (planned).

### 3. NAS100 previous day's high (BD time)

In the London session (from about **1:00 PM BD** in summer), NAS100 pushes above the PDH to **18,360** and closes back below it. Sell **18,340**, stop **18,375**, target the PDL area at **18,250**: risk 35 points, reward 90 points, **R:R 1 : 2.6** (planned).

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake                                 | Fix                                                |
| --------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| Drawing lines instead of zones          | Bodies to wicks                                    |
| Drawing too many levels                 | Keep the 3–5 that matter on the higher timeframe   |
| Stop exactly at the level               | Beyond the zone, with a buffer                     |
| Buying support in a strong downtrend    | Trade levels in the direction of structure first   |
| Calling a wick through a level a break  | Wait for a close beyond it                         |

## Summary

| Aspect      | Key point                                       |
| ----------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| **Draw**    | Zones, bodies to wicks, at least 2–3 reactions  |
| **Strength** | Higher timeframe, clear reactions, confluence  |
| **Flip**    | A close through, then a retest that holds       |
| **Entry**   | After a rejection or a held retest              |
| **Stop**    | Beyond the zone with a buffer                   |
