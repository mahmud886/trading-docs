## Why Exits Matter

The entry decides your risk; the **exit decides what you keep**. Two traders with the same entry and stop can finish the same trade at +1R and +3R just because they use different exit rules.

An **exit model** is that rule, written down before you enter.

---

## Five Exit Models

| Model            | Where you get out                                              | Strength                   | Weakness                        |
| ---------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| **Fixed target** | A set level: 2R, a measured move, a Fibonacci extension         | Simple, no decisions       | Leaves the rest of a big move   |
| **Structural**   | Just before the next opposing level: S/R, swing, liquidity pool | Uses what the chart shows  | Needs a clear level             |
| **Trailing stop**| Under each new higher low (long) / above each lower high (short), or an ATR distance | Rides trends | Gives back profit at the turn |
| **Partial exit** | Part at 1R / 2R, the rest held                                  | Books profit, eases pressure | Smaller result if the trend runs |
| **Time exit**    | At a set time: session close, N hours/days                     | Ends stale trades          | Ignores price                   |

Buy GBP/USD **1.2700**, stop **1.2680** (1R = 20 pips), resistance above at **1.2760 – 1.2770** (yesterday's high). Times are Bangladesh time in US summer.

| Exit                                    | Rule                                                       | Exit price | Result     |
| --------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- | ---------- | ---------- |
| **A · Fixed target**                    | Sell at 2R                                                 | 1.2740     | **+2.0R**  |
| **B · Structural**                      | Sell 2 pips below resistance                               | 1.2758     | **+2.9R**  |
| **C · Trailing**                        | After each new high, stop 1 pip under the latest higher low (1.2721, then 1.2735) | 1.2735 | **+1.75R** |
| **D · Time**                            | Flat at the New York close, 2:00 AM BD (4 PM New York)       | 1.2726     | **+1.3R**  |

What it shows:

- **B** won here because a clear level sat in the way and price turned there. Without that level, B has nothing to aim at.
- **A** was the earliest and simplest, but missed the next 18 pips.
- **C** gave back profit at the turn — that's the price of riding a trend. If price had kept trending, C would have beaten everything.
- **D** ignored price entirely; useful for intraday traders who never hold overnight.

## Partial Exits

A common plan: **50% at 1R** (stop to break-even), **30% at 2R**, **20%** trailed or held to the final target. On the path above, that would book 0.5 × 1 + 0.3 × 2 + 0.2 × 2.9 (held to the structural exit) = **+1.68R**.

Partials smooth the result; they never beat holding everything to the best exit. See [Trade Management](/en/price-action/trade-management) for what moving the stop to break-even can cost.

## Exit by Trading Style

| Style      | Typical exit                       | Why                                    |
| ---------- | ---------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| Scalping   | Fixed target                       | No time to manage; costs matter        |
| Intraday   | Structural + time (session close)  | Levels for targets, flat before the close |
| Swing      | Partial + trailing                 | Lets multi-day moves run               |
| Position   | Structural + wide trailing         | Major levels, weekly swings            |

### Example (no chart) — gold fixed target vs structural

Short XAU/USD at **2,420**, stop **2,428** ($8 risk). Fixed 2R target: **2,404**. The next daily support is **2,391**, so a structural exit just above it at **2,393** plans **1 : 3.4**. Before the trade, decide which one you'll use — and don't switch while the trade is open.

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake                                        | Fix                                                    |
| ---------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| Deciding the exit after entering               | Write it in the plan before the order                  |
| Moving the target further away mid-trade       | Only the stop may move, and only toward profit         |
| Targets straight through an opposing level      | Exit before the level, not inside it                   |
| Trailing too tight on a fast timeframe          | Trail by structure on the timeframe you entered on     |
| Holding intraday trades overnight "for more"    | Use a time exit                                        |

## Practice

1. Take 20 past trades and record exits A–D for each, with the same entry and stop.
2. Total R for each exit model across all 20.
3. Note which market conditions (trend, range, news) favoured which exit.
4. Log it in your [trading journal](/en/tools/journal-template).

## Summary

| Rule                                   | Why                                       |
| -------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| Plan the exit before the entry         | Decisions in a trade are emotional        |
| Respect opposing levels                | Price often turns there                   |
| Know what each exit gives up           | No exit wins on every path                |
| Test your exit on your own trades      | Choose by data, not by the last trade     |
