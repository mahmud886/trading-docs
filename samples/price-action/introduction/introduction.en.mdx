## What Is Price Action Trading?

**Price action trading** means making decisions from the **price chart itself**: candles, swings and levels, rather than indicators such as RSI, MACD or Bollinger Bands.

Every indicator is calculated from price. Reading price directly means you see the information first-hand, without a formula in between.

<Callout type="info" title="A framework, not a single strategy">
  Price action is a way of reading the chart. Specific setups (breakouts, pullbacks, reversals) are built on top of it,
  and so are SMC and ICT, which this site also teaches.
</Callout>

---

## A Brief History

| When         | Who / what              | Contribution                                       |
| ------------ | ----------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| 1700s        | Munehisa Homma, Japan   | Rice-market price records; the roots of candlestick analysis |
| ~1900        | Charles Dow             | Dow Theory: trends defined by swing highs and lows |
| 1900s–1930s  | Richard Wyckoff         | Accumulation, distribution and the "Composite Man" |
| 2010s        | ICT and SMC educators   | Liquidity, order blocks and fair value gaps, taught online |

<Callout type="info" title="Clarified: who uses it">
  The original said price action is "the preferred method of institutional traders, hedge funds and prop firm traders
  worldwide". Many professionals do read price this way, but institutions use many methods, including quantitative
  and fundamental ones. There's no data showing price action is the preferred one.
</Callout>

---

## Why Traders Use It

| Reason               | What it means                                                         |
| -------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Direct information** | The chart shows what price did, now; indicators summarise it after the fact |
| **Works everywhere** | The same reading works on forex, gold, indices, crypto and stocks, on any timeframe |
| **Defined risk**     | Levels and swings give natural places for the stop and the target      |
| **Clean charts**     | Fewer conflicting signals than a chart full of indicators              |

Price action isn't magic. It doesn't predict the future; it gives you a structured way to decide where you're wrong and how much you could make.

| Pillar                | Question it answers            | In the chart                                      |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------- |
| **1. Market structure** | Which way is price going?    | Higher highs (1.0880 → 1.0960) and higher lows (1.0830 → 1.0876): an uptrend |

## Your First Trade, Step by Step

1. **Start on the daily chart.** Decide the trend from the swings.
2. **Mark key levels:** previous swing highs and lows, levels that flipped, round numbers.
3. **Drop to the 4H chart.** Wait for price to reach a level in the direction of the trend.
4. **Read the candles at the level.** Look for rejection or an engulfing candle.
5. **Plan the trade before entering:** entry, stop beyond the level, target at the next level. Aim for at least 1 : 2.
6. **Size it** so the stop costs 0.5–1% of the account.

**The example in the chart (EUR/USD, 4H):**

| Level   | Price  | Note                                  |
| ------- | ------ | ------------------------------------- |
| Entry   | 1.0885 | Buy limit just above the 1.0874 – 1.0880 level |
| Stop    | 1.0850 | Below the level and the retest low    |
| Target  | 1.0960 | The previous swing high               |
| **R:R** | **1 : 2.1** | Risk 35 pips, reward 75 pips     |

<Callout type="info" title="Clarified: the entry">
  The original said to enter at 1.0885 "above the engulfing candle", but that candle closed at 1.0890. Here 1.0885 is a
  limit order on the next dip toward the level. A buy-stop above the engulfing high (1.0892) is the other common choice:
  more confirmation, slightly worse R:R.
</Callout>

## Smart Money, in Plain Terms

Large traders can't fill big orders at one price, so they need many opposite orders. Clusters of stop losses beyond obvious highs and lows supply them. That's why price often runs **just past** an obvious level and then reverses, a pattern called a **liquidity sweep** or stop hunt.

<Callout type="info" title="Reframed: 'they engineer moves'">
  The original said institutions "engineer moves" and "manipulate structure" to trap retail traders. You can't see
  intent on a chart. What you can see is the pattern: a run past a level, a close back inside, and a reversal. The SMC
  section teaches it in detail; see [Manipulation](/en/smc/manipulation).
</Callout>

## Timeframes by Trading Style

| Style            | Analysis timeframe | Entry timeframe | Typical hold    |
| ---------------- | ------------------ | --------------- | --------------- |
| **Scalping**     | 1H / 15m           | 5m / 1m         | Minutes         |
| **Day trading**  | 4H / 1H            | 15m / 5m        | Hours           |
| **Swing trading** | Daily / 4H        | 4H / 1H         | Days to weeks   |
| **Position trading** | Weekly / Daily | Daily          | Weeks to months |

## Common Beginner Mistakes

| Mistake                         | Fix                                                        |
| ------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| Adding indicators back too soon | Give a clean chart a few months first                      |
| Treating every candle as a signal | Only candles **at key levels**, with the trend, count    |
| Skipping the higher timeframe   | Always start on the daily                                  |
| No written plan                 | Write entry, stop, target and risk rules down              |
| Risking too much                | 0.5–1% per trade while learning                            |
| Not keeping a journal           | Screenshot every trade and review weekly                   |

## Practice

1. Open a clean daily chart of EUR/USD. Mark the last four swing highs and lows and label the trend.
2. Mark two key levels, including one that flipped from resistance to support (or the reverse).
3. On the 4H chart, find one candle signal at a level and plan the trade: entry, stop, target, R:R.
4. Log it in your [trading journal](/en/tools/journal-template).
