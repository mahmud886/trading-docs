## What Is a Candlestick?

A **candlestick** shows four prices for one period (one minute, one hour, one day…):

| Price     | Meaning                             |
| --------- | ----------------------------------- |
| **Open**  | The first traded price of the period |
| **High**  | The highest price reached           |
| **Low**   | The lowest price reached            |
| **Close** | The last price of the period        |

A line chart shows only the close. A candle also shows **how** price got there: how far it travelled, and where it was pushed back.

| Part           | From → to                    | Tells you                              |
| -------------- | ---------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| **Body**       | Open → close                 | Who won the period, and by how much    |
| **Upper wick** | Top of the body → high       | Prices that were reached but not held  |
| **Lower wick** | Bottom of the body → low     | Prices that were reached but not held  |

- **Bullish** candle: close **above** open (usually green). The open is the bottom of the body.
- **Bearish** candle: close **below** open (usually red). The open is the top of the body.
- **Doji:** open and close (almost) equal. Neither side won the period.

## Single-Candle Patterns

| Pattern          | Rule                                              | Common reading                       |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| **Marubozu**     | Body is almost the whole range; tiny or no wicks  | One side controlled the whole period |
| **Spinning top** | Small body, wicks on both sides                   | Indecision                           |
| **Hammer**       | Small body near the top; lower wick ≥ 2× the body | Lower prices were rejected (bullish after a decline) |
| **Shooting star** | Small body near the bottom; upper wick ≥ 2× the body | Higher prices were rejected (bearish after a rally) |
| **Pin bar**      | General name for a candle with one very long wick | The long wick shows the rejected side |

A hammer and a shooting star are both pin bars; the names add *where* they appear (after a decline or a rally).

### Two-candle pattern: engulfing

- **Bullish engulfing:** a bearish candle, then a bullish candle whose **body covers** the previous body.
- **Bearish engulfing:** the mirror: a bullish candle, then a bearish body that covers it.

<Callout type="info" title="Clarified: what candles can tell you">
  The original said a marubozu "indicates institutional participation", that long wicks show "institutional
  rejection", and that dojis "often appear at reversal points". A candle shows who won a period and which prices were
  rejected; it can't show who was trading. Dojis appear everywhere, including mid-trend. These readings only become
  useful at a key level, which is the next point.
</Callout>

## Reading a Candle in Context

Read in this order:

1. **Location:** Is the candle at a key level: support/resistance, an order block, a range edge? A hammer at support is a signal; a hammer in the middle of a range is noise.
2. **Body:** Large = conviction; small = indecision.
3. **Wicks:** Which side was rejected, and how far?
4. **Sequence:** What did the last few candles do? Does this candle continue the move or stop it?
5. **Timeframe:** A daily candle summarises far more trading than a 5-minute one.

### Example (no chart) — hammer at support

EUR/USD has fallen to daily support at **1.0800**. The 4H candle opens at **1.0812**, drops to **1.0788**, and closes at **1.0815**: a small body near the top, and a lower wick of 24 pips against a 3-pip body. That's a hammer at support. A trader buys **1.0816**, stops below the low at **1.0782**, and targets the last 4H swing high at **1.0890**: risk 34 pips, reward 74 pips, **R:R 1 : 2.2** (planned).

## Timeframes and the Daily Close

| Timeframe   | What its candles are used for            |
| ----------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| Weekly / Daily | Direction and bias                    |
| 4H / 1H     | Setups at key levels                     |
| 15m / 5m    | Entry timing and confirmation            |

<Callout type="info" title="When the daily candle closes (Bangladesh time)">
  Most forex brokers close the daily candle at **5:00 PM New York**: that's **3:00 AM BD** in US summer and **4:00 AM
  BD** in US winter. A "daily candle" on your chart covers that 24-hour period, not the BD calendar day.
</Callout>

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake                                 | Fix                                                   |
| --------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| Trading a pattern with no context       | Only at key levels, in line with the higher timeframe |
| Judging a candle before it closes       | A hammer can become a bearish candle in the last minutes |
| Reading one candle alone                | Read the last few candles as a sequence               |
| Lower-timeframe signals against the higher timeframe | A 1m engulfing won't turn a strong 4H trend |
| Memorising dozens of patterns           | Learn the logic: body, wicks, location                |

## Practice

1. On a daily chart, find five hammers and five shooting stars.
2. For each, note whether it formed at a key level, and what price did over the next five candles.
3. Compare the ones at levels with the ones in the middle of nowhere.
4. Log it in your [trading journal](/en/tools/journal-template).

## Summary

| Part / pattern  | Meaning                                       |
| --------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| **Body**        | Who won and by how much                       |
| **Wicks**       | Prices that were rejected                     |
| **Doji / spinning top** | Indecision                            |
| **Marubozu**    | One side in control                           |
| **Hammer / shooting star** | Rejection of lower / higher prices |
| **Engulfing**   | One side overwhelmed the previous candle      |
| **Context**     | Where the candle forms decides if it matters  |
