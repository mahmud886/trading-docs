## From "Where" to "How"

The [Liquidity](/en/ict/liquidity) lesson explains **where** resting orders sit and why price is drawn to them. This lesson is about **execution**: how to recognise a sweep as it happens, how to confirm it, and where to enter, place the stop and take profit.

<Callout type="info" title="Definition">
  A **liquidity sweep** happens when price trades beyond a marked pool of liquidity, triggering the stops and pending
  orders there, and then **fails to hold beyond it**. Price closes back inside and displaces in the opposite direction.
  In ICT's AMD model, the sweep is the *manipulation* phase.
</Callout>

### Key terms

| Term                   | Meaning                                                                       |
| ---------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Raid**               | The push beyond the level that triggers the resting orders                    |
| **Close back inside**  | The candle (on your chosen timeframe) closes back on the original side of the level |
| **Displacement**       | A strong, large-bodied move away from the level                              |
| **MSS**                | Market Structure Shift: the displacement closes through the last opposing swing |
| **CE**                 | Consequent encroachment: the 50% level of an FVG                              |
| **Sweep extreme**      | The furthest price reached during the raid; the stop goes beyond it           |

## Anatomy of a Sweep

![Anatomy of a sweep — EUR/USD 5m: raid above PDH, close back inside with MSS, FVG retest entry](/images/ict/liquidity-sweep/eurusd-pdh-sweep-dark.png)

<video controls muted playsInline loop preload="metadata" src="/videos/ict/sweep-anatomy-explainer-dark.mp4" className="mt-6 w-full rounded-xl border border-border" />

Every tradable sweep has the same four parts:

| Part                        | What you see                                             | What it means                                  |
| --------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| **① Raid**                  | Price trades through a marked level (PDH, equal lows, session high …) | Resting stops and breakout orders are triggered |
| **② Close back inside**     | The candle closes back on the original side of the level | The breakout found no follow-through           |
| **③ Shift**                 | A displacement closes through the last opposing swing (MSS), often leaving an FVG | Order flow has turned                         |
| **④ Enter**                 | Price retraces into the FVG or OB left by the shift      | A defined entry with a stop beyond the raid    |

<Callout type="warning" title="Decide your timeframe before the session">
  In the EUR/USD chart, two 5-minute candles close above the PDH before the reversal. On the 15-minute chart the same
  move is a single candle with a wick above the PDH. Choose the timeframe you will judge "close back inside" on
  **before** the session starts, and keep to it. Switching timeframes after the fact makes every run look like a sweep.
</Callout>

## Types of Sweeps

| Type                     | Level to mark before the session                  | Typical timing                       |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| **Session sweep**        | Asian high/low; London high/low                   | London open takes Asia; NY takes London |
| **Daily / weekly sweep** | PDH/PDL, PWH/PWL                                  | NY AM, or early in the week          |
| **Equal highs / lows**   | Two or more touches at nearly the same price      | Any kill zone                        |
| **Swing sweep**          | The most recent clear swing high or low           | Any time; weakest alone              |
| **Trendline sweep**      | A line with three or more touches                 | Often the "breakout" that traps traders |

The more obvious the level, the more orders sit behind it, and the more meaningful the sweep. A random minor swing is weaker than a clean set of equal lows or the previous day's high.

## Sweep or Run?

![Sweep vs run — the same equal highs, two outcomes](/images/ict/liquidity/sweep-vs-run-dark.png)

A raid only becomes a sweep if price fails to hold beyond the level. If candles close beyond it and the retest holds, it is a **run** (a breakout), and fading it is trading against the market. The full comparison is in the [Liquidity](/en/ict/liquidity) lesson.

## Confirmation Checklist

Trade a sweep only when every line is ticked:

| ✓ | Condition                                                                 |
| - | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| ☐ | The level was **marked before** the session (not found afterwards)        |
| ☐ | Price traded **beyond** it (a touch is not a sweep)                       |
| ☐ | A candle **closed back inside**, on your chosen timeframe                 |
| ☐ | A **displacement** followed, with a body clearly larger than average      |
| ☐ | The displacement produced an **MSS** (a close through the last opposing swing) |
| ☐ | It left an **FVG or OB** to enter from                                    |
| ☐ | The sweep points toward the **higher-timeframe draw on liquidity**        |
| ☐ | It happened in a **kill zone**                                            |

### What invalidates a sweep

- Price **closes beyond** the level and holds; the retest holds too. That is a run.
- No MSS follows. Price drifts sideways or keeps going in the raid direction.
- After your entry, a candle **closes beyond the sweep extreme**. The raid has turned into a breakout, and the stop should already be there.

## Three Entry Models

| Model                        | Entry                                                     | Pros                          | Cons                                       |
| ---------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| **A. FVG CE** (Example 1)    | Limit at the 50% level of the FVG left by the displacement | Tight stop, good R:R          | Some moves never retrace that far          |
| **B. Order Block** (Example 2) | Limit at the proximal edge of the OB (often the sweep candle itself) | Clear level, stop just beyond | The OB can be wide on volatile pairs       |
| **C. Aggressive**            | At the close of the displacement (MSS) candle             | Rarely misses the move        | Wider stop, lower R:R, no retracement filter |

Models A and B wait for a retracement, so you will miss some trades. That is the price of a defined, tight risk.

## Stop and Target Placement

**Stop:** place it **beyond the sweep extreme**, plus a small buffer. Do not place it at the original level.

- The level has already been raided once. It can be tested again before the move starts.
- The buffer covers the spread and normal noise. In the examples it is 2 pips on EUR/USD (raid 1.0928 → stop 1.0930) and 2 points on gold (sweep 2,022 → stop 2,020).

**Targets:** go to the **opposite pool**, the draw on liquidity.

- **First target (optional partial):** internal liquidity, such as the nearest FVG or swing.
- **Final target:** the opposite external pool, such as PDL/PDH or equal lows/highs.

Calculate R:R from the actual entry and stop. If it is poor, skip the trade; do not move the stop to improve it.

## Timing: Kill Zones (Bangladesh Time)

| Kill zone        | BD time            | Typical sweep                                   |
| ---------------- | ------------------ | ----------------------------------------------- |
| **London**       | 12:00 PM – 3:00 PM | Asian high or low; equal highs/lows built overnight |
| **New York AM**  | 5:00 PM – 8:00 PM  | London high or low, PDH/PDL                     |
| **NY Lunch**     | 10:00 PM – 11:30 PM | Usually avoid: thin, choppy                    |
| **NY PM**        | 11:30 PM – 2:00 AM | Late-day sweeps of the NY AM range              |

These follow the [ICT Kill Zones](/en/ict/ict-kill-zones) lesson. Sweeps outside the kill zones happen too, but they come with less volume and are harder to read.

## Worked Example 1 — EUR/USD PDH Sweep, Sell (5m, NY AM)

<Callout type="info" title="About these examples">
  Both charts use **synthetic candles** built around the levels in this lesson. They show how the method works and are
  not records of real trades. R:R values are the planned risk-to-reward, not results.
</Callout>

**Before the session:** PDH **1.0920** has buy stops above it (BSL). PDL **1.0870** has sell stops below it (SSL), which is the draw.

**Observations** (chart above)

1. **Raid:** in the NY AM kill zone, price climbs into the PDH and spikes to **1.0928**.
2. **Close back inside + MSS:** a 12-pip bearish displacement, against a 4-pip average, closes back below the PDH and below the **1.0918** higher low. That is the 5m MSS.
3. **FVG:** the displacement leaves a bearish FVG at **1.0915 – 1.0920**, with its CE at 1.09175.
4. **Enter:** at **5:45 PM BD** price retraces to the CE. Sell at **1.0918**.

| Level     | Price  | Rationale                           |
| --------- | ------ | ----------------------------------- |
| Entry     | 1.0918 | FVG CE (model A)                    |
| Stop      | 1.0930 | Above the 1.0928 raid high + 2 pips |
| Target    | 1.0870 | PDL, the sell-side liquidity        |
| Risk      | 12 pips | 1.0930 − 1.0918                    |
| Reward    | 48 pips | 1.0918 − 1.0870                    |
| **R:R**   | **1 : 4.0** | 48 ÷ 12                       |

**Invalidation:** a 5m close above 1.0928.

## Worked Example 2 — Gold Equal-Lows Sweep, Buy (15m, London)

![Equal lows sweep — Gold 15m: sweep to 2,022, MSS, OB retest, buy 2,027](/images/ict/liquidity-sweep/gold-eql-sweep-dark.png)

**Before the session:** three relative equal lows near **2,025** hold sell stops below them (SSL). A prior high at **2,060** holds buy stops above it (BSL), which is the draw.

**Observations**

1. **Raid:** in the London kill zone, the 12:15 PM candle drops to **2,022**, sweeping the equal lows, and closes back above 2,025.
2. **Shift:** the next candle is a 7-point bullish displacement, against a 2.2-point average. It closes above the **2,031** lower high, which is the 15m MSS.
3. **OB:** the sweep candle itself (2,022 – 2,027.5) is the last bearish candle before the displacement. It is a bullish Order Block.
4. **Enter:** at **1:30 PM BD** price retraces into the OB. Buy at **2,027**.

| Level     | Price  | Rationale                              |
| --------- | ------ | -------------------------------------- |
| Entry     | 2,027  | OB proximal edge (model B)             |
| Stop      | 2,020  | Below the 2,022 sweep low + 2 points   |
| Target    | 2,060  | BSL at the prior high                  |
| Risk      | 7 pts  | 2,027 − 2,020                          |
| Reward    | 33 pts | 2,060 − 2,027                          |
| **R:R**   | **1 : 4.7** | 33 ÷ 7                            |

**Invalidation:** a 15m close below 2,022.

## The Process, Step by Step

1. **Prepare.** Mark PDH/PDL, PWH/PWL, the Asian range and any clean equal highs/lows. Write down the draw on liquidity.
2. **Wait.** Let price come to a marked level inside a kill zone. Do not chase.
3. **Watch the close.** On your chosen timeframe, did the candle close back inside? If not, stand aside.
4. **Confirm.** Require a displacement with an MSS.
5. **Plan.** Mark the FVG/OB, the entry, the stop beyond the sweep extreme, and the target at the opposite pool. Calculate R:R.
6. **Execute and manage.** Place the limit order. If price never retraces, there is no trade.

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake                                     | Fix                                                       |
| ------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| Fading every touch of a level               | A touch is not a sweep. Price must trade beyond and close back inside |
| Entering during the raid                    | Wait for the close back inside **and** the MSS            |
| Judging the close on whichever timeframe "looks right" | Fix the confirmation timeframe in advance        |
| Stop at the swept level                     | Stop beyond the sweep extreme, plus a buffer              |
| Trading sweeps against the HTF bias         | Those sweeps often turn into runs                         |
| Trading outside kill zones                  | Prefer London and NY AM; avoid NY lunch                   |
| Moving the stop to improve R:R              | If R:R is poor at the correct stop, skip the trade        |

## Risk Management

| Component            | Guideline                                                     |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Risk per trade**   | 1–2% of the account; less while learning                      |
| **Position size**    | Account risk ÷ stop distance ([calculator](/en/tools/position-calculator)) |
| **Stop**             | Beyond the sweep extreme + buffer                             |
| **Partial (optional)** | At internal liquidity                                        |
| **Max attempts**     | One attempt per level per session. If it fails, the level has probably become a run |

## Practice

1. Mark PDH/PDL and the Asian range for the last 20 trading days. For each, record whether London or NY swept it or ran it.
2. For every sweep, check whether an MSS followed within the next few candles of your chosen timeframe.
3. Replay five sweeps candle by candle. Write down the entry, stop and target **before** revealing the next candle.
4. Log the results in your [trading journal](/en/tools/journal-template). After 30 or more examples you will know how your pair behaves.

## FAQ

### How long can price stay beyond the level and still count as a sweep?

There is no fixed number of candles. Use the confirmation timeframe you chose in advance. If candles on *that* timeframe close beyond the level and the retest holds, treat it as a run.

### Is a sweep the same as Turtle Soup?

They are closely related. Turtle Soup is a specific way to trade a failed breakout of a recent high or low. It is covered in the next lesson, [Turtle Soup](/en/ict/turtle-soup).

### Can I enter as soon as the sweep candle closes back inside?

You can, but you are trading before the MSS confirms that order flow has turned. Expect more losing trades. If you do it, keep the stop beyond the sweep extreme and use a smaller size.

## Summary

| Aspect           | Key point                                                       |
| ---------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Definition**   | Price trades beyond a marked pool, fails to hold, closes back inside |
| **Anatomy**      | Raid → close back inside → shift (MSS) → enter                 |
| **Confirmation** | Close back inside + displacement + MSS, in a kill zone, toward the HTF draw |
| **Entry**        | FVG CE, OB proximal edge, or (aggressive) the MSS candle close  |
| **Stop**         | Beyond the sweep extreme + buffer                               |
| **Target**       | The opposite pool (draw on liquidity)                           |
| **Invalid**      | A close beyond the sweep extreme, or no MSS                     |
