## Why Time Matters

The earlier lessons describe **what** to look for: liquidity, a sweep, an MSS, an FVG. Kill zones describe **when**. The same pattern is more useful when it appears while the big sessions are opening and volume arrives, and less useful during quiet hours.

<Callout type="info" title="Definition">
  **Kill zones** are fixed time windows, set in New York time, in which ICT expects the day's most important moves to
  start: London (2–5 AM New York), New York AM (7–10 AM), and New York PM (1:30–4 PM). The New York lunch hour in
  between is usually avoided.
</Callout>

## The Windows in Bangladesh Time

![The kill zones on a 24-hour Bangladesh-time axis, for US summer and winter time](/images/ict/ict-kill-zones/kill-zone-clock-dark.png)

The windows are fixed in **New York time**. New York changes its clocks twice a year and Bangladesh does not, so the BD times move by one hour.

| Window          | New York time    | BD — US summer (EDT, mid-Mar → early Nov) | BD — US winter (EST, early Nov → mid-Mar) |
| --------------- | ---------------- | ----------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| Asian range     | 8 PM – 2 AM      | 6:00 AM – 12:00 PM                        | 7:00 AM – 1:00 PM                         |
| **London KZ**   | 2:00 – 5:00 AM   | **12:00 – 3:00 PM**                       | **1:00 – 4:00 PM**                        |
| **NY AM KZ**    | 7:00 – 10:00 AM  | **5:00 – 8:00 PM**                        | **6:00 – 9:00 PM**                        |
| NY lunch (avoid) | 12:00 – 1:30 PM | 10:00 – 11:30 PM                          | 11:00 PM – 12:30 AM                       |
| **NY PM KZ**    | 1:30 – 4:00 PM   | **11:30 PM – 2:00 AM**                    | **12:30 – 3:00 AM**                       |

<Callout type="warning" title="Corrected: EST vs EDT">
  The original lesson labelled the New York times "EST" but paired them with BD times that are only 10 hours later.
  That is the **US summer** (EDT) offset. During US **winter** time (EST), every BD time in the lesson is **one hour
  later**. The other lessons on this site use the summer times. Add one hour from early November to mid-March.
</Callout>

## What Usually Happens in Each Window

| Window      | Typical behaviour                                                       | Common setup                                     |
| ----------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| Asian range | Quiet, narrow range; builds liquidity on both sides                     | Mark its high and low; don't trade the middle    |
| **London**  | Often sweeps one side of the Asian range, then sets the day's direction | Asian-range sweep → MSS → OB/FVG entry           |
| **NY AM**   | Continues London's move, or reverses it if London ran to liquidity      | Pullback into a London FVG/OB; PDH/PDL sweeps    |
| NY lunch    | Thin, choppy, prone to false moves                                      | Usually none                                     |
| **NY PM**   | Late continuation, or a reversal of the NY AM move                      | Small setups; managing open trades               |

The original lesson tied these windows to the AMD phases: London as manipulation, NY as distribution. That is a useful way to read a day, not a rule. See [Power of 3 / AMD](/en/ict/power-of-3).

## The Asian Range

The Asian range is the reference for the London kill zone:

1. Mark the **high and low** from 6 AM to 12 PM BD (summer).
2. Expect London to trade beyond one side. That's where the stops are.
3. A sweep **plus a close back inside and an MSS** turns it into a setup. A clean break and hold is a trend day; don't fade it.

## Macro Times

ICT also points to short **macros** inside the sessions, about 20 minutes each, where displacement often starts. The original lesson lists these (BD times, US summer):

| Macro          | New York        | BD (summer)          |
| -------------- | --------------- | -------------------- |
| London         | 1:33 – 4:00 AM  | 11:33 AM – 2:00 PM   |
| NY AM 1        | 9:50 – 10:10 AM | 7:50 – 8:10 PM       |
| NY AM 2        | 10:50 – 11:10 AM | 8:50 – 9:10 PM      |
| NY PM          | 1:50 – 2:10 PM  | 11:50 PM – 12:10 AM  |

Treat macros as a refinement, not a requirement. The setup has to be there first.

## London Kill Zone Setup, Step by Step

1. **Before 12 PM BD:** mark the Asian high and low, PDH/PDL, and decide the [draw on liquidity](/en/ict/liquidity).
2. **12–3 PM BD:** wait for London to sweep the side opposite the draw.
3. **Confirm:** a close back inside, then an [MSS](/en/ict/mss) with displacement on 5m or 15m.
4. **Enter:** on the retrace into the [order block](/en/smc/order-blocks) or [FVG](/en/ict/fvg) the move left.
5. **Stop** beyond the sweep extreme; **target** the opposite liquidity.

## Worked Example — London Kill Zone, EUR/USD (15m)

![London kill zone — EUR/USD 15m: Asian range, sweep of the Asian low, MSS, order-block entry](/images/ict/ict-kill-zones/eurusd-london-kz-dark.png)

<Callout type="info" title="About this example">
  The chart uses **synthetic candles** built around the levels in this lesson (US summer time). It shows how the
  method works and is not a record of a real trade. R:R is the planned risk-to-reward, not a result.
</Callout>

1. **Asian range:** **1.0870 – 1.0885** between 6 AM and 12 PM BD.
2. **Sweep:** at **12:15 PM BD**, in the London kill zone, price takes the Asian low to **1.0867**.
3. **MSS:** at **12:30 PM BD** a 17-pip displacement closes above the 1.0879 lower high.
4. **Entry:** the sweep candle is the last down-close candle before the move, so it is the bullish order block. At 1:30 PM BD price returns to it. Buy at **1.0872**.

| Level     | Price  | Rationale                        |
| --------- | ------ | -------------------------------- |
| Entry     | 1.0872 | Bullish OB                       |
| Stop      | 1.0864 | Below the 1.0867 sweep low       |
| Target    | 1.0910 | Buy-side liquidity               |
| Risk      | 8 pips | 1.0872 − 1.0864                  |
| Reward    | 38 pips | 1.0910 − 1.0872                 |
| **R:R**   | **1 : 4.75** | 38 ÷ 8                     |

### Second example (no chart) — NY AM, Gold

London set the day's high at **2,048** and reversed. At **5:30 PM BD**, in the NY AM kill zone, price pulls back into a 15m bearish FVG. Sell at **2,040** (the FVG's CE), stop **2,049**, target **2,010**: risk $9, reward $30, **R:R 1 : 3.3**.

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake                                   | Fix                                                       |
| ----------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| Using summer BD times in winter           | Add one hour from early November to mid-March             |
| Trading the middle of the Asian range     | Wait for London to take one side                          |
| Trading NY lunch                          | Step away, or manage open trades only                     |
| Trading every kill zone                   | Master one first; London fits a Bangladesh working day well |
| A good time with no setup                 | Time filters setups; it doesn't create them               |

## Risk Management

| Component            | Guideline                                                        |
| -------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Risk per trade**   | 1–2% of the account; less while learning                         |
| **Trades per session** | One or two; stop after a loss in the same kill zone           |
| **Outside kill zones** | No new entries; manage existing ones                          |
| **News**             | Check the [economic calendar](/en/tools/economic-calendar); NY data often lands at 6:30 PM BD (summer) |

## FAQ

### Why does the time change in November and March?

The windows follow New York clocks. When the US moves its clocks, the BD time of each window moves by one hour.

### Do kill zones work for gold and indices?

Yes. They're tied to the London and New York sessions, which drive gold and US indices as well as major FX pairs.

### Can I trade outside the kill zones?

You can, but moves outside them tend to be smaller and less reliable. Most of the ICT models in these lessons assume a kill zone.
