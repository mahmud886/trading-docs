## What Is AMD?

**AMD** stands for **Accumulation, Manipulation, Distribution**. It is ICT's model for how price moves from one pool of liquidity to another. ICT also calls it **Power of 3**. This lesson covers the *why* of each phase and how to read it; [Power of 3](/en/ict/power-of-3) covers the daily trading template.

<Callout type="info" title="Not every move">
  The original lesson said "every candle, every session, every trading day" follows AMD, and quoted ICT to that effect
  without a source. Neither holds up: plenty of days range or trend without a clear false move. Treat AMD as a pattern
  to recognise when it appears.
</Callout>

---

## The Three Phases

| Phase            | What price does                                   | Why it matters                                           | Your job                   |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| **Accumulation** | Ranges quietly, often in Asia                     | Stops build above the range high and below the range low | Mark the high, low and any equal highs/lows |
| **Manipulation** | Runs out of one side of the range, then fails     | Takes those stops; breakout traders join the wrong side  | Don't chase; wait for the shift |
| **Distribution** | Moves hard the other way                          | Delivers price to the liquidity on the opposite side     | Enter on the retrace, hold to the target |

The manipulation leg is the [Judas swing](/en/ict/judas-swing). The confirmation that it has ended is an [MSS](/en/ict/mss) with displacement.

## AMD on Three Timeframes

| Scale       | Accumulation             | Manipulation                       | Distribution                 |
| ----------- | ------------------------ | ---------------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| **Session** | Asian range              | London takes one side              | London/NY deliver the other side |
| **Day**     | Around the daily open    | Low of day (bullish) or high of day (bearish) forms early | The rest of the day          |
| **Week**    | Monday                   | Often Monday–Wednesday sets the week's high or low | Rest of the week     |

Times in Bangladesh: Asia 6 AM – 12 PM, London kill zone 12 – 3 PM, NY AM kill zone 5 – 8 PM (US summer). Add one hour in US winter.

<Callout type="info" title="About these examples">

  not records of real trades. R:R is the planned risk-to-reward.

1. **Accumulation:** the Asian range is **2,035 – 2,042**. Bias is bearish.
2. **Manipulation:** at **12:15 PM BD** London spikes above the Asian high to **2,046**, taking the buy stops.
3. **MSS:** at **12:45 PM BD** a 6.4-point bearish candle closes below the 2,043 higher low and leaves a bearish FVG.
4. **Entry:** price retraces into the FVG. Sell **2,041**.

| Level   | Price | Note                       |
| ------- | ----- | -------------------------- |
| Entry   | 2,041 | Bearish FVG                |
| Stop    | 2,047 | Above the 2,046 spike high |
| Target  | 2,018 | Sell-side liquidity        |
| **R:R** | **1 : 3.8** | Risk $6, reward $23  |

## Reading AMD From a Candle

| Candle      | Open          | Wick beyond the open             | Body                       |
| ----------- | ------------- | -------------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| **Bullish** | Accumulation  | Lower wick = manipulation        | Open → close = distribution |
| **Bearish** | Accumulation  | Upper wick = manipulation        | Open → close = distribution |

<Callout type="warning" title="Corrected: the first move doesn't tell you the day">
  The original said "if the daily candle goes down first, expect a bullish day". That's backwards logic: on a bearish
  day price also often goes down from the open and keeps going. The first move only becomes a manipulation **after**
  price reverses with an MSS. Set the bias first from the higher timeframe, then read the first move against it.
</Callout>

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake                       | Fix                                                     |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| Trading the manipulation      | It's the trap; wait for the MSS                         |
| No clear accumulation         | No range, no AMD setup yet                              |
| Entering before the MSS       | Wait for the shift and the retrace                      |
| Ignoring session timing       | Manipulation usually comes at the London or NY open     |
| Fading the distribution       | Once it starts with displacement, don't counter-trade it |

## Practice

1. On 20 days, mark the Asian range, which side was taken first, and whether an MSS followed.
2. On 10 weeks, note which day made the week's high and low.
3. For each clean AMD day, write the entry, stop, target and planned R:R in your [trading journal](/en/tools/journal-template).
