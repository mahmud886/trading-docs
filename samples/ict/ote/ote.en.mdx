## Where OTE Fits

The previous lessons give you the **direction** (the [dealing range](/en/ict/dealing-range) and the [MSS](/en/ict/mss)) and the **zones** ([FVG](/en/ict/fvg), [order blocks](/en/smc/order-blocks)). **OTE** answers the last question: *how deep into the pullback should I be waiting?*

<Callout type="info" title="Definition">
  The **Optimal Trade Entry (OTE)** is the zone between the **62 %** and **79 %** retracement of an expansion leg.
  After a displacement shifts structure, price often pulls back into this zone before continuing. **70.5 %**, the
  middle of the zone, is ICT's preferred level.
</Callout>

OTE is the dealing-range idea applied to a single leg. Buying a bullish leg at 62–79 % means buying deep in that leg's **discount**. Selling a bearish leg at 62–79 % means selling deep in its **premium**.

---

## Key Levels

| Level      | Meaning                                                                  |
| ---------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **0 %**    | The end of the leg (the swing high for a bullish leg)                    |
| **50 %**   | Equilibrium of the leg; **not** OTE                                      |
| **62 %**   | Top of the OTE zone (some charts show 61.8 %; the difference is tiny)     |
| **70.5 %** | The "sweet spot": the middle of the zone                                 |
| **79 %**   | Bottom of the OTE zone                                                   |
| **100 %**  | The start of the leg; a close beyond it breaks the structure              |

---

## Drawing It

### Choosing the right leg

1. The leg must contain the **displacement that caused the MSS**. Without that, there is no shift to join.
2. **Start** at the swing that the move came from: for a bullish OTE, the low that was swept or that started the move.
3. **End** at the extreme of the move **before** the pullback starts.
4. If price makes a new extreme before retracing, move the end point and redraw.

### Direction of the Fibonacci

| Setup        | Draw from        | To                 | OTE zone is…                  |
| ------------ | ---------------- | ------------------ | ----------------------------- |
| **Bullish**  | Swing **low**    | Swing **high**     | Below the high, deep in the leg |
| **Bearish**  | Swing **high**   | Swing **low**      | Above the low, deep in the leg  |

**Formula** (works for both): level = leg end − (leg end − leg start) × %

Example 1: leg 1.0820 → 1.0880. 62 % = 1.0880 − 0.0060 × 0.62 = **1.0843**; 79 % = **1.0833**; 70.5 % = **1.0838**.

## Too Shallow, OTE, Too Deep

![How deep is the retracement — too shallow, OTE, too deep](/images/ict/ote/ote-depth-dark.png)

| Retracement              | What to do                                                            |
| ------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Shallower than 62 %**  | No OTE entry. Don't chase. A 50 % entry is not OTE                    |
| **62–79 %**              | The zone: look for a PD array and a reaction                          |
| **Beyond 79 %, inside 100 %** | Weaker; the zone has been passed. Some traders still use a PD array here; most wait |
| **Close beyond 100 %**   | Invalid: the leg's start is broken, so the structure that justified the trade is gone |

<Callout type="warning" title="One consistent invalidation rule">
  The original lesson says both that blowing through 79 % invalidates the setup and that the setup is invalid at
  100 %. This guide separates them. Beyond 79 % the zone has been **missed**; a **close beyond 100 %** makes the setup
  **invalid**.
</Callout>

## OTE Needs a PD Array

The zone is about 17 % of the leg, which is often wide. Treat OTE as **where** to look, and a PD array inside it as **the exact level**.

| Confluence             | Entry                                                    |
| ---------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| **OTE + FVG**          | The FVG's CE, if it sits in the zone (Example 2)          |
| **OTE + Order Block**  | The OB's proximal edge or mean threshold (Example 1)      |
| **OTE + Breaker**      | The breaker's edge, if it sits in the zone                |

If no array sits inside 62–79 %, entering on the Fibonacci level alone is a weaker trade.

---

## Rules

1. **Displacement first:** the leg must be a real impulse (body ≥ 2× average) with an **MSS**.
2. **HTF alignment:** the leg points the same way as the Daily/4H bias.
3. **First pullback:** the first return into the zone is the cleanest.
4. **Timing:** a kill zone (London 12–3 PM BD, NY AM 5–8 PM BD) adds participation.
5. **Stop beyond 100 %:** below the leg's low for buys, above its high for sells.

## Example 1 — Bullish OTE, EUR/USD (15m, NY AM)

![Bullish OTE — EUR/USD 15m: leg 1.0820 → 1.0880, retrace to 1.0836 into the OTE and a bullish OB](/images/ict/ote/eurusd-bullish-ote-dark.png)

<video controls muted playsInline loop preload="metadata" src="/videos/ict/ote-explainer-dark.mp4" className="mt-6 w-full rounded-xl border border-border" />

<Callout type="info" title="About these examples">
  The charts use **synthetic candles** built around the levels in this lesson. They show how the method works and are
  not records of real trades. R:R values are the planned risk-to-reward, not results.
</Callout>

**Observations**

1. **Expansion:** from the **1.0820** low, a 19-pip displacement (against a 5-pip average) closes above the 1.0842 lower high, which is the MSS. The leg tops at **1.0880**.
2. **The zone:** OTE is **1.0843 – 1.0833**, with the 70.5 % level at **1.0838**.
3. **Confluence:** the last bearish candle before the displacement forms a **bullish OB at 1.0831 – 1.0839**, which overlaps the zone.
4. **Entry:** at **6:15 PM BD** the first pullback reaches **1.0836**, inside both, and the candle closes back up. Buy at 1.0836.

| Level     | Price  | Rationale                           |
| --------- | ------ | ----------------------------------- |
| Entry     | 1.0836 | OTE + bullish OB                     |
| Stop      | 1.0818 | 2 pips below the 1.0820 leg start   |
| Target    | 1.0920 | Buy-side liquidity                  |
| Risk      | 18 pips | 1.0836 − 1.0818                    |
| Reward    | 84 pips | 1.0920 − 1.0836                    |
| **R:R**   | **1 : 4.7** | 84 ÷ 18                        |

**Invalidation:** a 15m close below 1.0820.

## Example 2 — Bearish OTE, Gold (5m)

![Bearish OTE — Gold 5m: leg 2,048 → 2,020, retrace into the OTE where a bearish FVG's CE sits on 70.5 %](/images/ict/ote/gold-bearish-ote-dark.png)

**Observations**

1. **Expansion:** from the **2,048** high, an 8.5-point displacement closes below the 2,043 higher low (MSS) and leaves a **bearish FVG at 2,038 – 2,042**. The leg bottoms at **2,020**.
2. **The zone:** OTE is **2,037 – 2,042**, with 70.5 % at **2,040**.
3. **Confluence:** the FVG sits almost exactly on the zone, and its CE (2,040) is on the 70.5 % level.
4. **Entry:** the first pullback reaches 2,040. Sell.

| Level     | Price  | Rationale                           |
| --------- | ------ | ----------------------------------- |
| Entry     | 2,040  | OTE 70.5 % + FVG CE                  |
| Stop      | 2,049  | $1 above the 2,048 leg start         |
| Target    | 2,005  | Sell-side liquidity                 |
| Risk      | $9     | 2,049 − 2,040                       |
| Reward    | $35    | 2,040 − 2,005                       |
| **R:R**   | **1 : 3.9** | 35 ÷ 9                         |

**Invalidation:** a 5m close above 2,048. The original lesson gives no session or time for this example, so the chart's times are placeholders.

## Stops and Targets

- **Stop:** beyond the 100 % level plus a small buffer. A stop at 79 % is tighter, but it is inside the leg, so a normal deep pullback can hit it.
- **TP1 (optional):** the leg's 0 % (the swing high or low that ended the expansion).
- **Final target:** the next external liquidity in the trade's direction.
- **Extensions:** some traders also use the −27 % and −62 % Fibonacci extensions as targets. Treat them as optional, and only where liquidity sits nearby.

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake                                          | Fix                                                       |
| ------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| Drawing OTE on a leg without displacement        | Only legs that caused an MSS                              |
| Entering at 50 %                                 | 50 % is equilibrium, not OTE; wait for 62–79 %            |
| Using the Fibonacci level alone                  | Look for an OB or FVG inside the zone                     |
| Drawing the wrong leg                            | From the move's origin to its extreme before the pullback |
| Stop at 79 %                                     | Beyond 100 %, plus a buffer                               |
| Chasing a shallow pullback                       | Missed is fine; wait for the next setup                   |

## FAQ

### 62 % or 61.8 %?

ICT uses 62 %. Many charting tools show 61.8 %. The difference is a fraction of a pip on intraday charts, so use whichever your platform has.

### What if price only reaches 60 %?

Then it didn't reach the zone. Some traders accept a PD array just above 62 %, but by the strict rule there is no OTE entry.

### Can I use OTE without an MSS?

You can draw it on any leg, but without a shift in structure you have no confirmed direction. The method assumes the MSS came first.
