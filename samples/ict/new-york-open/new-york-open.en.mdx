## Why New York Matters

New York overlaps the end of London. For a few hours every major market is active at once: FX, gold, US indices and US bonds. That overlap often produces the **largest move of the day**.

What New York does depends on **what London already did**:

- If London made a false move and reversed, NY often **continues** London's real direction.
- If London ran straight to liquidity, NY often **reverses** it.

In the AMD framework this is the *distribution* leg (see [Power of 3](/en/ict/power-of-3)). Treat that as a way to read the day, not a promise.

## Read London First

Before NY opens, answer three questions:

1. **Which side did London take?** The Asian high, low, or neither?
2. **Did London reverse after it?** If yes, the day's high or low may already be in.
3. **What did London leave behind?** FVGs and order blocks from London's displacement are NY's entry zones.

---

## Three NY Models

| Model                        | When                                            | Entry                                         |
| ---------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| **1. London continuation**   | London faked, reversed and displaced            | NY pullback into London's FVG or OB           |
| **2. Sweep of London's high/low** | London ran into liquidity without reversing | NY sweeps London's extreme, MSS, then OB/FVG |
| **3. Post-news FVG**         | High-impact data at 8:30 AM NY                  | After the release, retrace into the FVG the news candle left |

For model 3, never trade the release candle itself. Spreads widen and fills slip. Wait for it to close, mark its FVG, and treat the retracement like any other FVG entry. See the [economic calendar](/en/tools/economic-calendar).

1. **London:** sweeps the Asian low at **1.0850**, then rallies to **1.0890**. The displacement leaves a bullish FVG at **1.0871 – 1.0879**, with its CE at 1.0875.
2. **Afternoon:** price drifts above the FVG without touching it.
3. **NY AM:** from 5 PM BD price pulls back. At **6:15 PM BD** it dips to 1.0873 and fills the limit at the **CE, 1.0875**.

## Example 2 — NY Sweep of the London High, Gold (5m)

![NY reversal — Gold 5m: NY runs above the 2,048 London high to 2,050, MSS, order-block entry](/images/ict/new-york-open/gold-ny-sweep-dark.png)

1. **London:** high **2,048**, low **2,030**.
2. **Sweep:** at **6:30 PM BD** NY runs above the London high to **2,050**.
3. **MSS:** a 6.3-point bearish displacement closes below the 2,043 higher low.
4. **Entry:** the up-close sweep candle is the bearish OB. Price retraces to its low edge. Sell at **2,046**.

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake                                   | Fix                                                        |
| ----------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| Trading NY without reviewing London       | Answer the three London questions first                    |
| Trading the news candle                   | Wait for it to close; trade the retrace into its FVG       |
| NY lunch entries                          | Avoid 10–11:30 PM BD (summer)                              |
| Several mediocre trades                   | One clear setup per session is enough                      |
| Using summer BD times in winter           | Add one hour from early November to mid-March              |

## Practice

1. For 20 days, write down London's result (which side, reversal or not), then what NY did.
2. Count how often NY continued London versus reversed it, for your pair.
3. Mark London's FVGs and check how often NY's first pullback reached them.
4. Log the results in your [trading journal](/en/tools/journal-template).
