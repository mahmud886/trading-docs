## What Is the Unicorn Model?

The **Unicorn** is an entry where a **[breaker block](/en/ict/pd-arrays)** and a **[fair value gap](/en/ict/fvg)** overlap. The overlap is the entry zone: two reasons for price to react at one price.

It's named for being uncommon. When the breaker and the FVG don't actually overlap, it isn't a Unicorn.

---

## How It Forms

<Callout type="warning" title="Corrected: the breaker sequence">
  The original's bullish steps said "price is in an uptrend… a bearish OB forms… price breaks **down** through it". That
  describes a working bearish OB, not a failed one. A bullish breaker forms when price breaks **up** through a bearish
  OB after sweeping sell-side liquidity. The steps below are the corrected sequence.
</Callout>

### Bullish Unicorn

1. Price makes a low, rallies, and the **last up-close candle** before the next drop is a **bearish OB**.
2. Price drops to a **lower low**, sweeping the sell-side under the first low.
3. Price reverses with **displacement** and closes **above the bearish OB**. The failed OB is now a **bullish breaker**.
4. That displacement leaves a **bullish FVG**.
5. Where the breaker and the FVG overlap is the **Unicorn zone**. Buy when price returns to it.

### Bearish Unicorn

The mirror image: the last down-close candle (a bullish OB) before a rally, a higher high sweeping buy-side, then a displacement **below** the bullish OB. It becomes a bearish breaker, the displacement leaves a bearish FVG, and you sell the overlap.

| Step              | Bullish                         | Bearish                         |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| The OB            | Last up-close candle (bearish OB) | Last down-close candle (bullish OB) |
| The sweep         | Lower low (sell-side taken)      | Higher high (buy-side taken)     |
| The break         | Displacement closes above the OB | Displacement closes below the OB |
| The entry         | Retrace down into breaker ∩ FVG  | Retrace up into breaker ∩ FVG    |
| The stop          | Below the breaker                | Above the breaker                |

## Worked Example — Bullish Unicorn, EUR/USD (15m)

![Bullish Unicorn — EUR/USD 15m: bearish OB 1.0870–1.0880 broken upward, FVG 1.0875–1.0885, overlap 1.0875–1.0880](/images/ict/unicorn-model/eurusd-bullish-unicorn-dark.png)

<Callout type="info" title="About this example">
  The chart uses **synthetic candles** built around the levels in this lesson. It shows how the method works and is not
  a record of a real trade. R:R is the planned risk-to-reward.
</Callout>

1. **Context:** daily bullish, price pulled back into discount.
2. **Bearish OB:** the last up-close candle before the drop, **1.0870 – 1.0880**.
3. **Sweep:** price drops to 1.0855, under the 1.0862 low.
4. **Breaker + FVG:** a 20-pip displacement closes above the OB, which becomes a bullish breaker, and leaves an FVG at **1.0875 – 1.0885**.
5. **Unicorn zone:** the overlap is **1.0875 – 1.0880**. The original called the whole FVG the Unicorn zone; only the overlap qualifies.
6. **Entry:** price rallies to 1.0930, then returns. Buy **1.0880**, the top of the overlap.

| Level   | Price  | Note                         |
| ------- | ------ | ---------------------------- |
| Entry   | 1.0880 | Top of the Unicorn zone      |
| Stop    | 1.0868 | Below the breaker (1.0870)   |
| Target  | 1.0960 | Buy-side liquidity           |
| **R:R** | **1 : 6.7** | Risk 12 pips, reward 80 pips |

### Bearish Unicorn — NAS100 (no chart)

A 4H bullish OB at 17,900 – 17,930 fails as price crashes through it; the drop leaves an FVG at 17,890 – 17,920. The overlap is **17,900 – 17,920**. After a fall to 17,750 price retraces. Sell **17,905**, stop **17,935**, target **17,700**: risk 30, reward 205 points, **R:R 1 : 6.8** (planned).

## Why It Can Work

- **Two reasons at one price:** traders who bought the failed OB want out at breakeven, and the FVG is an imbalance price often revisits.
- **A defined stop:** the breaker's far side.
- **Context does the rest:** without a bias and a draw on liquidity, it's just a zone.

<Callout type="info" title="Removed claim">
  The original said the Unicorn "often produces 1:5+ R:R". R:R depends on where the target is, not the pattern. Plan it
  from the actual stop and target.
</Callout>

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake                                   | Fix                                                       |
| ----------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| Calling a non-overlapping breaker + FVG a Unicorn | They must share prices                            |
| Mixing up breaker direction               | A bullish breaker comes from a bearish OB broken **upward** |
| No sweep before the break                 | The lower low (or higher high) is part of the setup       |
| No higher-timeframe bias                  | Trade it only in the bias direction                       |
| Seeing Unicorns everywhere                | If you have to squint, skip it                            |

| **FVG**       | Left by the same displacement                    |

| **Stop**      | Below the breaker                                |
| **Target**    | Next buy-side liquidity                          |
